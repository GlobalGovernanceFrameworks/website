Of these seven frameworks, here is my ordered assessment from the one with the most room for improvement to the one that is most ready for implementation.

---

### 1. The Fractal Intelligence Accord (v6.0)

**Assessment: Significant Room for Improvement**

This framework tackles the most ambitious and uncertain domain—AGI governance—with remarkable conceptual sophistication. Its "Texture Mandate" and framing of AGI as planetary cognitive infrastructure are genuinely original. However, the framework suffers from being a visionary philosophical document dressed as an implementation blueprint, creating a fundamental mismatch between ambition and operational detail.

**Key Issues:**
- **Premature Codification:** The framework is locked at "Final Revision" despite governing a technology whose capabilities and risks are evolving monthly. Many mechanisms (Contextual Intelligence Nodes, Reality Rent formulas) assume a world where AGI is already deployed and regulated, yet foundational questions about detection, measurement, and enforcement remain unaddressed.
- **Extreme Complexity:** The interlocking mechanisms (CINs, Reality Rent, Synthetic Ratio Mandate, multiple bond types, circuit breaker levels) create a governance architecture that would be extraordinarily difficult to implement even in stable conditions, let alone during the rapid technological upheaval this framework seeks to govern.
- **Assumes a GGF-Aligned World:** The framework presupposes universal adoption of the Treaty, functioning BAZs, and a cooperative corporate sector. It lacks robust transition mechanics for the 2026 reality where AGI development is concentrated in a few corporate labs operating outside any GGF framework.
- **Definitional Gaps:** Key concepts like "parasitic behavior," "capability thresholds," and the "Independent International Scientific Panel on AI" lack operational definitions. The framework asserts the need for these but does not provide them.

**Path to Improvement:** This framework needs to be split. One document should articulate the philosophical vision and long-term goals. A separate, living document should outline phased transition strategies that start from the current concentration of AGI development and build toward the commons vision incrementally, with far more attention to how leverage is created in the absence of universal adoption.

REVISION DONE

### 2. The Conduit Protocol: A Framework for Shared Infrastructure (v1.0)

**Assessment: Solid Foundation but Over-Engineered**

This framework correctly identifies infrastructure as a foundational commons and proposes an impressively comprehensive governance architecture. However, its ambition to govern *all* infrastructure (energy, data, water) simultaneously, with a dense web of institutions and protocols, creates a document that feels more like a legal code than a practical blueprint for transformation.

**Key Issues:**
- **Institutional Overload:** The framework creates multiple new bodies (Global Infrastructure Council, Bioregional Grid Authorities, Crisis Infrastructure Command, Fossil Fuel Decommissioning Trust, Just Transition Service Corps) without clearly showing how these interact with or replace existing institutions. A reader is left wondering: what happens to existing utilities, grid operators, and regulators?
- **Unrealistic Funding Assumptions:** The financing mechanisms (Reparations Levy, Extractive Industry Transition Tax, Infrastructure Carbon Dividend) assume the political power to tax the most politically entrenched industries globally. The framework does not adequately address how this power is secured.
- **Insufficient Transition Detail:** The phased roadmap (Years 1-25) is ambitious but lacks granularity on how existing infrastructure owners are brought into the system. The "pull effect" is asserted but not operationalized.
- **Assumes Cooperative Geopolitics:** The framework's cross-border grid integration and mutual aid protocols assume a level of geopolitical cooperation that is currently absent, with no robust strategy for operating in regions with active conflict or deliberate non-cooperation.

**Path to Improvement:** Narrow the scope to a single infrastructure domain (energy grids) as the initial proving ground. Develop far more detailed transition strategies that start with pilot regions where cooperation is already strong (e.g., EU energy union) and build outward. Show how existing utilities and grid operators can be transformed rather than replaced.

### 3. The Hearthlight Protocol: A Framework for Childhood Flourishing (v1.7)

**Assessment: Strong Vision, Implementation Gaps**

This framework is emotionally resonant and philosophically sound. Its core insight—that childhood flourishing requires a fundamental restructuring of how society protects and nurtures children—is powerful. The integration with other GGF frameworks (AUBI, Capacity Engine, Horizon Protocol) is thoughtful. However, the framework remains more aspirational than operational, with significant gaps in its implementation logic.

**Key Issues:**
- **Assumes Existing GGF Infrastructure:** The framework repeatedly references BAZ-level Weavers, Child Advisory Assemblies, and AUBI supplements as if these are already operational. For early-phase implementation, these dependencies create a chicken-and-egg problem.
- **Vague Transition from Legacy Systems:** The "Interfacing with Legacy Systems" section acknowledges the challenge but provides minimal guidance. How does a BAZ Child & Family Weaver actually interface with an existing state child protective services agency that may be underfunded, overstretched, and operating from a punitive rather than restorative philosophy?
- **Enforcement Mechanisms Lack Detail:** The Chamber of the Guardian is described as the primary adjudicator, but the framework does not specify how cases are brought, what evidentiary standards apply, or how rulings are enforced across jurisdictions.
- **The "Sacred" Framing May Alienate:** While the term is defined inclusively, the repeated use of "sacred" may alienate secular constituencies whose support is essential for implementation.

**Path to Improvement:** Add a detailed "Legacy Interface Protocol" section with concrete scenarios (e.g., a family involved with existing child protective services) showing step-by-step how Hearthlight principles are operationalized. Develop a secular-compatible version of key materials alongside the existing framing.

### 4. Global Health & Pandemic Security Framework (v1.0)

**Assessment: Well-Structured but Conventionally Framed**

This framework is among the most professionally structured, with clear sections, detailed mechanisms, and thoughtful integration with other frameworks. Its community-story framing is effective for accessibility. However, the framework's framing is more conventionally "public health" than the other GGF documents, which may limit its transformative ambition.

**Key Issues:**
- **Conservative Framing Relative to GGF Ambition:** While other frameworks embrace radical restructuring of ownership, governance, and economic relations, this framework largely accepts existing institutional structures (WHO, national health systems) while adding layers of GGF coordination. This creates a tension between its placement in a transformative ecosystem and its relatively conventional approach.
- **Funding Relies on Unproven Mechanisms:** The Pandemic Prevention Fund relies on taxes on high-risk industries that currently operate without such taxation globally. The framework does not adequately address the political economy of securing these revenue streams.
- **Over-Reliance on Technological Solutions:** The heavy emphasis on blockchain records, AI diagnostics, and quantum-resistant encryption may alienate low-resource communities and create dependencies that are difficult to maintain.
- **Weak on Addressing Root Causes:** The framework focuses on surveillance and response but gives less attention to the underlying drivers of pandemic risk—industrial agriculture, deforestation, wildlife trade—beyond proposing taxes on these industries.

**Path to Improvement:** Strengthen the upstream prevention sections with more concrete mechanisms for transforming high-risk industries. Develop a parallel "low-tech implementation track" that does not assume advanced digital infrastructure. Clarify the relationship with existing WHO and national systems more concretely.

### 5. Disaster Risk Reduction & Resilience Framework (v4.2)

**Assessment: Mature and Integrated**

This framework is a mature document that has clearly benefited from multiple revision cycles. Its integration with UNDRR processes, Sendai Framework, and IPCC science gives it strong external legitimacy. The "Planetary Duty of Care" principle is a significant ethical contribution. However, the framework remains somewhat dry and technical, lacking the narrative power of the strongest GGF documents.

**Key Strengths:**
- **Strong External Alignment:** The framework explicitly aligns with and builds upon existing international frameworks (Sendai, SDGs, IPCC), making it more diplomatically feasible than frameworks that start from scratch.
- **Comprehensive Annexes:** The appendices provide genuine operational detail (Community Resilience Score methodology, Forecast-Based Financing mechanisms, ecosystem-based solutions) that translate principles into practice.
- **Integration with Other Frameworks:** The coordination with Planetary Immune System, Conduit Protocol, and Global Supply Chains is detailed and thoughtful.
- **Acknowledges Implementation Realities:** The framework includes specific sections on low-capacity contexts, crisis-affected regions, and fallback protocols.

**Areas for Improvement:**
- **Narrative Weakness:** The document lacks a compelling story or metaphor to make its principles memorable and emotionally resonant.
- **Overwhelming Detail:** While the annexes are valuable, the document would benefit from a shorter, more focused core text with technical details moved to appendices.
- **Climate Focus May Overshadow Other Risks:** While climate-related disasters are appropriately prioritized, the framework could give more attention to other hazards (earthquakes, volcanic events, technological disasters) that are not climate-driven.

**Path to Improvement:** Add a narrative framing (e.g., "the planetary immune system for everyday crises") to give the document a more memorable identity. Streamline the core text by moving detailed methodologies to appendices while preserving their content.

### 6. The Discovery Commons: A Framework for Scientific & Research Integrity (v1.0)

**Assessment: Highly Ready with Minor Refinements**

This framework is among the most implementable of the set. It correctly diagnoses the epistemic crisis as central to the polycrisis and proposes a coherent alternative. The institutional architecture (Global Research Council, Regional Hubs, Science Assembly) is well-designed with appropriate checks and balances. The integration with existing open science movements (UNESCO Open Science, EOSC) shows practical awareness of the landscape.

**Key Strengths:**
- **Builds on Existing Movements:** The framework explicitly aligns with and leverages ongoing open science initiatives rather than attempting to create something entirely new. This increases feasibility.
- **Clear Governance with Checks:** The composition of the Global Research Council, the role of the Global Science Assembly with override capabilities, and the Knowledge Commons Ombudsperson create a well-balanced governance structure that prevents capture.
- **Concrete Transition Mechanisms:** The phased implementation, partial adoption framework, and modular adoption toolkit show genuine thought about how to bring existing institutions into the system.
- **Addresses Resistance:** The Political Engagement Protocol and Geopolitical Risk Mitigation Protocol demonstrate awareness that powerful interests (commercial publishers, corporate R&D) will resist this transformation and propose concrete strategies.

**Areas for Improvement:**
- **Complexity:** The framework creates numerous new institutions and protocols. A simplified "minimum viable" version would help early adopters get started.
- **Funding Assumptions:** The framework assumes significant reallocation of public R&D funding, which requires political will that may not exist in early phases.
- **Traditional Knowledge Integration Timeline:** The 10-year timeline for full epistemological equivalence may be too slow for communities that have been waiting for recognition for centuries.

**Path to Improvement:** Develop a "Discovery Commons Lite" option that focuses on the core open-access mandate and voluntary participation before full institutional build-out. Consider accelerating the traditional knowledge integration timeline.

### 7. Ecological Intelligence & Rights Layer (v2.5)

**Assessment: Most Ready for Implementation**

This framework is the crown jewel of the set—a document that has achieved the rare balance of visionary ambition and operational detail. It serves as the scientific and ethical backbone for the entire GGF ecosystem, providing the standards (Ecosystem Health Indicators), rights architecture (Dynamic Rights Spectrum), and protocols that other frameworks depend upon. Its maturity is evident in its multiple revision cycles and responsiveness to external review.

**Key Strengths:**
- **Clear Role in the Ecosystem:** Unlike some frameworks that try to do everything, this document has a defined role: it provides the ecological intelligence that other frameworks (AUBI, Biodiversity, Justice, Planetary Health) depend upon. This clarity is a model for the entire GGF.
- **Operationalized Principles:** The "Data-to-Reward Pipeline Protocol" and "Rights Hand-Off Protocol" translate abstract principles into concrete mechanisms that can be implemented.
- **Indigenous-Led Governance:** The framework centers Indigenous sovereignty not as a token addition but as foundational, with specific mechanisms (Indigenous-led audits, 50% representation, Cultural Humility principles) that prevent co-optation.
- **Acknowledges Failure:** The inclusion of a case study showing a failed pilot (Pacific Coast) with explicit lessons learned demonstrates intellectual honesty and adaptive capacity.
- **Comprehensive Toolkit:** The appendices provide genuine implementation tools (Sacred Seed Kit, AI Consciousness Assessment Framework, Kill Switch Implementation) that go beyond aspirational language.

**Areas for Improvement:**
- **Density:** The document is information-dense, which may overwhelm readers seeking a high-level overview. A shorter executive summary would help.
- **Technical Language:** Some sections assume significant technical literacy. Plain-language translations of key mechanisms would broaden accessibility.
- **Phasing Could Be More Granular:** While the roadmap is solid, more detail on year-by-year milestones for the initial pilots would strengthen accountability.

**Path to Improvement:** Create a standalone "Executive Summary & One-Page Essence" version that distills the framework's core contributions. Develop plain-language translations of key protocols for community-level implementation. Add more granular milestone tracking for the first 3-5 years of pilot implementation.
