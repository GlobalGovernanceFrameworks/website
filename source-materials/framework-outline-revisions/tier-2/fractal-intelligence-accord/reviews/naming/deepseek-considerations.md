## The Name: "Fractal Intelligence Accord"

### What Works

**"Fractal"** is doing important conceptual work. It signals:
- **Self-similarity across scales** — the governance structure mirrors the intelligence it governs (local autonomy nested within global coordination)
- **Decentralization without fragmentation** — not a flat network, but organized coherence
- **Resistance to centralization** — the opposite of the monolithic corporate AGI model

**"Intelligence"** is more precise than "AGI." It leaves room for the possibility that intelligence takes forms we don't yet recognize, and signals that we're governing the *capability* not just the *technology*.

**"Accord"** implies:
- Agreement among diverse parties, not imposition
- Volitional participation, not coercion
- A living document, not a static treaty

### What Doesn't Work

**"Fractal" is obscure.** For most readers, it's a math term they vaguely remember from high school. It signals sophistication but at the cost of accessibility. If you need to explain the name, the name isn't doing its job.

**It's long.** Five syllables. "Fractal Intelligence Accord" is a mouthful. In conversation, it becomes "FIA" (another acronym) or "the Accord" (vague).

**It doesn't evoke what it's protecting.** The name tells you about the structure (fractal) but not about the purpose (human meaning, texture, sovereignty). The Texture Mandate is the heart of this framework, but the name doesn't reflect it.

---

## The Alternative: "The AGI Accord"

### What Works

**Immediately clear.** Everyone knows what AGI is (or thinks they do). No explanation needed.

**Short.** Three syllables. "The AGI Accord" is easy to say, easy to remember.

**Direct.** It names what it governs. No ambiguity.

### What Doesn't Work

**It centers the technology, not the human.** AGI is the *object* of governance, but the purpose of this framework is to protect what makes life meaningful. The name doesn't reflect that.

**It may age poorly.** "AGI" is a term from a specific moment. What if we develop intelligence that doesn't fit the "general" framing? What if the term falls out of use?

**It cedes the naming ground.** By using "AGI," we're playing on their terms. The corporate AGI project has already defined the language. Using their terms subtly accepts their framing.
