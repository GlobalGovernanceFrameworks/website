The structure is strong and the diagnosis is right — it correctly identifies that AGI governance is fundamentally a question of controlling three things: compute, context/data, and capability deployment. The five-pillar architecture maps onto those cleanly.

A few honest observations:

**What's working well:** The Contextual Intelligence Nodes concept is the most original contribution here and the most important. The "Reality Rent" framing is sharp — it reframes the Global South's relationship to AI development from passive displacement to structural participation. That's a genuine policy innovation, not just rhetoric. The Risk Bonding mechanism (Pillar IV) is also concrete in a way that many governance frameworks aren't — it has actual teeth.

**Where I'd push back:**

Pillar III (Fractal Architecture) is the weakest as currently described. "The Autonomy Rule — local branches can adjust parameters without permission from the Global Trunk" sounds clean but sidesteps the hard question: what happens when a BAZ-level branch fine-tunes in ways that conflict with another BAZ, or that the Global Trunk considers a safety violation? The subsidiarity mechanics here need an actual arbitration process, not just a statement of autonomy.

Pillar II's tier classification is also underdeveloped. The outline places this at Tier 2 (Economic & Resource Transition), but the Compute Commons (Pillar I) is clearly Tier 1 global infrastructure — potentially more significant than the Aurora Accord in terms of power concentration risk. There may be a mismatch between the frame and the actual weight of what's being governed.

**The biggest gap:** There's no section on *transition mechanics* — how do you get from the current state (a handful of corporations controlling frontier compute and training runs) to this architecture? The Oracle Protocol and TGIF both have implementation roadmaps. Without one here, this risks being aspirational without a theory of change.

Gemini's recommendation to start with Pillar II makes sense — it's the most developed and most novel. But I'd suggest adding a short section on the transition problem either before or after, even as a placeholder, so the structural gap doesn't get forgotten.
