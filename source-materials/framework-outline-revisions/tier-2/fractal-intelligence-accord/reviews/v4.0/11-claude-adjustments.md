Most of them are good, but not all equally. Let me go through them honestly:

**Agree strongly:**

The synthetic ratio surcharge is excellent — concrete, creates market incentives, and addresses model collapse risk which is underspecified in the current outline. The dynamic recalibration of Reality Rent annually is also necessary; a static formula will ossify.

The "continuous monitoring clause" for Pillar V (automatic compute throttling if SGC classification is delayed) is the kind of teeth the handoff protocol needs.

The Shadow Commons Acceleration sub-phase is realistic — generating empirical evidence before regulatory fights is exactly the right sequencing.

**Agree with caveats:**

The Ostrom mapping table is intellectually valuable but probably belongs in a companion document or appendix, not the outline itself. Don't let it slow down the drafting phase.

The "no foreign-adversary influence" clause is politically fraught. It's framed in U.S. national security rhetoric that could easily be weaponized to exclude Global South actors or Chinese researchers from CIN participation — which would undermine the entire subsidiarity premise. Worth flagging this tension explicitly if included rather than importing the clause uncritically.

**Disagree:**

The "AI sovereignty compatibility" preamble bullet is unnecessary. The framework already implies this through the fractal architecture — adding an explicit bullet reads as political marketing rather than governance design, and it could actually weaken the anti-monopoly mandate by giving states an excuse to build isolated national trunks.

The horizon review mandate (binding adjustment triggers if >5% probability of catastrophic misalignment) sounds rigorous but isn't — probabilistic forecasting of AGI risk over 2040-2100 timescales has no credible methodology yet. Making governance triggers depend on these forecasts creates false precision and potential manipulation. Better to tie the review mandate to capability thresholds than probability estimates.
