### 1. The Danger of a "Secondary Market" for Risk Bonds (Disagree)

DeepSeek suggested allowing Proof-of-Context bonds to be traded or reinsured to "prevent risk concentration."

* **The Hidden Risk:** This is exactly how the 2008 financial crisis happened (Mortgage-Backed Securities). If a tech giant can buy a bond, and then chop up the risk and sell it to a secondary reinsurance market, they lose their "skin in the game."
* **The Fix:** We must explicitly state that **Proof-of-Context Bonds are non-transferable and non-financializable**. The entity deploying the AI must hold the bag. If the risk is too high for them to hold alone, they shouldn't deploy the Swarm.

### 2. The "Circuit Breaker" Bottleneck (Modify)

DeepSeek suggested that triggering the Kill Switch should require a "2/3 majority of active CINs *and* confirmation from two independent technical systems."

* **The Hidden Risk:** If an autonomous AI swarm is actively hallucinating and destroying a local supply chain in Kenya, the local Kenyan BVN cannot wait for a two-thirds global vote from thousands of other CINs to hit the brakes. That is a catastrophic bottleneck.
* **The Fix:** Split the Circuit Breaker into two levels:
* *Local Emergency Brake:* A single BVN can sever the AI's access to its specific local context instantly if harm is detected.
* *Global Kill Switch:* Halting the *Global Trunk* entirely requires the 2/3 global consensus DeepSeek mentioned.



### 3. The 20% Compute Cap (Modify)

DeepSeek pointed out that a hard 20% cap on compute might be too bold/rigid and suggested making it dynamic.

* **The Hidden Risk:** Setting arbitrary percentages in a constitutional document means it will be outdated in 18 months as hardware architectures (like quantum or photonic computing) evolve.
* **The Fix:** Instead of a hard 20%, use the language: *"Algorithmic Anti-Monopoly Thresholds tied to the Gini coefficient of global compute distribution."* This makes it a mathematical principle rather than a rigid political number.
