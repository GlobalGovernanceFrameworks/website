### 1. The High Seas Fix: Weaponizing "Port State Control"
The assessment correctly points out that enforcing bans on destructive practices in the High Seas (areas beyond national jurisdiction) is underdeveloped. You cannot easily police the middle of the Pacific Ocean.
* **The Fix:** Shift the enforcement mechanism from the ocean to the land via **Absolute Port State Control**. Every deep-sea mining vessel and illegal fishing trawler eventually has to dock to offload its cargo or refuel. The framework must mandate that Bioregional Autonomous Zones (BAZs) and participating nations close their ports entirely to any vessel, corporation, or flag-state that violates High Seas regenerative protocols. If they cannot dock, they cannot profit. You govern the High Seas by locking the gates at the shore.

### 2. Confronting the ISA: The Deep-Sea Moratorium
The assessment notes that the relationship with existing bodies like the International Seabed Authority (ISA) is underdeveloped. This is a critical vulnerability, as the ISA is actively preparing to issue permits for deep-sea mining, which could permanently destroy benthic ecosystems.
* **The Fix:** The framework must move from passive coexistence to active legal confrontation. Introduce the **ISA Non-Recognition Clause**. The Global Governance Frameworks explicitly refuse to recognize the legitimacy of any deep-sea mining permit issued by the ISA until a 50-year independent scientific moratorium is completed. Any corporation acting on an ISA permit is automatically added to the Tier 1 Blacklist Protocol and subjected to the Port State blockades mentioned above.

### 3. Phasing Legal Personhood (The Subsidiarity Ladder)
The assessment rightly critiques the 2030 target for granting legal personhood to marine ecosystems as politically implausible. 
* **The Fix:** Replace the blanket 2030 global target with a **Cascading Jurisdictional Timeline**. 
    * *Phase 1 (2026-2030):* Legal personhood is established *only* at the BAZ level for hyper-local, easily defined bodies of water (e.g., a specific bay, a local coral reef, a river delta).
    * *Phase 2 (2030-2038):* Participating nations scale this to their 200-mile Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZs), using the successful BAZ precedents to satisfy national courts.
    * *Phase 3 (2038+):* Legal personhood is scaled to the High Seas via the Treaty for Our Only Home, only after the legal mechanics have been perfected at the local and national levels.

To fix the "tacked on" feeling of the Silent Seas Protocol, it must be wired directly into the economic engine of Pillar 2 (Circulation).
* **The Fix:** The proposed "Acoustic Tax" should not be a separate global fee. It should be applied as a **Dynamic Docking Tariff**. When commercial ships enter BAZ-controlled ports, their docking fees are calculated based on their submitted acoustic logs. Ships that utilized "Slow Steaming Corridors" or installed quiet-propulsion technology receive massive fee reductions (subsidized by the Global Commons Fund).
