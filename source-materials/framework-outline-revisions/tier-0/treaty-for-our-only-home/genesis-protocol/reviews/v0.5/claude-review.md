**The Genesis Protocol is where I'd focus.** It's impressively ambitious and the steel-manning has clearly strengthened it, but there are structural issues that the steel-manning rounds may have addressed rhetorically without resolving architecturally:

**1. The cold-start bootstrapping is still circular.** The "Pluriversal Sovereign" is philosophically elegant — three-source consent creates new authority, infinite regress solved. But operationally: getting 30+ nation ratifications, 20+ Indigenous Letters of Intent, AND a founding referendum majority requires institutional infrastructure (the Interim Secretariat, the Genesis Fund, the civic lottery apparatus) that doesn't exist yet, and can't exist until the funding sources (Peace Tithe) are activated, which requires ratifications. **2. The Network Effects Protocol contradicts GGF's own values.** "Make cost of non-participation catastrophic" and "economic isolation from world's regenerative finance engine" is structurally identical to the economic coercion the GGF elsewhere calls a parasitic control mechanism. If nations join because exclusion is "economically suicidal," that's not pluriversal sovereignty — that's replacing one coercive system with another. The protocol needs a theory of *voluntary* attraction that doesn't depend on manufactured exclusion dynamics. The Permeation Mandate (Section XI) actually provides this — upgrade from within, make participation attractive through demonstrated superiority — but it runs directly against the Network Effects Protocol's "stick" logic.

**3. The Dual-Track creates exactly the legitimacy shadow it claims to prevent.** Emergency Directors with real power (existential threat response, emergency adjudication) for 24 months, operating before democratic bodies are established, is exactly how "temporary" authorities become permanent structural features. The 24-month sunset and cooling-off period are mitigation, not prevention. Historical precedent is overwhelmingly against voluntary dissolution of emergency powers — every example I can think of (Rome's dictator → Caesar, post-9/11 surveillance → permanent, wartime economic controls → decades) shows the institutional inertia problem. The steel-manning response to the "Tech Billionaire" scenario ("I could deploy 100 aligned AI systems in 24 months") is real, but the cure may be as dangerous as the disease.

**4. The Peace Tithe has no enforcement mechanism pre-ratification.** It's described as "mandatory" but the word is doing no work until there's an institution that can mandate it. The $2B figure (0.1% of $2T global military spending) assumes universal participation, but the whole point is that P5 nations may opt out. The realistic early-stage budget is much smaller and the document doesn't model this honestly.

**5. The timeline is at least 3-5x too aggressive.** The EU took 7 years from the Schuman Declaration to the Treaty of Rome with 6 countries sharing cultural affinity and a fresh memory of war. The ICC took 4 years from Rome Statute to entry into force. The Genesis Protocol proposes 24 months for the most complex international institutional founding in history, across every culture and legal system on Earth.
