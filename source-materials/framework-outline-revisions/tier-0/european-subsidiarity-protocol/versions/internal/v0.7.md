### An operational enhancement for Article 5 and the European project

**Status:** Revision 0.7 (GSI Integration — Fully Specified Diagnostic Engine)
**Target systems:** European Commission, European Central Bank (ECB), Committee of the Regions
**Parent frameworks:** Adaptive Universal Basic Income (AUBI), Financial Systems Framework, Nested Economies

## 1. Executive summary: Solving the latency crisis

The European Union represents the most advanced polycentric governance experiment in modern history, anchored constitutionally by the Principle of Subsidiarity (TEU Article 5). However, the EU is currently experiencing a severe structural latency crisis (as diagnosed by the Draghi Report on Competitiveness). The attempt to manage 27 diverse national economies through centralized, standardized regulation has triggered an **Averaging Problem**—stifling local innovation and generating massive bureaucratic friction.

The European Subsidiarity Protocol is not a political treaty; it is an **operational enhancement to subsidiarity implementation**. It provides the mathematical diagnostic tools (The Global Subsidiarity Index) to execute Article 5 accurately, and introduces specific routing protocols to unlock regional autonomy while preserving European interoperability.

**This protocol does not change what the EU is. It clarifies what it has always been attempting to become: a subsidiarity-native governance system.**

**Memetic shield:** All components of the protocol are voluntary, locally governed, and designed to increase—not bypass—democratic accountability at the closest level to citizens.

---

## 2. The Averaging Problem: A concrete case

Consider the **demographic and care crisis**—one of the EU's most politically toxic challenges.

**Without GSI:** Centralized approaches attempt to solve aging populations and care deficits through top-down migration quotas (high political friction) and massive macro-healthcare budgets (unsustainable). These policies average across regions with vastly different cultural care capacities, informal network densities, and demographic profiles. Political backlash intensifies. Budgets strain. Outcomes remain uneven.

**With GSI:** The index identifies regions where informal care capacity is high (measured through the European Civic Value Registry) and routes resources toward recognition infrastructure rather than formal care hiring. Regions with low capacity receive different support. The same problem—care deficit—is solved at different scales without uniform policy triggering uniform backlash.

*The data enabling this diagnostic comes from the European Civic Value Registry (Section 8), which measures informal care capacity at the local level—something traditional macroeconomic indicators cannot capture.*

**Beyond social outcomes:** By shifting localized care burdens from formal healthcare budgets to recognition-based infrastructure, the protocol removes **massive fiscal pressure from the primary Euro and national healthcare systems**. This is not merely a social innovation—it is a macroeconomic stabilization mechanism.

## 3. The diagnostic engine: Operationalizing Article 5

Currently, the EU relies on political negotiation to determine if a decision should be made at the local, national, or Union level. This protocol introduces a rigorous engineering standard.

### 3.1 The Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI)

The GSI is a diagnostic measurement protocol deployed across the EU to identify the exact geographic and institutional scale where relevant information exists. It is grounded in Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety: only variety can absorb variety—a governance system must match the complexity of the society it regulates.

The GSI measures the **ratio** between societal variety (complexity, diversity, dynamism of the governed population) and governance variety (flexibility, adaptability, differentiation of the governing system).

The GSI will be co-developed with **Eurostat** for methodological transparency and validated through existing **Regulatory Impact Assessment** frameworks.

### 3.2 Four dimensions, sixteen indicators

The GSI measures governance architecture across four dimensions, each composed of four indicators scored on a 0–10 scale:

**Dimension 1 — Decision Proximity:** How close are decisions to those affected?

| Indicator | Measurement | Data Sources |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 1.1 Administrative Distance | Average layers between citizen and decision-maker across 5 policy domains | Government org charts, Eurostat administrative data, policy tracing |
| 1.2 Fiscal Sovereignty | % of subnational budget controlled locally vs. mandated from above | National budget analyses, OECD fiscal decentralization data |
| 1.3 Regulatory Autonomy | Ability to adapt EU/national rules to local conditions | Legal analysis, opt-out provisions, regulatory sandbox inventories |
| 1.4 Emergency Response Latitude | Local authority during crises | EU Civil Protection Mechanism data, crisis case studies |

**Dimension 2 — Knowledge Inclusion:** Whose intelligence informs decisions?

| Indicator | Measurement | Data Sources |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 2.1 Expert vs. Experiential Balance | % policy inputs from experts vs. citizens | Committee of the Regions consultations, parliamentary transcripts |
| 2.2 Participatory Mechanism Quality | Quality of citizen involvement in decision-making | Municipal records, European Civic Value Registry (where deployed) |
| 2.3 Traditional & Local Practice Integration | Recognition of regional knowledge in policy design | Land management agreements, regional cultural practice inventories |
| 2.4 Feedback Loop Efficiency | Time from problem identification to policy adjustment | Ombudsman reports, policy revision logs, emergency declarations |

**Dimension 3 — Resilience Architecture:** How systems handle disruption and diversity.

| Indicator | Measurement | Data Sources |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 3.1 Critical System Redundancy | Backup mechanisms in key infrastructure systems | EU Critical Infrastructure assessments, system audits |
| 3.2 Adaptive Capacity | Speed of institutional response to shocks | Crisis response data, COVID recovery analyses |
| 3.3 Diversity Resilience | Handling of cultural/regional differences | Policy impact assessments, regional diversity metrics |
| 3.4 Long-term Sustainability | Balance of short vs. long-term planning | National planning documents, EU Semester reports |

**Dimension 4 — Cohesion & Integration:** How distributed systems maintain unity without fragmentation.

| Indicator | Measurement | Data Sources |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| 4.1 Inter-Level Coordination | Effectiveness of protocols linking local → national → EU | Joint coordination agreements, multi-level governance exercises |
| 4.2 Shared Value Alignment | Degree of common principles across governance levels | Eurobarometer, constitutional analyses |
| 4.3 Conflict Resolution Mechanisms | Speed and fairness of dispute resolution | CJEU case data, mediation outcomes |
| 4.4 Solidarity Metrics | Resource sharing between regions | EU cohesion fund distribution, cross-regional transfer analysis |

### 3.3 The EU Complexity Adjustment Factor (EU-CAF)

Comparing Portuguese rural municipalities with the Paris metropolitan region requires contextual calibration. The EU-CAF adjusts raw GSI scores to account for intra-EU variation.

**Formula:** `EU-CAF = (G × E × D × T) ÷ C`

Where:
- **G** = Geographic Diversity Score (0–10): Climate zones (Köppen), terrain types, ecological regions per NUTS-2 area. Sources: EEA, Copernicus.
- **E** = Ethnolinguistic Diversity Score (0–10): Effective language groups, ethnic fractionalization. Sources: Eurostat population statistics, regional census data.
- **D** = Development Disparity Score (0–10): Regional Gini coefficient, NUTS-2 GDP variance, urban-rural service access ratio. Sources: Eurostat regional GDP, EU-SILC.
- **T** = Threat Environment Score (0–10): Climate vulnerability (EEA indicators), border tension, disaster frequency (EM-DAT). Sources: EEA, EU Civil Protection.
- **C** = Central Capacity Score (1–10, floor of 1): Government effectiveness (World Bank WGI), Corruption Perceptions Index, digital infrastructure maturity. Sources: World Bank, Transparency International, DESI.

**Typical EU-CAF ranges:**
- Low-complexity regions (e.g., Luxembourg, Malta): EU-CAF ≈ 0.3–0.8
- Medium-complexity (e.g., Sweden, Netherlands): EU-CAF ≈ 1.5–3.0
- High-complexity (e.g., Spain, Italy, Romania): EU-CAF ≈ 3.0–6.0

For each indicator, the EU-CAF generates **contextual target ranges** rather than universal benchmarks. A high Administrative Distance score that is optimal for Malta may signal dangerous overcentralization for a high-CAF region like Catalonia or Sicily.

### 3.4 The Optimal Governance Tier (OGT) Matrix

The GSI does not merely score—it **routes**. The OGT Matrix translates diagnostic outputs into explicit subsidiarity recommendations, mapped to the EU's existing NUTS classification (Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics).

**Routing logic:**

| GSI Signal | EU-CAF Context | OGT Recommendation |
|------------|----------------|---------------------|
| High Knowledge Inclusion requirement + High local complexity | EU-CAF > 3.0 | Devolve to **NUTS-3** (municipal/county) |
| High Knowledge Inclusion requirement + Low local complexity | EU-CAF < 1.5 | Devolve to **NUTS-2** (regional) |
| High Inter-Level Coordination need + Low local complexity | EU-CAF < 1.5 | Retain at **EU Directive** level |
| High Resilience Architecture deficit + High local diversity | EU-CAF > 3.0 | Split: EU minimum standard + **NUTS-2** implementation autonomy |
| High Decision Proximity deficit + Medium complexity | EU-CAF 1.5–3.0 | Devolve to **NUTS-2** with coordination protocol |
| Balanced across all dimensions | Any | Current allocation validated; no routing change recommended |

**Critical constraint:** The OGT Matrix produces *recommendations*, not mandates. During the Parallel Diagnostic Phase (Phase 1), outputs are advisory. Binding routing authority requires Enhanced Cooperation adoption (Phase 2). The matrix thresholds will be empirically calibrated during the diagnostic phase—initial values are derived from the GSI v4 pilot methodology but will be refined through comparison with actual EU policy outcomes.

### 3.5 Data Confidence Tiers

Not all regions will have equal data quality. The protocol uses a tiered confidence system to enable deployment without waiting for perfect data:

**Tier 1 (Gold):** Verified by 3+ independent sources (Eurostat + academic/NGO + citizen validation panel from European Civic Value Registry). Full transparency. Available in regions with mature Registry deployment.

**Tier 2 (Silver):** Eurostat data corroborated by at least one independent check (e.g., OECD, regional audit). Partial citizen validation. Available in most EU member states from Year 1.

**Tier 3 (Bronze):** Estimated via proxy inferences from existing EU datasets (EU-SILC, Labour Force Survey, Eurobarometer). Proxies explicitly labeled with inference logic and margin of uncertainty. Available EU-wide from Day 1.

**Bootstrapping pathway:** The Parallel Diagnostic Phase launches at Bronze/Silver across all participating regions using existing data infrastructure. As the European Civic Value Registry grows, specific indicators migrate from Bronze → Silver → Gold. The protocol documents the convergence trajectory and publishes confidence tier status per region per indicator. This solves the chicken-and-egg problem: the GSI does not wait for the Registry; it improves as the Registry matures.

### 3.6 Parallel Diagnostic Phase

Before any policy authority is transferred, the GSI runs as a **non-binding analytical pilot** alongside existing EU decision processes. Outputs are compared to actual policy outcomes to demonstrate:

- Where subsidiarity routing was suboptimal
- The measurable cost of that error
- The efficiency gains possible through improved routing

This is not a request for policy change—it is a **diagnostic audit tool** that makes hidden systemic inefficiencies visible.

### 3.7 Anti-gaming protocols

| Mechanism | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| Data triangulation | 3+ independent sources per indicator required for Gold tier |
| Anomaly detection | AI-assisted flagging of score jumps inconsistent with institutional change |
| Citizen validation panels | Random 1,000-citizen surveys per region to confirm indicator accuracy |
| Full transparency | All raw data, weightings, and confidence tiers published openly |
| Forkability | Any implementation can use alternative weightings—the protocol enables comparison, not enforcement |

### 3.8 Co-production with data sovereignty

The diagnostic process is **co-produced** between central institutions and edge-level actors to ensure full-spectrum visibility across scales. The European Commission establishes macro-audit standards, but the **cryptographic provenance of edge data remains immutable and locally hosted**. The center does not aggregate or alter raw local telemetry; it co-authors the dashboard while the edge retains ownership of the sensor network.

### 3.9 Cryptographic data firewall

The Civic Value Registry operates using **Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) architecture**. The EU Commission and Eurostat receive mathematically verifiable *proof* of informal care capacity and civic activity—aggregate statistics, trends, and confidence intervals—but they never receive the underlying raw data.

**Specified proof classes:**

1. **zk-SNARKs (or zk-STARKs) for Aggregate Capacity Proofs:** Used to prove to the EU Commission that a municipality possesses "X hours of latent informal care capacity" *without* revealing the individual identities or localized ledgers of the citizens providing that care. (The choice between SNARKs and STARKs is a design decision for the technical implementation phase: SNARKs offer smaller proof sizes but require a trusted setup ceremony; STARKs are trustless but produce larger proofs. Both satisfy the core requirement of verifiable aggregation without data exposure.)

2. **Zero-Knowledge Set Membership Proofs:** Used to prove that a citizen is a verified resident of a specific Regional Subsidiarity Zone (eligible for the Resilience Dividend) without exposing their identity to the central ECB/Commission databases.

This allows the protocol to satisfy harmonization demands while cryptographically preventing centralization of the sensor network.

### 3.10 Institutional anchor: Shield & Engine

The protocol employs a dual-track institutional strategy:

- **The Shield** — Committee of the Regions (CoR): An established, treaty-recognized EU advisory body. Provides political legitimacy, legal cover, and neutralizes "rogue regionalism" framing. The CoR hosts the formal governance of the Parallel Diagnostic Phase.

- **The Engine** — Eurocities municipal consortium (or a dedicated Nordic municipal coalition): Provides actual operational capacity—data hosting, technical implementation, GSI computation, and pilot coordination. The Engine executes; the Shield legitimizes.

This separation ensures the protocol is both politically safe and operationally capable. The CoR provides institutional cover while the Engine delivers results that the CoR alone could not produce.

### 3.11 Migration path

- Phase 1: Advisory diagnostic (Parallel Diagnostic Phase) — Bronze/Silver data tiers
- Phase 2: Soft-trigger (recommendation with Enhanced Cooperation approval) — Silver/Gold data tiers
- Phase 3: Automated routing (long-term, after demonstrated efficacy) — Gold tier standard

### 3.12 Neutralizing the Averaging Problem

By applying Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, the GSI mathematically identifies which EU regulations are actively destroying local economic variety. The OGT Matrix then routes those regulations to the governance tier where the relevant knowledge actually resides.

## 8. The European civic value registry (Solving the demographic crisis)

Europe is facing a catastrophic demographic shift, with an aging population and a shrinking tax base to fund traditional welfare state care.

- **The deployment of the Love Ledger:** The establishment of a decentralized, GDPR-compliant registry that recognizes and validates informal, non-monetized care work.

- **Explicit safeguards:**
  - Voluntary participation
  - User-owned data
  - Local governance
  - Opt-in only
  - **Zero-Knowledge Proof architecture:** Statistical proofs shared with EU institutions; raw data never leaves local control (see Section 3.9 for cryptographic specification)

  The registry operates under strict GDPR principles to prevent surveillance or coercion risks. It is a voluntary civic infrastructure, not a mandatory system.

- **Structural compassion:** Instead of trying to commodify all eldercare and community support into taxable formal employment—which the system can no longer afford—the registry uses recognition mechanisms to build high-trust local social safety nets that operate outside strained municipal budgets.

- **Data source for GSI:** This registry provides the **high-fidelity edge data** that enables the GSI to measure informal care capacity—something traditional macroeconomic indicators cannot capture. During early deployment, the GSI operates at Bronze/Silver data confidence tiers using existing Eurostat proxies. As Registry adoption grows, indicators migrate to Gold tier, progressively improving diagnostic accuracy (see Section 3.5).

**Constraint: Local autonomy ≠ systemic fragmentation.** Registry outputs are standardized enough for cross-regional recognition while preserving local implementation flexibility.

**Tied to crisis:** Demographic collapse, eldercare funding gap, healthcare budget sustainability.

## 10. Dependency & deployment architecture (MVD)

The protocol is designed for coherent scaling. Components are structurally interdependent to prevent partial adoption that breaks system logic.

| Layer | Component | Dependency | Function |
|-------|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Layer 1: Sensor** | European Civic Value Registry (Love Ledger) | None—foundational | Provides high-fidelity edge data on informal care capacity, social capital, and local resilience |
| **Layer 2: Diagnostic** | Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI) + OGT Matrix | Requires Layer 1 (at Bronze tier minimum) | Algorithm that identifies optimal governance scale and routes policy to the appropriate NUTS level |
| **Layer 3: Actuation** | Purpose-Bound Money + Resilience Dividend | Requires Layer 2 | Routing mechanisms that implement GSI outputs; convert diagnostic insight into fiscal action |

**If only one component is adopted:**
→ Protocol does not deploy. The GSI cannot function without the Civic Value Registry's edge data (or Eurostat proxy equivalents at Bronze tier).

**If two components are adopted:**
→ Sensor + Diagnostic layers. Provides visibility into subsidiarity inefficiencies without actuation capability. This is a valid diagnostic-only deployment.

**If three components are adopted:**
→ Full protocol. Sensor + Diagnostic + Actuation layers deliver complete subsidiarity routing capability.

This dependency architecture ensures that the EU cannot "pick the parts they like and ignore the rest." To access the efficiency dashboard (GSI), they must adopt the localized care-recognition infrastructure (Civic Value Registry).

  - Demonstrated efficiency gains drive organic adoption

  - Gold tier data confidence becomes standard in participating regions

## 17. ECB-Aligned Pilot Specification: Conditional Service Credits (CSC)

This section specifies the initial pilot for the Purpose-Bound Money mechanism, designed to meet ECB technical requirements and establish empirical proof of monetary neutrality and localized efficiency.

---

### 17.1 Pilot Framing

> This pilot tests a **targeted fiscal transfer mechanism with embedded spending conditions** in sectors characterized by weak monetary policy transmission.
>
> The instrument is **not legal tender**, not a general unit of account, and not a store of value.

This classification is the ECB entry ticket.

---

### 17.2 Legal Positioning

- Falls under **member state fiscal competence**
- Implemented via **Enhanced Cooperation (Article 20 TEU)**
- Consistent with the **price stability mandate of the European Central Bank**

**Critical clause:**

> "The instrument is designed to operate outside aggregate monetary policy channels and does not target macroeconomic demand management."

---

### 17.3 Scope Constraints

| Constraint | Specification |
|------------|---------------|
| Geographic scope | Västra Götaland (Sweden) — non-eurozone initially, reducing monetary risk |
| Scale cap | Total issuance ≤ 0.1% of regional GDP (Phase 1), expanding to 0.5% upon milestone achievement |
| Sector restriction | Low-price-elasticity, underutilized sectors: eldercare (informal support), community health support, volunteer coordination, local ecological maintenance |
| Explicit exclusions | Housing, energy, food retail, tradable goods |

---

### 17.4 Instrument Design: Conditional Service Credits (CSC)

| Property | Specification |
|----------|---------------|
| Official name | Conditional Service Credits (CSC) |
| Legal status | Not legal tender |
| Convertibility | Non-convertible to Euro |
| Transferability | Restricted (peer-to-peer within system only) |
| Expiry | Fixed 90-day validity (no dynamic adjustment; no policy lever) |
| Storage | Digital wallet (closed-loop system) |

**Fixed-term validity:** Time-based validity ensures circulation and prevents accumulation. The term "demurrage" is avoided in pilot documentation; replaced with "fixed-term validity."

---

### 17.5 Monetary Containment Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Closed-loop system | CSC can only be used with approved providers for defined services; no leakage into general economy |
| No price formation role | Prices remain denominated in Euro. CSC acts only as a partial settlement mechanism |
| Euro primacy clause | All economic valuation remains anchored in the Euro |

---

### 17.6 Monetary Policy Neutrality Test

Success criteria defined upfront:

**Must demonstrate:**

- No measurable impact on:
  - Regional CPI
  - Wage levels in formal sector
  - Euro liquidity demand

**Positive signals:**

- Increased activity in:
  - Informal care
  - Underutilized labor capacity
- **Velocity of localized exchange:** CSC circulates faster than Euro in target sectors (measured as turnover per unit per time period)

The **velocity metric** is critical: if 100 CSC credits circulate 5× faster than 100 Euro in the care economy, the protocol mathematically proves it generates more fiscal stimulus per unit than the primary currency—turning ECB's fear of "leakage" into measurable proof of "efficiency."

### 17.9 Pre-Registered Threshold Agreement

Before the Västra Götaland pilot begins, all parties (ECB observer, Eurostat, Swedish national treasury, and the regional consortium) must sign a **Pre-Registered Threshold Agreement** that locks in the exact mathematical definitions of success and failure.

**Pre-registered success thresholds (illustrative):**

| Metric | Success Threshold | Failure Threshold |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Local informal care capacity change | > +5% (measured via Registry + municipal data) | < 0% (net decrease) |
| Regional CPI impact | < 0.01 percentage points deviation | > 0.05 pp deviation |
| Euro substitution rate | < 2% of CSC transactions displacing Euro transactions | > 10% displacement |
| CSC velocity ratio | > 2× Euro velocity in target sectors | < 1× (no circulation benefit) |
| Formal sector wage impact | Within ±0.5% of control region | > ±2% deviation |

**Mixed results protocol:** If some metrics meet success thresholds while others are inconclusive (neither success nor failure), the pre-agreement specifies that the pilot enters a **6-month extension** with intensified monitoring on inconclusive metrics, rather than being terminated or declared failed. The independent evaluation body (not any participating institution) adjudicates borderline cases.

**Purpose:** By locking in the success metrics *before* the data is generated, the protocol strips all parties of the ability to move the goalposts after results emerge. This is standard practice in clinical trial design (pre-registration) adapted for policy piloting.

---

### 17.10 Duration & Exit Conditions

- **Timeline:** 12–18 months

- **Automatic shutdown triggers:**
  - Inflation deviation > pre-registered failure threshold
  - Evidence of Euro substitution above pre-registered threshold
  - Political misuse

- **Algorithmic Expansion Trigger:** If the 12–18 month pilot triggers zero ECB inflation warnings and successfully meets pre-registered success thresholds, the 0.1% GDP cap automatically enters a "controlled easing phase":
  - Cap expands to 0.5% of regional GDP
  - Sector whitelist expands to adjacent non-tradable services
  - Renewed ECB consultation at each threshold

**This makes scaling the default mathematical outcome of success,** requiring political effort to *stop* it rather than political effort to expand it—preventing permanent pilot caging.

---

### 17.11 Strategic Framing

> "This pilot explores whether targeted fiscal instruments can activate underutilized social capacity without entering monetary policy space."

Not: "We are testing a new economic system."
But: "We are testing whether constrained fiscal tools can reduce pressure on existing systems."
