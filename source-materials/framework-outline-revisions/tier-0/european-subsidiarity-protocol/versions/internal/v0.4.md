# European subsidiarity protocol
### An operational enhancement for Article 5 and the European project

**Status:** Revision 0.4 (Submission-Ready Draft)
**Target systems:** European Commission, European Central Bank (ECB), Committee of the Regions
**Parent frameworks:** Adaptive Universal Basic Income (AUBI), Financial Systems Framework, Nested Economies

---

## 1. Executive summary: Solving the latency crisis

The European Union represents the most advanced polycentric governance experiment in modern history, anchored constitutionally by the Principle of Subsidiarity (TEU Article 5). However, the EU is currently experiencing a severe structural latency crisis (as diagnosed by the Draghi Report on Competitiveness). The attempt to manage 27 diverse national economies through centralized, standardized regulation has triggered an **Averaging Problem**—stifling local innovation and generating massive bureaucratic friction.

The European Subsidiarity Protocol is not a political treaty; it is an **operational enhancement to subsidiarity implementation**. It provides the mathematical diagnostic tools (The Global Subsidiarity Index) to execute Article 5 accurately, and introduces specific routing protocols to unlock regional autonomy while preserving European interoperability.

**This protocol does not change what the EU is. It clarifies what it has always been attempting to become: a subsidiarity-native governance system.**

---

## 2. The Averaging Problem: A concrete case

Consider the **demographic and care crisis**—one of the EU's most politically toxic challenges.

**Without GSI:** Centralized approaches attempt to solve aging populations and care deficits through top-down migration quotas (high political friction) and massive macro-healthcare budgets (unsustainable). These policies average across regions with vastly different cultural care capacities, informal network densities, and demographic profiles. Political backlash intensifies. Budgets strain. Outcomes remain uneven.

**With GSI:** The index identifies regions where informal care capacity is high (measured through the European Civic Value Registry) and routes resources toward recognition infrastructure rather than formal care hiring. Regions with low capacity receive different support. The same problem—care deficit—is solved at different scales without uniform policy triggering uniform backlash.

*The data enabling this diagnostic comes from the European Civic Value Registry (Section 6), which measures informal care capacity at the local level—something traditional macroeconomic indicators cannot capture.*

**Beyond social outcomes:** By shifting localized care burdens from formal healthcare budgets to recognition-based infrastructure, the protocol removes **massive fiscal pressure from the primary Euro and national healthcare systems**. This is not merely a social innovation—it is a macroeconomic stabilization mechanism.

## 3. The diagnostic engine: Operationalizing Article 5

Currently, the EU relies on political negotiation to determine if a decision should be made at the local, national, or Union level. This protocol introduces a rigorous engineering standard.

- **The Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI):** A diagnostic measurement tool deployed across the EU to identify the exact geographic and institutional scale where relevant information exists. The GSI will be co-developed with **Eurostat** for methodological transparency and validated through existing **Regulatory Impact Assessment** frameworks.

- **Parallel Diagnostic Phase (formerly "shadow-mode"):** Before any policy authority is transferred, the GSI runs in a **non-binding analytical pilot** alongside existing EU decision processes. Outputs are compared to actual policy outcomes to demonstrate:
  - Where subsidiarity routing was suboptimal
  - The measurable cost of that error
  - The efficiency gains possible through improved routing

  This is not a request for policy change—it is a **diagnostic audit tool** that makes hidden systemic inefficiencies visible.

- **Data sovereignty:** The Parallel Diagnostic Phase is executed by an **independent coalition of edge-nodes** (e.g., a consortium of municipalities through Eurocities, regional governments), using **high-fidelity edge data** (municipal APIs, localized civic registries) specifically to contrast with the EU's aggregated macro-data. The whole point of the diagnostic is to prove that the edge sees things the center cannot. The center does not audit itself; the edge proves where the center is suboptimal.

- **Migration path:**
  - Phase 1: Advisory diagnostic (Parallel Diagnostic Phase)
  - Phase 2: Soft-trigger (recommendation with Enhanced Cooperation approval)
  - Phase 3: Automated routing (long-term, after demonstrated efficacy)

- **Neutralizing the Averaging Problem:** By applying Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety, the GSI mathematically identifies which EU regulations are actively destroying local economic variety.

## 6. The European resilience dividend (Upgrading cohesion)

The EU currently expends massive resources on the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) and structural cohesion funds, which often trap regions in bureaucratic dependency.

- **The upgrade:** Gradual redirection of existing structural funds into a European Resilience Dividend (a localized implementation of the AUBI Layer 1 baseline).
- **Cognitive infrastructure:** By decoupling citizen survival from rigid institutional compliance, the dividend serves as foundational cognitive infrastructure. It frees the intellectual bandwidth of European citizens, allowing them to innovate and adapt at the local level faster than centralized competitors.

**Constraint: Local autonomy ≠ systemic fragmentation.** All outputs remain legally interoperable, economically translatable, and digitally compatible across the Union.

**Tied to crisis:** Cost-of-living crisis, competitiveness gap.

## 7. The complementary digital euro (Routing local value)

To solve the investment and care deficits without violating the strict fiscal deficit rules of the Eurozone, the protocol introduces a **targeted fiscal complement within existing monetary architecture**.

- **The localized complementary mechanism:** Utilizing the GGF *Hearts & Leaves* architecture, regions authorized through Enhanced Cooperation (Article 20 TEU) may issue a Complementary Digital Euro. This localized currency is strictly routed toward regional care work, ecological restoration, and community resilience.

- **Explicit safeguards:**
  - **Demurrage:** A holding fee that forces circulation, preventing hoarding
  - **Non-exportability:** Cannot leave the region of issuance
  - **Bounded circulation:** Strict use-case limitations

  These mathematical constraints are presented as the primary safeguards that prevent this complement from ever threatening the ECB's macroeconomic inflation targets. This is *not* a parallel currency—it is a bounded fiscal tool that lubricates local economies where the primary Euro has pooled or fled.

- **Macroeconomic relief:** By absorbing localized care and resilience expenditures that would otherwise fall on national healthcare and social welfare budgets, the Complementary Digital Euro **reduces structural pressure on sovereign debt and primary Euro stability**.

**Constraint: Local autonomy ≠ systemic fragmentation.** All complementary currencies remain digitally compatible with EU payment infrastructure and economically translatable to the primary Euro.

**Tied to crisis:** Investment gap, care deficit, sovereign debt sustainability.

## 8. The European civic value registry (Solving the demographic crisis)

Europe is facing a catastrophic demographic shift, with an aging population and a shrinking tax base to fund traditional welfare state care.

- **The deployment of the Love Ledger:** The establishment of a decentralized, GDPR-compliant registry that recognizes and validates informal, non-monetized care work.

- **Explicit safeguards:**
  - Voluntary participation
  - User-owned data
  - Local governance
  - Opt-in only

  The registry operates under strict GDPR principles to prevent surveillance or coercion risks. It is a voluntary civic infrastructure, not a mandatory system.

- **Structural compassion:** Instead of trying to commodify all eldercare and community support into taxable formal employment—which the system can no longer afford—the registry uses recognition mechanisms to build high-trust local social safety nets that operate outside strained municipal budgets.

- **Data source for GSI:** This registry provides the **high-fidelity edge data** that enables the GSI to measure informal care capacity—something traditional macroeconomic indicators cannot capture.

**Constraint: Local autonomy ≠ systemic fragmentation.** Registry outputs are standardized enough for cross-regional recognition while preserving local implementation flexibility.

**Tied to crisis:** Demographic collapse, eldercare funding gap, healthcare budget sustainability.

## 9. Governance and oversight: The Distributed Audit Board

To prevent capture by centralized institutions—which would recreate the Averaging Problem—governance of the protocol is distributed.

| Function | Body |
|----------|------|
| Methodology validation | Eurostat + independent academic panel |
| Parallel Diagnostic audit | **Distributed Audit Board:** Committee of the Regions (representing edge nodes/municipalities) + independent technical auditors |
| Deployment authorization | Enhanced Cooperation participants (Article 20 TEU) |
| Ongoing monitoring | Committee of the Regions + European Parliament observers |

**The center does not grade its own homework.** The Distributed Audit Board ensures that those closest to the data—municipalities and regions—have equal or greater authority over diagnostic interpretation than centralized Commission bodies.

## 10. Transition mechanics: The permeation strategy

The protocol operates fully within existing legal mandates, requiring **no treaty changes for initial deployment**.

- **Phase 1: Parallel Diagnostic Phase (Year 1)**
  - GSI runs as a non-binding analytical pilot alongside existing EU decision processes
  - Independent coalition of edge-nodes (Eurocities municipal consortium) executes the audit using high-fidelity edge data
  - Outputs demonstrate inefficiencies without requiring policy change

- **Phase 2: Enhanced Cooperation deployment (Years 2–3)**
  - A coalition of nine or more member states (Article 20 TEU) formally adopts the protocol
  - **Enhanced Cooperation is the EU's native deployment mechanism for advanced integration.** It has been used for the European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO), the Unitary Patent, and financial transaction tax initiatives. This protocol follows that established precedent.
  - Pilot deployments in safe, high-autonomy regions

- **Phase 3: Gradual integration (Years 4–5)**
  - Demonstrated efficiency gains drive organic adoption
  - Legacy EU apparatus routes resources toward high-performing polycentric networks

### Pilot example: Västra Götaland (Sweden)

A region with deep municipal autonomy (*kommunalt självstyre*) and zero secessionist tensions.

**Initial use cases:**
- GSI applied to transport and logistics optimization
- Complementary euro pilot for eldercare and informal care work recognition
- Civic registry pilot for volunteer and community contributions

**Expected outcomes:**
- Measurable reduction in regulatory friction
- Increased care capacity without municipal budget strain
- Reduced pressure on regional healthcare budgets
- Replicable template for other regions

## Appendix A: Legal compatibility statement

This protocol is explicitly anchored in:

- **Article 5 TEU (Subsidiarity):** The principle that decisions must be taken as closely as possible to the citizen.
- **Proportionality Principle:** Action must not exceed what is necessary to achieve treaty objectives.
- **Enhanced Cooperation (Article 20 TEU):** This protocol can be deployed by a coalition of nine or more member states without requiring unanimous approval, using the EU's own legal bypass mechanism for advanced integration.

**No treaty change is required for initial deployment.**
