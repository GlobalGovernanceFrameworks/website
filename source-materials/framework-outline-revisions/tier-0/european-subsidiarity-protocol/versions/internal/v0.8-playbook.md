## 1. Core strategic principle

Institutions do not move on logic alone. They move on career-safe incentives. Every element of the public protocol is designed to make adoption the path of least resistance for individual decision-makers within EU institutions.

The public document presents the protocol as a technical enhancement. This playbook documents the political strategy behind it.

## 4. Coalition strategy

### 4.1 The Madrid/Berlin problem

The public document treats member states as unitary actors. They are not. Subsidiarity is a three-way tug-of-war: EU ↔ Nation ↔ Region. A tool that empowers Catalonia or Bavaria may be killed by Madrid or Berlin before the Commission reads it.

**Counter-strategy: National Fiscal Relief.** The protocol must be sold to national capitals on macro-economic terms. If Spain believes precision subsidiarity will reduce national healthcare and pension expenditures by activating informal care capacity at the regional level, Madrid will tolerate increased regional autonomy as a fiscal trade-off. The public document embeds this argument in Section 2 ("mechanism for national fiscal relief through precision allocation").

**Operational implication:** Initial coalition conversations should target finance ministries, not regional affairs ministries. The pitch to Berlin is not "empower the Länder" — it's "reduce federal healthcare transfer costs."

### 4.2 Likely early adopters

**Tier 1 — Natural champions (strong subsidiarity tradition + institutional capacity + political alignment):**
- **Sweden** — Pilot host (Västra Götaland); strong municipal autonomy tradition; non-eurozone reduces ECB friction; Social Democrats historically supportive of local governance innovation
- **Denmark** — Advanced municipal governance; complementary currency precedents; strong digital infrastructure
- **Finland** — DESI leader; strong local governance; open to governance experimentation
- **Netherlands** — Deep polycentric tradition; advanced data infrastructure; pragmatic coalition politics

**Tier 2 — Strategic interest (high EU-CAF regions where the Averaging Problem is politically visible):**
- **Spain** — Autonomous communities have structurally different governance needs; fiscal relief argument resonates given healthcare cost pressures. Risk: Madrid may resist anything that looks like Catalan empowerment. Mitigate by framing as fiscal efficiency, not autonomy.
- **Italy** — North-South divide makes averaging costs visible; strong regional identity in multiple regions. Risk: Rome may see it as undermining national cohesion. Mitigate with Dimension 4 (Cohesion) emphasis.
- **Germany** — Federal structure already implements informal subsidiarity; Länder may champion formal tools. Risk: Länder may view EU diagnostic as unwanted oversight. Mitigate by emphasizing that GSI validates existing German federalism rather than imposing new constraints.
- **Belgium** — Complex federal/regional/community structure; natural laboratory. Risk: Flemish/Walloon dynamics make any governance tool politically charged. Approach through federal government, not regions.

**Tier 3 — Opportunistic alignment:**
- **Portugal, Ireland, Austria** — Smaller states where enhanced local autonomy provides disproportionate benefit relative to political cost.

**Target: 9 from Tiers 1–3.** Nordic bloc (4) + 2 from Tier 2 + 3 from Tier 3 = achievable. EPPO launched with 20; the Unitary Patent with 17. Nine is the minimum.

### 4.3 Sequencing

1. **Nordic bloc first.** Sweden (pilot host), Denmark, Finland, Netherlands. These four can be engaged simultaneously through existing Nordic Council channels and Eurocities networks.
2. **Finance ministry track.** Engage Spanish and Italian finance ministries on fiscal relief framing before approaching regional affairs.
3. **CoR as political cover.** Use Committee of the Regions endorsement to legitimize the initiative before approaching reluctant member states.
4. **Commission co-option.** Invite DG REGIO and DG CONNECT into co-development of the GSI methodology. If they help build it, they can't reject it without rejecting their own work.

## 6. Negotiation fallbacks

### 6.1 If the Commission demands to host the data

**Position:** Co-authorship of the dashboard; no hosting of raw edge data. Offer: Commission receives all aggregate statistics, trends, confidence intervals via ZKP proofs. This gives them everything they need for policy analysis without giving them the surveillance capability they want.

**Fallback:** If Commission insists, propose a "data embassy" model — Commission analysts access anonymized data within locally hosted secure environments, similar to Eurostat's existing arrangements with national statistical offices. No data leaves the edge; analysis travels to the data.

### 6.2 If the ECB refuses observer status

**Position:** ECB observation is offered as a courtesy, not a requirement. The CSC pilot operates under member state fiscal competence (Swedish jurisdiction for Phase 1).

**Fallback:** Proceed without ECB involvement. Publish all monetary neutrality data openly. The ECB's absence from the pilot becomes a reputational risk for them if the results are positive.

### 6.3 If Enhanced Cooperation fails to reach 9 members

**Fallback:** Proceed as an informal coalition under the Eurocities umbrella. The Parallel Diagnostic Phase requires no legal framework — it's an analytical exercise. Phase 1 can proceed with any number of willing municipalities. Enhanced Cooperation becomes the Phase 2 formalization of a Phase 1 fait accompli.

### 6.4 If the GSI underperforms in Phase 1

**Position:** The framework revision protocol (Public doc Section 3.6) is the response. Frame Phase 1 as calibration, not validation. Expected outcome: the model will need refinement. This is normal scientific practice.

**Fallback:** If the four-dimension model proves structurally inadequate, the published data still has value as governance diagnostics. Pivot to "the EU's first comprehensive subsidiarity audit" — a contribution to knowledge even if the routing algorithm needs redesign.

## 7. Strategic framing: What to say and what not to say

### Say:
- "Operational enhancement to Article 5"
- "Precision subsidiarity"
- "Evidence-based governance routing"
- "National fiscal relief through local activation"
- "Targeted fiscal instrument" (for CSC)
- "Fixed-term validity" (not "demurrage")
- "Co-produced diagnostic"

### Do not say:
- "Complementary currency" / "alternative currency" / "parallel money"
- "Decentralization" (use "precision subsidiarity" or "governance optimization")
- "Radical reform" / "system redesign" / "paradigm shift"
- "Disruption" (use "enhancement" or "upgrade")
- "Memetic shield" / "institutional capture" / "trojan horse" (internal language only)
- "Love Ledger" (use "European Civic Value Registry")
- "Purpose-Bound Money" (use "Conditional Service Credits" or "targeted fiscal instrument")

### The one-sentence pitch per audience:

**To a Commissioner:** "We've built a diagnostic tool that gives you measurable evidence of where subsidiarity implementation is working and where it isn't — co-developed with Eurostat, coordinated through the CoR."

**To a Finance Minister:** "This protocol can reduce your national healthcare and pension transfer costs by activating informal care capacity that currently sits outside your fiscal system."

**To an ECB official:** "We've designed a closed-loop fiscal instrument that reduces demand on the Euro in underserviced sectors — and we want you as an observer so you can verify it in real time."

**To a Eurocities mayor:** "Your city already does precision governance. This gives you the measurement tools to prove it and the EU framework to scale it."

## 8. The Sweden non-eurozone gap

The CSC pilot in Västra Götaland operates outside the eurozone. The ECB can dismiss results as irrelevant to monetary policy within the euro area.

**Counter-strategy:**
1. The public document specifies that Phase 2 must include at least one eurozone pilot region (Section 12).
2. The Phase 1 pilot design includes a pre-specified comparison methodology for extrapolating to eurozone conditions.
3. Frame the non-eurozone pilot as *advantageous*: it demonstrates the mechanism in a lower-risk environment before scaling to eurozone complexity. This is standard regulatory practice (sandbox → mainnet).
4. If Phase 1 succeeds, the political pressure to include a eurozone pilot in Phase 2 will come from the eurozone coalition members themselves — you won't need to push it.
