Fair point on cross-referencing — agreed. The v0.2 revisions are solid; you addressed the major gaps well. Remaining issues, roughly by significance:

**1. Regiões de Saúde are missing.** Brazil's health regions (422 of them, grouping municipalities for medium/high complexity care under Decreto 7.508/2011) are a critical intermediate governance unit between município and estado. The OGT matrix routes to União → Estado → Município, but in practice, specialist referrals and hospital networks are organized at the health region level. The GSI routing needs to acknowledge this — otherwise the "referral system effectiveness" indicator (4.3) and the CIB routing recommendations are floating above the actual institutional structure.

**2. Circular data source in indicator 2.3.** "Informal Care Integration" lists the Civic Value Registry as a data source, but the Registry is the thing the pilot is building. The indicator table should note what the proxy data source is *before* the Registry exists (probably PNAD Contínua care questions or ACS qualitative records), and then flag the Registry as the intended Gold-tier source.

**3. RAIS misplaced in BR-CAF.** Line 117 — RAIS (Annual Social Information Report) is an employment database, not a geographic data source. It's listed under Geographic Diversity Score (G). Either move it to Development Disparity (D), where it fits, or replace with something like IBGE's REGIC (Regions of Influence of Cities) or IBGE territorial/cartographic data.

**4. The 15% referral target is unanchored.** "≥15% reduction in referral wait times" (line 256) — what's the basis? If it's arbitrary, say "target to be calibrated against DATASUS baseline in Month 1." An ungrounded number in an otherwise rigorous document invites easy critique.

**5. Racial health disparities deserve more than an indicator cell.** Indicator 3.3 references racial dimensions, but Brazil's Black/pardo population has systematically worse maternal mortality, infant mortality, and life expectancy — and this is structurally tied to the same informality/geographic patterns the document already foregrounds. Even a sentence in Section 1.2 connecting the Averaging Problem to racial health inequity would strengthen the argument considerably for the Fiocruz audience.

**6. Language of deliverables.** The spec is in English, which works for methodology documentation. But the working paper (Month 1-3, CEBRAP/FGV) and municipal engagement materials will need to be in Portuguese. Worth a one-line note in the next steps table — trivial, but it signals awareness of the operational context.
