Here is my assessment of the brilliant additions to adopt, the hard pushbacks, and the crucial clarification.

### 1. What to Adopt Immediately (The Gold Standard)

* **Non-Verbal & Unconventional Care Validation (1.3):** This is one of the most innovative and compassionate pieces of systems design I have seen. Allowing *peer attestation* and *community vouching* for non-verbal emotional support—without requiring a medical diagnosis—is a massive leap forward for neurodivergent and severely disabled inclusion in the post-capitalist economy.
* **The "Prevention" Reframe (2.1 & 2.2):** Replacing "preventable disability" with "preventable harm to disabled people" perfectly aligns the framework with the core tenets of modern Disability Justice. 
* **Closing the Veto Loophole (4.1 & 4.2):** Outlining the exact 90-day remediation timeline and explicitly giving the Digital Justice Tribunal the power to enforce the veto fixes the "toothless" design of the previous draft.
* **The Layered Economic Model (1.1):** Separating the fiat-funded infrastructure (building ramps, buying assistive tech) from the Hearts-funded interpersonal care completely resolves the economic tangle.

### 2. The Hard Pushbacks

**Pushback 1: The "Segregation Trap" (Suggestion 8.3: Neurodiversity Schools Model)**
DeepSeek suggests creating "dedicated neurodiversity schools."
* **The Danger:** Within the disability rights movement and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD), "dedicated" or "separate" schools are highly controversial. They often lead to segregation and echo the dark history of institutionalization. 
* **The Fix:** We must reject the creation of separate schools. The goal of the framework is **Radical Inclusion**. The mandate must be that *all* schools in the Educational Systems Framework are transformed to be universally designed, sensory-friendly, and neuro-inclusive, so that disabled and non-disabled peers learn together. 

**Pushback 2: The "Phantom Fine" (Suggestion 3.3: Procurement Fines)**
DeepSeek suggests replacing the Ableism Tax with "Procurement fines (set at 10% of excluded contract value)."
* **The Danger:** This is legally incoherent. You cannot fine a corporation 10% of a contract *they were just excluded from bidding on*. If a company is excluded from the ecosystem, the BAZ has no legal jurisdiction to extract a penalty fee from their corporate revenue. 
* **The Fix:** Stick to pure **Market Exclusion**. If a company fails the DCIA, they are simply banned from receiving any BAZ public funds, grants, or contracts, and they lose their Ethical Trade Zone certification. The financial punishment is the *loss of the contract itself*, not an arbitrary fine.

### 3. The Crucial Clarification: Dynamic DCIA Weighting

In **Suggestion 9.1**, DeepSeek proposes a rigid weighting system for the Disability & Cognitive Impact Assessments (e.g., Physical 25%, Cognitive 25%, Healthcare 10%). 
* **The Clarification:** A rigid global weighting system will fail when applied to wildly different policies. If a BAZ is designing a new digital voting app, "Physical Accessibility" shouldn't be 25% of the score—Cognitive and Digital should be 90%. Conversely, if they are designing a hospital triage protocol, "Healthcare Impact" should be 80%, not 10%.
* **The Fix:** Define these as **Dynamic Categories**. The GDA must calibrate the weight of the DCIA categories based on the specific domain of the policy being audited.
