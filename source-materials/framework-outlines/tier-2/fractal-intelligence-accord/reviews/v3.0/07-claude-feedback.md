This is genuinely strong for an outline — the six pillars have good internal logic and the transition section addresses the gap I flagged. A few suggestions:

**Missing: the Multi-Stakeholder Trust definition.** It's referenced repeatedly (Pillar III, Pillar V) but never constituted. Who sits on it, how are they selected, what are the anti-capture provisions specific to *this* body (versus the general ones in section 8)? This is the trunk of the trunk — it needs at least a placeholder structure.

**Reality Rent pricing is absent.** The CCL is the most novel mechanism in the framework, but there's no mention of who sets the rent, how it's calculated, or how disputes over valuation are resolved. Even a formula sketch like the bond calculation in Pillar IV would help.

**The Oracle Protocol handoff threshold is too vague.** "Signs of emergent consciousness" doesn't give developers actionable criteria. Given the TGIF already has TRRT tiers, this should specify something like: "when a system exceeds [capability threshold X] on [benchmark Y], the SGC is notified and has 30 days to classify." Vagueness here is a real governance risk.

**Pillar VI feels thin relative to the others.** Epistemic hygiene is important enough to either develop further or fold into Pillar II (it's essentially about context integrity, which is already Pillar II's domain).

**The Decision Tree appendix** would work better integrated into Pillar III's arbitration mechanism — that's where conflicts actually get resolved.

**One structural question:** should "Rights of Future Generations" in the preamble have a corresponding governance body — a Future Generations Advocate — with actual standing in the arbitration process? The Oracle Protocol's Horizon Protocol connection already establishes this precedent in GGF.
