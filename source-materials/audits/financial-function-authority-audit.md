# Financial-Function Authority Audit

**Scope:** 107 corpus files (79 framework/spec `.md`, 28 GaE papers excluded from hit counts, 19 cluster `.ts` files).
**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Method:** regex scan of every framework document for each candidate function, with each hit classified as *disclaimer*, *ownership claim*, or *bare mention*; schema entity-ID scan for corresponding objects; manual reading of every hit in the thin rows.
**Question:** for each consequential economic function outside the current GGF canopy, is external governance sufficient to preserve GGF viability, or does GGF require an explicit interface, observation architecture, or internally owned capability?

---

## Amendment to the test

The conforming-failure test alone over-generates orphans: external education, labour law, land registries and military force all pass it. The discriminator used here is narrower:

> **Endogenous amplification** — does GGF machinery, operating exactly as intended, worsen the external function's harmful effect or convert a GGF success into an input to the failure?

External harm GGF merely suffers alongside everyone else is weather. External harm GGF's own success *feeds* is architecture.

A second column, **corpus visibility**, separates three states that the four-way classification collapses:

- **allocated** — named, with a real external owner identified;
- **disclaimed** — named and pushed out of scope, owner not necessarily identified;
- **invisible** — the function does not appear in the corpus in any register.

Invisibility is the finding that matters. A deliberate boundary and an unnoticed dependency look identical in a framework's text; they are distinguishable only by whether the boundary was ever stated.

## Row notes

### 1 — Commercial credit creation · architecture orphan · severity 1

Zero hits across 79 framework documents for `credit creation`, `creation of credit`, `commercial bank`, `bank lending`, `credit allocation`, `credit guidance`. The only near-matches are SCSI's fund-level borrowing and leverage limits — a fund as *borrower*, never a bank as *creator*. The corpus does not disclaim this function. It has never noticed it.

Schema confirms: no entity ID in any of the 19 cluster files contains `credit`, `monetary`, or `bank`. The only economic protocol IDs are `protocol_debt_transformation` and `protocol_shared_capital`.

Endogenous amplification is established: AUBI raises household income, Leaves-funded restoration raises amenity values near funded sites, and both capitalize into land prices against which credit is extended. GGF's success is an input to the loop.

This is the one genuine architecture orphan in the list.

### 2 — Sectoral concentration / crowding-out · observability orphan · severity 1

`crowding out` appears once, inside SCSI's fund-investment discussion (`crowding in or crowding out`). `Dutch disease` and `resource curse`: zero. `financialization` / `financialisation`: zero in any economic sense — the single hit is Fractal Intelligence Accord describing non-financializable bonds.

The corpus has no vocabulary for the mechanism that generates the finance curse.

Nearest existing sensor: Financial Systems §5.6, which flags a BAZ above 50% GCF reserve dependence at 24 months. That is funding-source dependence at one variable, not sectoral crowding-out.

Endogenous amplification is definitional here: a successful Hearts economy produces a wage premium in Hearts validation and administration, bids skilled labour away from ordinary local production, and raises rents around funded projects.

Owner should be Nested Economies, which already owns dependency, gateway concentration, common-mode and material-exit analysis (Appendix E.4, E.7). This needs a diagnostic, not a framework.

Legitimate actuator already exists: Financial Systems §7.3 gives BAZ Councils the right to decline to formalize or scale a local Hearts economy.

### 3 — Central-bank monetary policy · governed seam, partly built · severity 2

The interface object partly exists and nobody in the current discussion knew it. **Gaian Trade v0.9.1 contains a Monetary Coexistence Protocol**: opt-in central-bank partnership agreements respecting monetary sovereignty, standardized Hearts–fiat exchange windows managed by participating central banks, with a stated pilot target of 2–3 willing central banks and central-bank outreach on the implementation checklist.

Financial Systems §7.4 states that Treasuries and partner credit unions *execute but do not unilaterally determine* monetary policy. Housing §1.4 places monetary policy outside scope.

So the boundary is stated, an interface mechanism exists, and it is listed in Gaian Trade's mechanism inventory — but it has **no schema entity**, no version, no interface specification, and no owning framework beyond a bullet list in a trade document.

The cheapest correct move is promoting the Monetary Coexistence Protocol into a real object rather than drafting a Monetary & Credit Architecture from scratch. Rows 1 and 3 then have one plausible common owner.

### 4 — Mortgage & land-backed credit · governed seam + unowned loop · severity 2

Well-disclaimed. Housing §1.4 excludes mortgage underwriting, creditor priority, bank solvency and monetary policy. §9.7 handles foreclosure as a habitation-continuity consequence while stating that Financial Systems does not replace national mortgage, banking, secured-credit or insolvency law. §14.11 names external law as the owner and adds that *no hypothetical future Monetary/Credit framework is treated as a current owner*.

§10.1 goes further and makes affordability a deliberately **monitored distributed outcome** rather than one sovereign function, listing candidate instrument owners including external credit law, and stating that no metric automatically creates policy.

The seam is therefore correctly formed. What is unowned is the amplification loop, not the authority: nothing observes AUBI or Leaves value capitalizing into rents and land prices. Bioregional Polis carries the only counterweights — land-value tax escalating to 5% of assessed value on speculative holdings, vacancy taxation, anti-speculation surcharges, CLTs, rights of first refusal — and Adaptive Tax explicitly routes those instruments to Polis. But they are optional municipal policy, adopted BAZ by BAZ, with no requirement that a BAZ deploying AUBI also deploy them.

Remedy is a coupling condition, not a framework: AUBI or Leaves deployment at scale without a land-value or anti-speculation instrument should be an observable state.

### 5 — Bank failure / reserve custody · governed seam, unwritten · severity 2

One hit in the entire corpus: Housing §1.4 listing bank solvency as out of scope. Nothing else — `deposit protection`, `deposit insurance`, `lender of last resort`, `bank resolution` all return zero.

Financial Systems has a detailed internal resolution architecture: §5.3 reserve hierarchy at 50/30/20, §5.4 liquid-adjusted reserve ratio, §5.5 reserve-breach protocol, §16.1 instructing Treasury managers to enter resolution rather than conceal insolvency or redefine the threshold.

Unexamined: the Treasury's fiat reserves sit somewhere. If the external institution holding them fails or de-banks the Treasury, the liquid-adjusted reserve ratio changes without any GGF actor doing anything wrong, and the §5.5 breach protocol fires on a cause it cannot diagnose. Nested Economies' common-mode analysis names shared funders, platforms, vendors, auditors and settlement rails — but not reserve custodians.

Cheap patch: add reserve custodian to the common-mode dependency classes, and add external-custodian failure to the §5.5 cause list alongside the existing liquidity/insolvency distinction.

### 6 — Sovereign debt · declared deferred capability · severity 2

Financial Systems §15.8 and Treaty §19.3 both defer the mechanism explicitly and enumerate what a dedicated protocol would need. The document layer is correct.

The schema is not. `protocol_debt_transformation` in `cluster_economic-os.ts` is tier 1, `implementationPriority: 'Critical'`, described as converting sovereign debt into regenerative obligations serviced by Hearts and Leaves, with `framework_financial_systems ESTABLISHES` it.

The serious defect is `enables: ['framework_aubi', 'framework_planetary_health']` — the graph asserts that two operating frameworks are downstream of a mechanism two frozen documents disown.

Fix ordering: clear the `enables` array first, then `status: 'Draft'` → `'Proposed'`, then rewrite the description to state the deferral, then downgrade `implementationPriority`. Change `ESTABLISHES` only if a suitable relationship type already exists in the enum — extending the type union for one edge costs more than an accurate description does.

### 7 — Corporate governance / shareholder primacy · undeclared non-goal · severity 3

`shareholder` appears twice in 79 documents: Cairn listing shareholder actions as an activist tactic, and Pathfinder explaining transition to shareholders. `corporate governance`, `company law`, `shareholder primacy`, `fiduciary duty of directors`: zero substantive hits. SCSI §1.3 lists general corporate law among matters it does not own — the corpus's only acknowledgement that the territory exists.

Endogenous amplification is absent: conventional shareholder-primacy firms can coexist with a functioning GGF, and the corpus already routes around them through procurement preference, tax, alternative enterprise forms and stewardship conversion.

So this is a legitimate non-goal that has simply never been declared. Currently it reads as oversight rather than decision. Remedy is one paragraph in Work in Liberation or Nested Economies, not an Enterprise Interface Conditions framework.

If enterprise-facing conditions are wanted anyway, note that they already exist in five places — SCSI's public-risk/shared-upside and loss-allocation rules, Gaian Trade's RTZ membership conditions, Work in Liberation's provider charters and asset locks, Hearthstone's conversion conditions, Genesis §14.3 donor-independence rules. That is reinvention, and the remedy for reinvention is consolidation into a shared conditions grammar, not a new object.

### 8 — Competition / antitrust · diagnostic-only boundary · severity 3

The widest footprint in the audit: 33 files. Polycentric §12.1.1 treats economic dependency as a capture vector, naming finance, employment, land access, infrastructure, credit, contracting, custody, platform and data access, and asks which constituted function is constrained and whether material exit survives. §20.1 lists creditor and provider concentration in the coordinated-capture stress test.

It then refuses jurisdiction explicitly: does not determine whether a firm is too big, define antitrust thresholds, impose ownership limits, or create competition-policy jurisdiction.

That is a correctly declared boundary. The residual risk is handoff realism — every clause that routes concentration to a competent competition authority should name one that exists in the adopting jurisdiction, or state that none does.

Mild endogenous amplification: Community Providers and shared settlement rails can become local monopolies inside a BAZ. Nested Economies §11.5 already asks whether control of shared infrastructure has become practical governance authority, which covers most of it.

### 9 — Payment & settlement access · observability gap inside an existing owner · severity 3

Nested Economies treats settlement rails thoroughly as a common-mode dependency class — E.3 gateway concentration, E.7 shared settlement rails, §11.5 infrastructure dependency review, and fallback provisions including bilateral clearing and offline queues.

`correspondent bank` and `de-risking` return zero. The specific mechanism by which a jurisdiction is cut off from fiat settlement — historically the sharpest instrument used against small financial jurisdictions, and directly relevant to the Christensen material — is unnamed.

Endogenous amplification: the more activity a BAZ routes through Hearts and Treasury rails, the more a single fiat access point matters.

Cheap patch inside an existing owner: add correspondent/fiat-access dependency to E.7 and §11.5.

### 10 — Valuation / accounting / rating standards · reinvented orphan · severity 3

`credit rating`, `rating agency`, `accounting standard`, `fair value` return zero. One hit for `mark-to-market`, in Adaptive Tax's realization-treatment discussion.

Yet three frameworks each build a private valuation regime: Financial Systems §5.4 applies asset-class haircuts and distinguishes five liquidity tiers; SCSI defines investment mandates, permitted instruments, and borrowing/leverage/derivatives authorization; Adaptive Tax defines capital and gains bases including accrual and mark-to-market treatment.

None of them names valuation as a shared function, and no framework asks who decides what a Leaf or a restored watershed is worth on a balance sheet. This is upstream of every other row and is the place GGF is best positioned to contribute something external institutions would actually use.

Remedy is consolidation, not creation.

### 11 — Private equity / leveraged acquisition · governed seam, correctly scoped · severity 4

Two defensive instruments exist and both protect GGF-side assets: Hearthstone `HSP-09` interim anti-dissipation and non-prejudice request against asset stripping without deciding ownership, and Pathfinder's trust/foundation conversion as a shield against hostile takeover. Technology Governance handles the analogous case for maintainership and dependencies.

No governance of the practice itself, which is correct — GGF protects what it stewards and leaves conventional acquisition to external law. Mild endogenous amplification: successful regenerative firms become acquisition targets, which Pathfinder already anticipated.

### 12 — Cross-border capital flows · owned internally · severity 4

Financial Systems §11.8 is titled Volume Caps and Capital Controls; §11.5 classifies changes to capital controls or circuit breakers as Tier B structural changes; permanent capital control is prohibited. Polycentric lists triggering capital flight among capture-adjacent effects; AUBI carries capital-flight mitigation in its risk register.

Hearts-side flows are owned. Fiat-side flows fold into rows 3 and 9.

### 13 — Securities markets · clean external boundary · severity 5

SCSI §1.3 excludes securities law explicitly. Aurora's transparency provisions guard against beneficial-ownership disclosure becoming a re-identification directory. Nothing in the corpus depends on GGF possessing prospectus, listing, market-abuse or broker-licensing capability.

### 14 — Pensions / institutional investors · done · severity 5

SCSI already worked this. v0.1.1 reclassified pensions as deferred individual lifecycle-capital claims distinct from worker or member pooled capital, and raised pension prominence to Phase 0. v0.1.2 distinguished lawful pension-fund investment from impermissible repurposing of accrued beneficiary claims in pension-backed vehicles, and added risk, fiduciary, beneficiary and loss-allocation conditions.

Endogenous amplification exists — GGF funds absorbing pension capital makes GGF a fiduciary for retirement claims — and SCSI is the framework that saw it.

### 15 — Insurance / reinsurance / insurability · done · severity 5

Better handled than most rows. Aethelred states it does not constitute an insurer, guarantor, claims body or public backstop, and separately enumerates the competent insurer, mutual, guarantor, issuer, custodian, fiscal authority, regulator or court as the legally constituted owner. DRR v4.4.2 removed a reinsurance levy from an earlier version as an unowned power. Financial Systems may supply settlement rails, reserve interfaces and crisis liquidity to a **separately constituted** insurance or guarantee body.

Named actor, named non-power, named interface. This row was proposed as a gap and turns out to be a model for how the others should read.

## Conclusion

**One architecture orphan** — commercial credit creation.

**One observability orphan** — sectoral crowding-out, belonging in Nested Economies.

**One partly built seam that needs an owner** — the Monetary Coexistence Protocol, currently a bullet list inside Gaian Trade with no schema entity.

**One undeclared non-goal** — corporate governance.

**Four cheap patches inside existing owners** — reserve-custodian failure (Financial Systems §5.5, Nested Economies E.7), correspondent-banking dependency (Nested Economies E.7, §11.5), AUBI/Leaves-to-land-value coupling condition (Housing §10.1 or Adaptive Tax's Polis interface), valuation-standard consolidation.

**One schema fix** — `protocol_debt_transformation`.

**Two rows retracted** — pensions and insurance are already correctly seamed.

The result does not support a Monetary & Credit Architecture framework. Rows 1 and 3 share one plausible owner, and that owner already has a name inside Gaian Trade. Everything else is a patch, a declaration, or a consolidation.

The general finding is that the corpus's constitutional cleanup produced a reliable pattern: where a function was *named*, it was allocated well — insurance, pensions, securities, competition, mortgage law and sovereign debt are all handled with real external owners and stated non-powers. The failures are entirely in functions that were never named at all. Credit creation, crowding-out, shareholder primacy and valuation standards are not badly governed; they are invisible.

That suggests the durable methodological addition is not a new test but a new question in the framework-review checklist:

> Which economic functions does this framework depend on without mentioning?
