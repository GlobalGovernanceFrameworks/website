What if sovereign debt could be partially repaid through ecological and social regeneration instead of austerity?

A thought experiment. Grounded in real crises, where current approaches seem to struggle for both debtors and creditors.

The reality we know

- Greece’s post-2008 austerity led to a ~25% GDP contraction, mass unemployment, and strained public services - while creditors still recovered only a fraction of what they were owed.
- Similar dynamics appear across many countries: debt servicing crowds out climate adaptation, healthcare, and care infrastructure.
- Much of this is framed as economic necessity, but it often functions more like a political constraint, with very real human and ecological costs.

An alternative lens
What if part of sovereign debt servicing could happen through verified regeneration rather than only through fiscal contraction?

- Land restoration - soil, forests, water systems, biodiversity
- Care economy - health, education, local resilience, care work

The intuition is simple: these are not 'costs' in the long-term, but investments in the conditions that make economies viable.

A possible direction (very open)
One could imagine creditors accepting a mix of conventional payments and some form of verified regeneration outcomes: tracked transparently, perhaps via a shared ledger. Some form of trusted, plural verification would likely be needed, bringing together creditors, debtor nations, and independent actors—to recognize which outcomes count.

What I keep wondering about
I’m not sure where this breaks (or if it does), but I keep coming back to the framing shift itself:

What if repayment was tied to healing rather than contraction?

I’d be very curious how this lands for you, especially in light of your work on austerity, and whether something like this could ever be seen as useful rather than naive or co-optable.

No blueprint behind this note, just a line of inquiry. Happy to share more if it’s of interest, but equally interested in your critique.
