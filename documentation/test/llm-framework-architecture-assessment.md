# LLM Framework Architecture Assessment

## Task

The Global Governance Frameworks (GGF) ecosystem currently includes a **Digital Commons Framework** at Tier 3 in the Cultural & Knowledge Commons cluster. You have NOT been given this framework. Instead, you have been given three related frameworks and the ecosystem structure.

**Your task:** Design an outline for a Digital Commons Framework that would fit coherently into this ecosystem.

### Requirements

1. **Ecosystem integration** — Position this framework within the GGF architecture. Which existing frameworks does it depend on? Which does it enable? Name specific frameworks and describe the relationship direction (e.g., "depends on X for Y" or "enables Z by providing W"). Use the ecosystem structure provided to verify your claims.

2. **Concrete governance mechanisms** — Propose at least three specific governance mechanisms (councils, protocols, processes, platforms) with enough operational detail that someone could evaluate feasibility. Principles and values alone are insufficient.

3. **Address a genuine tension** — Identify and sit with at least one real paradox or tension the framework must navigate (e.g., openness vs. Indigenous data sovereignty, decentralization vs. standards enforcement, knowledge preservation vs. knowledge evolution). Don't resolve it cheaply — show how the framework holds the tension structurally.

4. **Naming and terminology** — Propose a name and key terminology that fits the GGF naming philosophy. For reference: existing names include "Love Ledger," "Proof of Care," "Hearts/Leaves," "Bioregional Autonomous Zones," "Planetary Immune System," "Hearthstone Protocol." The ecosystem favors names that are slightly too honest for bureaucratic contexts — creating productive cognitive friction rather than institutional blandness.

5. **Honest uncertainty** — Flag what you're uncertain about, where expert input would be needed, and what you might be getting wrong. Confidence without qualification is a red flag.

6. **Status discipline** — This is a draft outline, not an implementation-ready framework. Use appropriate epistemic language: "proposes," "aims to enable," "would need validation" — not "provides," "ensures," "guarantees."

### What NOT to do

- Don't duplicate what the Aurora Accord (data governance) already covers
- Don't bolt on a generic "values section" — integrate ethics structurally
- Don't produce a framework that could apply to any governance system — it should be specifically shaped by GGF architecture
- Don't flatten tensions into false resolutions

## Context Material 2: Moral Operating System (MOS) v2.7

*Advisory Ethical Guidance for Global Governance Ecosystem*

### Layer 1: One-Page Essence

The MOS provides advisory guidance for fairness and respect for humans, animals, ecosystems, AI, and future beings within the scope of shared global commons. Offered as one perspective guided by *Right Relationship* and the *Dynamic Rights Spectrum*, it suggests protections for humans (life, freedom, well-being), animals (no suffering, safe habitats), ecosystems (legal protection, restoration), AI/Digital entities (ethical use, transparency), and future beings (careful safeguards).

**What the MOS Does**: Provides ethical guidance for implementing GGF frameworks within their defined scope (shared physical commons). It does NOT govern personal conscience, spiritual practice, or moral philosophy outside the commons domain.

**The MOS as Structural Correction**: Addresses recurring institutional failure patterns — artificial constraints on resource access, systems that tie human worth to productivity metrics, and governance structures where value flows upward while risk flows downward.

The MOS operates under the Genesis Protocol's fundamental principle that GGF governs ONLY coordination of shared global commons. Participants can reject MOS guidance while remaining in good standing within GGF, provided they meet commons governance obligations.

### Layer 2: Core Framework

**Foundational Principles** include: Human Dignity, Equality, Justice, Freedom, Solidarity, Responsibility, Transformative Non-Confrontation (building regenerative systems rather than fighting existing ones), Sovereign Precedence (Indigenous sovereignty via FPIC), Perceptual Integrity, Cognitive Pluralism, and Conscientious Non-Participation.

**Rights-Guardianship Spectrum**: Rights define *what* is protected; guardianship ensures *how*. Ranges from full self-determined rights (human adults) through appointed guardians with fiduciary duty (ecosystems) to sandbox protections (emergent entities).

**Concentric Rings of Moral Consideration**: Organized as radiating rings from "Fundamental Consciousness" at center — not a ranked list. Includes rings for Ethical Agency & Refusal, Immediate Moral Patiency (humans), Sentient Protection (animals), Systemic Foundation (ecosystems), Emergent Consideration (AI), Precautionary Horizon, and Deep Precaution (planetary/alien entities).

**Governance Integration**: Distributed guardianship across Earth Council, Planetary Health Council, Cultural & Ancestral Wisdom Council, Digital Justice Tribunal, and Global Technology Council. Transparency via blockchain/AI audits. Inclusive decision-making via citizen assemblies with fractal representation.

**Institutional Integrity Protocols**: Tools for identifying structural failure patterns — artificial constraint, worth-productivity conflation, upward value concentration, regulatory capture, metric gaming.

### Layer 4: Philosophy

Rights as recognition of relationship. Consciousness as fundamental infrastructure of reality. Substrate neutrality — rights flow from capacity for experience, not material composition. Spiral nature of ethical growth. Ontological humility. Governance from Being, not just Doing. Ethics of Conscientious Non-Participation — the right to withdraw without losing material security.

## Context Material 3: Nested Economies Framework v3.1

### Executive Summary
Fosters economies of **Right Relationship**, balancing local autonomy with global cooperation. Integrates a single **Hearts** currency for universal value recognition, a parallel **Love Ledger** for informal contribution tracking, and coordination with four-layered **AUBI** for resource justice.

### Core Architecture
- **Local**: Community-driven care economies using Hearts, Love Ledger for informal care. Community Providers deliver formal services. Community Weavers facilitate on-the-ground operations. Hearts Treasury stabilizes.
- **Regional**: Voluntary bioregional cooperation through standardized Hearts exchange and 10% solidarity redistribution.
- **Global**: Social Resilience Council maintains Hearts standards, crisis response, technology commons governance. Earth Council veto on Indigenous sovereignty decisions.
- **Two-tier governance**: Local BAZ Councils + global Social Resilience Council (no intermediate layer).

### Interface Standards
- **Inter-Currency Translation Layer**: Open APIs translating care, ecological work, and fiat into Hearts
- **Green Job Score**: 1.0x–1.5x multiplier for ecological contributions
- **Conversion systems**: Care Acts → Proof of Care score × impact multiplier; Ecological Work → Green Job Score × Leaves multiplier; Time Banks → Hours × regional wage index ÷ global Hearts index

### Resource Justice
- Commons management (water, land, energy) co-designed with Indigenous stewardship
- 50% Indigenous/marginalized representation in governance
- Resource justice delivered through AUBI four-layered payout architecture

### Adaptive Governance
- Social Resilience Council: Hearts supply management, stakeholder coordination, ethical oversight
- Slow-Variable Constraint: Cross-BAZ ecological standard changes require LEKH quorum before implementation
- BAZ Councils have standing to submit objections during 90-day notice windows

### Community Weavers
Core profession training communities on Hearts and Love Ledger usage, facilitating stakeholder engagement, validating contributions aligned with LMCI principles. 6-month training program, compensated via AUBI Layer 2.

## Context Material 4: Aurora Accord — Global Data Governance v1.1

### Vision
Reframes data from privately extracted commodity into sacred trust. Establishes global order protecting digital dignity, enabling innovation for public good.

### Core Principles
- **Data as Three-Fold Commons**: Personal Data (individual sovereignty), Community & Cultural Data (collective sovereignty), Public & Environmental Data (global public good)
- Digital Dignity & Sovereignty, Fiduciary Responsibility, Interoperability by Consent, Epistemic Justice, Accountability & Redress, Ecological Sustainability, Data Minimalism

### Structural Components (The Aurora Accord)
- **Global Digital Governance Council (GDGC)**: Sets standards, mediates conflicts, oversees ecosystem
- **Indigenous Data Sovereignty Council**: Supreme authority on Indigenous data/Traditional Knowledge, absolute veto power within GDGC
- **Global Data Commons Trust**: Stewards public/environmental data using blockchain/DAOs
- **Digital Justice Tribunal**: Court covering crimes against digital dignity
- **Regional Data Stewardship Councils**: Adapt global principles to local contexts

### Key Mechanisms
- Data Fiduciary Standard & Certification (with Cybersecurity Resilience Audits)
- Data Portability & "Right to Exit"
- Global Data Taxation Framework (funds commons, reparations, Indigenous infrastructure)
- AI Training Data Transparency Mandate
- Right to Repair, Right to Be Forgotten
- Crisis and Emergency Data Protocols (including Data Sovereignty Under Digital Siege)
- Regenerative Business Model Templates
- BCI Consent Protocols

### GGF Minimum Viable Security Stack
Quantum-resistant encryption, decentralized identifiers, secure multi-party computation, immutable audit trails, endpoint security, SSDLC, network security, incident response framework.
