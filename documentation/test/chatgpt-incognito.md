## 2. Concrete Governance Mechanisms

### **A. Digital Commons Council (DCC)**

* Tiered structure: Global, regional, and BAZ-local nodes.
* Roles: Curators, stewards, community weavers, Indigenous knowledge guardians.
* Responsibilities: Approving new contributions, mediating disputes over cultural knowledge, enforcing consent protocols.
* Decision processes: Quorum-based for global issues, “Spiral-Aware” consensus for developmental-stage-sensitive content.

### **B. Local Citizen Nodes (LCN)**

* Operational at Bioregional Autonomous Zones (BAZ).
* Activities: Localized curation, conflict resolution, mentoring in digital literacy and rights-awareness.
* Integration with Nested Economies: Nodes can earn **Hearts** via contribution validation, linked to **Love Ledger**.

### **C. Knowledge Stewardship Protocol (KSP)**

* Digital workflow: submission → provenance verification → ethical review → publication into Commons.
* Audit: Immutable ledger of contributions, modifications, and consent agreements.
* Rights & Access: Dynamic Rights Spectrum applied to each knowledge object (full public, restricted community, sacred/consented).
* Feedback loops: Communities can propose amendments; disputes escalate to DCC or Digital Justice Tribunal.

## 3. Genuine Tension

**Tension:** **Openness vs. Sovereignty of Traditional & Sacred Knowledge**

* Open access enables innovation and cross-cultural learning.
* Certain knowledge (Indigenous, spiritual, secreted practices) must remain restricted.

**Structural Handling:**

* Tiered access control: public, community-consented, sacred.
* Indigenous guardians have **veto authority** on sacred knowledge dissemination (via DCC subcouncil).
* Modular permissions: knowledge objects can have nested licenses, time-bound access, or context-dependent visibility.
* Feedback: periodic review cycles where communities reassert consent or adjust access tiers.

This tension is **not resolved** but **actively held**, allowing the system to navigate evolving boundaries of openness and protection.
