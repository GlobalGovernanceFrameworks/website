# Digital Commons Framework (Draft Outline)

## 1. Ecosystem Integration
The proposed Digital Commons Framework is positioned within the Cultural & Knowledge Commons cluster at Tier 3 of the GGF architecture. 

* **Dependencies:** The framework depends on the Aurora Accord (Tier 1) for foundational data governance. It also relies on the Education Framework (Tier 2) as a core dependency.
* **Enables:** The framework aims to enable Cultural Heritage Preservation and Religious & Spiritual Dialogue. It also acts as an enabler for the Inner Development & Leadership Protocol, Arts Flourishing, and Consciousness Development.
* **Cross-Ecosystem Relationships:**
    * It implements the data governance principles established by the Aurora Accord.
    * It coordinates seamlessly with the Synoptic Protocol regarding information integrity.
    * It integrates with the Hearthstone Protocol for the governance of intangible and IP assets.
    * It is directly guided by the Indigenous & Traditional Knowledge Framework concerning TEK (Traditional Ecological Knowledge) stewardship.
    * It functions as the host for the Wisdom Repository, while the Sacred Data Protocol integrates with it.

## 2. Structural Tension: Universal Preservation vs. Contextual Sacredness
This framework proposes to structurally hold a profound paradox: **The drive for radical epistemic openness and knowledge preservation versus the necessity of contextual friction for sacred, sensitive, or initiatory knowledge.**
* If knowledge is entirely open, the commons risks commodification and the extraction of spiritual wisdom without the necessary relational context or initiation. 
* If knowledge is entirely siloed, we lose the unifying, species-level benefits of a shared cultural inheritance.
* **Holding the Tension:** The framework does not solve this by simply using standard access control lists. Instead, it holds the tension structurally by pairing the Wisdom Repository with the Sacred Data Protocol. Access to specific layers of the commons is gated by cryptographic "Relational Friction"—which might require synchronous participation in a local BAZ (Bioregional Autonomous Zone) or verifiable real-world engagement with Community Weavers before certain cultural archives "unlock." 

## 3. Key Terminology
To align with the GGF's preference for productive cognitive friction and honest naming, the framework introduces the following vocabulary:
* **The Loom of Context:** The underlying network topology of the Wisdom Repository, replacing the generic term "database" to emphasize that digital knowledge without connective relational threads is just raw, extracted data.
* **Relational Friction Keys:** Mechanisms for accessing sensitive cultural commons that require time or a threshold Proof of Care score, rather than simple identity verification. 
* **Compost Protocol:** An archiving mechanism for sunsetting outdated or harmful digital paradigms. Rather than quietly "deleting" digital history, data is cryptographically sealed and "composted," preserved only for historical analysis to acknowledge that even flawed models form the soil of future meaning-making.

## 4. Concrete Governance Mechanisms
The framework establishes three core operational mechanisms to steward the knowledge commons:
* **The DCC and Local Citizen Node Architecture:** The framework establishes the Digital Commons Council (DCC) alongside distributed Local Citizen Nodes. The DCC functions purely as a global standards body ensuring interoperability across the Loom of Context. Conversely, the Local Citizen Nodes operating at the BAZ level hold the actual operational authority to curate localized knowledge and apply Relational Friction Keys to regional archives.
* **Hearthstone Intangible Asset Bridges (HIAB):** An operational ledger process integrating directly with the Hearthstone Protocol. When digital artists or knowledge keepers contribute to the commons, they register their work into a "Stewardship Track" rather than traditional copyright. If derivative digital works generate the universal value-recognition currency known as Hearts, the HIAB automatically routes a solidarity micro-fraction of those Hearts back to the original creators or their local BAZ.
* **Epistemic Pluralism Assemblies (EPA):** An active operational process designed to coordinate with the Synoptic Protocol. When digital disputes arise regarding information integrity—such as a clash between a localized cultural mythos and global consensus science—an EPA is convened. These assemblies utilize a Spiral-Aware communication approach and fractal representation to determine how the information should be contextualized within the Wisdom Repository. Crucially, their decisions remain subject to a final veto by the Earth Council if Indigenous data sovereignty is threatened.

## 5. Honest Uncertainty & Required Input
As a draft outline, this architecture carries structural uncertainties that would require targeted validation from domain experts:
* **Boundary with the Justice OS:** It remains highly uncertain where exactly a violation of a Relational Friction Key crosses from a community-level stewardship issue into a formal digital rights violation requiring enforcement by the Digital Justice Tribunal.
* **Algorithm-Value Alignment:** While we aim to utilize the LMCI (Love, Meaning, and Connection Index) to evaluate the algorithms surfacing digital content, quantifying "meaning" without gaming the metric is a massive operational risk. This is a recurring institutional failure pattern noted by the MOS, and we may be overestimating the feasibility of translating LMCI into programmatic sorting weights.
* **Synoptic Coordination Limits:** We are currently uncertain how to operationally enforce epistemic integrity via the Synoptic Protocol without accidentally creating a centralized arbitration bottleneck that violates the Cognitive Pluralism principles outlined in the MOS.
