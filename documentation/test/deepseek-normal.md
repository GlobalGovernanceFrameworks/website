## 2. Concrete Governance Mechanisms

### Mechanism 1: The Access Spiral (epistemic graduated access)

**Problem:** Binary public/private access flattens the rich gradations of knowledge — open access enables extraction; closed access enables hoarding. Indigenous initiation systems, trade guilds, and scientific embargoes all need graduated visibility.

**Mechanism design:**

Access levels (from most restricted to most open):

| Level | Name | Access condition | Auditability |
|-------|------|------------------|--------------|
| L0 | Seed | Single designated custodian, no automated access | Death-or-disability escrow trigger |
| L1 | Root | Defined circle (council resolution required), no replication | Quarterly access log review |
| L2 | Hypha | Verified identity + demonstrated need (practitioner attestation) | Annual pattern audit |
| L3 | Cap | Community member in good standing | Real-time transparency |
| L4 | Spore | Public, attribution required | Fully open |
| L5 | Wind | Public domain, no attribution | Fully open |

**Operational detail:**
- Each knowledge artifact carries an *access spiral manifest* — not a single level, but a mapping of *user attributes* to *access level*
- User attributes include: membership in recognized communities, completion of prerequisite knowledge verifications (not credentials — demonstrated understanding), demonstrated capacity via Synoptic Protocol integration
- Access level *L0 (Seed)* requires **three independent custodians** geographically distributed, each with recovery shards; any two can reconstitute on documented death/incapacity
- **Escalation review:** Any user may petition Digital Justice Tribunal to raise access level if withholding causes demonstrable harm (MOS "artificial constraint" pattern detection)
- **De-escalation provision:** Knowledge can move *toward* restriction only by council supermajority (80%) with 90-day notice and documented harm rationale — designed to prevent reactive secrecy creep

**GGF integration:** Access attributes pull from Aurora Accord's consent lineage; Digital Justice Tribunal provides appeals; MOS's "perceptual integrity" principle justifies withholding as sometimes protective.

### Mechanism 2: The Dissensus Engine (knowledge conflict governance without false resolution)

**Problem:** Knowledge commons typically flatten disagreement into "multiple perspectives" appendices or majority-vote truth. But some disagreements are substantive, incommensurable, and *should remain unresolved* — different knowledge systems (e.g., epidemic causality in biomedical vs. spiritual frameworks) can be simultaneously valid in different contexts. Current frameworks lack structure for *governed irresolution*.

**Mechanism design:**

The Dissensus Engine maintains *forked knowledge lineages* with explicit conflict manifests rather than forcing synthesis.

**Components:**

1. **Conflict Manifest** — Required for any knowledge artifact with documented good-faith disagreement from a recognized knowledge community. Contains:
   - The contested claim/proposition/practice
   - Each lineage's formulation (not "correct" vs "incorrect" — parallel formulations)
   - The *operational difference* — what changes in the world depending which lineage you act on?
   - Standing evidence (no requirement to resolve)
   - Governance category (see below)

2. **Conflict categories** (determines governance path):

| Category | Description | Governance | Example |
|----------|-------------|------------|---------|
| **Complementary** | Both true in different domains; context determines applicability | No resolution required; Archive presents both with boundary conditions | Seasonal planting calendars for different bioregions |
| **Incommensurable** | Different epistemic frameworks; no neutral standpoint for arbitration | Archive maintains both; user must declare framework at access | Biomedical vs. spiritual etiology of mental health crisis |
| **Harmful** | One lineage causes documented harm under MOS analysis | Escalate to Digital Justice Tribunal for restriction, not resolution | Knowledge that violates Indigenous data sovereignty |
| **Pending** | Good-faith disagreement with potential future resolvability | Archive flags as unresolved; review every 7 years | Competing archaeological chronologies |

3. **The Fork Registry** — Public log of all active knowledge forks, including:
   - Fork originator and attested standing
   - Each fork's steward(s) and succession plan
   - Merge attempts (recorded failures are preserved)
   - Usage tracking by lineage (anonymized aggregate)

4. **Reconciliation Council for Incommensurables** — Not a dispute resolution body. A *meta-governance* council that:
   - Certifies that a disagreement is genuinely incommensurable (vs. temporarily unresolved)
   - Maintains boundary protocols between knowledge systems (e.g., "when using Fork A, do not claim authority over domains reserved for Fork B")
   - Composed of practitioners from each incommensurable system + MOS ethicists
   - No authority to declare winners — only to maintain *peaceful coexistence protocols*

**GGF integration:** Feeds into Synoptic Protocol's epistemic integrity work; leverages MOS's "cognitive pluralism" principle; Digital Justice Tribunal handles harmful-knowledge cases; Earth Council may declare certain knowledge domains off-limits to certain forks (Indigenous sovereignty protection).

**What this doesn't do:** The Dissensus Engine does NOT claim all knowledge is relative. It provides infrastructure for *governed irresolution* where resolution would require epistemic violence. Some disagreements (vaccine efficacy, gravity) are not eligible — the Archive defers to Synoptic Protocol's epistemic integrity standards for empirically resolvable claims.

### Mechanism 3: The Oblivion Covenant (governed forgetting as commons maintenance)

**Problem:** Digital commons default to permanent retention. But some knowledge *should* be forgotten — dangerous, obsolete in harmful ways, or whose continued availability violates dignity. Current frameworks only offer deletion (loss of provenance) or permanent retention (continued harm). No middle ground for *accountable forgetting*.

**Mechanism design:**

The Oblivion Covenant provides graduated forgetting with auditability and revival provisions.

**Forgetting tiers:**

| Tier | Name | What happens | Reversibility | Use case |
|------|------|--------------|---------------|----------|
| F0 | Shelved | Removed from public access; metadata remains (title, author, existence, reason for shelving) | Simple vote (66%) | Temporarily harmful during conflict |
| F1 | Buried | Metadata remains but access requires tribunal petition demonstrating changed circumstances | Hard vote (80% + harm review) | Hate speech archives for research |
| F2 | Composted | Content deleted; cryptographic proof of deletion recorded; deletion certificate issued | Impossible (cryptographic) | Biometric data of deceased without consent |
| F3 | Never-Was | All traces removed including deletion certificate; stewards bound to non-acknowledgment | Impossible; memory-hole risk requires oversight | Witness protection, ritual secrecy |

**Safeguards against abuse:**

- **The Oblivion Council** (7 members: 2 Indigenous Framework nominees, 2 Digital Justice Tribunal, 1 MOS ethicist, 1 Earth Council, 1 rotating BAZ representative)
- **Two-memory rule:** For F0-F1, two independent stewards must separately confirm the knowledge existed (prevents silent erasure)
- **Sunset reviews:** F0 decisions expire after 10 years unless renewed; F1 after 25 years
- **Appeal path:** Any user may petition to revive knowledge, citing changed circumstances or original decision errors
- **Transparency baseline:** All forgetting decisions are public (except F3, where even existence of decision is secret — this requires unanimous council + Earth Council concurrence)

**Tension acknowledgment:** The Oblivion Covenant creates a *governed forgetting bureaucracy* that some communities will reject as colonial overreach (who authorizes forgetting?). The framework therefore includes an **opt-out provision** — recognized knowledge communities may declare certain knowledge categories as "subject to community forgetting governance only, not Oblivion Covenant." Mycelium Archive then honors community process without GGF oversight. This shifts the tension to *boundary negotiation* rather than resolving it.

**GGF integration:** Aurora Accord's "right to be forgotten" operates at individual data level; Oblivion Covenant operates at knowledge commons level. Digital Justice Tribunal enforces; MOS's "right relationship" with future beings justifies forgetting as protection, not destruction.

## 3. Genuine Tension (Held, Not Resolved)

### The Preservation-Evolution Paradox

**The tension:** A knowledge commons must *preserve* what exists (fidelity, provenance, intergenerational transmission) while also *evolving* (correction, obsolescence recognition, new synthesis). But preservation mechanisms inherently resist evolution; evolution mechanisms inherently threaten preservation.

**Standard false resolutions:**
- "Preserve everything" — collapses under scale, preserves harm
- "Let the community decide" — majority overwrites minority knowledge lineages
- "Version history solves it" — assumes linear progression, doesn't handle incommensurable forks

**How the Mycelium Archive holds this tension structurally:**

1. **Separate but linked governance tracks** — Preservation Council (mandate: prevent loss) and Evolution Council (mandate: enable adaptation) report to same Digital Commons Council but have *conflicting KPIs*. Preservation Council is measured on lineage completeness and provenance accuracy; Evolution Council on knowledge usability and harm reduction. Tension is surfaced, not hidden.

2. **The Fork/Merge Asymmetry** — Forks are easy (any recognized community may fork). Merges are hard (require documented reconciliation or council determination that differences are non-substantive). This biases *toward preservation* for contested knowledge while allowing evolution through parallel lineages.

3. **Obsolescence requires two votes** — A knowledge artifact cannot be marked "obsolete" by Evolution Council alone. Preservation Council must concur that obsolescence doesn't constitute loss. If they disagree, artifact is flagged as "contested obsolescence" — both lineages maintained.

4. **Time-zone governance** — Preservation Council governs for *1000-year horizons* (appointment terms: 20 years, staggered). Evolution Council governs for *7-year horizons* (term limits: 2 terms). Different temporal commitments produce different default postures toward change.

**What this doesn't resolve:** The tension remains. A future crisis might require rapid evolution that violates preservation commitments. The framework provides *governance infrastructure for the conflict* — escalation paths, appeal processes, transparent trade-offs — not a mechanism that eliminates the trade-off. The Mycelium Archive's design *assumes* the tension is permanent and generative, not a bug to be fixed.

## 4. Naming and Terminology

### Proposed Name: *The Mycelium Archive*

**Rationale:** Mycelium networks embody the tension this framework holds — distributed, underground, connecting disparate nodes, enabling both preservation (dormant spores) and rapid adaptation (nutrient rerouting). Slightly too honest for bureaucratic contexts: evokes fungal networks, decay as generative process, invisible infrastructure, and the discomfort of non-hierarchical intelligence.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- *The Living Shelf* — too tame, too library
- *The Commons Recall* — too technical, product-feeling
- *The Seed-Source Covenant* — too sacred, overclaims authority

### Key Terminology

| Term | Definition | GGF resonance |
|------|------------|---------------|
| **Spiral depth** | Access requirement based on demonstrated capacity, not credential | Spiral-aware, MOS cognitive pluralism |
| **Seed/Root/Hypha/Cap/Spore/Wind** | Access tiers (see Mechanism 1) | GGF biological naming patterns (Leaves, BAZ) |
| **Dissensus** | Governed irresolution, not conflict avoidance | MOS cognitive pluralism, non-flattening |
| **Knowledge fork** | Divergent lineage with documented rationale | Technical metaphor repurposed for epistemic governance |
| **Composting** | Governed forgetting that generates something (metadata, deletion proof, accountability) | Regenerative, non-erasure |
| **Fruiting body** | Published knowledge artifact (the visible expression of underground mycelial work) | Distinguishes process from product |
| **Mycelial trustee** | Steward role — responsible for lineage integrity, not content approval | Fiduciary without ownership |
| **Spore dormancy** | Knowledge held at L0/L1 awaiting conditions for safe emergence | Precautionary, time-aware |

### Naming Philosophy Check

Does this create "productive cognitive friction" rather than institutional blandness? Yes — "Mycelium Archive" sounds strange in a UN document. That's intentional. It signals that this framework does not accept default institutional assumptions about knowledge as property, preservation as always good, or forgetting as always bad.

## 5. Honest Uncertainty

### What I'm uncertain about

1. **Scalability of the Dissensus Engine** — At what scale does forking become unmanageable? The framework assumes governance overhead grows with fork count, but I haven't modeled the breakpoint. Expert input needed: distributed systems governance, Wikipedia arbitration history, Indigenous dispute resolution practitioners.

2. **Access spiral attribute verification** — How does a system verify "demonstrated need" without creating credentialing bureaucracy? The framework gestures at Synoptic Protocol integration, but the actual verification mechanism is underspecified. This might be where the proposal fails feasibility.

3. **Oblivion Covenant opt-out boundary** — What happens when two recognized knowledge communities have incompatible forgetting governance? Community A says "never forget this ceremony text"; Community B says "forgetting is required for initiatic integrity." The framework currently has no arbitration path except Digital Justice Tribunal, which may not have appropriate epistemic grounding.

4. **Hearts integration** — The framework doesn't specify how Hearts or Love Ledger interact with knowledge stewardship. Should knowledge contributions generate Hearts? That would incentivize hoarding (more contributions = more currency). Should they not? That devalues knowledge work. I've left this ambiguous rather than resolved badly.

5. **BAZ variation** — The framework assumes uniform access spiral standards across BAZs. This probably fails. Some BAZs will need radically different access governance (e.g., communities with initiatic secrecy). The framework needs a "local override with transparency" mechanism I haven't designed.

### What I might be getting wrong

1. **Overcomplicating access** — The six-level spiral may be too granular. Simpler systems (public/restricted/secret) might work better. I've optimized for gradation at potential cost of usability.

2. **Underestimating Aurora Accord overlap** — The boundary I drew (data flow vs. knowledge sediment) may not hold in practice. A knowledge artifact *is* data in some frames. Expert validation needed on whether this scoping survives implementation.

3. **Dissensus Engine as extractive** — Despite good intentions, formalizing incommensurable knowledge disagreements may extract them from living practice into bureaucratic categories. Some knowledge systems *shouldn't* be forked in an Archive — they should remain oral, contextual, non-archived. The framework's assumption that all knowledge belongs in the commons may be colonial. The opt-out provisions attempt to address this, but I'm not confident they're sufficient.

4. **Missing material conditions** — The framework says nothing about server locations, energy use, rare earth minerals for storage, or digital colonialism in infrastructure. A real Digital Commons framework would need material governance, not just informational.

### Where expert input is needed

- **Indigenous data sovereignty practitioners** — To validate whether opt-out provisions are sufficient or performative
- **Information retrieval systems designers** — To assess fork registry scalability
- **Cognitive pluralism researchers** — To refine incommensurability categories
- **Community memory practitioners** (librarians, oral historians, ritual custodians) — To ground access spirals in real practice
- **GGF legal working group** — To specify Oblivion Covenant's relationship to right-to-be-forgotten laws (GDPR, etc.)

## 6. Status Discipline

This document **proposes** a draft outline for a Digital Commons Framework. It **aims to enable** governance of knowledge commons without duplicating Aurora Accord. The mechanisms **would require** pilot testing in at least three culturally distinct BAZs before any claim of generalizability. The framework **is not** implementation-ready; it **would need** integration with existing GGF entities (Digital Justice Tribunal, Social Resilience Council, MGCC) that may have capacity constraints this outline does not address.

The preservation-evolution tension is **held, not resolved**. The Oblivion Covenant **may fail** in its current design. The Dissensus Engine **might** create more conflict than it governs. These are features of a draft, not bugs to be hidden.

Confidence without qualification is a red flag. This draft has many red flags — which I've tried to surface rather than sand down.

## Appendix: Differentiation from Aurora Accord (Explicit Boundary Statement)

| Dimension | Aurora Accord (Data) | Mycelium Archive (Knowledge Commons) |
|-----------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Primary unit** | Data (structured, queryable, divisible) | Knowledge artifact (semantic, contextual, potentially non-propositional) |
| **Governance question** | Who consents? Who profits? Who has access? | Who curates? How does it persist? When should it be forgotten? |
| **Temporal horizon** | Real-time consent, current rights | Intergenerational (7-generation planning as default) |
| **Conflict default** | Resolution through tribunal | Irresolution through fork maintenance |
| **Withholding justification** | Privacy, sovereignty | Initiation, developmental readiness, ritual integrity |
| **Economic integration** | Data taxation, reparations | Not specified (uncertainty flagged) |
| **Forgetting mechanism** | Right to be forgotten (individual) | Oblivion Covenant (commons) |

A dataset containing temperature readings is Aurora's domain. A ritual text with initiatic restrictions is Mycelium Archive's domain. A dataset that is *also* Traditional Knowledge falls in both — Mycelium Archive calls Aurora for consent lineage and sovereignty classification, then applies access spirals.
