**Digital Commons Framework Outline (Draft)**  
**Proposed Name: "Wisdom Hearth" Framework** (or "Hearthwise Commons Protocol" – evoking a shared digital hearth where knowledge is tended like a living fire: warm enough for gathering, contained enough not to burn the village, and sacred enough that some coals belong only to certain keepers).

This draft outline positions the **Wisdom Hearth** as a Tier 3 framework in the Cultural & Knowledge Commons cluster. It focuses on the stewardship, circulation, and co-evolution of **non-raw knowledge assets** (curated archives, cultural narratives, spiritual insights, open educational resources, collective wisdom repositories, and derivative cultural expressions) rather than raw personal, community, or environmental data (which Aurora Accord governs). It treats knowledge as a relational, living commons that must be both generously shared and respectfully bounded.

### 1. Ecosystem Integration

The Wisdom Hearth depends on and implements several Tier 1 and Tier 2 elements while enabling higher-order cultural and consciousness work in Tier 3/4.

**Dependencies (what it draws from):**
- **Aurora Accord (Tier 1 Data Governance)**: Implements its three-fold data classification (personal/community/public) and fiduciary standards for any underlying data layers. It does **not** duplicate Aurora's data sovereignty mechanisms, portability rules, or taxation; instead, it applies them to decide when curated knowledge derived from data can enter the commons (e.g., via consent-based "knowledge release protocols" that reference Aurora's BCI consent and Right to Exit). Relationship: "Depends on Aurora for baseline digital dignity and sovereignty enforcement so it can focus on relational stewardship of derived knowledge."
- **Moral Operating System (MOS)**: Guided by its concentric rings of moral consideration, Dynamic Rights Spectrum, and Right Relationship principles. Uses MOS's Institutional Integrity Protocols to flag risks like "upward value concentration" in knowledge (e.g., elite curation capture).
- **Indigenous & Traditional Knowledge Framework / Earth Council**: Guided by TEK stewardship and FPIC (Free, Prior, Informed Consent). Earth Council holds veto on matters involving sacred or ancestral knowledge.
- **Hearthstone Protocol (Property & Stewardship, Tier 2)**: Integrates its ownership-to-stewardship transition for intangible/IP assets. Wisdom Hearth treats "knowledge titles" as stewardship deeds rather than exclusive property.
- **Education Framework (Tier 2)** and **Synoptic Protocol (epistemic integrity)**: Relies on these for baseline literacy and information quality before knowledge enters the Hearth.
- **Nested Economies / Love Ledger / Hearts / AUBI**: Uses these for contribution validation and resource allocation (e.g., Hearts multipliers for care-oriented curation).

**Enables (what it supports):**
- **Cultural Heritage Preservation** and **Religious & Spiritual Dialogue**: By hosting verifiable, contextualized archives that respect sacred boundaries.
- **Inner Development & Leadership Protocol / Consciousness Development (Tier 4)**: Provides the Wisdom Repository as a substrate for spiral-aware, stage-sensitive knowledge practices.
- **Arts Flourishing**: Offers platforms for co-creative remixing under guardianship rules.
- **BAZ-level (Bioregional Autonomous Zone) activities**: Local Citizen Nodes adapt global knowledge to bioregional contexts.

**Key relationships in practice:**
- Coordinates with **Global Technology Council** and **Digital Justice Tribunal** for enforcement.
- Hosts the **Wisdom Repository** (cross-referenced in the ecosystem description).
- Integrates **Sacred Data Protocol** outputs without owning the raw data.
- Local **Digital Commons Council (DCC)** nodes report upward to a lightweight **Meta-Governance Coordination Council (MGCC)** interface, avoiding duplication of higher governance.

This positioning keeps the framework GGF-specific: it operates within the Genesis Protocol's "only shared global commons" scope and uses Hearts/Love Ledger incentives rather than market or state defaults.

### 2. Concrete Governance Mechanisms

Three operational mechanisms (designed to be evaluable for feasibility):

**a. Digital Commons Council (DCC) with Fractal Node Structure**  
A distributed council, not a central bureaucracy. Global DCC sets minimal interoperability standards (e.g., metadata schemas for provenance and guardianship levels). Local **Citizen Nodes** (one per BAZ or cluster of BAZs) handle day-to-day curation and disputes.  
- **Composition**: 40% elected via spiral-aware sortition (citizens), 30% rotating stewards validated by **Proof of Care** + Love Ledger contributions, 20% Indigenous/TEK representatives (Earth Council-nominated), 10% technical guardians (Global Technology Council liaison).  
- **Process**: Quarterly "Hearth Circles" (hybrid physical-digital assemblies using Synoptic Protocol tools for epistemic checking). Decisions use consent-based escalation: local nodes decide first; disputes escalate to Digital Justice Tribunal with 90-day public notice window (mirroring Nested Economies' objection process).  
- **Funding/Compensation**: Stewards receive AUBI Layer 2 top-ups + Hearts multipliers for documented "care acts" (e.g., contextualizing knowledge to prevent misuse). Feasibility note: Nodes would need lightweight tooling (e.g., decentralized identifiers from Aurora's security stack) to avoid capture by tech-literate elites.

**b. Knowledge Guardianship Protocol (bundle-of-rights inspired, adapted from commons literature)**  
Knowledge assets entering the Hearth receive a "Guardianship Deed" (non-fungible, blockchain-audited via Aurora-compatible immutable trails) that bundles rights differently from traditional IP:  
- **Access tiers**: Open (default for public-derived knowledge), Gated (community consent required), Sacred (Earth Council veto + physical/cultural protocols for access).  
- **Remixing rules**: Derivative works must include "Lineage Tags" (provenance metadata) and offer reciprocal contribution (e.g., via Love Ledger or reinvestment into the Hearth, echoing reciprocity ideas in digital commons discussions).  
- **Sunset & Evolution Clause**: All deeds include a review trigger (e.g., every 7–21 years, or when LMCI metrics flag cultural harm) where guardianship can shift via DCC vote. Operational detail: Submissions go through an automated + human "Care Filter" (Proof of Care score + MOS integrity check) before acceptance. This draws on but does not duplicate Aurora's data fiduciary certification.

**c. Wisdom Repository Hosting & Contribution Validation Platform**  
A federated, decentralized archive (not a single database) using Aurora's Minimum Viable Security Stack. Contributions are validated via a three-step process:  
1. **Epistemic Intake** (Synoptic Protocol + community weavers from Nested Economies).  
2. **Relational Impact Assessment** (scored against LMCI principles and concentric moral rings).  
3. **Steward Assignment** (automatic or nominated, with Hearts incentives).  
Local BAZ nodes maintain mirrors or subsets, with global search layered on top (interoperability by consent). Enforcement: Violations (e.g., unauthorized extraction for proprietary AI) trigger Digital Justice Tribunal redress, potentially including Hearts-based reparations or temporary node suspension.

These mechanisms aim to be concrete enough for pilot testing in a few BAZs while remaining adaptive.

### 3. Genuine Tension Addressed: Open Circulation vs. Relational/Sacred Boundaries

A core paradox the Wisdom Hearth must navigate is **radical openness for collective flourishing versus the need for protective boundaries** — especially around Indigenous/Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK), sacred narratives, or culturally sensitive wisdom that could be distorted, extracted, or weaponized when decontextualized (a real tension highlighted in global digital commons and data sovereignty debates).

Cheap resolutions (e.g., "just make everything open with attribution" or "let communities gate everything") fail because they either enable epistemic colonialism or stifle cross-cultural dialogue and evolution.

The framework holds this structurally through **tiered guardianship and relational consent flows** rather than binary open/closed:  
- Knowledge enters with explicit "boundary metadata" (who can access under what conditions, what contextual obligations apply).  
- Open layers feed global innovation and consciousness work (enabling Tier 4).  
- Sacred layers remain under Earth Council veto and localized protocols, with "conscientious non-participation" options (per MOS).  
- Evolution happens via scheduled reviews and "remix reciprocity" that requires contributors to demonstrate care (Proof of Care) before gaining broader access rights.  

This creates productive friction: the system slows harmful extraction while accelerating regenerative circulation. It does not "resolve" the tension — it institutionalizes ongoing negotiation at the DCC/Hearth Circle level, with escalation paths that surface power imbalances. Unresolved edge cases (e.g., wisdom that multiple overlapping communities claim) would route to mediated processes involving MGCC and Earth Council.

### 4. Naming and Terminology

- **Core name**: Wisdom Hearth (or Hearthwise Protocol) — honest about its domestic, care-oriented scale (a hearth is local and relational, not a grand "global knowledge bank").
- **Key terms**: Knowledge Guardianship Deeds, Hearth Circles, Lineage Tags, Care Filter, Relational Consent Flows, Sacred Coals (for non-circulating elements). These introduce cognitive friction: they sound slightly too intimate or folkloric for bureaucratic comfort, signaling that this is about tending living relationships, not managing assets.

This fits the GGF aesthetic (Love Ledger, Proof of Care, Hearthstone) by grounding digital abstraction in embodied, care-based imagery.

### 5. Honest Uncertainty and Epistemic Discipline

This is a **draft outline**, not a complete or validated framework. It proposes structures that would need extensive co-design with existing GGF participants, especially Indigenous representatives via Earth Council and local BAZ practitioners.

**Key uncertainties**:
- Scalability of fractal DCC nodes without creating coordination overload or elite capture (expert input needed on DAO/ blockchain governance failures and successes).
- How "spiral-aware" decision tools interact with Proof of Care validation in practice — risk of developmental-stage bias.
- Technical feasibility of lineage tagging and relational metadata at scale without becoming cumbersome (would need validation against Aurora's security stack and real-world knowledge commons experiments).
- Potential missing elements: I may have under-emphasized integration with Technology OS or underestimated conflicts with commercial AI training incentives. The tension-holding mechanism could still flatten in implementation if review cycles are gamed.
- Broader risk: Over-reliance on Hearts/Love Ledger incentives might reproduce "metric gaming" patterns that MOS warns against.

Expert input is essential from: knowledge commons researchers, TEK stewards, digital governance practitioners, and pilots in diverse bioregions. What might be wrong: the proposal could still lean too globalist or not sufficiently differentiate from existing open knowledge initiatives; it assumes GGF's other frameworks (e.g., MOS, Nested Economies) function as described, which they may not in messy reality.

This outline aims to enable coherent integration without claiming guarantees. It is offered in the spirit of conscientious participation in the broader GGF experiment — open to revision through the very mechanisms it proposes.
