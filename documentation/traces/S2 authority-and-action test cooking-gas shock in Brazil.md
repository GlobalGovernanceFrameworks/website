# S2 authority-and-action test: cooking-gas shock in Brazil

Nov 23, 2026 · @Björn Kenneth Holmström

*v0.2, 23 November 2026: reconciled after an external review. Adds the official redemption dashboard and alternative service plans; records Brazil's fuel-supply contingency duties and the suspended ANP contingency-plan work; marks the 2031 extension as provisional under MP 1.400/2026; restates the retailer and fiscal figures as conditional stress tests. Frameworks stay frozen.*

## Summary

S2 runs a cooking-gas shock through Brazilian institutions as they stand in November 2026, without GGF and then with the frozen frameworks (Climate & Energy v2.2, AUBI v2.7.1, Gaian Trade v0.9.3) acting only through Brazilian authorities. It tests the same functional problem as S1, access to essential energy in a shock, using bottled LPG rather than networked gas.

- **Case A (price triples, cylinders available): Brazil has the instrument Germany lacked.** Gás do Povo gives about 15 million families free refills by law, so a voucher holder is protected on day 0, and the programme publishes redemption data (79% of vouchers redeemed in the latest completed period). The weak points lie elsewhere. Retailers are paid a reference price with no published update trigger; if their costs rise faster than it, they may drop out or favour cash buyers, a hypothesis supported by the April 2026 adjustment but not yet a demonstrated failure. One-person households, families with stale registrations, those just above the income line, and refills beyond the four or six a year are unprotected.
- **Case B (supply disrupted): no operational LPG allocation rule was identified.** Brazil has fuel-supply contingency duties (MME, ANP) and monitoring, and ANP had begun a plan for supply-restriction situations before suspending it in June 2026. S2 found no operational, pre-authorized rule that gives vulnerable households or voucher holders priority, and no LPG reserve. A voucher protects price, not access. Firewood is the de facto fallback.
- **The two countries have different structures, not opposite ones.** Germany has a designated shortage-allocation authority and no pre-built price-shock instrument; Brazil has a pre-built price-shock instrument for its target group and a shortage response made of monitoring, regulation, programme service plans and emergency legislation, without a household-priority rule.
- **Pre-authorization is necessary but not sufficient.** Brazil adds three failure points it does not reach: coverage, payment to intermediaries that tracks the shock, and physical availability. Brazil's fast executive law (MPs) partly substitutes for pre-authorization but lapses by default.

The frameworks stay frozen. As in S1, the first draft missed existing capabilities (here, the redemption dashboard and alternative service plans). That points to a test for GGF itself: can it find and assess what institutions already have before declaring a function missing? S2 records candidate refinements to the readiness profile and four interface notes for the synthesis after S3 Sweden.

## Scope, method and comparability

- **Jurisdiction:** Brazil, for both cases.
- **Comparability:** Brazilian households mainly cook with bottled LPG; natural gas reaches few homes. LPG and natural gas have different supply chains, so S2 keeps S1's functional problem (access to essential energy in a price shock and in a shortage) rather than S1's commodity.
- **Window:** the first six weeks from day 0.
- **Cases:** A, the retail LPG price triples and cylinders remain available; B, LPG supply or distribution is disrupted.
- **Baseline:** existing Brazilian institutions at their state on 23 November 2026.
- **Counterfactual:** Climate & Energy v2.2, AUBI v2.7.1 and Gaian Trade v0.9.3, frozen for the comparison, plus GMEAIA v0.2.2. No new GGF authority or record type; every GGF action passes through a Brazilian competent authority. The market-power step is tested as a GMEAIA-08 referral to CADE.
- **Outputs and grading:** as S1 v0.2: first executable protective action, the four timing measures, real-resource constraints, authority gaps, coordination burden; instruments graded specified, enacted, funded or operational; recorded handoff states kept separate from readiness; the six-question readiness profile.
- **Separate pathway:** fertilizer imports and food security, traced apart from LPG to keep the causal chains distinct.
- **Limits:** a desk test from public sources. Redemption, active-retailer and service-plan figures come from the official MDS dashboard (reference date 19 November 2026) as reported in the external review; S2 could not open the dashboard directly. These are routine monitoring figures, not performance under stress. No data on consumption beyond the allowance. Retailer cost and fiscal figures are conditional stress tests with stated assumptions. GGF response times are specifications.

## Brazilian baseline (November 2026)

Same grading as S1: **specified**, **enacted**, **funded**, **operational**. The commodity is bottled LPG in 13 kg cylinders (P13), which most Brazilian households cook with, not networked natural gas.

**Live context.** Brazil has already had a real 2026 shock. After the Hormuz disruption in March, ANP reported that LPG import parity rose about 60%, and Brazil imports roughly a fifth of its LPG. Retail prices moved far less: about R$110 per cylinder before the war, R$114.61 in late April, and R$114.98 in the week of 18–24 October. Petrobras held its regular contract prices for household LPG and covered import costs through auctions. Case A's tripling is therefore well beyond anything observed, while Case B has a real precedent in the 2018 truckers' strike.

**What the baseline shows.** Brazil has what Germany lacked: a funded, operational, pre-authorized household instrument for the essential fuel, monitored through a public redemption dashboard and backed by alternative service plans where no retailer participates, plus an executive power to legislate within days. The open questions are who is outside the programme, whether the reference price keeps retailers delivering when costs jump, whether its 2031 extension survives conversion, and what a voucher is worth when there is no cylinder to exchange.

## Case A: LPG price triples, cylinders available

**Scenario.** On day 0 the retail price of a 13 kg cylinder triples, from about R$115 to about R$345, and stays there. Cylinders remain on retailers' shelves.

| Week | Baseline (Brazilian institutions) | GGF counterfactual (frozen frameworks, acting only through Brazilian authorities) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | ANP's weekly survey shows the rise within days. Nothing is triggered automatically. Families holding a current voucher can redeem it free at once: the law bars retailers from charging them anything | As specified, C&E §7 classifies a price shock and AUBI §5.1.5 looks for a pre-authorized host instrument. For the first time in either study it finds one: Gás do Povo. Classification speed remains a design target, as in S1 |
| 1 | ANP is already obliged to report signs of competition infractions to CADE; Procons can act on unjustified price rises under the consumer code. The executive can issue an MP within days, as it did twice in spring 2026 | GMEAIA-08 referral to CADE. Recorded handoff state: `offered`. It duplicates ANP's statutory duty, so the GGF referral adds a record, not a pathway |
| 2–4 | Retailers are still paid the old reference price. Whether that hurts depends on where the rise originates: in January 2026 a cylinder's price split roughly into producer R$37, distribution margin R$35 and retail margin R$29, plus taxes. **Stress-test assumption:** if the whole rise comes upstream, a retailer's procurement cost rises about R$230 while the reference price stays fixed. Two pressures follow: cost pressure (voucher sales lose money) and opportunity cost (a cash sale pays more even if a voucher sale still covers cost). Either could lead retailers to drop out or favour cash buyers, creating a delivery-access shortage for voucher holders while the country has enough LPG. An extraordinary update is discretionary and needs budget: about 65 million refills a year with an extra R$200 each would add roughly R$13 billion a year (a conditional calculation, not a forecast). In 2026 the update came in late April, about seven weeks into the shock, after retailers said a much smaller rise was hurting participation | The readiness profile finds the weak point: delivery depends on paying intermediaries a price that tracks the shock, and nothing published says when the update happens. AUBI §5.1.5's conditions are partly met: the instrument exists and is funded, but there is no pre-published threshold for scaling it and no defined temporary supplement |
| 5–6 | Non-beneficiaries pay triple: households just above the income line, single-person households, families whose CadÚnico record is out of date, and beneficiaries buying refills beyond their four or six a year. Bolsa Família is not linked to the price. Some households turn to firewood. Wider relief would come by MP, probably as a subsidy to producers or importers, as in April 2026 | C&E §6.2 affordability options and the AUBI handoff point to the excluded groups, but no Brazilian instrument covers them without a new measure. Recorded handoff state for non-beneficiaries: `constitutional_gap` |

**First executable protective action.** For voucher holders, a free refill on day 0. Timing by household: earliest legally possible is day 0 for families with a current voucher; for a typical beneficiary it is their next voucher date, between zero and twelve weeks away, since vouchers come every two or three months. For everyone else, an MP, within days once the government decides. Protection actually received is partly observable: about 79% of vouchers were redeemed in the latest completed period under normal prices. Whether that rate would hold in this scenario is unknown.

**Delay.** None in law for about 15 million families' covered refills. For the rest, the delay is political, not legislative.

**Real-resource constraints.** No national physical constraint. The constraints are fiscal (programme cost rises steeply under the stress-test assumption) and commercial (retailer margins under a lagging reference price), which could produce a local delivery-access shortage for voucher holders.

**Authority gaps.**

1. No published trigger for an extraordinary reference-price update.
2. Coverage gaps: one-person households, stale registrations, households above the line, households without a cylinder.
3. Refills beyond the annual allowance are bought at market price.
4. ANP's abusive-pricing power lapsed with the MPs; CADE acts after the fact; the consumer-code route remains.
5. The producer-price buffer is Petrobras's discretion, not an instrument.

**Coordination burden.** MDS, Caixa, MME and the Finance Ministry (the reference price), ANP, CADE, Senacon and Procons, Petrobras, 19 distributors, about 27,000 credentialed retailers, and municipal CadÚnico offices. More actors in the delivery chain than in Germany, but one programme channel to reach the target group.

**Case A result.** The pre-authorization gap that dominated S1 is largely closed for Brazil's target group, and redemption is monitored. The binding risks are the indexation of the delivery chain, which could turn adequate national supply into a delivery-access shortage for voucher holders, and the people outside the programme.

## Case B: LPG supply or distribution disrupted

**Scenario.** On day 0 an import-terminal outage coincides with a trucking disruption. Many retailers run out of cylinders for two to four weeks, and prices spike where stock remains. The question is who gets a cylinder, and what a voucher is worth without one.

| Days and weeks | Baseline (Brazilian institutions) | GGF counterfactual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 0–3 | MME and ANP act under their supply duties and monitoring; the executive can issue an MP, as it did for diesel in 2026. ANP's planned guidelines for supply-restriction situations were suspended in June 2026. S2 identified no operational, pre-authorized LPG allocation rule and no rule giving households priority | C&E §7 classifies a shortage. GMEAIA §3.8 allows an indicator to activate only a pre-authorized, reversible safe state; S2 identified none for LPG household allocation. Recorded handoff state: `constitutional_gap` for the allocation rule; the supply duties themselves are held by MME and ANP |
| Week 1 | Voucher holders queue with everyone else. A retailer with few cylinders earns the reference price from a voucher but the spot price from a cash buyer, so scarcity can pull stock away from voucher holders (a hypothesis, not an observed failure). Refusing a voucher while selling to cash buyers may breach the consumer code's duty to sell within available stock, enforceable by ANP and Procons after the fact. Vouchers stay valid for two to three months, which buffers a short disruption | C&E §6.1 includes cooking in the essential minimum, but §6.3 is written for networked supply: there is nothing to disconnect. The bottled-fuel equivalent is access to a refill, which the framework does not yet state. Gaian Trade's reserve rules (humanitarian release before price stabilization) have no Brazilian LPG reserve to act on |
| Weeks 1–3 | Households fall back on firewood where they can gather it; urban households without that fallback struggle to cook. Distributors' alternative service plans cover places without a participating retailer, but they are not designed as a substitute when cylinders are physically unavailable. The 2018 strike shows shortages can outlast the disruption by two weeks or more | DRR and GMEAIA-59 handoffs to disaster and social authorities for households with no fallback. The readiness profile records no Brazilian host for allocation priority |
| Weeks 3–6 | Supply recovers. Unused vouchers may expire if the disruption is long. No published record of who went without | As specified, GGF adds the record trail, a time limit and an after-action review that counts households left without access |

**First executable protective action.** None specific to allocation. The nearest are an MP, within days once the government decides, and after-the-fact enforcement of the duty to sell within stock.

**Delay.** Political, with no automatic threshold, as in Germany's emergency declaration, but with no operational allocation rule identified to activate.

**Real-resource constraints.** Physical: LPG volume, import terminals, filling plants and trucks. Firewood is the de facto reserve, with health costs.

**Authority gaps.**

1. No operational allocation priority identified for households or voucher holders in an LPG shortage.
2. The reimbursement design may steer scarce stock towards cash buyers (hypothesis).
3. ANP's work on guidelines for supply-restriction situations is suspended; no LPG reserve was found.
4. Alternative service plans address places without retailers, not a national shortage; no organised fallback for households without firewood when cylinders are unavailable.

**Coordination burden.** Case A's list plus Petrobras logistics, terminal operators, the ports ministry and state civil-defence bodies, with no lead named in advance.

**Case B result.** Brazil's shortage response is less absent than the first draft said: supply duties, monitoring, programme service plans and emergency legislation all exist. What S2 did not find is an operational, pre-authorized rule that gives vulnerable households or voucher holders priority. Germany's structure differs: a designated allocation authority with protected-customer priority. In a shortage the voucher protects price, not access.

## Separate pathway: fertilizer imports and food

This pathway is kept apart from the LPG cases. A cylinder shortage does not cause a fertilizer shortage; both can share a common cause, such as the 2026 Gulf disruption that raised energy and fertilizer prices at once.

- **Dependence.** Brazil imports well over 80% of its fertilizer; one industry source puts it at about 85%.
- **2026 response.** In July the agriculture ministry formally asked the ports ministry to prioritize berthing and unloading of fertilizer ships. In August the national ports-authorities commission (Conaportos) issued a recommendation that public ports give priority to fertilizer imports for the 2026/27 crop, without relaxing sanitary or customs controls. The government also secured a supply commitment of 3.8 million tonnes of phosphates from Morocco (about 80% of the national need for that input), enacted an industry programme (Profert, Lei 15.496/2026), and pointed to Petrobras resuming nitrogen production. In September the agriculture minister said the risk of shortage for the 2026/27 crop had passed.
- **Grading.** Port priority is a non-binding recommendation, operational within weeks. Supply diplomacy and Profert are long-run measures, not shock instruments.
- **GGF reading.** This is the closest Brazilian analogue to Gaian Trade's reserve and to the DRR cascade map: a logistical priority rule for an essential input, adopted administratively within about six weeks of the request. Food-price effects fall outside the six-week window and are not traced here.

**What the comparison shows.**

- **Different structures, not opposites.** Germany has a designated shortage-allocation authority and no pre-built price-shock instrument. Brazil has a pre-built price-shock instrument for its target group and a shortage response built from monitoring, regulation, service plans and emergency legislation, without a household-priority rule.
- **Pre-authorization is necessary but not sufficient.** Where it exists (German curtailment, Brazilian vouchers) the first protective action is fast. But Brazil shows further links in the chain that pre-authorization does not secure: coverage, intermediaries' incentives and the cost of keeping the delivery chain going, practical redemption, and physical availability.
- **In-kind protection moves the risk, not removes it.** The voucher shields the household from the price and puts the shock on the federal budget and on retailers' margins. If the reference price lags, the risk returns to the household as missing stock.
- **Fast executive law is a partial substitute for pre-authorization.** Brazil's MP route acted within weeks in 2026; Germany has no equivalent. But MPs lapse by default, which matches GGF's ending rules in form while risking a premature end, as ANP's abusive-pricing power showed.

## Implications

The frameworks stay frozen until S3 and the synthesis. The points below are recorded for that synthesis, not applied now.

**Candidate refinements to the readiness profile**

1. **Coverage and capacity** should test intermediaries' incentives (cost pressure and opportunity cost) and whether payments to them track the shock under a published trigger, plus the fiscal cost of keeping the delivery chain going.
2. **Delivery** should distinguish entitlement, practical redemption through the protected channel, and physical access to the good. A voucher can be valid while the channel fails or the good is absent.
3. **Termination and correction** should separate an orderly sunset from an unplanned legislative lapse, including an extension that rests on provisional law (MP 1.400).

**Interface notes carried forward** (from the external review of S1 v0.2 and from S2)

1. **C&E Appendix E.** The Household Energy Continuity Record should be read as a domain template composed from existing GMEAIA records, not a new record class with its own lifecycle. To be confirmed in the synthesis.
2. **C&E §6.1 essential minimum.** It protects against cut-off for inability to pay. It is a protection objective, not a volume guarantee in a physical shortage, and S1's Case B should be read that way.
3. **C&E §6.3 assumes networked supply.** For bottled fuels the equivalent is access to a refill at a protected price, plus an allocation rule in shortage. Relevant wherever households cook with LPG, kerosene or charcoal.
4. **GMEAIA-08 duplicates host referral duties.** ANP already must report to CADE, and in Germany the market-transparency unit sits inside the Bundeskartellamt. A GGF referral should attach to the existing duty rather than run beside it.

**Discovery before absence.** Both country studies first classified as missing a capability that existed (Germany's value-chain study, Brazil's redemption dashboard and service plans). Before S3 classifies anything as absent, it will search explicitly for monitoring dashboards, operational contingency plans and administrative datasets. If GGF cannot reliably find existing capabilities, it risks duplicating them, as GMEAIA-08 already does in both countries.

**What S2 does not show.** It does not show that GGF improves the outcome; GGF response times remain specifications. Redemption data exists, but S2 has not tested how well it measures household deprivation, nor how redemption, retailer participation or consumption beyond the allowance would behave under stress. Retailer withdrawal, stock diversion and the fiscal figure are hypotheses under stated assumptions. Fiscal capacity is noted, not tested.

**Next.** S3 Sweden, as a transferability check on a different welfare state and energy system (electricity and district heating), using the same profile and rubric.
