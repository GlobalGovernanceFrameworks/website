Adaptive Coordination
Protocol (ACP)

Draft v0.5 Resilience & Accountability Upgrade
Status: Ready for Practitioner Review
Pilot-Ready
When multiple states must coordinate a response to a shared crisis, systems break down — not because of technical failure, but because of funding delays, misaligned incentives, fragmented narratives, and eroded trust. ACP is designed to keep coordination working under exactly these pressures.
The Problem It Solves

In the EU–North Africa pilot simulation, coordination nearly collapsed at three points: Tunisia needed emergency funds and waited too long; Spain hedged its commitments, weakening the whole system; public narratives in each country told incompatible stories. ACP was built to address all three simultaneously.

Core Mechanisms
Emergency Response Fund
Pre-positioned capital with automatic 48-hour disbursement to frontline states in crisis mode. No committee delays.
Tiered Participation
Three tiers (Core / Cooperating / Observing) with escalating benefits tied to compliance, reviewed quarterly.
Contribution Registry
Public, color-coded tracking of every commitment made and fulfilled. Trust made visible in real time.
Narrative Commons
Community-led "Living Narrative Brief" replaces top-down messaging. Diverse voices, not a single official story.
Participation Tiers at a Glance
Tier Compliance Key Benefit
Core ≥80% of cycles Priority ERF access + enhanced voting weight
Cooperating 50–79% of cycles Standard ERF access + technical assistance
Observing <50% of cycles Data access only; no ERF; reduced future priority
On Power — An Honest Note

ACP is voluntary: no state is legally compelled to participate. But it uses transparency, tiered benefits, and reputational mechanisms to make cooperation attractive and defection costly. This is soft power, not coercion — and the protocol names it plainly rather than pretending the system is neutral.

"Coordination fails not because of technical flaws, but because of financial friction, incentive misalignment, narrative incoherence, and external disruption."
What Happens When Resources Run Short

If the Emergency Response Fund falls below its minimum threshold, ACP enters Constrained Coordination Mode — with a pre-defined, publicly announced priority order:

Protected first: Direct life-saving assistance (shelter, food, medical).
Reduced next: Frontline state stabilization (by a defined %).
Suspended: Long-term integration support and non-essential administration.

These decisions are made in advance, not ad hoc during a crisis. The public always knows what is and isn't being funded.

Built-In Accountability

Due process for "Disruptive Actor" labeling: 30-day notice, evidence from two independent sources, peer review panel, time-limited designation, and a two-thirds Assembly override path.

Measurable success: Six operationalized indicators including ERF disbursement time (<72 hrs), community trust surveys (≥60% "fair and understandable"), and compliance registry accuracy (100% updated within 7 days).

Biennial simplification review: A standing commitment to ask — what is the minimum viable version of each mechanism? — and remove what proves unnecessary.

Beyond Migration

ACP was developed through migration simulations. A Domain Adaptation Protocol governs how it transfers to climate finance, pandemic response, or AI disruption — with external domain experts involved before any new Participant Assembly exists. The protocol earns its place in each new domain; it does not assume it.
