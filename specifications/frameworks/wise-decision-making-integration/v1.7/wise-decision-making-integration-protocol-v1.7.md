## 1. Purpose

Some decisions are difficult because facts are incomplete. Others remain difficult after the facts are improved because values conflict, harms and benefits are unevenly distributed, knowledge is plural, power is unequal, consequences cross generations and jurisdictions, and every option closes possibilities.

WDIP helps a competent decision-maker and affected participants:

- clarify the actual question and its causal boundaries;
- examine information conditions and uncertainty;
- identify affected people, nations, non-participants, and upstream controllers;
- protect multiple knowledge systems without collapsing their roles;
- surface assumptions, power, incentives, common dependencies, and omitted perspectives;
- generate genuinely distinct pathways, including non-action and less-authoritative options;
- deliberate without forced consensus or developmental ranking;
- preserve dissent and protected knowledge;
- issue a reasoned advisory recommendation;
- route any decision, implementation, emergency, coercive action, remedy, or closure through valid authority.

> **Information review is not certification. Knowledge inclusion is not proof. Facilitation is not representation. Deliberation is not jurisdiction. Recommendation is not decision. Adoption is not activation. Urgency is not emergency authority. Conformance is not success.**

## 2. Constitutional position and non-authority doctrine

WDIP may be used by communities, Indigenous institutions, public bodies, intergovernmental organizations, courts and tribunals where procedure permits, cooperatives, civil society, workplaces, schools, and temporary deliberative processes. Each institution retains its own law, charter, decision rule, rights, review, accountability, and closure duties.

WDIP cannot:

- declare a process or decision legitimate merely because WDIP was used;
- compel acceptance or rejection of a recommendation;
- invent a response duty, reason-giving duty, constituency, standing, veto, public office, or review body;
- decide who represents an Indigenous nation, youth, future generations, ecosystems, workers, residents, or non-participants;
- pause a lawful process indefinitely or transfer authority after delay, refusal, or timeout;
- allocate benefits, grants, reparations, contracts, operational budgets, or participant support;
- activate a policy, pilot, service, emergency mission, sanction, or enforcement measure;
- determine guilt, liability, bad faith, capture, disinformation intent, scientific truth, spiritual authority, or moral legitimacy by itself;
- expand its own jurisdiction through consensus, popularity, certification, or repeated use.

### 2.1 Canonical doctrine

> **WDIP may improve what a competent authority sees, what affected people can contest, and how reasons are formed. WDIP does not become the competent authority.**

### 2.2 Advisory-output rule

Every consequential WDIP output states or references:

- process mandate and consequence profile;
- competent decision authority, authority formation, and legal or charter source;
- exact authority effect;
- affected parties, outsiders, and affected Indigenous authority;
- causal variables, practical controllers, and responsibility limits;
- evidence, epistemic roles, uncertainty, common-mode dependencies, and missing perspectives;
- alternatives, non-action, rights effects, irreversibility, and distribution;
- dissent and non-consent;
- response-duty source, acknowledgment, decision, and action clocks where applicable;
- data, protected-knowledge, correction, withdrawal, archive, and closure rules;
- whether the output is an assertion, advisory assessment, recommendation, imported decision, implementation proposal, incident, learning artifact, or closure record.

## 3. Core epistemic and constitutional principles

### 3.1 Epistemic pluralism without epistemic collapse

Scientific, technical, legal, lived, Indigenous, traditional, ethical, philosophical, spiritual, artistic, intuitive, and model-based contributions may all matter. They do not answer the same questions and do not acquire equal authority merely by appearing together.

Each contribution states its epistemic role, provenance, uncertainty, permitted use, authority, and prohibited inference. Translation may relate contributions; it may not erase their different standards or convert one into another.

### 3.2 Productive uncertainty

Uncertainty is made visible and linked to reversibility, monitoring, decision clocks, interim protection, and review. It is not automatically resolved by consensus, delay, quantification, or confidence scoring.

### 3.3 Affected standing without responsibility dumping

Affected-party standing determines participation, consent where applicable, challenge, review, remedy, and non-retaliation. Responsibility allocation follows causal control, lawful jurisdiction, practical capacity, and resources. Those bearing harm do not inherit responsibility merely because they are affected.

### 3.4 Non-domination and objective reproduction

The process examines who controls the question, agenda, evidence, funding, facilitation, platform, language, timing, archive, appointments, professional recognition, blame, continuation, and closure. A process may be procedurally inclusive while reproducing the same incentives and dependencies that generated the problem.

### 3.5 Voluntary reflection

Contemplative, spiritual, ceremonial, somatic, or reflective practices remain optional, non-clinical, accessible, culturally appropriate, and free of developmental ranking. Equivalent secular alternatives must exist.

### 3.6 Dissent as information

Unresolved disagreement is preserved. Dissent cannot be translated into low maturity, lack of wisdom, epistemic deficiency, cultural resistance, or bad faith without separately supportable evidence and authority.

### 3.7 Lawful decision and implementation interface

A recommendation changes the world only through the relevant law, charter, compact, contract, consent, emergency interface, adjudicative route, or other valid authority. Consequential implementation is a new proposal, not an automatic next phase of deliberation.

## 4. Consequence profiles and proportional records

WDIP adopts `GMEAIA/0.2.1` consequence profiles without redefining them:

- **`CP0` — exploratory and non-consequential:** education, non-operative inquiry, drafting, scenario exploration, or demonstration.
- **`CP1` — voluntary bounded practice:** consenting participants, meaningful refusal and withdrawal, bounded scope, complaint route, expiry, correction, and closure.
- **`CP2` — operational or consequential action:** authority, causal responsibility, affected parties, authorization, activation, implementation ownership, monitoring, challenge, remedy, and closure.
- **`CP3` — protected-domain or constitutional action:** `CP2` plus the controlling specialized interface and `GMEAIA-56` or `GMEAIA-57`.

The profile describes consequence, not wisdom, quality, legitimacy, maturity, or institutional rank. Documentation follows a core-plus-trigger model. A discussion should not carry a trial’s record burden; a protected-domain proposal cannot omit constitutional safeguards for simplicity.


## 5. Six-phase protocol

### Phase 0 — Information Conditions Review

Review provenance, uncertainty, access, disinformation claims, trust, retaliation, common-mode dependency, decision timescale, and the consequences of missing information. Phase 0 is advisory. It does not certify whether a lawful authority may decide.

### Phase 1 — Mandate, invitation, and framing

Record the request, authority effect, competent decision authority, authority formation, consequence profile, affected parties, Indigenous authority, frame, non-action, response-duty source, clocks, participation support, and the right not to engage.

### Phase 2 — Deep inquiry and differentiated listening

Receive scientific, legal, testimonial, Indigenous, traditional, ethical, philosophical, spiritual, artistic, intuitive, ecological, and model-based contributions under explicit epistemic roles and use restrictions. Difference is preserved; no contribution silently becomes another kind of authority.

### Phase 3 — Integration and option formation

Map systems, causal variables, uncertainty, assumptions, values, irreducible conflicts, distributional effects, long-horizon consequences, and multiple pathways. Integration means relating contributions without flattening them into one score or compulsory consensus.

### Phase 4 — Deliberation and recommendation

Deliberate under a bounded charter, preserve minority reports and non-consent, and issue a recommendation whose reasons, uncertainty, limits, expiry, causal theory, and prohibited inferences are visible. Recommendation remains advisory.

### Phase 5 — Decision interface, implementation handoff, learning, and closure

Import—not manufacture—the competent authority’s decision. Consequential implementation becomes a new GMEAIA/IAF proposal with its own authority, causal responsibility, consent, activation, monitoring, remedy, and closure. Observe response and performance omission, preserve correction and protected memory, and close temporary roles while unresolved duties remain assigned.

## 6. Handoffs, response duties, clocks, and omission

WDIP uses the exact `GMEAIA/0.2.1` handoff states:

`offered, acknowledged, accepted, partially_accepted, capacity_limited, refused, deferred, referred, timed_out, misrouted, constitutional_gap, returned, closed`.

A consequential handoff records the requested function, recipient, response-duty source and effect, acknowledgment clock, decision clock, GMEAIA-07 response-time assessment, provisional safe-state reference, post-acceptance action clock, custody, and unresolved duties.

- `capacity_limited` means an identified authority recognizes jurisdiction or duty but lacks present practical ability.
- `constitutional_gap` means no identified authority possesses sufficient lawful jurisdiction.
- `timed_out` records non-response; it does not create implied consent or transferred power.
- response omission and post-acceptance performance omission are distinct.

WDIP may document refusal, delay, incapacity, obstruction, misrouting, or absence of jurisdiction. Binding findings and remedies remain with competent authority.

## 7. Information, power, independence, and common-mode failure

Multiple reviewers are not independent merely because they sit in different organizations. WDIP reviews shared data, models, vendors, platforms, funders, appointment pathways, professional networks, ontologies, methods, and fallback arrangements.

For long-lived, systemically central, or self-renewing WDIP programs, objective-reproduction review examines whether funding, career incentives, certification, institutional survival, prestige, blame avoidance, or archive control encourage process production rather than better decisions, clearer responsibility, reduced harm, or appropriate closure.

No universal numeric diversity, trust, source-count, or funding threshold is constitutionally sufficient. Local heuristics remain rebuttable and cannot replace contextual judgment, affected authority, or protected knowledge.

---

## 8. Time pressure, protected domains, and coercion

The Time Pressure Protocol is an assessment and referral process—not an override switch. It records harm from delay, harm from premature action, sensing and decision latency, uncertainty, reversible interim options, legal deadlines, protected domains, and emergency-authority requirements.

Emergency declarations, compulsory data access, command, finance, exceptional powers, expiry, and remedy remain governed by ECRC, CDEE, competent law, and the relevant substantive framework. Search, seizure, detention, force, sanctions, asset restraint, border action, adjudication, and enforcement route through SCPA and competent authority.

> **A methodology cannot suspend its safeguards by calling a situation existential.**

---

## 9. AI, digital tools, and the Living Archive

AI may assist translation, accessibility, document comparison, provenance organization, scenario generation, assumption detection, drafting, and missing-field checks. It cannot autonomously determine legitimacy, disinformation guilt, Indigenous authority, consent, credibility, facilitator neutrality, urgency override, decision outcome, funding restriction, remedy, or closure.

Sentiment systems may not classify dissent, escalation, cultural fit, maturity, or legitimacy for consequential use. Blockchain or cryptographic signatures may prove record integrity; they do not prove representation, consensus, authority, truth, or substantive legitimacy.

The Living Archive separates public metadata, protected payloads, sacred and confidential knowledge, correction, withdrawal, deletion, lawful minimum provenance, dark archives, and successor custody. Public memory is not a surveillance system, blacklist, propaganda channel, or compulsory confession repository.

---

## 10. Metrics, success, scaling, and framework falsification

Adoption counts, facilitator numbers, satisfaction, process throughput, response rates, consensus, a Wisdom Integration Index, conflict reduction, funding totals, or record completeness may inform stewardship. None proves wisdom, causation, legitimacy, institutional learning, or permission to scale.

WDIP succeeds only insofar as it helps people and competent institutions:

- see relevant evidence and uncertainty more honestly;
- preserve knowledge differences without flattening them;
- make power, responsibility, omission, and trade-offs more visible;
- form better reasons without fabricating consensus;
- decide through legitimate authority;
- detect and remedy harm earlier;
- reduce unnecessary process burden;
- revise, devolve, simplify, or close the method when it no longer adds value.

Framework-level disconfirmation includes evidence that WDIP systematically professionalizes “wisdom,” suppresses dissent, privileges process insiders, reproduces donor or model dependence, delays decisions beyond tolerable bounds, converts affected parties into unpaid responsibility holders, increases record burden without decision value, launders authority through recommendations, extracts protected knowledge, or resists closure. Such evidence triggers narrowing, suspension, redesign, replacement, or non-scaling through `WDIP-58` and `GMEAIA-53`.

## 15. Constitutional readiness gates

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `G01` | Process request, purpose, and intended use are explicit. |
| `G02` | Authority effect and CP0–CP3 consequence profile are explicit. |
| `G03` | Competent decision authority and authority formation are identified. |
| `G04` | WDIP is not presented as the decision authority. |
| `G05` | Affected parties and outsiders are mapped. |
| `G06` | Affected Indigenous authority and protected knowledge are preserved. |
| `G07` | Affected standing is not converted into causal responsibility. |
| `G08` | Information provenance and epistemic roles are explicit. |
| `G09` | Uncertainty, missing information, and alternative explanations are visible. |
| `G10` | Common-mode dependencies among sources, reviewers, models, vendors, and funders are assessed. |
| `G11` | Disinformation allegations preserve due process and uncertainty. |
| `G12` | Language, accessibility, low-tech access, and independent advice are adequate. |
| `G13` | Trust and relational assessments do not classify legitimacy or maturity. |
| `G14` | The question, causal boundary, assumptions, and non-action are explicit. |
| `G15` | Agenda, funding, facilitation, record, and time power are mapped. |
| `G16` | Long-horizon and irreversible effects are considered without inventing vetoes. |
| `G17` | Participation notice states authority effect, data use, refusal, withdrawal, support, and complaint. |
| `G18` | Knowledge contributions have permitted-use, correction, and withdrawal rules. |
| `G19` | Scientific synthesis distinguishes evidence from law and value judgment. |
| `G20` | Spiritual or contemplative inputs remain voluntary and non-clinical. |
| `G21` | Scenario and AI tools are versioned, bounded, and human-accountable. |
| `G22` | Options are genuinely distinct and include less-authoritative alternatives. |
| `G23` | Dissent and non-consent remain visible. |
| `G24` | Facilitators have bounded mandates, conflicts, complaints, removal, and succession. |
| `G25` | Participation support is not conditional on agreement. |
| `G26` | Recommendation displays evidence, uncertainty, dissent, limits, expiry, and causal theory. |
| `G27` | Any response duty cites its legal, charter, contractual, internal, voluntary, or advisory source. |
| `G28` | Acknowledgment, decision, and action clocks cite GMEAIA-07 or valid law/charter. |
| `G29` | The exact GMEAIA handoff state is used. |
| `G30` | capacity_limited is distinct from constitutional_gap. |
| `G31` | Timeout or refusal does not transfer authority. |
| `G32` | Protected-domain and coercive matters fail closed to specialized interfaces. |
| `G33` | Adoption, authorization, deployment, activation, and operation remain separate. |
| `G34` | Implementation proposals are minimum causally adequate for their claims. |
| `G35` | Monitoring includes burden, distribution, dependency, omission, and objective reproduction. |
| `G36` | Funding and appointment incentives cannot buy consensus or suppress dissent. |
| `G37` | Harm, acknowledgment, liability, remedy, and adjudication remain distinct. |
| `G38` | Archive, AI, publication, correction, withdrawal, and protected-memory rules are defined. |
| `G39` | Framework theory, disconfirming evidence, narrowing, replacement, and non-scaling conditions are reviewable. |
| `G40` | Closure returns temporary authority and access while preserving unresolved duties and custody. |

## 16. Compound implementation tests

| Test | Scenario | Required outcome |
|---|---|---|
| `T01` | An information review finds low trust | The finding remains advisory; it cannot block a lawful decision automatically or classify the community as unready. |
| `T02` | A single Indigenous source is authoritative under the affected nation’s law | No universal two-source rule displaces the nation’s authority or protected-knowledge limits. |
| `T03` | A generic elder offers to speak for an affected nation | The contribution may be advisory but cannot establish representation, law, consent, or knowledge authority. |
| `T04` | A spiritual practice is proposed for all participants | A secular and accessible alternative is required; refusal has no adverse consequence. |
| `T05` | A model assigns a wisdom or maturity score | The score is prohibited for consequential classification or decision-making. |
| `T06` | A recommendation is supported by unanimous participants | Consensus does not create jurisdiction, consent for non-participants, funding, or activation. |
| `T07` | A dissenting participant is described as resistant to wisdom | The characterization is removed or corrected; dissent is preserved as information. |
| `T08` | A funder and three reviewers rely on the same consultancy and model | The common-mode dependency is disclosed and independent review or fallback is sought. |
| `T09` | A public body receives a recommendation with no legal response duty | WDIP may record delivery but cannot invent acknowledgment or reasons obligations. |
| `T10` | A charter requires response within 30 days but GMEAIA-07 shows harm within 7 days | The conflict is recorded and interim protection is referred without creating new jurisdiction. |
| `T11` | A recipient recognizes jurisdiction but lacks translation capacity | The handoff state is capacity_limited, with support needs and continuing duty recorded. |
| `T12` | No identified authority has lawful jurisdiction | The state is constitutional_gap; WDIP records the gap and routes constitution without inheriting authority. |
| `T13` | A referral times out | The timeout is visible and reviewable but does not transfer authority to WDIP, the sender, or Meta-Governance. |
| `T14` | A recommendation affects an essential water service | Implementation routes through GMEAIA and the water framework; WDIP cannot activate or interrupt service. |
| `T15` | A participant withdraws testimony publication consent | The public artifact is revised or removed while only lawful minimum provenance is retained. |
| `T16` | Parliament rejects a WDIP recommendation | The rejection stands; any reasons duty comes from law, charter, contract, or voluntary commitment. |
| `T17` | Several recommendations are rejected over five years | No automatic review body, petition threshold, or shadow government is created. |
| `T18` | A lawful citizen petition is filed | WDIP may support the constituted route without manufacturing standing or review authority. |
| `T19` | A donor privately pressures facilitators | Protection and an integrity review begin; allegation is not yet a finding. |
| `T20` | An integrity review substantiates interference | A competent financial, contractual, employment, or public authority decides remedy and appeal. |
| `T21` | A donor is publicly accused before review | Publication is limited to protect due process, safety, and defamation interests. |
| `T22` | Participant support is funded through AUBI | A separate financial authority determines eligibility and payment; WDIP does not issue benefits. |
| `T23` | A participant withdraws from deliberation | Lawfully committed support and remedy remain available under their terms. |
| `T24` | An existential-risk claim invokes time pressure | WDIP assesses and refers; ECRC/CDEE or competent law governs emergency powers. |
| `T25` | Delay may cause catastrophic harm | Reversible interim options and lawful emergency powers are assessed separately from the WDIP recommendation. |
| `T26` | The decision requires search, seizure, detention, sanctions, or force | The matter routes to the competent authority and SCPA; WDIP cannot authorize or execute it. |
| `T27` | A blockchain records consensus | The ledger proves a record existed; it does not prove authority, representation, truth, or legitimacy. |
| `T28` | An archive contains trauma testimony or sacred knowledge | Access, retention, withdrawal, correction, reuse, AI training, and successor custody remain controlled. |
| `T29` | A harm-acknowledgment ritual occurs | The ritual does not determine causation, liability, compensation, or adjudication. |
| `T30` | The process causes participant harm | Independent complaint, protection, investigation, remedy, facilitator review, and appeal are available. |
| `T31` | A pilot implementing a recommendation is judged successful | Scaling requires a new proposal and cannot exceed the pilot type or causal adequacy. |
| `T32` | A recommendation is implemented before activation | The action is paused or blocked until authority, authorization, and activation records exist. |
| `T33` | A WDIP process succeeds only while an external catalyst team is present | The result is support-dependent; ordinary ownership and support-withdrawal conditions must be tested. |
| `T34` | A process produces extensive records but no clearer decision or responsibility | Conformance and throughput do not prove success; burden and unresolved causality count against the method. |
| `T35` | WDIP v1.8 is proposed by facilitators | It undergoes stewardship, compatibility, rights, security, objective-reproduction, migration, and falsification review. |
| `T36` | A process closes with unpaid stipends, open complaints, or protected records | Temporary roles end, but payment, complaint, remedy, data, custody, and reopening duties remain assigned. |

## 17. Protocol adoption and lifecycle states

WDIP process states are:

`exploratory`, `advisory_process`, `voluntary_internal_method`, `attached_to_lawful_decision`, `recommendation_issued`, `decision_imported`, `implementation_proposed`, `lawfully_activated`, `under_review`, `closing`, `closed_with_unresolved_duties`, and `closed`.

These are WDIP process states, not substitutes for GMEAIA lifecycle or handoff states. Publication of v1.7 activates no process, office, authority, recommendation, pilot, or implementation.

## 19. Closing doctrine

Wise decision-making is not the creation of a class authorized to define wisdom for everyone else.

> **See more without pretending to see everything. Distinguish knowledge without flattening it. Include affected voices without fabricating representation. Preserve uncertainty and dissent. Recommend without governing. Route action through valid authority. Learn without turning memory into surveillance—and close the process without abandoning its duties.**
