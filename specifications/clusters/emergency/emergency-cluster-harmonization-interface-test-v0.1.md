# Emergency and Existential-Risk Cluster Harmonization and Interface Test v0.1

## Executive verdict

The revised emergency and existential-risk cluster is **substantively constitutionally coherent but not yet interoperable enough for binding implementation**. The strongest result is that the major authority defects identified in the earlier cluster audit have not reappeared: observers do not become commanders, the Planetary Immune System does not take over, metrics do not create powers, Indigenous authority is not proxied by a universal council, public communication is not a propaganda function, courts remain separate, and termination, restoration, compensation, and the legitimacy of non-escalation are preserved.

The remaining defects are mostly interface-engineering failures. Five domain successors import the common emergency specification, but they do not yet share a canonical record namespace, complete activation-package inheritance, one cross-domain event envelope, or fully reciprocal handoff protocols. Those gaps are serious because the cluster is explicitly designed for compound events in which health, biosafety, disaster, catastrophic-risk, and converted civilian capabilities operate simultaneously.

> **Release decision:** PASS for continued drafting, simulation, tabletop exercises, and non-coercive pilots. CONDITIONAL FAIL for binding cross-framework emergency implementation until all S2 findings are resolved and regression-tested.

No S0 or S1 defect was found. The test records **six S2 major findings and two S3 moderate findings**.

## 2. Method

The test applies five layers:

1. **Structural conformance:** versions, source integrity, governing interfaces, and declared non-authority.
2. **Constitutional gate regression:** the 24 gates in the Emergency Interface.
3. **Record interoperability:** the 33 canonical records against each domain record list and lifecycle.
4. **Handoff testing:** authority, evidence, assets, data, public communication, work, finance, renewal, termination, restoration, and unresolved dissent across framework boundaries.
5. **Scenario testing:** fifteen compound and failure cases, including false alarms, disputed origin, laboratory incidents, Indigenous non-transfer, converted assets, judicial handoff, long-duration response, and version substitution.

A textual statement that another framework “controls” is treated as necessary but not sufficient. A passing interface must also identify the record, responsible function, acceptance or refusal path, time limit, and return obligation needed for operation.

## 3. Severity taxonomy

- **S0:** Catastrophic/systemic: creates or enables unbounded emergency sovereignty, force, mass rights deprivation, or irreversible cross-system failure.
- **S1:** Critical: a direct authority, command, rights, or restoration defect that could enable grave unlawful action during implementation.
- **S2:** Major: a material interoperability or responsibility gap likely to cause conflicting command, missing review, evidence loss, or wrongful action.
- **S3:** Moderate: ambiguity, drift, or incomplete schema that impairs reliable implementation but is normally containable through manual review.
- **S4:** Minor: editorial, naming, or documentation inconsistency with low immediate operational risk.

### ECRC-HIT-001 — Canonical emergency-record inheritance is not explicit

**Severity:** S2  
**Confidence:** High  
**Status:** Open — release-blocking for interoperable implementation  
**Affected:** PIS v2.2, Global Health v2.2, Aethelred v1.2, Living Shield v4.4, Aegis v1.3

**Evidence pointers**

- `emergency-catastrophic-risk-continuity-interface-specification-v0.1.md:L1846-L1882`
- `planetary-immune-system-v2.2.md:L1190-L1225`
- `global-health-preparedness-outbreak-response-biosecurity-v2.2.md:L1416-L1454`
- `aethelred-accord-v1.2.md:L1176-L1214`
- `living-shield-v4.4.md:L1287-L1327`
- `aegis-protocol-v1.3.md:L1233-L1274`

**Finding.** The Interface defines 33 minimum interoperable records. Each domain framework then publishes its own list with renamed, split, merged, or omitted equivalents, but none supplies a complete normative alias map stating which domain record satisfies which canonical record and which canonical records remain directly imported. Implementers could therefore believe a domain list replaces the interface list.

**Required remediation.** Add a common Record Inheritance Clause and canonical IDs ECRC-01 through ECRC-33. Each domain record must declare `implements`, `extends`, `splits`, or `does_not_apply` against those IDs. Unmapped canonical records remain required whenever their triggering condition exists.

**Regression scenarios:** SCN-02, SCN-03, SCN-07, SCN-12, SCN-14.

### ECRC-HIT-002 — Aethelred and Aegis do not enumerate complete emergency activation packages

**Severity:** S2  
**Confidence:** High  
**Status:** Open — release-blocking for emergency activation  
**Affected:** Aethelred v1.2, Aegis v1.3

**Evidence pointers**

- `aethelred-accord-v1.2.md:L699-L743`
- `aethelred-accord-v1.2.md:L1176-L1214`
- `aegis-protocol-v1.3.md:L975-L1007`
- `aegis-protocol-v1.3.md:L1233-L1274`

**Finding.** Both frameworks correctly import the Emergency Interface in prose, but their operational-record lists omit or ambiguously rename several lifecycle records. Aethelred lacks an explicit Emergency Declaration Record, Standing and Jurisdiction Map, Rights and Non-Derogation Record, Renewal State Estimate, and Emergency Data Disposition Record. Aegis emergency re-tasking lacks explicit canonical declaration, provenance, rights, renewal, cross-border assistance, and data-disposition records. The omission is dangerous because facility shutdown and re-tasking can affect bodies, territory, infrastructure, classified systems, and workers.

**Required remediation.** Define two mandatory imported record bundles: `Biotechnology Emergency Activation Package` and `Emergency Asset Re-tasking Package`. Each package must enumerate the applicable ECRC canonical IDs, including declaration, provenance, power/prohibition schedule, command, host authority, rights, data, work, finance, renewal, termination, restoration, evidence, and compensation.

**Regression scenarios:** SCN-03, SCN-05, SCN-07, SCN-11, SCN-12.

### ECRC-HIT-003 — The Global Health–Aethelred handoff lacks a single incident identity and responsibility-acceptance protocol

**Severity:** S2  
**Confidence:** High  
**Status:** Open — release-blocking for biological incident handoff  
**Affected:** Global Health v2.2, Aethelred v1.2, PIS v2.2

**Evidence pointers**

- `global-health-preparedness-outbreak-response-biosecurity-v2.2.md:L814-L821`
- `global-health-preparedness-outbreak-response-biosecurity-v2.2.md:L1567-L1582`
- `aethelred-accord-v1.2.md:L651-L690`
- `aethelred-accord-v1.2.md:L751-L780`

**Finding.** The substantive division is sound: Aethelred covers facility, construct, release, provenance, and containment responsibilities; Global Health covers population-health observation, clinical response, assistance, allocation, and continuity. Both use a Laboratory or Field-Release Handoff Record. However, neither defines one cross-framework event identifier, the actor responsible for maintaining the master incident envelope, acknowledgement and rejection deadlines, concurrent rather than transferred responsibility, or a rule for resolving conflicting origin classifications.

**Required remediation.** Add a shared Biological Incident Coordination Addendum with one `cluster_event_id`, parent/child event links, origin hypotheses, current responsible authorities by function, handoff offer/accept/reject timestamps, evidence-custody owner, public-information owner, unresolved conflicts, and termination/handback state. Handoff transfers a named responsibility only; it never erases the sender’s retained duties.

### ECRC-HIT-005 — Several domain records recombine evidence handoff with adjudication or enforcement handoff

**Severity:** S2  
**Confidence:** High  
**Status:** Open — release-blocking for justice handoff automation  
**Affected:** Global Health v2.2, Living Shield v4.4, Aegis v1.3

**Evidence pointers**

- `emergency-catastrophic-risk-continuity-interface-specification-v0.1.md:L1873-L1875`
- `global-health-preparedness-outbreak-response-biosecurity-v2.2.md:L1448-L1451`
- `living-shield-v4.4.md:L1319-L1321`
- `aegis-protocol-v1.3.md:L1269-L1271`

**Finding.** The Interface deliberately separates Investigation and Evidence Handoff from Adjudication and Enforcement Handoff. Global Health and Living Shield use `Justice and Evidence Handoff Record`; Aegis uses the same combined pattern. Their prose preserves justice separation, but the combined record name can blur who gathers evidence, who files, who adjudicates, and who executes a remedy—especially in automated workflow design.

**Required remediation.** Split the transport schema into two records or make the combined domain record a container with two independently authorized subrecords. The evidence subrecord must not imply standing, liability, judgment, warrant, sanction, or execution.

**Regression scenarios:** SCN-08, SCN-10, SCN-13.

## 7. Interface tests by domain boundary

### 7.1 PIS to domain frameworks

The central boundary passes in substance. PIS is an observer, readiness network, exercise system, and coordination interface. It cannot declare, command, allocate, sanction, shut facilities, or absorb domain mandates. Domain frameworks likewise state that catastrophic scale does not transfer jurisdiction. The remaining defect is version pinning and transport-schema inheritance, not authority doctrine.

### 7.2 Global Health to Aethelred

The substantive factorization is strong and mutually stated. Aethelred retains facility, construct, release, provenance, containment, dual-use, and decommissioning responsibilities. Global Health retains case detection, clinical response, population-health estimates, countermeasure allocation, health assistance, and essential-care continuity. The interface fails only at the operational seam: no single event identity, no master responsibility table, no handoff acknowledgment state, and no explicit rule for concurrent responsibilities.

### 7.3 Living Shield to PIS

This boundary passes. Living Shield may submit a hazard alert or request catastrophic-risk review; PIS may compare models and dependencies. Neither a numerical threshold nor disaster scale transfers disaster authority. The legal result may remain local, voluntary, unresolved, or non-escalated.

### 7.4 Living Shield to Aegis

This boundary is incomplete. Aegis contains the emergency re-tasking lifecycle for converted assets and names Living Shield. Living Shield contains mutual aid and operational command but does not identify Aegis or the additional separation, intelligence, custody, handback, and re-militarization controls required for converted security assets.

### 7.5 All domains to Justice Systems

The substantive boundary passes: domain bodies do not adjudicate or enforce their own findings. The record layer is weaker. A combined “Justice and Evidence Handoff” can cause workflow designers to treat evidentiary transfer as a judicial referral, finding of liability, or enforcement instruction. The interface must preserve two independently authorized handoffs.

### 7.6 All domains to Aurora, Technology Governance, and Synoptic

The cluster consistently defers data authority, AI and technical systems, public communication, correction, and protected information to the revised external frameworks. No universal public ledger, mandatory blockchain, automatic model authority, or official narrative function was found. The remaining need is canonical data-disposition inheritance after emergency termination.

### 7.7 Indigenous and community interfaces

The cluster passes the substantive test. Affected Indigenous nations determine representation, consent, territorial access, knowledge conditions, non-transfer, and non-engagement. Traditional or local knowledge may be offered through bounded attestation without raw disclosure. Immediate rescue is narrow and does not create a general override or permanent authority. Community liaison is less consistently represented as a canonical record and should be covered by the alias schema in ECRC-HIT-001.

### 7.8 Work, continuity, finance, and restoration

All successors reject unpaid moral conscription and protect wages, safety, refusal, insurance, demobilisation, essential services, and AUBI Layer 1. They also reject self-authorized taxes and debt punishment. Termination and wrongful-action doctrine is strong. The operational weakness is that emergency finance, work, continuity, renewal, data disposition, and authority restoration are not transported through one uniform lifecycle package in every domain.

### Scenario interpretation

The seven unconditional passes demonstrate that the cluster can now refuse unlawful escalation, respect Indigenous non-transfer, preserve non-coercive observation, distinguish communication from command, protect good-faith forecast errors, and route wrongful action to remedy. The conditional results are not calls for a new central institution. They are calls for better interface contracts among already separated functions.

The single failure, SCN-15, is deliberate: a versioned constitutional stack must fail closed when a later document is substituted without compatibility review. Floating references such as “the successor” do not meet that standard.

## 9. Required harmonization package

The cluster does not need another conceptual rewrite. It needs a small, versioned interoperability package.

### 9.1 Cluster Record Profile v0.1

Publish canonical IDs **ECRC-01 through ECRC-33**, JSON Schemas, cardinality, required fields, access classes, and lifecycle states. Domain labels remain readable, but each record carries its canonical ID and schema version.

### 9.2 Record Inheritance Clause

Insert the same clause into PIS, Global Health, Aethelred, Living Shield, and Aegis:

> A domain record list specializes but does not replace the Emergency Interface record set. Every domain record shall identify the canonical ECRC record or records it implements, extends, splits, or contains. Any canonical record not mapped remains directly required whenever its triggering condition occurs. A record name, model output, dashboard, or framework reference does not create authority.

### 9.3 Cross-Domain Event Envelope

Create a transport wrapper—not a new source of authority—with:

- immutable cluster event identifier;
- parent, child, and merged-event identifiers;
- observed time and affected territory;
- hazard and origin hypotheses;
- current state estimates and dissent;
- domain functions engaged;
- responsible authority for each function;
- handoff offered, accepted, rejected, or concurrent;
- evidence and data custodians;
- public-information owner;
- review, expiry, termination, and unresolved status.

The envelope may point to protected records but must not become a universal public risk ledger.

### 9.4 Biological Incident Coordination Addendum v0.1

Bind Global Health v2.2 and Aethelred v1.2 to the same event envelope, origin taxonomy mapping, responsibility table, handoff acknowledgment, evidence-custody state, and termination protocol. Add PIS only as observer or separately authorized coordinator.

### 9.5 Civilian Converted-Capability Assistance Addendum v0.1

Bind Living Shield v4.4 and Aegis v1.3 for disaster and humanitarian use of converted assets. Require host authorization, mission separation, intelligence prohibition, command and professional autonomy, labour protection, finance, claims, data, withdrawal, handback, and re-militarization controls.

### 9.6 Justice Handoff Split

Standardize two records:
1. Investigation and Evidence Handoff; and
2. Adjudication and Enforcement Handoff.
A domain may display them within one package, but their authorities, actors, legal effects, and timestamps remain separate.

### 9.7 Lifecycle completion

Import Renewal State Estimate and Emergency Data Disposition Record into every action lasting beyond the provisional layer or processing emergency data. Make authority restoration dependent on verified completion, not merely publication of a termination statement.

### 9.8 PIS version-pinning erratum

Replace prospective references with exact versions and add a compatibility rule: later versions do not enter the stack until the cluster manifest records a successful interface test.

## 10. Recommended patch order

1. **P0 — schema foundation:** Cluster Record Profile, canonical IDs, and Record Inheritance Clause.
2. **P0 — activation safety:** complete Aethelred and Aegis emergency activation packages.
3. **P0 — biological handoff:** shared event identity and responsibility acceptance.
4. **P0 — converted-asset handoff:** reciprocal Living Shield–Aegis interface.
5. **P1 — justice transport:** split evidence from adjudication/enforcement.
6. **P1 — lifecycle:** renewal and data disposition across every domain.
7. **P1 — version control:** PIS exact-version erratum and compatibility manifest.
8. **P2 — controlled vocabulary:** user-facing aliases plus canonical machine IDs.

This order avoids five full framework rewrites. Most changes can be implemented as short interface clauses, record crosswalk appendices, and two shared addenda.

## 11. Release and regression gates

The cluster may advance to binding cross-framework implementation only when:

1. all S2 findings are marked resolved with deterministic patches;
2. each domain publishes a complete canonical-record crosswalk;
3. the fifteen scenarios are re-run with zero FAIL and no unresolved CONDITIONAL result involving declaration, command, rights, renewal, evidence, or restoration;
4. all source hashes and the cluster compatibility manifest match;
5. no patch silently changes the substantive authority boundaries;
6. later framework versions fail closed until explicitly tested;
7. Living Shield legacy annexes, if later incorporated, undergo their own constitutional and schema audit; and
8. armed, intelligence, sanctions, and collective-defence functions remain outside Aegis until the security-and-coercion cluster audit.

## 12. Final assessment

The cluster has successfully moved from an imagined planetary emergency hierarchy to a distributed constitutional system. Its frameworks now know what they are **not**: PIS is not a world executive; Global Health is not a health sovereign; Aethelred is not a biosafety sovereign; Living Shield is not a disaster sovereign; Aegis is not a disguised planetary force.

The next failure mode is more mundane but equally important: separated institutions can still fail if their records do not match, their handoffs are not acknowledged, their event identities fork, or their temporary powers cannot be mechanically closed. Harmonization should therefore proceed through schemas and interface contracts—not renewed centralization.

> **The cluster is constitutionally aligned. It becomes operationally trustworthy only when responsibility can cross a boundary without authority, evidence, rights, data, work, or restoration disappearing at that boundary.**
