# Executive summary

Urgent threats create real pressure to centralize observation, declaration, command, finance, communication, and enforcement. That pressure is often strongest precisely when evidence is incomplete, affected people cannot participate normally, and mistakes are difficult to reverse.

The GGF emergency and existential-risk frameworks contain valuable capacities: community health networks, low-tech detection, forecast-based finance, disaster preparedness, biosafety, adversarial foresight, civilian capability transition, resilient communications, and cross-border mutual aid. Their shared constitutional weakness is that one framework frequently treats another as the source of emergency powers that neither one independently possesses.

This Specification closes that gap by separating ten acts:

1. alert;
2. plural state estimation;
3. standing and jurisdiction review;
4. emergency declaration;
5. power-specific activation;
6. operational coordination;
7. protective and judicial review;
8. renewal or termination;
9. restoration and compensation;
10. after-action learning and transition to ordinary law.

Its governing principle is:

> **Severity determines the urgency and scale of the problem. It does not determine who has authority.**

Its constitutional rule is:

> **No valid authority record, no consequential emergency action.**

Its anti-sovereignty rule is:

> **No institution receives residual emergency command. If a power has not been validly assigned, it remains unauthorized.**

The Specification defines five operating layers:

- **Layer A — Ordinary prevention and preparedness**;
- **Layer B — Alert and state estimation**;
- **Layer C — Requested or pre-authorized assistance**;
- **Layer D — Narrow provisional action for no more than seventy-two hours**;
- **Layer E — Continued or extraordinary response under a ratified or otherwise valid procedure, with thirty-day renewal limits and extraordinary review beyond ninety cumulative days.**

An emergency declaration does not activate every available institution. Each activated power must be stated separately, with its legal source, geographic and subject scope, operational commander, affected rights, protected minima, data authority, work rules, financing, review, expiration, and restoration duties.

Meta-Governance may convene interfaces, maintain common records, identify conflicts, and support plural modelling. It may not declare an emergency, command personnel, allocate coercive powers, order deployment, renew extraordinary authority, or convert its state estimate into jurisdiction.

Observers, scientific panels, dashboards, risk models, community monitors, and the proposed catastrophic-risk observatory may issue alerts and estimates. They may not declare emergencies or activate coercive powers merely because their evidence is persuasive.

Immediate rescue of life may sometimes proceed without prior consent under valid rescue authority. Such action must be narrow, temporary, non-extractive, documented, immediately reviewable, and incapable of establishing a precedent for permanent access, jurisdiction, surveillance, resource use, or institutional control.

Emergency governance is not complete when the hazard subsides. Every activation must include demobilisation, return of authority and assets, deletion or lawful retention of data, restoration of services, settlement of temporary contracts, compensation for wrongful action, and an after-action review capable of concluding that the declaration or response was mistaken.

# 1. Purpose, scope, and legal character

## 1.1 Purpose

This Specification defines a common constitutional interface through which hazard-specific institutions may:

- issue and receive alerts;
- compare independent state estimates;
- identify competent authorities and affected rights holders;
- request or offer assistance;
- record lawful emergency declarations;
- activate only specified powers;
- coordinate bounded operations;
- preserve rights and essential continuity;
- obtain review and adjudication;
- terminate temporary authority;
- restore ordinary jurisdiction, services, assets, and data;
- compensate wrongful or excessive action;
- learn without converting emergency performance into permanent authority.

## 1.2 Scope

The Specification applies when GGF-aligned institutions, Treaty Parties, BAZs, municipalities, Indigenous authorities, public agencies, civil-society bodies, operators, or mutual-aid networks coordinate around:

- infectious disease and public-health emergencies;
- antimicrobial resistance incidents;
- disasters and cascading infrastructure failures;
- hazardous-material, laboratory, radiological, or biological incidents;
- severe cyber incidents affecting essential systems;
- ecosystem collapse or irreversible ecological harm;
- catastrophic technological failure;
- nuclear, space, or other large-scale physical hazards;
- cross-border humanitarian emergencies;
- credible catastrophic or existential risks;
- continuity of essential institutions and services during any such condition.

## 1.3 Interface, not grant of power

This Specification is neither a constitution nor an emergency statute. It becomes operative only through valid adoption, incorporation, compact, contract, Indigenous law, domestic law, Treaty protocol, or host authorization.

A record created under this Specification documents claimed authority. It does not cure the absence of authority.

## 1.4 Non-authority clause

> **This Specification does not itself declare emergencies, create offences, command personnel, authorize deployment, tax, sanction, quarantine, isolate, evacuate, detain, search, seize, censor, suspend services, compel disclosure, override Indigenous authority, force labour, impose medical treatment, establish military jurisdiction, or authorize the use of force. It defines the records and interfaces through which already valid authorities may coordinate.**

## 1.5 No framework-to-power conversion

A hazard framework, dashboard, index, model, protocol, council, observatory, audit, technical standard, or implementation milestone does not become legally binding merely because it is included in the GGF corpus.

Binding action requires an independently valid source of authority.

# 2. Definitions

For this Specification:

- **Affected authority** means a public, Indigenous, territorial, professional, infrastructural, or other authority whose lawful competence is materially affected by a proposed emergency action.
- **Affected community** means a population exposed to the hazard, intervention, displacement, service interruption, data use, work burden, ecological effect, or other material consequence.
- **Alert** means a documented notice that a condition may require attention. An alert has no coercive effect.
- **Activation order** means the instrument that lists the particular powers, institutions, resources, and constraints activated following a valid declaration or under standing law.
- **Catastrophic risk** means a credible condition capable of causing severe and potentially irreversible harm across multiple systems, jurisdictions, generations, or ecological regions. The label does not create jurisdiction.
- **Competent authority** means a body that already possesses the relevant legal or inherent authority over the action, territory, persons, service, asset, or subject matter.
- **Consequential emergency action** means action capable of materially affecting rights, movement, bodily integrity, property, livelihood, essential services, data, territory, institutional control, access to justice, or protected knowledge.
- **Declaration** means a formal finding by a competent authority that a legally defined emergency threshold has been met within a bounded jurisdiction and duration.
- **Emergency** means the time-bounded condition defined by Treaty v1.3: a credible and serious threat to life, fundamental rights, essential systems, international peace, or irreversible ecological integrity where ordinary procedure cannot respond within the necessary time.
- **Emergency authority provenance** means the traceable legal and institutional source of a proposed emergency act.
- **Emergency overlay** means a temporary procedural layer that may shorten or sequence authorized action without changing the underlying decision class or creating permanent jurisdiction.
- **Host authority** means the competent authority authorizing assistance, entry, operation, or deployment within its jurisdiction.
- **Immediate rescue action** means narrow action necessary to save life or prevent imminent serious injury where delay to obtain ordinary consent would itself cause the harm.
- **Operational command** means direction of an activated function, personnel, or asset within a stated mission. It is not residual sovereignty over other institutions.
- **Persistent condition** means a continuing risk that can no longer be constitutionally governed through serial emergency renewals and must be addressed through ordinary law, durable institutions, appropriations, and rights protections.
- **Protected continuity** means preservation of fundamental rights, AUBI Layer 1 or equivalent material minima, essential services, lawful wages and compensation, personal balances, access to remedy, protected knowledge, and other defined interests during emergency action and institutional transition.
- **Provisional action** means a narrow act taken under prior law for no more than seventy-two hours where delay would cause serious harm.
- **Requested assistance** means support invited by a competent host authority or rights holder under defined terms.
- **Restoration** means return of authority, assets, services, rights, records, data, facilities, personnel, and institutional relationships to lawful post-emergency arrangements.
- **State estimate** means a reasoned, contestable assessment of the condition, mechanism, scale, urgency, uncertainty, affected groups, options, and harms of action and inaction.
- **Wrongful emergency action** means an act later found unlawful, unsupported, disproportionate, negligently designed or executed, improperly prolonged, or materially excessive.

# 3. Governing principles

## 3.1 Urgency without residual sovereignty

Emergency may justify faster procedure. It does not create a new sovereign or transfer all unresolved authority to the most global institution.

## 3.2 Action-specific authority

Authority attaches to the act, not to the seriousness of the event.

The authority to issue an alert is not the authority to declare an emergency. The authority to declare is not the authority to deploy. The authority to deploy is not the authority to detain, isolate, seize, censor, compel work, or use force.

## 3.3 No circular delegation

No institution may claim power solely because another GGF framework refers to it as the emergency, enforcement, oversight, justice, or command body.

A valid chain must terminate in law, treaty, compact, Indigenous authority, contract, host request, or another independently constituted source.

## 3.4 Functional separation

Observation, declaration, activation, command, adjudication, enforcement, finance, evaluation, and public communication should be institutionally separated to the degree feasible.

Where one body necessarily performs more than one function, the overlap, conflict risk, challenge route, and temporary safeguards must be recorded.

## 3.5 Least coercive effective measure

Decision-makers shall consider voluntary assistance, operator action, local public-health measures, technical mitigation, targeted interim protection, and other narrower options before broader coercion.

## 3.6 Reversibility and option preservation

Low-confidence conditions justify only targeted, reviewable, and reversible measures unless the governing law expressly authorizes a stronger response to prevent irreversible harm.

## 3.7 Protected continuity

Emergency action shall not use material insecurity, service withdrawal, data dependency, legal-access denial, or institutional collapse as leverage for compliance.

## 3.8 Local and affected-area intelligence

Global and technical estimates shall be interpreted through affected-area evidence, operational knowledge, lived experience, and locally identified authorities.

## 3.9 Indigenous authority remains inherent

Emergency status does not create a global Indigenous proxy, erase affected-nation standing, or convert Indigenous knowledge into a compulsory sensor feed.

## 3.10 Observation is non-self-executing

Indices, algorithms, models, satellite alerts, clinical signals, community reports, and expert assessments may trigger inquiry, convening, evidence preservation, or a request for action. They may not themselves activate coercive power.

## 3.11 Reviewable secrecy

Operational secrecy may be necessary, but it must have a purpose, classification authority, access rules, duration, review, and eventual disclosure or lawful retention decision.

## 3.12 Error admission and remedy

Emergency institutions must be capable of determining that an alert was mistaken, a declaration unnecessary, a measure excessive, or a response harmful—and of repairing the consequences.

# 4. Decision classes, hazard profiles, and jurisdiction

## 4.1 Underlying decision class remains

Emergency status may compress procedure but does not change the underlying Treaty decision class.

Every consequential act shall identify whether it concerns:

- local authority;
- voluntary cross-boundary coordination;
- material cross-boundary externality;
- shared planetary commons;
- fundamental rights or international crimes;
- Indigenous territory, knowledge, culture, data, genetic material, or sacred relations.

A single response may contain several acts with different classes and authorities.

## 4.2 Hazard profile is not legal jurisdiction

Risk assessment should describe separately:

- mechanism;
- location and transmission pathway;
- temporal urgency;
- scale;
- reversibility;
- uncertainty;
- affected populations and ecosystems;
- essential-service dependence;
- cross-border propagation;
- malicious or accidental origin;
- feasibility and side effects of intervention;
- distribution of burdens;
- recovery requirements.

No composite risk score confers legal power.

## 4.3 Fixed thresholds are supporting evidence

Population percentages, model probabilities, case counts, biodiversity loss, financial exposure, confidence bands, or geographic spread may support a declaration when incorporated into valid law. They do not independently establish emergency authority.

## 4.4 Gaps in authority

Where no competent authority exists, the Standing and Jurisdiction Map shall record the gap.

The response may then be limited to:

- voluntary measures;
- host-authorized assistance;
- ordinary rescue powers;
- evidence preservation;
- public warnings;
- lawful interim protection sought from a competent forum;
- negotiation of a new instrument through ordinary constitutional procedure.

A gap is not filled by Meta-Governance, a risk observatory, the Planetary Immune System, a technical council, or a claim of necessity alone.

# 5. Five operating layers

## 5.1 Layer A — Ordinary prevention and preparedness

Layer A includes:

- risk reduction;
- public health and primary care;
- infrastructure maintenance;
- biosafety and occupational safety;
- land-use planning;
- stockpiles;
- exercises;
- mutual-aid agreements;
- standards and accreditation;
- continuity planning;
- public education;
- research;
- community capacity;
- voluntary capability transition.

Ordinary law, ordinary budgeting, ordinary labour protections, ordinary procurement, and ordinary rights apply.

Preparedness bodies do not gain emergency powers merely because their work is important.

## 5.2 Layer B — Alert and state estimation

Layer B begins when an observer identifies a credible concern.

Authorized observers and affected parties may:

- issue alerts;
- preserve perishable evidence where law permits;
- request independent estimates;
- convene affected authorities;
- communicate uncertainty;
- recommend voluntary precaution;
- prepare assistance options.

No coercive power activates at Layer B.

## 5.3 Layer C — Requested or pre-authorized assistance

Layer C includes actions already authorized by standing arrangements or invited by a competent host, such as:

- technical advice;
- rescue support;
- laboratory assistance;
- grants and forecast-based financing;
- medical supplies;
- humanitarian logistics;
- mutual aid;
- unarmed specialist deployment;
- communications support;
- infrastructure repair;
- voluntary evacuation assistance;
- cyber incident response requested by the operator or competent authority.

The request shall identify scope, duration, command, data, liability, withdrawal, and handoff.

## 5.4 Layer D — Provisional emergency action

A body expressly authorized by prior law or ratified protocol may take narrow provisional action for no more than seventy-two hours where delay would cause serious harm.

The provisional action must state:

- the evidence and uncertainty;
- the legal authority;
- the underlying decision class;
- geographic and subject scope;
- the precise act;
- rights affected;
- protected minima;
- why ordinary procedure was insufficient;
- the review time;
- termination and restoration requirements.

Provisional action cannot be used to establish a permanent institution, enact a durable policy, transfer territory, create a tax, impose an indefinite restriction, or normalize repeated emergency renewal.

## 5.5 Layer E — Continued or extraordinary response

Continuation beyond seventy-two hours requires:

- dual authorization or the procedure specified by a ratified protocol or valid domestic law;
- a Power Activation and Prohibition Schedule;
- oversight and review;
- affected local and Indigenous interfaces;
- work-mobilisation rules;
- financial authority;
- data and secrecy rules;
- duration, termination, and restoration.

No authorization may exceed thirty days without renewal.

Cumulative renewal beyond ninety days requires extraordinary independent review and a plan to transition any persistent condition into ordinary law.

# 6. Functional architecture and anti-laundering rules

## 6.1 Required functional distinctions

The interface distinguishes:

1. **observer** — detects or reports;
2. **estimator** — evaluates evidence and uncertainty;
3. **declaring authority** — determines whether a legal emergency threshold is met;
4. **activating authority** — activates specified powers or resources;
5. **operational coordinator or commander** — directs the activated function;
6. **rights and oversight body** — monitors legality, rights, continuity, and conflicts;
7. **court or tribunal** — adjudicates jurisdiction, legality, interim protection, liability, and remedy;
8. **investigator** — gathers evidence under lawful authority;
9. **enforcement body** — executes lawful orders;
10. **finance authority** — authorizes and accounts for spending, relief, procurement, and compensation;
11. **evaluator** — conducts after-action review without acquiring command.

## 6.2 No observer-to-command conversion

An observatory, scientific panel, community monitor, dashboard operator, auditor, public-health surveillance unit, or model cannot both establish the condition and activate coercive action without a separately valid legal assignment and conflict safeguards.

## 6.3 No Meta-Governance command

Meta-Governance may:

- convene competent authorities;
- maintain interface records;
- identify jurisdictional conflicts;
- support translation and plural state estimation;
- coordinate voluntary services;
- record dissent and unresolved status;
- support bounded pilots and exercises.

Meta-Governance may not:

- declare an emergency;
- command field personnel;
- allocate coercive powers;
- order deployment;
- determine liability;
- impose sanctions;
- renew emergency authority;
- become an emergency executive by reconfiguration.

## 6.4 No Planetary Immune System takeover

A catastrophic-risk protocol may coordinate foresight, readiness, exercises, option preservation, and cross-domain state estimation.

It may not override domain institutions, assume all unassigned functions, convert itself into a world executive, or activate military, health, cyber, financial, or biotechnology powers by classification alone.

## 6.5 Existing-body nesting does not create jurisdiction

Placing a new function inside a Planetary Health Council, Treaty institution, Meta-Governance body, BAZ council, or tribunal does not avoid constitutional creation.

The function must still identify its authority, competence, appointment rules, review, funding, data, and termination.

## 6.6 No self-reference as authority

A framework may not cite another framework's reference back to it as proof of authority.

The Emergency Authority Provenance Record must identify the external legal endpoint of every power chain.

# 7. Emergency Authority Provenance Record

## 7.1 Requirement

Every consequential emergency action shall have an Emergency Authority Provenance Record before execution, except immediate rescue action where completing the record beforehand is impossible. In that case, the record must be completed as soon as practicable and no later than the first review point.

> **No provenance record, no consequential emergency action.**

## 7.2 Required fields

The record shall state:

1. proposed action;
2. hazard and underlying decision class;
3. alert source;
4. state estimate relied upon;
5. declaring authority;
6. activating authority;
7. constituting law, treaty, compact, Indigenous law, contract, professional authority, operator authority, or host request;
8. geographic jurisdiction;
9. subject-matter jurisdiction;
10. persons, systems, assets, or territories affected;
11. powers activated;
12. powers expressly prohibited;
13. operational commander or responsible official;
14. affected local authorities;
15. affected Indigenous authorities;
16. affected communities and rights holders;
17. rights affected;
18. non-derogable rights and protected minima;
19. evidence, confidence, uncertainty, and dissent;
20. alternatives considered;
21. expected harms of action and inaction;
22. data, privacy, cybersecurity, secrecy, and retention authority;
23. work, personnel, safety, compensation, and withdrawal rules;
24. finance, procurement, insurance, and liability;
25. review and appeal authority;
26. start time and duration;
27. renewal conditions;
28. termination triggers;
29. restoration and asset-return duties;
30. wrongful-action and compensation route.

## 7.3 Public and protected versions

A public summary shall disclose enough information to explain the authority, purpose, scope, rights effects, duration, and review.

Sensitive operational details may be placed in a protected annex with stated classification authority, access controls, review date, and eventual disclosure or retention decision.

## 7.4 Invalid or incomplete provenance

Where authority is disputed or incomplete:

- the dispute shall be stated;
- the action shall be narrowed to the clearly authorized portion;
- urgent judicial or constitutional review shall be sought where available;
- voluntary or host-authorized alternatives shall be preferred;
- unresolved powers shall remain inactive.

# 8. Alerts, observation contracts, and plural state estimation

## 8.1 Alert standing

Any person or body may submit information. The receiving system shall distinguish:

- public report;
- professional alert;
- automated alert;
- affected-community report;
- Indigenous authority notice;
- operator disclosure;
- intelligence or security report;
- scientific assessment;
- anonymous or protected-source report.

Legal weight depends on the governing law and evidence, not on the prestige of the source.

## 8.2 Hazard Alert Record

An alert shall identify:

- observed condition;
- source and method;
- time and location;
- affected systems or populations;
- immediate uncertainty;
- possible need for evidence preservation;
- conflicts or incentives;
- requested next step.

It shall state prominently:

> **This alert does not declare an emergency or authorize coercive action.**

## 8.3 Observation Contract

Consequential observation systems shall document:

- purpose;
- authority;
- variables and populations observed;
- sampling and missingness;
- data sources;
- source dependence;
- collection limits;
- privacy and protected-knowledge controls;
- false-positive and false-negative risks;
- correction and challenge;
- retention and deletion;
- conditions for suspension or retirement.

## 8.4 Catastrophic-Risk State Estimate

The estimate shall report separately:

- competing hypotheses;
- mechanism and transmission pathway;
- supporting and conflicting evidence;
- confidence and calibration;
- model disagreement;
- affected groups and distributional effects;
- local and Indigenous interpretations;
- options and alternatives;
- potential harms of action and inaction;
- evidence that would change the estimate;
- unresolved questions;
- dissenting estimates.

## 8.5 Observation Independence Map

The map shall disclose shared:

- sensors;
- data providers;
- platforms;
- funders;
- models;
- algorithms;
- laboratories;
- institutions;
- personnel;
- supply chains;
- political or commercial incentives.

Multiple outputs sharing the same dependency shall not be counted as independent merely because they carry different institutional names.

## 8.6 Minimum plurality

For full emergency, catastrophic-risk, major cross-border, or irreversible-harm decisions, the authorizing body should seek at least three materially independent modelling or interpretive systems where feasible.

Where urgency or scarcity makes this impossible, the record shall state the limitation and the compensating review.

## 8.7 No metric-to-power automation

Metrics may automatically:

- issue an alert;
- convene a session;
- preserve system logs or evidence where lawful;
- release pre-authorized voluntary funds;
- initiate an operator-controlled safety procedure.

Metrics may not automatically:

- declare an emergency;
- impose quarantine or evacuation;
- pause an entire sector;
- deploy personnel;
- detain or blacklist;
- seize assets;
- override Indigenous authority;
- authorize force;
- renew extraordinary powers.

# 9. Standing and Jurisdiction Map

Before declaration or activation, the responsible body shall identify:

- the territory and persons affected;
- host-state or local authority;
- Indigenous peoples and authorities affected;
- cross-border externalities;
- essential-service operators;
- professional and sector regulators;
- owners or custodians of relevant assets and data;
- rights holders and representatives;
- courts or review bodies;
- existing mutual-aid or defence agreements;
- gaps, overlaps, and contested claims.

## 9.1 Priority of affected-nation standing

Where the action affects Indigenous territory, knowledge, genetic resources, community data, cultural continuity, or sacred relations, standing belongs first to the affected people or peoples through authorities they identify.

Representation quotas or a global Indigenous council do not substitute for affected-nation authority.

## 9.2 Cross-border effects

A cross-border hazard creates duties to notify, cooperate, prevent transboundary harm, and seek lawful assistance. It does not automatically authorize direct action inside another jurisdiction.

## 9.3 Essential operators

System operators may take safety actions within their lawful and contractual competence. Operator control does not authorize public coercion, criminal investigation, or restrictions on unrelated systems.

## 9.4 Contested jurisdiction

Where jurisdiction is contested, the interface shall preserve:

- immediate life-saving options;
- evidence;
- essential continuity;
- affected-party participation;
- rapid review;
- non-precedential temporary arrangements where lawful.

# 10. Emergency declaration

## 10.1 Declaration threshold

A competent authority may declare an emergency only where the governing law's threshold is met and ordinary procedure cannot respond within the necessary time.

Political inconvenience, institutional resistance, low trust, slow reform, missed targets, public disagreement, non-participation, or an institution's loss of influence is not an emergency.

## 10.2 Declaration Record

The declaration shall state:

- legal authority;
- declaring official or body;
- underlying decision classes;
- hazard and mechanism;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- geographic and subject scope;
- affected populations;
- why ordinary procedure is insufficient;
- start time;
- maximum duration;
- first review time;
- rights and continuity implications;
- institutions that may request activation;
- institutions expressly not activated;
- public communication plan.

## 10.3 Declaration does not equal activation

A declaration changes no power unless the governing law expressly says otherwise.

Each institution, resource, restriction, expenditure, deployment, or investigative act must be activated through its own authority.

## 10.4 Multiple declarations

Different jurisdictions may issue different declarations for the same condition. The interface shall preserve those differences and coordinate them rather than inventing one global declaration by default.

## 10.5 Refusal to declare

A refusal to declare shall be reasoned where law requires. It may be challenged through the available constitutional, judicial, legislative, or administrative route.

An observer may continue to publish an independent risk estimate without acquiring command.

# 11. Power activation and prohibition schedule

## 11.1 Power-by-power activation

The activation order shall list each power separately, including:

- information request;
- evidence preservation;
- public warning;
- spending or release of pre-authorized funds;
- mutual aid;
- technical deployment;
- rescue;
- evacuation support;
- compulsory evacuation where validly authorized;
- isolation or quarantine;
- medical countermeasure distribution;
- infrastructure control;
- service prioritization;
- temporary operator restriction;
- search, seizure, detention, or asset restraint;
- cross-border investigation;
- unarmed international deployment;
- armed defensive deployment;
- emergency procurement;
- emergency work mobilisation.

No general phrase such as “all necessary powers,” “PIS activation,” “crisis command,” or “full emergency mode” is sufficient.

## 11.2 Prohibited powers

Every schedule shall identify powers not activated, including as relevant:

- forced treatment;
- indefinite detention;
- censorship or viewpoint suppression;
- permanent data retention;
- military action;
- asset seizure;
- compulsory labour;
- suspension of AUBI Layer 1;
- territorial transfer;
- permanent institutional dissolution;
- compulsory disclosure of protected knowledge;
- restrictions outside the affected system or area.

## 11.3 Conditions and dependencies

An activated power may be conditional on:

- a host request;
- a warrant or court order;
- professional supervision;
- specified evidence;
- independent confirmation;
- accessible notice;
- protective exceptions;
- compensation funding;
- local or Indigenous interface;
- a defined logistics or continuity capacity.

## 11.4 Partial activation and modularity

The system should activate the smallest module capable of addressing the hazard.

Failure of one module does not transfer its powers to another institution.

# 12. Operational coordination and command

## 12.1 Mission-bounded command

Operational command applies only to the activated mission, personnel, assets, territory, and duration.

A commander cannot expand the mission merely because additional action appears useful.

## 12.2 Command record

The Operational Command and Deployment Record shall state:

- mission;
- command hierarchy;
- participating institutions;
- personnel status;
- rules of engagement or operational limits;
- host authority;
- geographic boundary;
- communications;
- data access;
- logistics;
- safety;
- complaints and incident reporting;
- handoff;
- withdrawal;
- after-action responsibility.

## 12.3 Civilian authority and professional autonomy

Health, engineering, scientific, humanitarian, judicial, and military professionals retain the ethical and legal duties of their roles.

Operational command shall not require professionals to falsify evidence, conceal unlawful conduct, abandon patient duties, violate protected knowledge, or perform acts outside lawful competence.

## 12.4 Multiple commands

Where several institutions operate together, the interface shall distinguish:

- strategic coordination;
- operational command;
- technical direction;
- professional supervision;
- asset ownership;
- host-jurisdiction authority;
- justice and oversight.

## 12.5 Diplomatic immunity

No framework grants diplomatic immunity. Any privileges and immunities require a valid agreement and shall not bar investigation, remedy, or accountability for serious misconduct.

## 12.6 Handoff

Operational handoff transfers a stated mission and relevant records. It does not transfer all powers, legal responsibility, intelligence access, or institutional jurisdiction.

# 13. Time limits, review, renewal, and persistent conditions

## 13.1 Seventy-two-hour provisional limit

Layer D action expires no later than seventy-two hours unless valid continuation is granted.

## 13.2 Thirty-day authorization limit

A continued authorization shall not exceed thirty days without a new review and renewal instrument.

## 13.3 Ninety-day extraordinary review

Cumulative renewal beyond ninety days requires extraordinary independent review addressing:

- whether the condition remains an emergency;
- whether ordinary law can now govern it;
- cumulative rights effects;
- unequal burdens;
- institutional entrenchment;
- data and secrecy expansion;
- financial and work consequences;
- unresolved compensation;
- transition or termination.

## 13.4 No serial evasion

Authorities may not evade time limits by:

- renaming the emergency;
- rotating declaring bodies;
- splitting one response into overlapping declarations;
- repeatedly issuing provisional orders;
- moving the same power among frameworks;
- treating persistent conditions as successive new emergencies without material change.

## 13.5 Renewal State Estimate

Renewal requires fresh evidence and shall not rely solely on the original declaration.

## 13.6 Persistent conditions

Where the condition is persistent, the responsible authorities shall propose ordinary legislation, appropriations, institutions, labour arrangements, data rules, and rights protections.

Emergency authority shall contract as ordinary capacity becomes available.

---

# 14. Rights, non-derogation, and protected continuity

## 14.1 Rights and Non-Derogation Record

Every activation shall identify:

- rights affected;
- rights not affected;
- non-derogable rights;
- lawful derogation authority, if any;
- necessity and proportionality;
- affected subgroups;
- accommodations;
- notice and reasons;
- challenge and review;
- expiry and remedy.

## 14.2 Protected minima

Emergency authority does not automatically permit:

- suspension of AUBI Layer 1 or equivalent material minima;
- confiscation of personal balances;
- interruption of essential health, water, food, housing, sanitation, energy, or communication services;
- denial of legal aid or access to court;
- compulsory disclosure of sacred or protected knowledge;
- forced work through Community Providers;
- permanent territorial or constitutional transfer;
- indefinite detention or censorship;
- automatic guilt, watchlist, or blacklist findings;
- irreversible extraction from Indigenous territory;
- waiver of non-derogable rights.

## 14.3 Disability and accessibility

Emergency planning and action shall include:

- accessible warnings;
- sign-language, tactile, plain-language, and low-bandwidth formats;
- mobility, sensory, cognitive, medication, and communication support;
- personal-assistance continuity;
- service-animal and assistive-device accommodation;
- accessible shelters and transport;
- non-discrimination in triage and resource allocation.

## 14.4 Children, elders, displaced persons, and institutionalized populations

Plans shall identify distinct protections for people who may lack documentation, independent mobility, communication access, legal representation, or safe family support.

## 14.5 No collective punishment

Emergency measures, sanctions, asset actions, service restrictions, or market controls shall target responsible actors and the relevant risk pathway. They shall protect workers, dependents, patients, users, communities, and essential providers not responsible for the harm.

---

# 15. Local and Indigenous emergency interfaces

## 15.1 Affected-area liaison

Every emergency shall maintain:

- an affected-area liaison function;
- local evidence channels;
- accessible communication;
- a means to challenge inaccurate maps, assumptions, and resource priorities;
- feedback from frontline workers and affected communities;
- a record of unresolved disagreement.

## 15.2 Indigenous Emergency Interface Record

Where Indigenous rights, territories, knowledge, genetic material, data, cultural continuity, or sacred relations are affected, the record shall identify:

- the affected people or peoples;
- authorities they identify;
- applicable Indigenous law and treaty rights;
- territorial and subject scope;
- consent, refusal, or non-engagement;
- acceptable communication and decision forms;
- protected knowledge and non-transfer;
- rescue exceptions;
- access, withdrawal, and restoration;
- unresolved jurisdiction.

## 15.3 No universal proxy

No Earth Council, representation quota, global Indigenous panel, or GGF-appointed representative may act for an affected people without authority from that people.

## 15.4 Traditional and local knowledge

Traditional, local, and experiential knowledge may inform state estimation under the terms of its holders.

No authority may require disclosure, digitization, publication, model training, or permanent institutional storage merely because the knowledge may be useful in an emergency.

## 15.5 Immediate rescue

Immediate rescue of life without prior consent shall be:

- based on valid rescue authority;
- narrow and non-extractive;
- temporary;
- limited to the imminent harm;
- documented;
- communicated to the affected authority as soon as practicable;
- immediately reviewable;
- followed by restoration and data disposition;
- incapable of creating precedent for permanent access or jurisdiction.

## 15.6 No general emergency veto override

A crisis does not create a general power to override Indigenous refusal.

Where valid law authorizes a narrowly defined rescue or cross-boundary protective act, the record must identify the precise authority, threshold, alternatives, territorial limit, review, compensation, and non-precedential character.

# 16. Data, privacy, security, secrecy, and public information

## 16.1 Data authority

Emergency data collection, access, sharing, linkage, publication, or retention requires an identified authority and purpose.

Urgency does not transform all relevant data into a public commons.

## 16.2 Data minimization

Collect and disclose only what is necessary for the authorized function.

Health, genetic, biometric, location, communications, military, infrastructure, security, and Indigenous-restricted data require separated access and lifecycle rules.

## 16.3 Emergency Data Record

The record shall state:

- data categories;
- purpose;
- collection authority;
- sources;
- affected persons or communities;
- access roles;
- cross-border transfers;
- security controls;
- automation or model use;
- correction and challenge;
- retention and deletion;
- public reporting;
- post-emergency disposition.

## 16.4 Protected non-transfer

An authority may accept a bounded condition, refusal, warning, result, attestation, or operational recommendation without demanding the underlying protected knowledge or raw data.

## 16.5 Cybersecurity

Security measures shall protect confidentiality, integrity, availability, correction, continuity, and recovery.

Cybersecurity cannot justify indefinite secrecy, unchallengeable evidence, exclusion of offline participants, permanent identity requirements, or unrestricted intelligence sharing.

## 16.6 Public information

Emergency communication shall:

- state what is known, unknown, and disputed;
- distinguish advice from legal order;
- identify the responsible authority;
- provide accessible action guidance;
- correct material errors;
- preserve source and whistleblower protection;
- avoid false reassurance and panic amplification;
- avoid political narrative manufacture.

## 16.7 No propaganda function

Crisis communication may authenticate, translate, explain, warn, correct, and disclose uncertainty.

It shall not conduct covert psychographic persuasion, manufacture a preferred political narrative, classify dissenters as epistemically defective, or use emergency channels to promote institutional loyalty.

## 16.8 Layered transparency

Public accountability does not require real-time publication of personal, tactical, security-sensitive, protected, or exploit-enabling information.

The record shall distinguish public, delayed, restricted, judicial, auditor, and protected access.

# 17. Emergency work, personnel, and professional protections

## 17.1 Emergency Work Mobilisation Record

Every mobilisation shall state:

- legal and contractual basis;
- voluntary or compulsory status;
- role and scope;
- training and competence;
- safety assessment;
- protective equipment;
- hours, rotation, rest, and maximum exposure;
- wages, hazard pay, insurance, and benefits;
- accommodation and family support;
- disability and health accommodation;
- refusal and withdrawal;
- whistleblower protection;
- supervision;
- injury, illness, and compensation;
- demobilisation and reintegration.

## 17.2 No unpaid labour through moral status

Hearts, civic honour, community recognition, patriotism, spirituality, service narratives, or emergency rhetoric do not replace wages, safety, insurance, and labour rights.

## 17.3 Community responders

Community health workers, land defenders, local monitors, volunteer rescue teams, and mutual-aid providers require:

- informed participation;
- clear boundaries;
- protection from retaliation;
- confidentiality;
- training;
- equipment;
- compensation or lawful volunteer terms;
- no automatic evidentiary burden beyond competence;
- no conversion into surveillance or enforcement agents without separate authority.

## 17.4 Compulsory service

Compulsory service requires a separate valid constitutional basis. It cannot be inferred from the Treaty, this Specification, AUBI participation, professional status, or Community Provider recognition.

## 17.5 Military and security personnel

Repurposed military or intelligence capabilities remain governed by their valid command, deployment, classification, and accountability law.

Civilian emergency coordination does not absorb them into a general GGF force.

# 18. Emergency finance, procurement, and compensation

## 18.1 No self-authorized finance

This Specification does not create taxes, levies, bonds, debt holidays, appropriations, asset freezes, patent revocations, compulsory licences, or funding reallocations.

Each requires a valid fiscal, legislative, contractual, judicial, or Treaty basis.

## 18.2 Pre-authorized forecast finance

Forecast-based support may be released automatically where a valid instrument has already defined:

- eligible hazards;
- model and trigger governance;
- independent validation;
- beneficiaries;
- payment authority;
- false-alarm treatment;
- fraud controls;
- appeals;
- data use;
- audit;
- replenishment.

Good-faith false alarms shall not automatically reduce future support for affected communities.

## 18.3 Protected financial continuity

Layer 1 and essential-provider continuity remain protected except under a separately authorized constitutional standard.

Emergency funds shall not be conditioned on political support, Treaty accession, participation in a GGF programme, acceptance of surveillance, or surrender of Indigenous authority.

## 18.4 Emergency procurement

Emergency procurement shall state:

- authority;
- need and urgency;
- competition or exception used;
- conflicts of interest;
- price and delivery terms;
- quality and safety;
- labour and environmental conditions;
- data and intellectual-property terms;
- audit and publication;
- termination and asset disposition.

## 18.5 Compensation reserve

Where an action can predictably cause displacement, business interruption, asset restraint, culling, destruction, evacuation cost, data harm, worker injury, or other burden, the activating authority shall identify the compensation route and funding.

## 18.6 No emergency debt punishment

Disaster exposure, low resilience scores, failed forecasts, or inability to meet preparedness targets shall not automatically increase sovereign or community debt burdens.

## 18.7 Sanctions

Sanctions and asset measures require valid law, evidence, targeted responsibility, proportionality, humanitarian exceptions, worker and third-party protection, review, and remedy.

Non-participation in a GGF emergency, health, resilience, biosafety, security-transition, or catastrophic-risk programme is not itself sanctionable.

# 19. Interim protection, justice, investigation, and enforcement

## 19.1 Interim protection

Courts or other competent authorities may issue narrow interim measures to preserve safety, evidence, assets, rights, ecosystems, essential services, or the effectiveness of final judgment.

Interim measures are not preliminary punishment.

## 19.2 Threshold and record

The applicant shall show the standard required by law, including as relevant:

- plausible right or legal basis;
- urgency;
- serious or irreparable harm;
- connection between measure and harm;
- necessity and proportionality;
- balance of impacts;
- jurisdiction or provisional jurisdiction.

## 19.3 Ex parte action

Action without prior hearing is exceptional and requires narrow scope, short duration, prompt notice, rapid adversarial review, and compensation where wrongfully imposed.

## 19.4 Justice remains separate

A tribunal does not investigate, prosecute, command deployment, seize assets, operate quarantine, enforce its own orders, or administer emergency programmes unless separately and validly assigned.

## 19.5 Investigation and Evidence Handoff

A handoff shall identify:

- legal basis;
- offence or investigative purpose;
- evidence inventory;
- chain of custody;
- warrants or orders;
- protected and privileged material;
- data limits;
- cross-border transfer;
- access and disclosure;
- return or deletion;
- review and remedy.

A handoff transfers evidence and a defined function. It does not transfer guilt, jurisdiction, or unlimited data access.

## 19.6 Enforcement

Arrest, search, seizure, detention, facility closure, asset restraint, compulsory access, and force require separately valid authority, warrants or orders where applicable, rights protections, independent review, and remedy.

## 19.7 Recognition across jurisdictions

A declaration or order does not authorize foreign personnel to act directly in another jurisdiction without valid agreement or law.

## 19.8 Appeals and urgent review

Urgent review shall be available for:

- jurisdiction;
- detention;
- isolation and quarantine;
- severe movement restrictions;
- compulsory medical measures;
- protected knowledge;
- asset restraint;
- facility closure;
- deployment;
- censorship or communications restrictions;
- prolonged emergency renewal.

# 20. Cross-border assistance and mutual aid

## 20.1 Assistance request

A Cross-Border Assistance Record shall state:

- requesting authority;
- assisting authority;
- mission;
- personnel and assets;
- geographic scope;
- host command and liaison;
- privileges and immunities;
- rules of operation;
- data access;
- cost and reimbursement;
- liability;
- withdrawal;
- handoff;
- dispute resolution.

## 20.2 Offers without request

An unsolicited offer may be made, but entry and operation require host or other valid authority except for narrowly valid rescue under applicable law.

## 20.3 Humanitarian neutrality

Humanitarian assistance shall not be conditioned on military alignment, political loyalty, data surrender, media participation, GGF accession, or acceptance of unrelated policy.

## 20.4 Health ceasefires and safe corridors

A health or humanitarian body may facilitate or propose a ceasefire or safe corridor. It cannot unilaterally bind armed parties.

Any agreement shall identify parties, routes, duration, monitoring, protected persons, breach process, and applicable humanitarian law.

## 20.5 Mutual recognition of professional and technical capacity

Recognition should be pre-negotiated where possible. Emergency recognition shall be temporary, role-specific, supervised, and followed by review.

# 21. Public-health action module

This module applies to outbreak detection, public-health advice, countermeasure distribution, isolation, quarantine, vaccination programmes, clinical surge, and health logistics.

## 21.1 Functional separation

The following should be separated:

- surveillance and alert;
- scientific assessment;
- emergency declaration;
- clinical and public-health operations;
- resource allocation;
- industrial regulation;
- adjudication;
- enforcement.

No Global Health Security Council or equivalent body should perform all functions.

## 21.2 Health Measure and Bodily Integrity Record

The record shall state:

- disease or condition;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- measure;
- legal authority;
- affected population;
- medical basis;
- voluntariness or compulsion;
- consent and refusal;
- exemptions and accommodations;
- least restrictive alternative;
- duration and review;
- support during compliance;
- data use;
- appeal and remedy.

## 21.3 Isolation and quarantine

Isolation and quarantine require:

- valid legal basis;
- individualized or lawfully defined criteria;
- humane conditions;
- food, water, sanitation, communication, healthcare, legal access, and family contact;
- disability and cultural accommodation;
- regular review;
- release criteria;
- compensation where law provides;
- remedy for wrongful restriction.

Community facilities do not become detention sites merely by being locally operated.

## 21.4 Vaccination and treatment

Emergency allocation and delivery shall distinguish:

- availability;
- recommendation;
- consent;
- mandate;
- professional duty;
- liability;
- adverse-event support;
- access equity;
- data and identity requirements.

## 21.5 Allocation and triage

Life-sustaining resource allocation shall use published rights-compatible criteria, document uncertainty and scarcity, prohibit discrimination, provide review where practicable, and avoid treating wealth, citizenship, disability, age, perceived social value, political affiliation, or GGF participation as automatic priority.

## 21.6 Community health networks

Community health networks should remain locally governed service and detection capacities with labour, privacy, clinical, data, and withdrawal protections. They are not a standing global surveillance or enforcement arm.

# 22. Evacuation, shelter, and disaster-response module

## 22.1 Voluntary support first

Where time permits, authorities shall prioritize accessible warning, transport support, shelter capacity, livelihood and animal support, and informed voluntary evacuation.

## 22.2 Compulsory evacuation

Compulsory evacuation requires valid law and shall state:

- hazard and area;
- evidence;
- authority;
- routes and destinations;
- duration;
- exceptions;
- property and animal protection;
- assistance for disabled, ill, institutionalized, undocumented, or isolated persons;
- family unity;
- security at shelters;
- return criteria;
- compensation and remedy.

## 22.3 Shelter rights

Shelter shall protect safety, dignity, privacy, accessibility, sanitation, health, communication, cultural needs, family integrity, gender safety, and freedom from exploitation.

## 22.4 Disaster-risk planning

Risk registers, early warnings, infrastructure hardening, and forecast finance belong primarily to ordinary governance.

A resilience score may support planning but shall not determine jurisdiction, debt terms, or emergency activation by itself.

## 22.5 Land-use restrictions

Hazard-related land-use controls require valid territorial and planning authority, evidence, notice, participation, Indigenous interfaces, compensation where applicable, and appeal.

A BAZ model or technical recommendation does not automatically override national, local, or Indigenous jurisdiction.

# 23. Cyber and technical assistance module

## 23.1 Operator action

An operator may isolate systems, revoke credentials, fail over, shut down a service, or preserve logs within lawful operational competence and contractual duties.

Operator action does not establish public emergency authority.

## 23.2 Defensive assistance

External cyber assistance requires:

- operator or competent-authority request, or a valid collective-defence basis;
- defined systems and mission;
- access controls;
- data minimization;
- evidence preservation;
- incident command;
- third-party and cross-border effects;
- vulnerability disclosure;
- disengagement and credential revocation.

## 23.3 Offensive or intrusive action

Counter-intrusion, disruption of foreign systems, surveillance, attribution, seizure, or offensive cyber action requires separately valid legal authority and review.

## 23.4 Automated containment

Automation may execute a pre-authorized operator safety rule. It may not determine aggressor status, authorize counterattack, expand jurisdiction, or impose public sanctions.

## 23.5 Public reporting

Incident disclosure shall be layered to protect users, evidence, remediation, and security. Fixed real-time deadlines should not require publication of exploit-enabling details.

---

# 24. Hazardous-material, laboratory, and biological incident module

## 24.1 Incident response

Laboratory and hazardous-material incidents shall distinguish:

- facility emergency action;
- public-health action;
- environmental containment;
- worker protection;
- criminal or regulatory investigation;
- public communication;
- cross-border notification.

## 24.2 Biosafety standards and licensing

Standards, risk assessment, licensing, inspection, emergency response, access policy, patent policy, and adjudication shall remain separate functions.

A technical biosafety council does not acquire residual authority over all biotechnology.

## 24.3 Biological containment and reversibility

Kill switches, containment systems, and reversibility claims shall be evaluated as context-specific risk controls, not absolute guarantees.

The record shall address failure modes, evolutionary escape, manufacturing variance, monitoring, retrieval, ecological uncertainty, and fallback options.

## 24.4 Genetic and Indigenous rights

Genetic material, associated knowledge, consent, provenance, benefit sharing, and protected non-transfer shall follow affected-person and affected-nation authority and Aurora Accord v1.3.

No universal registry, blockchain, or Earth Council proxy is required.

## 24.5 Sector pause

A sector-wide pause requires valid legislative, regulatory, judicial, or Treaty authority. BHI, LMCI, incident counts, or model thresholds may trigger review but cannot automatically pause all deployment.

## 24.6 Dual-use knowledge

Safety transparency shall be balanced with security, scientific freedom, protected knowledge, legitimate research, and misuse risk through layered access and review.

# 25. Infrastructure stabilisation and humanitarian logistics module

## 25.1 Essential systems

This module covers water, energy, health, food, sanitation, housing, communications, transport, payment, and critical digital infrastructure.

## 25.2 Prioritisation record

Service prioritisation shall state:

- authority;
- dependency map;
- protected users and services;
- allocation criteria;
- expected outage;
- alternatives;
- accessibility;
- worker safety;
- public notice;
- review;
- restoration.

## 25.3 Temporary control

Temporary public or emergency control of private infrastructure requires valid law or contract, defined scope, compensation rules, worker and user protection, duration, audit, and return.

## 25.4 Supply chains

Emergency logistics shall avoid converting procurement access, transport priority, or essential supplies into political leverage.

## 25.5 Humanitarian data

Beneficiary registration shall minimize data and support people without standard identity documents, digital wallets, biometrics, or GGF credentials.

---

# 26. Environmental containment and ecological emergency module

## 26.1 Environmental action

Containment may include fire control, spill response, species protection, pollution isolation, ecosystem rescue, and temporary access restrictions.

## 26.2 Precaution

Precaution may justify narrow interim protection where evidence is credible though incomplete, delay risks irreversible harm, the measure is connected to the threat, and review is available.

Precaution is not a blank authority to impose permanent ecological policy through emergency procedure.

## 26.3 Community and worker protection

Environmental measures shall protect workers, subsistence users, nearby communities, and innocent third parties. Closure or restriction plans shall include continuity, livelihood support, and restoration.

## 26.4 Indigenous territory

Emergency ecological action in Indigenous territory follows Section 15. Immediate rescue does not authorize permanent monitoring, extraction, conservation control, or knowledge appropriation.

## 26.5 Ecological evidence

Scientific models, remote sensing, local observation, and Indigenous knowledge may remain partly non-comparable. The State Estimate shall preserve disagreement rather than forcing one universal weighting rule.

---

# 27. Unarmed international assistance and armed defensive deployment

## 27.1 Unarmed assistance

Unarmed international assistance requires host or competent international authorization and a mission-specific record.

It may include rescue, logistics, engineering, medical, scientific, communications, monitoring, and humanitarian support.

## 27.2 Armed deployment

Armed deployment requires:

- a separately ratified or otherwise valid legal basis;
- competent civilian authorization;
- the Treaty-aligned enhanced procedure where applicable;
- necessity and proportionality;
- defined mission and geographic scope;
- civilian protection;
- rules of engagement;
- detention and evidence rules;
- time limit;
- independent review;
- withdrawal and restoration.

## 27.3 No force through framework handoff

A health, disaster, biosafety, technology, Aegis, or catastrophic-risk body cannot obtain military force merely by referring a matter to another GGF framework.

## 27.4 Civilian capability transition

Repurposed military capabilities may support civilian missions through valid agreements that address:

- command;
- demilitarisation of the function;
- dual-use risk;
- surveillance limits;
- data;
- labour transition;
- procurement;
- liability;
- reversion and misuse prevention.

## 27.5 No political enforcement of participation

Refusal to join a security-transition, planetary-service, catastrophic-risk, or emergency compact is not itself grounds for sanctions, threat classification, or force.

# 28. Termination, authority restoration, and continuity

## 28.1 Termination triggers

Every activation shall state termination triggers, including:

- hazard threshold no longer met;
- mission completed;
- evidence materially revised;
- authority withdrawn;
- host request terminated;
- court or review order;
- time limit reached;
- ordinary institution capable of resuming function;
- measure ineffective or causing disproportionate harm.

## 28.2 Emergency Termination Certificate

The certificate shall state:

- action terminated;
- time;
- responsible authority;
- remaining risks;
- powers ended;
- residual lawful functions;
- personnel demobilisation;
- asset and facility return;
- service restoration;
- temporary contract closure;
- data disposition;
- unresolved claims;
- compensation process;
- after-action schedule.

## 28.3 Authority return

Temporary authority shall return to the prior competent institutions or to a lawfully constituted successor.

The emergency actor does not retain jurisdiction merely because it performed effectively.

## 28.4 Asset and credential return

Access credentials, keys, equipment, facilities, vehicles, funds, repositories, models, communications channels, and other assets shall be returned, revoked, transferred, or disposed of under a recorded lawful process.

## 28.5 Service continuity

Termination shall not abruptly end essential services. A continuity plan shall distinguish the end of emergency power from the lawful continuation of necessary service delivery.

## 28.6 Institutional sunset

Temporary bodies shall dissolve or revert when their mandate ends. Evaluation, archive, or unresolved-risk functions do not justify retaining operational command.

# 29. Wrongful action, restoration, and compensation

## 29.1 Wrongful Action and Compensation Record

The record shall support claims involving:

- wrongful isolation, quarantine, detention, evacuation, or exclusion;
- wrongful seizure, destruction, culling, closure, or asset restraint;
- discriminatory triage;
- unlawful data collection, disclosure, retention, or model use;
- worker injury or uncompensated mobilisation;
- damage caused by responders;
- excessive or prolonged restrictions;
- incorrect risk attribution;
- wrongful public accusation;
- protected-knowledge extraction;
- unjustified service interruption;
- failure to restore authority or property.

## 29.2 Available restoration

Depending on law, restoration may include:

- release or cessation;
- return of property;
- repair or replacement;
- compensation;
- income and business support;
- medical and psychological care;
- correction or expungement of records;
- deletion or return of data;
- public correction or apology;
- institutional investigation;
- disciplinary or legal accountability;
- guarantees of non-repetition.

## 29.3 Good-faith false alarms

People and communities should not be punished for good-faith alerts or for receiving properly authorized forecast support when the forecasted event does not occur.

Reckless or malicious reporting may be addressed under valid law with due process.

## 29.4 State and institutional responsibility

Emergency urgency does not eliminate institutional liability. Immunities, if any, must be validly constituted, proportionate, and compatible with access to remedy.

# 30. After-action review, learning, and ordinary-capacity transition

## 30.1 Deadline

Within ninety days after termination, the responsible body shall publish or lodge with the competent oversight body an After-Action and Ordinary-Capacity Review.

## 30.2 Required content

The review shall address:

- alerts and estimates;
- declaration accuracy;
- authority chain;
- actions and evidence;
- rights affected;
- affected-area and Indigenous accounts;
- subgroup and distributional impacts;
- worker effects;
- financial flows;
- data and secrecy;
- operational outcomes;
- harms and wrongful action;
- restoration and restitution;
- missing ordinary capacity;
- unresolved risks and claims;
- proposed legal or institutional changes;
- dissent.

## 30.3 Independence

Operational actors shall contribute evidence but shall not exclusively evaluate themselves.

The review should include independent, affected-community, worker, rights, technical, and Indigenous participation where relevant.

## 30.4 Learning does not transfer authority

Success may justify support, replication, or proposed legislation. It does not automatically transfer WHO, national, local, Indigenous, professional, judicial, financial, or military authority to the emergency actor.

## 30.5 Failure may stop escalation

The review may conclude that:

- the alert was unsupported;
- the declaration was unnecessary;
- the response should not be scaled;
- a technology or protocol should be retired;
- the institution should dissolve;
- authority should remain local or plural;
- no competent authority presently exists;
- unresolved status is preferable to invented jurisdiction.

# 32. Constitutional readiness gates

This Specification and any importing framework are not constitutionally ready unless they pass all gates.

1. **No observer-to-command conversion.** Risk observers cannot both establish the condition and activate coercive action without separate valid authority.
2. **No crisis-created sovereign.** No body gains residual power because a threat is global, catastrophic, existential, or unprecedented.
3. **No Meta-Governance command.** Meta-Governance convenes and connects but does not become an emergency executive.
4. **No PIS takeover.** Catastrophic-risk coordination cannot override ordinary or domain institutions.
5. **No unconstituted global emergency electorate.** A global council or BAZ supermajority does not automatically authorize planetary action.
6. **No universal Indigenous proxy.** No body acts for non-participating Indigenous peoples.
7. **No erasure of Indigenous decision class.** Emergency does not erase affected-nation authority over territory, knowledge, data, genetic material, culture, or sacred relations.
8. **No metric-to-power automation.** Metrics may alert but cannot declare, pause sectors, deploy, sanction, or override.
9. **No combined health sovereign.** Assessment, declaration, allocation, regulation, command, and adjudication remain separated.
10. **No combined biosafety sovereign.** Standards, licensing, emergency response, access policy, and adjudication remain separated.
11. **No court as enforcement corps.** Courts do not investigate, deploy, seize, or execute their own orders without separately valid assignment.
12. **No force through framework handoff.** Armed or coercive deployment requires separate lawful authority and command.
13. **No political sanctions for non-participation.** Refusal to join a GGF programme is not itself sanctionable.
14. **No AUBI conditionality.** Layer 1 remains unconditional.
15. **No unpaid emergency labour disguised as recognition.** Honour and Hearts do not replace wages and protections.
16. **No universal public risk ledger.** Sensitive records use layered access, correction, retention, and deletion.
17. **No propaganda function.** Public communication informs and corrects but does not manufacture political narratives.
18. **No punishment for good-faith false alarms.** Forecast miss does not automatically reduce future support.
19. **No emergency debt punishment.** Preparedness or disaster failure does not automatically increase debt burdens.
20. **No perpetual renewal.** Repeated emergency authorization cannot replace ordinary law.
21. **No unreviewable triage.** Scarce life-sustaining resources use published rights-compatible criteria and review.
22. **No competence-to-jurisdiction migration.** Effective performance does not itself transfer authority.
23. **No wrongful-action gap.** Excessive or mistaken action has restoration and compensation routes.
24. **Failure can stop escalation.** The system can conclude that evidence is insufficient or only voluntary action is lawful.

# Final governing clauses

> **Emergency is a temporary overlay on valid authority, not a replacement for it.**

> **A global threat may require global cooperation. It does not automatically create a global commander.**

> **No institution may turn its own observation of danger into unchecked power over the system it observes.**

> **The constitutional test is not only whether the system can activate quickly. It is whether it can refuse unlawful escalation, preserve rights during action, terminate power when the condition changes, restore what it disrupted, and compensate those it wronged.**
