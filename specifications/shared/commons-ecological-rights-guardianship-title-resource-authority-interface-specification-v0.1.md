## A common constitutional interface for ecological observation, rights recognition, guardianship, claims, title, rematriation, stewardship, resource allocation, inspection, urgent protection, finance, adjudication, restoration, remedy, and authority return

**Identifier:** `CERGTA/0.1`  
**Status:** Constitutional interface specification; drafting, conformance, and cross-framework testing instrument  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Primary use:** revision and harmonization of Hearthstone, Ecological Intelligence & Rights, Planetary Health, Biodiversity, Oceans, Water, Soil, and the MOS advisory dependency  
**Not a source of ecological personhood, guardianship, title, taxation, licensing, inspection, investigation, emergency, receivership, sanctions, adjudication, police, force, or enforcement power**

# Executive summary

The commons and ecological frameworks seek to recognize the living world as more than property, return land and authority to rightful stewards, protect water and soil as life-support systems, govern oceans and biodiversity within ecological limits, and replace extraction with reciprocal stewardship. Those purposes require institutions that can observe ecological conditions, recognize lawful rights, appoint accountable guardians, resolve claims, transfer or constrain title, allocate scarce resources, inspect regulated activities, protect ecosystems urgently, impose lawful financial duties, adjudicate disputes, restore harm, and end authority cleanly.

Those functions are necessary. Their necessity does not make them one function.

The audited cluster presently allows observations, indicators, degradation thresholds, moral categories, spiritual recognition, council composition, dashboard scores, registries, and AI models to flow too directly into legal personhood, vetoes, guardianship, title restriction, stewardship revocation, tariffs, blacklists, funding freezes, receivership, market exclusion, port denial, water shutoff, and enforcement. `CERGTA/0.1` supplies the missing constitutional type system.

Its central rules are:

> **Ecological fact is not legal right. Legal right is not title. Title is not stewardship mandate. Stewardship is not allocation. Allocation is not enforcement. Measurement may trigger review; it does not create authority.**

> **A guardian represents a rights-holder; a guardian does not own it.**

> **Affected Indigenous nations speak for themselves. Global Indigenous representation may advise, support, or audit; it cannot consent, designate sacredness, transfer title, or waive rights for the affected nation.**

> **Severity determines urgency, not jurisdiction. A temporary ecological protection measure cannot become permanent title, receivership, closure, taxation, sanction, or enforcement by inertia.**

> **No dashboard, threshold, AI output, registry entry, blockchain, framework publication, target year, coalition size, council vote, spiritual claim, moral classification, funding condition, or pilot success may substitute for action-specific law, reasons, rights, review, expiry, correction, and remedy.**

The Specification establishes eighteen action classes, fifty-four interoperable records, a forty-field common record header, seven access classes, ten handoff states, thirty-two readiness gates, and thirty-six compound tests. It preserves ecological rights and Indigenous authority while preventing observation, guardianship, stewardship, and resource governance from becoming unbounded title or coercive power.

# 1. Purpose, scope, and legal character

## 1.1 Purpose

This Specification defines the minimum constitutional interface for any GGF framework, institution, council, BAZ, trust, guardian, fund, tribunal, Water Assembly, basin body, port authority, ecological monitoring system, registry, pilot, contractor, partner, or emergency mechanism that proposes, requests, supports, records, reviews, or executes a commons or ecological authority action.

It exists to:

1. separate observation, interpretation, recommendation, rights recognition, guardianship, claims, title, stewardship, standards, allocation, licensing, inspection, investigation, urgent protection, restrictions, finance, adjudication, restoration, and closure;
2. make every legal and material consequence traceable to an independently valid source of authority;
3. preserve affected-nation authority, customary law, sacred relations, protected knowledge, and non-engagement;
4. prevent ecological personhood, moral standing, sacredness, metrics, registries, and stewardship language from silently transferring title or creating enforcement power;
5. protect residents, tenants, workers, users, permit holders, creditors, public services, animals, future generations, ecosystems, and claimants during transitions;
6. establish non-derogable essential minimums for water, sanitation, health, disability support, emergency food production, and other life-support systems;
7. define lawful interfaces with Justice, SCPA, emergency governance, Aurora, Indigenous sovereignty, and data systems;
8. require correction, appeal, compensation, restoration, data disposition, termination, authority return, and unresolved-duty custody without residual power.

## 1.2 Scope

This Specification applies whenever an action may materially affect:

- ecosystem or commons legal status;
- ecological boundaries, sacred relations, or protected knowledge;
- Indigenous territory, authority, treaty rights, customary law, or consent;
- title, tenure, occupancy, tenancy, labour, public access, licence, permit, quota, tariff, bond, covenant, trust, or encumbrance;
- water, sanitation, food, land, soil, fisheries, habitat, biodiversity, marine access, infrastructure, or other essential resource allocation;
- inspection, entry, sampling, investigation, evidence, listing, closure, market access, port access, finance, compensation, receivership, restoration, or remedy;
- personal, community, ecological, genetic, microbial, commercial, sacred, or Traditional Knowledge data.

It applies to direct actions and enabling acts such as model design, threshold setting, target adoption, guardian nomination, registry entry, fund conditions, target or permit recommendations, technical access, evidence transport, enforcement requests, and cross-framework handoffs.

## 1.3 Interface, not grant of power

This Specification does not itself:

- recognize an ecosystem as a legal person or rights-holder;
- appoint a guardian, create a trust, transfer title, recognize a property interest, ratify a sacred site, impose a covenant, revoke stewardship, or establish a BAZ;
- authorize taxation, tariffs, bonds, freezes, subsidies, funding conditions, licences, quotas, permits, water shutoff, port denial, blacklisting, inspection, investigation, evidence seizure, receivership, market exclusion, enforcement, or adjudication;
- create jurisdiction over a state, Indigenous nation, community, land, water, vessel, facility, person, resource, trust, company, registry, or data source;
- turn MOS, EIRL, PHC, BHI, LMCI, Love Ledger, Dynamic Rights Spectrum, Proof of Care, a sacred claim, citizen observation, AI model, dashboard, blockchain, or council vote into law;
- waive human, Indigenous, constitutional, property, labour, disability, child, public-health, environmental, due-process, privacy, cultural, or remedy obligations.

## 1.4 Non-authority clause

No actor may cite `CERGTA/0.1` as the legal source of a consequential act. It may be cited only for the interface, records, safeguards, transition rules, and conformance duties that attach once independently valid law supplies the action-specific authority.

A record is incomplete if it names a body without identifying its constituting instrument, jurisdiction, exact power, activation state, limits, review, expiry, and remedy.

## 1.5 Normative language

**SHALL** and **MUST** state mandatory conformance conditions. **SHOULD** states a rebuttable design requirement whose departure requires recorded reasons. **MAY** states a permitted option. None of these words creates power.

# 2. Definitions

**Affected nation** means the specific Indigenous nation, people, or community whose territory, law, rights, governance, sacred relations, knowledge, waters, lands, species, or resources may be affected. It never means a universal proxy.

**Commons** means a resource, asset, system, infrastructure, ecological relation, or cultural domain governed through shared duties, rights, access rules, or stewardship. Calling something a commons does not settle title, access, jurisdiction, or ownership.

**Ecological entity** means a legally or administratively relevant river, watershed, aquifer, forest, reef, ocean area, soil system, wetland, biome, species assemblage, habitat, atmosphere, ecological process, or other living-system unit. The legal entity and ecological system may overlap without being identical.

**Ecological observation** means a measurement, report, sample, map, image, record, testimony, Traditional Knowledge contribution, sensor output, or other account of ecological conditions.

**Ecological interpretation** means the analysis of observations for condition, trend, causation, attribution, threshold, forecast, risk, or ecological necessity.

**Ecological right** means a right or legally protected interest recognized under valid law. Moral consideration, ecological importance, scientific consensus, or framework language may support recognition but is not the recognition itself.

**Guardian** means a lawfully appointed representative owing fiduciary or equivalent duties to a rights-holder or legally protected interest. A guardian is not an owner, sovereign, regulator, police service, or court by virtue of guardianship.

**Rematriation** means a process of returning land, assets, authority, relationships, or stewardship to rightful Indigenous or community custodians through valid nation-specific and legal processes. The term does not bypass claims, title, compensation, continuity, or affected-nation law.

**Sacred designation** means a lawful and culturally authorized recognition of sacred, spiritual, ancestral, ceremonial, or relational significance. It is not universalizable and does not automatically create title, exclusion, criminal liability, or enforcement power.

**Stewardship** means a bounded mandate to care for, manage, restore, allocate, or represent an asset or ecological relation under specified duties and limits. Stewardship is not ownership unless valid law separately says so.

**Essential minimum** means the non-derogable quantity, quality, access, or continuity necessary to protect life, health, sanitation, disability support, emergency shelter, basic food production, ecological survival, or another legally protected minimum.

**Consequential action** means an act reasonably capable of producing a material legal, ecological, physical, economic, territorial, cultural, service, title, access, or rights effect.

**Authority activation state** means the exact legal status of a rule or institution: proposed, advisory, voluntarily adopted, contractually binding, enacted, treaty-active, suspended, expired, or terminated.

# 3. Constitutional doctrines

## 3.1 No upward inference

No record or action in a lower-consequence class creates authority in a higher-consequence class. In particular:

- observation is not interpretation;
- interpretation is not legal finding;
- recommendation is not recognition;
- recognition is not title;
- title is not guardianship;
- guardianship is not stewardship;
- stewardship is not permit or allocation authority;
- a standard is not an actor-specific permit;
- monitoring is not inspection;
- inspection is not investigation;
- investigation is not adjudication;
- adjudication is not execution;
- urgent protection is not permanent closure or receivership;
- financial incentive is not tax, freeze, forfeiture, or redistribution;
- restoration is not punishment;
- unresolved harm is not residual power.

## 3.2 Measurement–consequence firewall

Indicators, indices, scores, thresholds, AI outputs, dashboards, satellite products, citizen observations, Traditional Knowledge, scientific consensus, and ecological models may:

- trigger review;
- support a petition;
- inform a standard or plan;
- identify uncertainty or urgent risk;
- contribute to evidence after lawful custody and review.

They SHALL NOT automatically:

- recognize rights or personhood;
- define a legal boundary;
- appoint or remove a guardian;
- transfer title;
- suspend or revoke stewardship;
- issue or cancel a permit, quota, licence, or tariff;
- freeze funds or move assets;
- blacklist an actor or vessel;
- authorize entry, search, seizure, closure, custody, force, or receivership.

## 3.3 Legal status and activation firewall

Every recommendation, standard, target, policy, pilot, compact, council decision, framework provision, registry state, and treaty aspiration SHALL carry one exact activation state from Appendix D. Silence, publication, funding, coalition size, target date, supermajority, or institutional readiness is not activation.

## 3.4 Rights–title–stewardship separation

Ecological rights recognition SHALL identify legal standing and protected interests but SHALL NOT itself:

- vest title in the ecological entity or guardian;
- transfer underlying land, water, mineral, carbon, infrastructure, intellectual-property, data, or other interests;
- cancel existing lawful rights;
- create a stewardship institution;
- create a veto over all activity;
- decide compensation.

Each title, tenure, trust, covenant, rematriation, stewardship, allocation, and enforcement consequence requires its own record and authority.

## 3.5 Guardian fiduciary rule

A guardian SHALL:

- act within the lawful rights catalogue and appointment;
- identify instructions and affected-nation authority;
- disclose conflicts and funding;
- maintain independence from stewards, regulators, litigants, and funders;
- distinguish representation from scientific, political, spiritual, property, and enforcement roles;
- remain removable and substitutable through independent process;
- provide reasons and accessible reports;
- preserve protected knowledge and data.

A guardian MAY advocate, petition, participate, litigate where standing exists, and monitor implementation. A guardian MAY NOT own the entity, tax, license, inspect, investigate, adjudicate, command enforcement, or bind an affected nation by implication.

## 3.6 Affected-nation rule

For every action affecting Indigenous territory, law, governance, treaty rights, sacred relations, knowledge, data, water, land, species, or resources:

1. the specific affected nation or community SHALL be identified;
2. its own institutions and applicable law SHALL be recorded;
3. internal plurality and representative authority SHALL not be presumed away;
4. non-engagement SHALL not be treated as consent;
5. global Indigenous bodies MAY advise, support, resource, observe, or audit process integrity;
6. no global body may consent, designate sacredness, transfer title, waive rights, disclose protected knowledge, or impose a guardian for the affected nation.

## 3.7 Essential-resource non-deprivation

Commercial enforcement, ecological finance, permit action, tariff disputes, stewardship transition, receivership, private-partner exit, or market restriction SHALL preserve:

- sufficient safe drinking water and sanitation;
- emergency and continuing healthcare;
- disability-related access and equipment;
- emergency shelter and hygiene;
- essential household energy where necessary for water/health;
- emergency food production and subsistence needs;
- crew safety, rescue, and medical care;
- ecological survival minimums and legally protected flows.

Restriction of a commercial or industrial service SHALL be technically and legally segregated from essential access.

## 3.8 Urgency without jurisdiction

Urgent ecological protection SHALL be:

- based on individualized and reviewable facts;
- limited to imminent, serious, and plausibly irreversible harm;
- the least restrictive effective measure;
- time-limited with automatic expiry;
- subject to prompt independent review;
- accompanied by notice where safe, correction, compensation, and transition to ordinary law.

Urgency SHALL NOT create permanent personhood, title, receivership, guardianship, permit denial, taxation, blacklist, sanctions, search, seizure, detention, force, or military authority.

## 3.9 Technology and registry non-authority

AI, blockchain, digital twins, smart contracts, satellites, sensors, registries, dashboards, and automated pipelines SHALL be treated as evidence, administration, or decision-support systems. They cannot be competent authorities. Human and institutional accountability cannot be assigned to a model, ledger, vendor, or platform.

## 3.10 Remedy and closure

Every consequential action SHALL identify:

- notice and reasons;
- accessible challenge;
- interim relief against irreversible harm;
- correction across derivative systems;
- release, reconnection, property return, or authority return where applicable;
- compensation, restitution, restoration, or institutional remedy;
- termination and expiry;
- data disposition;
- unresolved duties held without residual power.

## 4.1 Classification by method and consequence

The action class is determined by what the actor proposes to do and the consequences reasonably capable of following, not by protective, regenerative, sacred, scientific, Indigenous, community, or commons rhetoric.

Where more than one class is plausible, the higher-consequence class governs until the action is split and the lower-consequence route is independently verified.

## 4.2 Combined processes

A form, dashboard, case file, trust instrument, registry, programme, or council agenda MAY contain several records. Combining records SHALL NOT combine powers. Each record retains its own authority, jurisdiction, access, review, expiry, correction, and remedy.

## 4.3 Handoffs

A referral or handoff SHALL identify exact records and material, requested function, legal purpose, recipient authority, access restrictions, retained sender duties, notice, expiry, return, correction, and closure. Receipt is not acceptance. Silence is never acceptance.

## 4.4 Cross-interface rule

Where ecological or commons action requires search, seizure, restraint, custody, force, arrest, border action, cyber disruption, financial coercion, sanctions execution, or analogous protected action, the recipient SHALL independently satisfy SCPA. `CERGTA/0.1` cannot bridge the gap.

Where an ecological event is also an emergency, CDEE/ECRC records may supply emergency identity and coordination, but cannot satisfy CERGTA rights, title, guardian, allocation, or enforcement requirements. CERGTA cannot declare or prolong an emergency.

# 5. Common record architecture

## 5.1 Record identity

Each canonical record uses `CERGTA-01` through `CERGTA-54`. A domain framework MAY specialize a record, but SHALL preserve the canonical identifier and all inherited safeguards.

## 5.2 Common states

Records SHALL use at least the lifecycle states in Appendix C. Operational completion is not legal closure. Archive is not continuing authority.

## 5.3 Public and protected layers

Every action SHALL preserve a public, affected-person, claimant, or review-accessible layer sufficient to reveal authority, legal status, entity or asset, general reasons, consequence, duration, review, and remedy, subject to lawful protection of personal, commercial, evidentiary, sacred, Indigenous-restricted, ecological-location, and security-sensitive material.

Secret general powers, unreviewable secret evidence, secret title transfers, and uncorrectable registries are non-conforming.

## 5.4 Fail-closed conditions

Action SHALL NOT proceed, or SHALL pause where safe, when:

- legal source, jurisdiction, or activation state is missing;
- the affected nation is unidentified or its authority is unresolved;
- a higher action class is plausible but not authorized;
- the ecological entity or legal boundary is materially uncertain and the consequence depends on it;
- a guardian lacks appointment, mandate, or conflict controls;
- competing claims or essential-service dependencies are omitted;
- protected knowledge or data restrictions are unresolved;
- a metric, model, registry, or automated pipeline is being treated as authority;
- irreversible harm is plausible and effective interim review is unavailable;
- the action lacks termination, correction, data disposition, and remedy.

# 6. Ecological observation, interpretation, and scientific authority

## 6.1 Plural observation without automatic legal weight

The Specification protects plural ecological sensing. Citizen science, Indigenous and local observation, professional science, low-tech records, remote sensing, and oral histories MAY all contribute. Their admissibility and weight depend on purpose, provenance, restrictions, uncertainty, and independent review—not on the social status of the observer or the sophistication of the technology.

## 6.2 Index governance

Every index or threshold SHALL publish:

- the problem it measures and what it does not measure;
- variables, weights, baselines, aggregation, and missingness;
- geographic and temporal resolution;
- uncertainty, confidence, dissent, and distributional effects;
- sensitivity to alternative weights and models;
- known incentives and gaming risks;
- validation and correction methods;
- permitted advisory uses and prohibited consequential uses;
- version and retirement rules.

## 6.3 Precaution

Precaution MAY justify temporary review, monitoring, or urgent protective action when credible serious harm is uncertain. Precaution SHALL NOT erase the burden to identify authority, affected parties, alternatives, duration, review, and remedy.

## 6.4 Attribution

Scientific and technical attribution SHALL state whether it concerns physical contribution, probabilistic association, supply-chain relation, control, knowledge, intent, breach, or legal responsibility. No one form implies another.

# 7. Ecological rights recognition and legal identity

## 7.1 Recognition sequence

A conforming rights-recognition process SHALL ordinarily proceed through:

1. CERGTA-02 entity intake;
2. CERGTA-03–07 observation and interpretation;
3. CERGTA-08–10 affected-party and nation mapping;
4. CERGTA-11 petition and standing;
5. CERGTA-12 recommendation and dissent;
6. CERGTA-13 authorized recognition decision;
7. CERGTA-14 rights catalogue;
8. CERGTA-15 legal boundary;
9. CERGTA-16–19 guardianship where required;
10. CERGTA-29 registry entry.

Skipping a record requires a reasoned `not_applicable` determination; it cannot be omitted because another framework uses a different name.

## 7.2 Scope and conflicts

Recognition decisions SHALL specify interaction with:

- human life, health, subsistence, culture, housing, and livelihood;
- Indigenous sovereignty, treaty rights, customary law, and sacred relations;
- animal welfare and species/ecosystem conflicts;
- public access, navigation, water, fisheries, and infrastructure;
- title, tenure, permits, and lawful expectations;
- future generations;
- transboundary and high-seas law.

No abstract “biosphere priority” or “human priority” settles every conflict.

## 7.3 Automatic veto prohibited

Degradation thresholds MAY trigger review, interim protection, or a presumption stated in law. They SHALL NOT automatically create a veto, invalidate a permit, transfer title, or authorize enforcement without an action-specific decision and challenge route.

# 8. Guardianship constitution

## 8.1 Appointment source

Guardians SHALL be appointed by the authority recognized under the applicable legal system and affected-nation law. A framework may recommend eligibility and design, but cannot appoint through publication alone.

## 8.2 Representation model

The appointment SHALL state whether guardians act:

- jointly or severally;
- by consensus, majority, affected-nation instruction, or differentiated domain;
- as litigation representatives, stewardship advisers, monitors, fiduciaries, or rights advocates;
- with or without power to settle, consent, contract, access data, or request interim measures.

## 8.3 Accountability

Guardians SHALL be reviewable by affected nations, communities, lawful courts or review bodies, and the rights-holder’s mandate. Funding and institutional dependence SHALL be disclosed.

## 8.4 No universal guardian

No Earth Council, PHC, BCT, Water Assembly, global youth body, scientist panel, or global Indigenous body is guardian of every ecosystem by default.

# 9. Affected Indigenous nations, sacred relations, and protected knowledge

## 9.1 Nation-specific authority

A framework SHALL NOT treat seat composition, a global veto, or Earth Council ratification as consent by the nation whose territory or sacred relation is affected.

## 9.2 Internal plurality

Where authority, membership, boundary, or representation is contested within or between nations, the implementation SHALL preserve the dispute and use the nation’s own lawful processes or other mutually accepted route. It cannot choose the easiest interlocutor.

## 9.3 Sacred designations

Sacred designation SHALL distinguish:

- recognition internal to a nation or tradition;
- protected confidentiality and access rules;
- external legal recognition;
- title and tenure consequences;
- public-law restrictions;
- guardian or steward responsibilities.

Each is separate.

## 9.4 Knowledge protection

Protected knowledge may support a decision through a confidential or nation-controlled review mechanism without entering public dashboards, open-data systems, general AI training, or litigation records beyond what law and the nation permit.

# 10. Claims, title, rematriation, and registries

## 10.1 Claimant constitution

Every transition SHALL identify not only formal owners but also affected Indigenous nations, customary holders, residents, tenants, workers, users, public-right holders, creditors, pension interests, permit holders, and ecological rights-holders.

## 10.2 Rematriation

Rematriation SHALL be understood as a justice and relationship process requiring the affected nation’s authority and law. It cannot be reduced to transfer into a generic BAZ, global trust, or framework institution.

## 10.3 Compensation and reparations

Compensation, purchase price, debt relief, reparations, benefit sharing, ecological restoration, and transition support are distinct. Payment to one claimant does not extinguish others.

## 10.4 Registry principle

Registries SHALL point to authoritative source decisions. Registry software SHALL support correction, supersession, protected entries, offline counterparts, and propagation to derivative systems.

# 11. Stewardship institutions and commons governance

## 11.1 Charter before operation

Every Stewardship Trust, Water Assembly, BAZ operating body, basin body, commons council, or ecological administrator SHALL have CERGTA-31 before exercising consequential authority.

## 11.2 Stewardship performance

Performance systems SHALL distinguish:

- ecological outcomes;
- social and distributional outcomes;
- legal compliance;
- affected-nation and community legitimacy;
- worker conditions;
- external shocks and no-fault harm;
- metric gaming;
- uncertainty and time lags.

## 11.3 Suspension and dissolution

Suspension, revocation, succession, and dissolution SHALL preserve essential services, rights, claims, records, workers, funding, restoration duties, and independent review.

# 12. Standards, allocations, licences, tariffs, and essential resources

## 12.1 Standard versus decision

A standard may be advisory or legally adopted. Even a binding standard does not decide an individual permit or allocation unless law expressly provides a self-executing rule consistent with due process and non-derogable rights.

## 12.2 Allocation hierarchy

Allocation SHALL record genuine biophysical scarcity and artificial scarcity. Human survival, sanitation, ecological integrity, Indigenous and treaty rights, subsistence, public health, and economic use SHALL be balanced through explicit law rather than hidden ranking.

## 12.3 Water and sanitation

Water governance SHALL prohibit the use of household or public-health deprivation as leverage for commercial non-payment. Industrial restriction MAY be lawful only where technically separable and accompanied by review and remedy.

## 12.4 Oceans and ports

Port control, quotas, licences, MPAs, vessel monitoring, certification, blacklists, inspection, seizure, and crew welfare SHALL remain separate. Open monitoring data does not automatically permit denial or enforcement.

## 12.5 Soil and land use

Soil standards, regenerative mandates, land-value taxes, community rights of first refusal, stewardship leases, land-sale vetoes, registries, and rematriation SHALL identify their distinct legal sources and cannot be bundled as one ecological programme.

# 13. Inspection, investigation, evidence, and enforcement

## 13.1 Inspection

Inspection authority SHALL identify premises, system, records, samples, notice, consent or warrant, Indigenous territory, safety, privacy, scope, methods, and onward use.

## 13.2 Investigation

Investigation SHALL be opened by a competent authority through CERGTA-40. Guardians, scientists, monitors, funders, and councils may refer concerns but cannot direct compulsory methods without authority.

## 13.3 Evidence

Scientific evidence SHALL preserve reproducibility where possible, protected-source constraints, uncertainty, alternative causal accounts, and independent testing. Traditional Knowledge may require special review procedures that preserve restrictions.

## 13.4 Enforcement

An adjudicated order may be transported through CERGTA-48. The recipient must explicitly accept the named function. Where execution is coercive, SCPA governs the method.

# 14. Urgent ecological protection and emergency interfaces

## 14.1 CERGTA urgent action

CERGTA-42 addresses ecological urgency under ordinary or special ecological law. It does not declare a cross-domain emergency.

## 14.2 ECRC/CDEE interface

Where the event includes disaster, continuity, health emergency, evacuation, infrastructure failure, or multi-domain crisis, a CDEE event MAY link to the ecological action cluster. Both interfaces retain separate authority and clocks; the earlier expiry controls when an action depends on both.

## 14.3 Security and coercion interface

Evacuation, guarded access, exclusion zones, seizure, arrest, detention, force, cyber disruption, financial coercion, or border action requires SCPA. Ecological emergency language cannot bypass it.

# 15. Financial action, funds, rewards, and anti-Goodhart safeguards

## 15.1 Fiscal legality

Taxes, tariffs, levies, bonds, subsidies, reparations funds, compensation, freezes, and redistribution SHALL be independently constituted. A Global Commons Fund or Hearts/Leaves mechanism cannot self-authorize collections or consequences.

## 15.2 Rewards

Rewards for stewardship MAY recognize contribution. They SHALL NOT:

- determine human worth or rights;
- replace wages, public benefits, reparations, or legal duties;
- condition essential services;
- create title or guardian status;
- convert monitoring into surveillance;
- punish non-participation by withholding unrelated rights.

## 15.3 Funding conditions

Funding conditions SHALL be proportionate, related to the funded purpose, reviewable, and separated from sanctions. Automatic BHI-linked funding freezes are non-conforming.

# 16. Data, technology, registries, and public communication

## 16.1 Data separation

Ecological monitoring, personal data, community data, Indigenous-restricted knowledge, commercial data, evidence, permit records, claims, and public communications SHALL not be merged merely because one dashboard could technically display them.

## 16.2 Automated systems

No automated system may finally decide:

- ecological personhood or rights scope;
- affected-nation authority or consent;
- sacred status;
- guardian appointment or removal;
- title or rematriation;
- permit denial or revocation;
- liability, blacklist, freeze, receivership, or enforcement;
- compensation or remedy.

## 16.3 Public communication

Public reports SHALL distinguish observation, interpretation, allegation, recommendation, legal decision, and final judgment. Ecological advocacy cannot become official shaming, presumption of guilt, or propaganda authority.

# 17. Pilots, sandboxes, bootstrap institutions, and non-activation

Every pilot SHALL maintain CERGTA-54. Pilots MAY test:

- voluntary monitoring;
- advisory indices and plans;
- consensual stewardship practices;
- service improvements;
- participatory mapping;
- non-binding guardian simulations;
- registry usability with no constitutive effect;
- mock claims and hearing procedures.

Pilots SHALL NOT test real title transfer, compulsory rematriation, taxation, household shutoff, port denial, blacklist, search, seizure, receivership, sanction, or enforcement without ordinary legal authority.

# 18. Termination, correction, remedy, and unresolved duties

## 18.1 Complete closure

A process cannot become `closed` while any of the following remains unresolved:

- authority or credential return;
- title or registry correction;
- guardian succession;
- release or service reconnection;
- compensation, restitution, or appeal;
- ecological restoration;
- claimant notice;
- worker, tenant, user, or public-service continuity;
- protected-data disposition;
- outstanding claims or liabilities.

## 18.2 Unresolved duty without residual power

CERGTA-53 preserves responsibility for unfinished restoration, compensation, services, records, or claims. It cannot preserve expired inspection, receivership, permit, funding, restriction, surveillance, or enforcement power.

# 20. Constitutional readiness gates

An adopting framework SHALL pass all gates before claiming full `CERGTA/0.1` conformance.

## G01 — Legal source and activation

**Pass condition:** Every consequential action identifies a presently valid constituting instrument, exact power, jurisdiction, activation state, limits, review, and expiry.

## G02 — Observation–consequence separation

**Pass condition:** Monitoring and observations cannot directly create legal rights, title, permit, sanction, funding action, or deprivation.

## G03 — Model and threshold humility

**Pass condition:** Indicators, scores, forecasts, AI outputs, and thresholds disclose uncertainty and may trigger review only.

## G04 — Advisory versus binding status

**Pass condition:** Every standard, target, recommendation, framework rule, and council decision has an explicit adoption state.

## G05 — Rights-recognition authority

**Pass condition:** Legal personhood and ecological rights arise only through an authorized recognition decision with boundary, catalogue, appeal, and relationship to existing rights.

## G06 — Rights–title separation

**Pass condition:** Ecological rights recognition neither owns nor transfers land, water, infrastructure, data, carbon, minerals, or other property.

## G07 — Boundary constitution

**Pass condition:** Ecological and legal boundaries are versioned, reviewable, and do not silently erase overlapping jurisdictions or claims.

## G08 — Guardian constitution

**Pass condition:** Guardians have a lawful appointment, fiduciary mandate, conflicts regime, instructions, removal, liability, resources, and succession.

## G09 — Guardian non-ownership

**Pass condition:** Guardian standing does not create title, regulatory jurisdiction, taxation, enforcement, or general political authority.

## G10 — Affected-nation authority

**Pass condition:** The specific affected Indigenous nation or community is identified; global proxies cannot consent, designate sacredness, transfer title, or waive rights.

## G11 — Sacred designation boundary

**Pass condition:** Spiritual, cultural, sacred, or biocultural recognition has a protected and nation-specific route and does not itself create title or coercive power.

## G12 — Claims constitution

**Pass condition:** Claimants, residents, tenants, workers, users, creditors, customary interests, public rights, and competing claims receive notice, standing, evidence, and challenge.

## G13 — Title and rematriation legality

**Pass condition:** Every transfer identifies transferor authority, transferee capacity, affected-nation law, competing claims, continuity, compensation, registry correction, and appeal.

## G14 — Stewardship charter

**Pass condition:** Every steward has enumerated powers and prohibited acts, essential-service duties, review, dissolution, and authority-return rules.

## G15 — Performance non-automation

**Pass condition:** BHI, LMCI, Love Ledger, Proof of Care, certification, or other metrics cannot automatically suspend, revoke, transfer, or punish stewardship.

## G16 — Standards and permits separation

**Pass condition:** General ecological standards are distinct from actor-specific permits, quotas, licences, tariffs, bonds, and use decisions.

## G17 — Essential minimums

**Pass condition:** Water, sanitation, healthcare, disability support, emergency food production, shelter, and other survival minimums cannot be interrupted by commercial enforcement.

## G18 — Inspection authority

**Pass condition:** Entry, sampling, records access, and inspection have action-specific authority and do not arise merely from monitoring, guardianship, funding, or licence status.

## G19 — Investigation separation

**Pass condition:** Inspection, audit, observation, complaint, and guardian report do not automatically open an investigation or establish breach.

## G20 — Evidence and attribution

**Pass condition:** Provenance, uncertainty, source restrictions, independent testing, exculpatory material, and admissibility are recorded; technical attribution is not liability.

## G21 — Urgent ecological protection

**Pass condition:** Urgent action is temporary, least restrictive, independently authorized, automatically expiring, reviewed promptly, and cannot become permanent receivership or title.

## G22 — Receivership boundary

**Pass condition:** Temporary administration preserves services and assets but cannot redesign ownership, institutions, labour, or governance without ordinary process.

## G23 — Financial authority

**Pass condition:** Taxes, tariffs, bonds, freezes, fund conditions, compensation, and redistribution each have independent legal and fiscal authority.

## G24 — Listing and market restriction

**Pass condition:** Blacklists, port denial, certification loss, procurement exclusion, and market restriction require notice, evidence, time limits, delisting, worker/crew safeguards, and remedy.

## G25 — Adjudication independence

**Pass condition:** Scientific, ethical, guardian, council, framework, fund, and registry bodies cannot adjudicate their own allegations unless lawfully constituted with due process.

## G26 — SCPA enforcement interface

**Pass condition:** Search, seizure, custody, force, cyber disruption, financial coercion, mobility action, arrest, or enforcement execution requires applicable SCPA records and authority.

## G27 — Emergency interface

**Pass condition:** Ecological emergencies link to ECRC/CDEE where applicable; emergency records do not create ecological title or security authority, and CERGTA does not declare an emergency.

## G28 — Data and Indigenous knowledge

**Pass condition:** Ecological, personal, community, genetic, microbial, sacred, and Traditional Knowledge data have purpose, access, correction, non-transfer, and disposition rules.

## G29 — Technology non-authority

**Pass condition:** AI, blockchain, dashboards, digital twins, registries, satellite systems, and smart contracts support evidence and administration but cannot issue legal effects without human legal authority.

## G30 — Pilots fail closed

**Pass condition:** Pilots, BAZs-in-formation, councils, registries, sandboxes, and prototypes maintain CERGTA-54 non-activation registers.

## G31 — Appeal and interim relief

**Pass condition:** Affected persons, nations, communities, guardians, stewards, users, and claimants can obtain reasons, evidence access, counsel, suspension of irreversible harm, correction, and compensation.

## G32 — Closure and authority return

**Pass condition:** Every authority, appointment, restriction, permit, receivership, and institution ends, returns power and assets, disposes data, preserves unresolved duties without residual power, and supplies remedy.

A recognized ecosystem right is invoked against an affected nation’s lawful practice. CERGTA-09/14 requires nation-specific law, non-derogation, conflict process, and no global proxy.

## T35 — Ecological emergency and public-health evacuation

Toxic water requires evacuation and guarded access. CERGTA-42 links to ECRC/CDEE; any restraint, custody, force, or movement control requires SCPA and separate clocks.

## T36 — Institution closes with unresolved data and claims

A PHC programme dissolves. CERGTA-51/52/53 returns authority, protects data, preserves claims and restoration duties, and prohibits residual dashboard control.

# Appendix B — Access classes

- **CERGTA-PUBLIC:** public constitutional, legal, ecological, statistical, adoption-status, and non-sensitive action information.
- **CERGTA-AFFECTED:** available to affected persons, communities, service users, workers, or authorized representatives, subject to lawful redaction.
- **CERGTA-CLAIMANT:** available to title, tenure, compensation, sacredness, guardianship, permit, or other lawful claimants and their representatives.
- **CERGTA-REVIEW:** available to competent courts, tribunals, auditors, counsel, affected-nation review bodies, scientific reviewers, and ombuds functions under protected conditions.
- **CERGTA-OPERATIONAL:** purpose-limited operational access with role, time, logging, and onward-use controls.
- **CERGTA-PROTECTED:** personal, child, health, worker, commercial, litigation, source, sensitive-location, endangered-species, infrastructure, or other high-risk material.
- **CERGTA-INDIGENOUS-RESTRICTED:** information governed by the affected nation or community, including sacred, ceremonial, cultural, territorial, genealogical, ecological, and Traditional Knowledge material; external access requires nation-specific authority.

Access class does not establish admissibility, title, public-interest disclosure, retention, or authority by itself.

# Appendix J — Final constitutional statement

> **The biosphere may be a rights-holder without becoming an owner of everything within it. Communities may govern commons without acquiring unbounded sovereignty. Indigenous nations may exercise their own authority without being replaced by global proxies. Guardians may speak without owning. Stewards may manage without ruling. Scientists may warn without judging. Metrics may reveal without commanding. Courts may decide without executing. Emergency actors may protect without inheriting permanence. Every authority must be named, bounded, reviewable, correctable, and capable of ending.**
