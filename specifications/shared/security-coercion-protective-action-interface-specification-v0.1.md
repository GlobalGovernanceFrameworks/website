# Security, Coercion, and Protective Action Interface Specification v0.1

## A common constitutional interface for prevention, intelligence, investigation, urgent protection, compulsory process, custody, force, cyber action, financial coercion, mobility decisions, adjudication, remedy, and authority return

**Status:** Constitutional interface specification; drafting and conformance instrument  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Primary use:** revision of Shield, Peace, Kintsugi, Migration, and Aegis; cross-framework testing; jurisdiction-specific implementation design  
**Not a source of police, military, intelligence, border, detention, cyber, sanctions, taxation, prosecution, or judicial power**

---

# Executive summary

The Global Governance Frameworks require institutions capable of preventing violence, rescuing people from immediate danger, investigating transnational crime, defending digital systems, protecting migrants and survivors, restraining harmful assets, enforcing lawful judgments, and supporting restoration. Those functions are necessary. Their necessity does not make them one function, and protective purpose does not manufacture jurisdiction.

This Specification supplies the missing constitutional type system for the security and coercion cluster. It does not create a global security institution. It defines the interfaces through which independently constituted authorities may perform bounded protective or coercive acts without laundering authority through another framework.

Its central rules are:

> **No valid authority record, no consequential security or coercive action.**

> **Protective purpose does not create protective jurisdiction.**

> **Observation is not intelligence authority. Intelligence is not investigation. Investigation is not compulsory process. Compulsory process is not arrest or detention. Detention is not adjudication. Adjudication is not enforcement. Enforcement is not rehabilitation. None creates border, military, cyber, fiscal, or territorial authority by implication.**

> **A threat tier, developmental model, spiritual classification, migration status, community vote, supermajority, emergency label, technical capability, or cross-framework referral cannot substitute for law, individualized facts, independent authorization, rights, review, termination, and remedy.**

The Specification establishes eleven action classes, forty-four interoperable records, strict handoff rules, protected-information firewalls, cross-border and non-refoulement safeguards, a security-use reversion gate for civilian or converted capabilities, and complete termination, correction, compensation, and authority-return duties.

It is designed to support the following successors without pre-deciding their institutional form:

- Shield v2.0 as a transnational crime cooperation, mutual legal assistance, cyber defence, and victim-protection framework;
- Kintsugi v2.2 as community care, unarmed response, survivor sovereignty, and lawfully bounded urgent protection;
- Migration v1.1 as mobility protection, asylum, identity reconstruction, reception, non-refoulement, and rights-preserving status administration;
- Peace v1.8 as voluntary prevention, mediation, ceasefire support, reconciliation, and reconstruction, insulated from intelligence and enforcement;
- Aegis v1.3.3 as a civilian capability-transition framework with a security-use reversion gate.

# 1. Purpose, scope, and legal character

## 1.1 Purpose

This Specification defines the minimum constitutional interface for any GGF framework, institution, pilot, partner, contractor, local implementation, treaty body, commons institution, or emergency mechanism that proposes, requests, supports, records, reviews, or executes a protective or coercive action.

It exists to:

1. separate prevention, observation, intelligence, investigation, compulsory process, urgent protection, custody, force, cyber intervention, financial coercion, mobility action, adjudication, enforcement, rehabilitation, and evaluation;
2. make every consequential act traceable to independently valid authority;
3. prevent cross-framework referrals from becoming hidden grants of power;
4. preserve individualized legality and rights under conditions of uncertainty, urgency, transnational cooperation, and technological complexity;
5. protect mediation, health, survivor, Indigenous, spiritual, migration, welfare, labour, and community data from silent security use;
6. establish review, correction, release, restoration, compensation, data disposition, and authority-return duties;
7. provide a common conformance surface for the security and coercion cluster.

## 1.2 Scope

This Specification applies when an action may materially affect liberty, bodily integrity, privacy, home, communications, property, livelihood, movement, legal status, family unity, political participation, reputation, digital systems, territorial authority, Indigenous jurisdiction, access to essential services, or another protected interest.

It covers domestic, cross-border, treaty, commons, Indigenous-interface, cyber, maritime, aviation, financial, humanitarian, community-safety, migration, peacebuilding, and converted-capability contexts.

It applies to direct actions and to enabling acts such as intelligence sharing, target nomination, watchlisting, warrant applications, funding conditions, data fusion, technical access, asset provision, mission command, referral, and handoff.

## 1.3 Interface, not grant of power

This Specification does not itself:

- create an offence, police service, intelligence service, prosecutor, court, prison, detention centre, border authority, military force, sanctions body, cyber command, financial regulator, or enforcement task force;
- authorize surveillance, undercover activity, search, entry, interception, disclosure, seizure, arrest, restraint, detention, deportation, extradition, sanctions, asset forfeiture, weapons, force, hacking, disruption, or armed action;
- turn the Treaty, MOS, a framework, dashboard, metric, model, community decision, council vote, supermajority, professional standard, or ethical claim into law;
- create jurisdiction over a non-member, territory, person, Indigenous nation, asset, digital system, vessel, aircraft, or data source;
- waive constitutional, human-rights, humanitarian, refugee, Indigenous, labour, disability, child, survivor, privacy, due-process, or remedy obligations.

## 1.4 Non-authority clause

No institution may cite this Specification as the legal source of a coercive act. It may cite this Specification only to describe the interface, records, safeguards, and conformance duties that attach once independently valid law supplies the action-specific authority.

A document that names a competent authority without identifying the constituting instrument, jurisdiction, exact power, limits, and review remains incomplete.

## 1.5 Normative language

**SHALL** and **MUST** state mandatory conformance conditions. **SHOULD** states a rebuttable design requirement whose departure requires recorded reasons. **MAY** states a permitted option. These terms do not create legal powers; they state what an implementation must demonstrate to claim conformity with this Specification.

## 1.6 Relationship to the constitutional stack

This Specification specializes, but does not replace:

- the Constitutional Interface Specification v0.1 for legitimacy, jurisdiction, plural authority, and decision-chain separation;
- Justice Systems Framework v1.4 for adjudication, warrants, due process, evidence, appeals, remedies, and enforcement handoffs;
- Aurora Accord v1.3 for lawful basis, minimization, identity separation, government access, protected non-transfer, security data, correction, deletion, and cross-border transfer;
- Technology Governance Framework v3.6 for governed technological use, AI limits, cyber systems, circuit breakers, and technical intervention boundaries;
- Synoptic Protocol v4.2 for public communication, provenance, platform conduct, crisis information, and the prohibition of official truth or propaganda authority;
- Emergency, Catastrophic-Risk, and Continuity Interface Specification v0.1 for emergency declaration, action-specific activation, 72-hour provisional limits, renewal, termination, restoration, and compensation;
- Indigenous Sovereignty and Planetary Healing v1.1 for affected-nation authority and non-creation of Indigenous jurisdiction;
- Genesis Protocol v0.9 for pilots, first seating, non-activation, and founder/funder/vendor handoff;
- Work in Liberation v2.0 for labour rights, safe refusal, wages, bargaining, injury, and transition.

Where a conflict exists, the more rights-protective and action-specific rule governs unless a valid higher law expressly provides otherwise within non-derogable limits.

# 2. Definitions

**Affected person** means any person whose rights, liberty, status, property, data, movement, safety, livelihood, family, or reputation may be materially affected.

**Authority** means a legally constituted power held by an identifiable actor under an independently valid instrument, not merely capacity, expertise, urgency, legitimacy rhetoric, funding, ownership, coordination, or public approval.

**Consequential action** means an act reasonably capable of producing a material adverse legal, physical, economic, digital, territorial, status, or rights effect.

**Coercion** means the use or credible threat of legal, physical, economic, technological, custodial, territorial, or status power to constrain choice or compel conduct.

**Protective action** means an action aimed at preventing or ending imminent or continuing harm. Protective purpose does not determine the legal class of the method used.

**Intelligence** means purpose-limited collection, receipt, analysis, or dissemination of information for security, crime, conflict, cyber, or threat understanding outside ordinary public information.

**Investigation** means a formally mandated inquiry seeking facts relevant to a defined legal issue, offence, liability, or protective decision.

**Compulsory process** means a legally enforceable demand, entry, search, inspection, preservation, interception, disclosure, production, seizure, or restraint.

**Custody** means control over a person, asset, evidence item, system, credential, or protected material. Custody is not ownership, jurisdiction, adjudication, or disposal authority.

**Force** means physical compulsion or threatened physical compulsion, including weapons, restraints, crowd-control tools, or remotely operated effects capable of causing injury or loss of liberty.

**Security allegation** means an allegation that a person, group, asset, system, or activity presents a criminal, violent, cyber, intelligence, public-order, or national-security concern. An allegation is not a legal finding.

**Protected source** means a person, community, professional relationship, technical channel, Indigenous knowledge holder, mediator, survivor, whistleblower, journalist, lawyer, health worker, or other source entitled to confidentiality or restricted use.

**Refoulement** includes direct or indirect transfer to a place where a person faces persecution, torture, cruel treatment, serious rights violations, or another legally protected risk.

**Converted capability** means an asset, system, facility, workforce, or technology transitioned from military, intelligence, police, border, detention, offensive-cyber, or other coercive use into a civilian mission under Aegis or equivalent law.

**Security-use reversion** means the suspension of civilian-conversion status when a capability is proposed for a police, border, detention, intelligence, targeting, crowd-control, raid, offensive-cyber, armed, or other coercive function.

# 3. Governing principles

## 3.1 Action-specific legality

Every consequential action SHALL have an SCPA-01 record identifying the exact act and its independently valid legal source. General mandates such as “protect security,” “combat crime,” “prevent extremism,” “defend the system,” “restore order,” or “coordinate globally” are insufficient without an enumerated power and applicable procedural route.

## 3.2 Protective purpose does not create jurisdiction

Rescue, safety, peace, anti-trafficking, anti-corruption, anti-cybercrime, migrant protection, survivor support, ecological protection, or public-interest language cannot supply personal, territorial, subject-matter, treaty, Indigenous, border, or extraterritorial jurisdiction.

## 3.3 Severity determines urgency, not power

Severity, threat tier, financial scale, network reach, cross-border character, model confidence, or catastrophic potential may affect priority, resources, and review speed. They do not alter the required legal basis or convert one action class into another.

## 3.4 Unknown jurisdiction remains unauthorized

A gap, disagreement, failed state, non-member status, contested territory, anonymous network, stateless person, offshore asset, or digital border does not create residual GGF jurisdiction. Actors may preserve life through narrowly valid urgent authority and seek lawful cooperation; they may not fill the gap by institutional self-reference.

## 3.5 No circular delegation

Framework A cannot claim authority because Framework B “handles enforcement” when B claims authority because A classified the threat. Every handoff SHALL preserve the original legal source, identify the recipient’s independent authority, and state what does not transfer.

## 3.6 Functional separation

Observation, intelligence, investigation, warrant application, authorization, execution, prosecution, adjudication, custody, force, sanctions administration, programme funding, rehabilitation, evaluation, and public communication SHALL remain distinguishable in institutional role, record, access, and review.

One institution MAY perform more than one function only where valid law permits, structural safeguards address conflicts, and no actor decides the legality of its own coercive conduct without independent review.

## 3.7 Individualized basis and prohibited group inference

Adverse person-specific action requires individualized facts. Group membership, association, nationality, migration route, geography, political belief, religion, spiritual practice, developmental-stage classification, cultural profile, race, caste, disability, poverty, homelessness, prior victimization, refusal to mediate, or algorithmic similarity cannot establish guilt, dangerousness, detention need, sanction eligibility, or immigration exclusion.

Network and systemic evidence MAY support investigation when valid law allows, but the inferential bridge to each affected person or asset SHALL be recorded and contestable.

## 3.8 Presumption against hidden use migration

Information collected for mediation, peacebuilding, health, welfare, survivor support, Indigenous governance, labour support, migration services, humanitarian aid, education, research, or community safety SHALL NOT migrate into intelligence, investigation, immigration enforcement, employment discipline, benefit decisions, or public watchlists without a separately valid legal basis, necessity assessment, notice or lawful exception, and protected-source analysis.

## 3.9 Least restrictive effective measure

Compulsory action SHALL use the least rights-restrictive measure reasonably capable of achieving the lawful objective. Availability of a faster, cheaper, more automated, more scalable, or more secret method does not satisfy the requirement.

## 3.10 Reversibility and innocent-party continuity

Temporary, reversible, targeted measures are preferred where effective. Actions affecting communications, finances, housing, food, medicine, caregiving, wages, public services, legal representation, journalism, humanitarian work, or essential digital infrastructure SHALL include continuity and innocent-party safeguards.

## 3.11 No security by vote alone

A council, supermajority, citizen assembly, community vote, stakeholder consensus, donor condition, professional certification, or regional compact cannot authorize a coercive act unless the body is legally constituted to exercise that exact power and all action-specific procedures are satisfied.

## 3.12 No model-to-power conversion

AI, behavioural profiling, Spiral Dynamics, spiritual-driver assessments, cultural-alignment algorithms, threat tiers, sentiment analysis, anomaly scores, risk forecasts, biometric matching, network graphs, or predictive systems may support bounded analysis. They cannot determine legal status, individual responsibility, credibility, guilt, detention, force, sanctions, immigration consequences, or sentence.

## 3.13 Protected non-transfer

Lawful security needs do not create a general override of Indigenous-restricted knowledge, mediation confidentiality, survivor choice, legal privilege, medical confidentiality, journalistic sources, whistleblower protection, or humanitarian confidentiality. Any exception requires specific law, strict necessity, independent authorization where applicable, minimization, safety planning, and remedy.

## 3.14 Assistance is not enforcement

Providing shelter, transport, logistics, medical aid, legal aid, cybersecurity assistance, mediation, or converted civilian capability does not authorize intelligence collection, identity expansion, border control, detention, search, seizure, targeting, or unrelated evidence gathering.

## 3.15 Complete lifecycle and remedy

Every consequential action SHALL state initiation, review, expiry, termination, authority return, release or handback, record correction, data disposition, public correction where needed, liability, and remedy. Persistent risk is assigned to a custodian; it does not silently preserve expired power.

# 4. Constitutional topology and prohibited authority laundering

## 4.1 No unified security sovereign

The cluster SHALL NOT presume a single Transnational Security Council, intelligence centre, enforcement task force, peace council, community safety council, tribunal, migration authority, emergency body, or Aegis institution competent across all security functions.

Cooperation may be federated and polycentric. A shared event identifier, coordination forum, intelligence standard, or mutual-assistance network is not a command hierarchy.

## 4.2 Required institutional distinctions

At minimum, implementations SHALL distinguish:

- voluntary support and mediation;
- public observation and warning;
- protected intelligence collection and analysis;
- investigation;
- application for compulsory process;
- independent authorization;
- execution of searches, seizures, arrests, or cyber actions;
- custody and welfare;
- prosecution or public enforcement case presentation;
- adjudication and appeal;
- sanctions administration and asset management;
- restorative, correctional, rehabilitation, and reintegration services;
- independent misconduct investigation and remedy;
- evaluation, research, and public reporting.

## 4.3 Referral is not transfer of power

A referral communicates information or requests another actor to consider acting under its own law. It does not:

- compel the recipient to accept;
- transfer jurisdiction;
- preserve the sender’s authority after handoff;
- convert intelligence into evidence;
- convert an operational attribution into a legal finding;
- authorize the sender to direct the recipient’s decision;
- authorize the recipient to reuse protected information outside stated limits.

Every consequential handoff SHALL record offered function, legal basis, recipient authority, accepted scope, rejected or deficient elements, concurrent duties, custody, use limits, return/destruction, and closure.

## 4.4 Peace-to-security firewall

Peace and mediation institutions MAY report immediate danger with the minimum necessary information and MAY comply with narrowly defined legal reporting duties. They SHALL NOT conduct covert intelligence, target nomination, developmental threat classification, political surveillance, immigration screening, or case-specific enforcement direction.

Mediation content, spiritual or religious disclosures, values mapping, trauma narratives, ceasefire communications, and reconciliation participation remain confidential except under specific lawful exceptions that are disclosed in advance where safety permits.

## 4.5 Community-safety boundary

Community safety institutions MAY provide unarmed response, de-escalation, accompaniment, crisis sanctuary, survivor support, and voluntary restorative processes. A community council’s legitimacy does not create arrest, detention, search, weapons, force, evidence, prosecution, or tribunal authority.

Urgent physical intervention requires Class D or F authority and records. A severe-harm response unit must be constituted under applicable law, externally reviewable, and prohibited from determining its own legality.

## 4.6 Migration-security firewall

Migration protection, asylum, identity reconstruction, family reunification, reception, shelter, legal aid, and essential services remain separate from criminal intelligence and enforcement.

Security allegations in migration proceedings require SCPA-30. Secret information may be handled through lawful protected procedures, but an affected person must receive the substance necessary for an effective challenge unless an independent body finds a strictly necessary limitation and supplies an equivalent protective mechanism.

## 4.7 Aegis security-use reversion gate

An Aegis or equivalent converted capability loses civilian-conversion status for the proposed use when requested for:

- police or border surveillance;
- identity screening or watchlisting;
- detention, deportation, extradition, prisoner transfer, or guarded custody;
- raids, searches, arrests, seizures, crowd control, or evidence collection;
- intelligence collection, targeting, undercover work, or offensive cyber action;
- weapons support, armed protection, military command, or coercive sanctions enforcement.

Before such use, the capability SHALL enter the SCPA-44 register, technical and organizational civilian assurances SHALL be suspended, and the requesting authority SHALL independently satisfy every applicable Class E, F, G, H, or K requirement. No Aegis mission charter can bridge the gap.

# 5. Action classes and transition rules

| **Class A** | Voluntary assistance and prevention | Mediation, shelter, accompaniment, legal aid, safety planning, cyber hygiene, voluntary support, capability mapping. | No coercive consequence; meaningful consent, refusal, and exit. |
| **Class B** | Observation, warning, and protected intelligence | Public alerts, confidential reports, source intake, pattern analysis, threat estimates, strategic intelligence. | No investigation, search, seizure, detention, sanction, immigration consequence, or command merely from observation. |
| **Class C** | Investigation | Case opening, interviews, open-source research, forensic examination under lawful custody, non-compulsory inquiries. | Requires a competent investigative mandate; compulsory methods require Class E or G authority. |
| **Class D** | Urgent protective action | Immediate separation, rescue, evacuation, protective shelter, emergency network isolation, preservation of life or evidence. | Temporary, least restrictive, action-specific, documented, promptly reviewed; not a general enforcement mandate. |
| **Class E** | Compulsory legal process | Search, entry, inspection, disclosure, preservation, interception, evidence seizure, asset restraint, subpoenas and production orders. | Specific legal authority and independent authorization where required. |
| **Class F** | Custody and physical force | Arrest, restraint, detention, transfer, armed protection, weapons, crowd-control measures. | Highest safeguards; individualized basis; medical duties; command responsibility; prompt independent review and remedy. |
| **Class G** | Cyber intervention | Defensive action on controlled systems, third-party access, disruption, counter-operation, evidence preservation, restoration. | Defensive and third-party actions are separate; foreign-system or third-party disruption needs specific authority. |
| **Class H** | Financial coercion | Freeze, sanctions listing, procurement exclusion, evidentiary hold, forfeiture, restitution, tax, asset return. | Each financial act remains legally distinct; no programme may self-fund from unadjudicated seizures. |
| **Class I** | Adjudication and remedy | Warrants, judicial review, liability, sanctions review, appeals, restitution, compensation, correction. | Independent from intelligence, investigation, command, enforcement, funding, and evaluation. |
| **Class J** | Restoration, rehabilitation, correction, and reintegration | Restorative justice, treatment, education, re-entry, accountability plans, survivor-selected repair. | Voluntary unless lawfully ordered after adjudication; no forced confession, spirituality, labour, forgiveness, or service. |
| **Class K** | Mobility, identity, and territorial action | Service access, identity reconstruction, asylum, temporary protection, residence, travel documents, admission, relocation, expulsion, extradition, return, rematriation. | Each status and movement act requires separate law, procedure, rights, review, and non-refoulement safeguards. |

## 5.1 Classification by method, not benevolent objective

An action is classified by what it does to rights and systems, not by the actor’s stated purpose. A “rescue” involving forced entry is Class D plus E. A “protective hold” restricting liberty is Class F. A “cyber defence” that disables a third-party server is Class G third-party disruption. “Victim restitution” funded by unadjudicated seizure is Class H and I. “Humanitarian relocation” imposed without lawful status and consent may be Class K and F.

## 5.2 No upward inference

A valid lower-class action does not imply a higher-class power. Class B information may support a Class C opening decision, but Class C requires its own mandate. Class C facts may support a Class E application, but only an independent authority may issue the order. Class E search may reveal grounds for Class F arrest, but the arrest requires its own power and record unless the law expressly and narrowly integrates them.

## 5.3 Composite actions

A mission containing multiple classes SHALL identify each component and satisfy each component’s legal and record requirements. One broad “operation order” is insufficient where the mission includes assistance, intelligence, search, custody, force, cyber disruption, sanctions, and public communication.

## 5.4 Reclassification

When facts or methods change, the action SHALL be reclassified before the higher consequence occurs, except where immediate protection of life makes prior documentation impossible. In that case, the actor may perform only the narrow act permitted by valid urgent authority and SHALL complete records and obtain prompt review.

# 6. Sources of authority, jurisdiction, standing, and applicable law

## 6.1 Valid sources

Authority may arise from a constitution, statute, regulation, treaty in force, Indigenous law, judicial order, administrative order, compact, contract, consent, property or system control, professional duty, or emergency instrument only to the extent that the source is valid, applicable, and competent for the exact act.

A framework, model law, protocol, standard, memorandum, dashboard, ethics code, technical specification, manifesto, or funding agreement is not independently coercive unless adopted by a competent authority through a valid legal act.

## 6.2 Operation-specific authority

Authority SHALL be mapped separately for collection, analysis, sharing, investigation, search, seizure, arrest, detention, force, cyber action, sanctions, immigration status, transfer, adjudication, enforcement, rehabilitation, and data retention.

An institution may possess one power and lack another. Lawful custody of evidence does not create authority to publish it. Ownership of an aircraft does not create territorial flight or mission-command authority. Control of a network does not create authority to investigate its users for unrelated offences.

## 6.3 Standing and affected parties

Affected persons, legal representatives, survivors, children through independent representation, detained people, migrants, workers, Indigenous nations, communities, asset owners, service providers, humanitarian actors, journalists, and others materially affected SHALL have routes to raise jurisdiction, rights, privilege, safety, proportionality, evidence, status, and remedy claims.

## 6.4 Indigenous authority

Indigenous authority is inherent or arises under the affected nation’s own law and valid relationships; it is not created by GGF recognition. No global Earth Council, generic elder verification, universal Indigenous data council, or external representative may substitute for the affected nation.

Cross-jurisdiction action affecting Indigenous territory or knowledge requires SCPA-39 and the applicable affected-nation interface. Internal plurality and individual rights SHALL be protected.

## 6.5 Non-member and cross-border limits

Non-members do not acquire duties merely because a GGF framework classifies conduct as harmful. Cross-border action requires consent, treaty, mutual legal assistance, valid extraterritorial jurisdiction, host authority, or another lawful basis. Foreign personnel may not directly exercise powers merely because they participate in a joint team.

## 6.6 Contested jurisdiction

Where jurisdiction is contested, actors SHALL record the dispute, preserve life and evidence through lawful narrow means, seek independent determination, and avoid irreversible action where possible. A coordination body may convene; it may not decide jurisdiction unless legally empowered to do so.

# 7. Common authority and lifecycle architecture

## 7.1 Required action bundle

Before a consequential action proceeds, the implementation SHALL assemble, as applicable:

1. SCPA-01 Authority Provenance;
2. SCPA-02 Standing and Jurisdiction Map;
3. SCPA-03 Action Classification;
4. SCPA-04 Individualized Factual Basis;
5. SCPA-05 Prohibited Inference Record where analytics contribute;
6. SCPA-06 Necessity and Alternatives;
7. SCPA-07 Rights and Accessibility Safeguards;
8. SCPA-08 Notice and Challenge;
9. the action-specific record or order;
10. SCPA-31 Independent Authorization or Review where required;
11. SCPA-36 Termination and Authority Return;
12. SCPA-33 Remedy where harm or wrong occurs;
13. SCPA-35 Data Disposition at closure.

An implementation MAY combine records operationally only where all fields, roles, access controls, and legal distinctions remain explicit. Combining records cannot combine authorities.

## 7.2 Common record states

Records SHALL use at least: `draft`, `submitted`, `authorized`, `partially_authorized`, `rejected`, `active`, `suspended`, `expired`, `terminated`, `under_review`, `corrected`, `superseded`, and `archived`.

Receipt is not acceptance. Submission is not authorization. Operational completion is not legal closure. Archive is not indefinite use permission.

## 7.3 Public and protected layers

Each action SHALL have a public, claimant-accessible, or review-accessible layer sufficient to reveal legal basis, authority, type of action, general reasons, duration, review, and remedy, subject to lawful protection of sources, methods, children, survivors, medical data, Indigenous-restricted knowledge, ongoing operations, and third parties.

Secret law, secret general powers, and unreviewable secret evidence are non-conforming. Particular operational facts may be protected under law, but a competent independent body must be able to review them.

## 7.4 Fail-closed conditions

Action SHALL NOT proceed, or SHALL pause where safe, when:

- the legal source or competent authority is missing;
- jurisdiction is unassigned;
- the action class is unclear and a higher class is plausible;
- individualized facts are absent for person-specific adverse action;
- protected-source restrictions are unresolved;
- independent authorization is required but missing;
- command, custody, or stop authority is ambiguous;
- the action has expired;
- the rights or review route is unavailable;
- a converted capability has not passed the security-use reversion gate.

# 8. Observation, warning, intelligence, and protected sources

## 8.1 Public observation and warning

Public warnings may describe verified conditions, uncertainty, protective steps, and responsible authorities. They SHALL distinguish allegation, estimate, and legal finding. They SHALL avoid stigmatizing populations, religions, migrants, neighbourhoods, developmental stages, or political groups.

## 8.2 Intelligence authority

Protected intelligence collection requires specific authority, purpose, subject matter, method, minimization, source safeguards, retention, dissemination, audit, and correction. “Mandatory cooperation” or “no safe havens” language cannot create unlimited sharing.

Intelligence fusion bodies MAY standardize formats and identify conflicts. They SHALL NOT authorize operations, adjudicate reliability, direct prosecutors, impose sanctions, or create foreign jurisdiction merely by integrating information.

## 8.3 Human and community sources

Source safety, informed expectations, coercion risk, compensation, conflicts, immigration consequences, criminal exposure, and exit SHALL be addressed. A person seeking help, asylum, health care, mediation, or survivor support is not presumptively an intelligence source.

## 8.4 Developmental, cultural, and spiritual models

Developmental, cultural, religious, and spiritual frameworks MAY inform voluntary communication or self-reflection. They SHALL NOT be used to profile dangerousness, select targets, allocate surveillance, infer ideology, assess credibility, recommend coercion, or determine rehabilitation.

## 8.5 Watchlists and alerts

Watchlists and operational alerts require defined criteria, individualized or lawfully scoped basis, source quality, expiry, review, correction, notification where possible, anti-discrimination testing, and removal. Inclusion cannot automatically trigger detention, border denial, sanctions, employment action, or public accusation.

# 9. Investigation and compulsory process

## 9.1 Investigation opening

A formal investigation requires SCPA-11. The opening threshold SHALL be defined by law and be more than disagreement, dissent, unusual behaviour, migration status, refusal to participate, anonymous accusation without assessment, or model anomaly.

Investigators SHALL seek exculpatory and alternative explanations, not merely confirmation. Case scope, subject, methods, review date, closure standard, and data disposition SHALL be defined.

## 9.2 Interviews and voluntary cooperation

A request for voluntary information SHALL state that it is voluntary, explain the purpose and potential uses, avoid deception about legal duty, provide accessibility and interpretation, and protect vulnerable people. Where law permits deception in an investigation, the method requires specific authorization and cannot fabricate legal powers, threaten unavailable consequences, exploit disability, or override survivor choice.

## 9.3 Undercover and infiltrative methods

Undercover operations, controlled deliveries, online personas, informant direction, network infiltration, and sting operations require method-specific authority, necessity, target limits, entrapment safeguards, protected-relationship limits, supervision, evidence rules, and closure.

Private contractors, ethical hackers, NGOs, or community actors do not acquire undercover or enforcement authority by partnership.

## 9.4 Search, entry, inspection, and disclosure

Compulsory access requires Class E authority. Warrants and orders SHALL identify place or system, target, object, scope, time, manner, privileges, minimization, execution limits, inventory, return, and challenge.

General warrants, bulk access untethered to lawful purpose, indiscriminate copying, and “irrefutable blockchain evidence” claims are non-conforming.

## 9.5 Evidence and assets

Evidence seizure and asset action SHALL remain distinct. Property may be evidence without being forfeitable. Funds may be restrained without being available for programme finance. Asset recovery cannot fund the investigator, prosecutor, adjudicator, or enforcement body in a manner that creates a seizure incentive.

# 10. Urgent protective action

## 10.1 Purpose and threshold

Class D permits narrow temporary action where there are reasonable grounds to believe imminent or continuing serious harm requires intervention before ordinary authorization can be obtained.

The actor SHALL identify the threatened interest, immediacy, factual basis, least restrictive option, authority, and maximum duration. The action ends when the immediate condition ends or the lawful limit expires.

## 10.2 Permitted forms

Depending on applicable law, urgent protection may include:

- separating persons during immediate violence;
- evacuation or rescue;
- emergency shelter and medical aid;
- temporary preservation of a scene or evidence;
- temporary isolation of a system under lawful control;
- immediate steps to prevent dissipation of an asset pending prompt independent review;
- temporary contact or proximity restrictions where legally available.

## 10.3 Prohibited expansion

Urgent protection does not by itself authorize interrogation, broad search, unrelated evidence gathering, prolonged detention, deportation, asset forfeiture, punitive sanctions, offensive cyber action, military command, or forced restorative participation.

## 10.4 Review clock

Every urgent action SHALL state a fixed expiry. No interface-level provisional authorization may exceed seventy-two hours without a separately valid continued authorization, and applicable law may require much shorter review. Arrest and detention SHALL receive prompt independent review under the stricter applicable standard; the seventy-two-hour outer interface limit is never a permission to delay review.

# 11. Arrest, restraint, custody, detention, release, and transfer

## 11.1 Arrest and restraint

Arrest and physical restraint require specific legal authority and individualized grounds. Officers SHALL identify themselves where safe, state the reason, use de-escalation and the least force reasonably necessary, protect health, record property, notify rights, and enable counsel and communication.

## 11.2 Community safety

Unarmed community responders may use consensual de-escalation and immediate defensive action permitted to any person under applicable law. They do not obtain arrest, detention, search, or interrogation powers from Kintsugi, a Community Safety Council, a Harm Escalation Protocol, or community trust.

A Severe Harm Response Unit or equivalent must be separately constituted. Its relationship to police, health, emergency, and justice authorities must be explicit.

## 11.3 Detention

Any confinement or guarded custody is Class F regardless of the label “protective,” “administrative,” “reception,” “shelter,” “stabilization,” “processing,” “rehabilitation,” or “community supervision.”

Detention requires law, necessity, individualized reasons, alternatives, independent review, counsel, humane conditions, medical care, communication, complaint, records, and release execution.

## 11.4 Immigration detention

Migration status, missing documents, identity uncertainty, asylum claim, irregular entry, poverty, or inability to secure housing does not itself justify detention. Where applicable law permits immigration detention, it remains exceptional, individually necessary, non-punitive, time-bounded, reviewed, and subject to alternatives, child and family protections, and non-refoulement.

## 11.5 Transfer and extradition

No transfer occurs without destination-specific authority and safeguards. Receiving assurances SHALL be assessed rather than merely recited. The transferring authority retains duties where it knew or should have known of torture, persecution, disappearance, arbitrary detention, denial of fair trial, trafficking, severe health risk, or other protected harm.

---

# 12. Force, weapons, command, and medical duties

## 12.1 Force threshold

Force must be lawful, necessary for an immediate legitimate objective, proportionate to the threat, and stopped when the objective is achieved or the need ends. Force may not punish, compel confession, disperse lawful dissent merely because it is disruptive, enforce cultural conformity, or protect institutional reputation.

## 12.2 Deadly force

Deadly force, or force carrying a substantial risk of death or serious injury, requires the highest legal threshold and may only be used where valid law permits and strictly to protect life from an imminent threat. Property, escape alone, general dangerousness, threat tier, or mission success is insufficient.

## 12.3 Weapons and equipment

No framework may declare a device “non-lethal” and thereby reduce safeguards. Chemical agents, kinetic projectiles, electrical devices, restraints, drones, acoustic tools, biometric systems, and remotely operated platforms require risk-specific authorization, training, medical protocols, reporting, and review.

## 12.4 Command separation

Every mission SHALL identify:

- the authority setting lawful objectives;
- operational command;
- technical and safety command;
- evidence and asset custody;
- data controller;
- medical authority;
- who may refuse or stop unsafe or unlawful action;
- independent review and misconduct jurisdiction.

A safety stop does not create mission command. Mission command does not override professional medical, legal, or technical duties.

## 12.5 Medical care and death or injury

Actors owe prompt medical care to injured persons, detainees, responders, and bystanders. Death, serious injury, disappearance, sexual harm, torture allegation, or major equipment failure triggers immediate preservation, notification, independent investigation, family and representative access, and remedy processes.

# 13. Cyber defence, disruption, and digital evidence

## 13.1 Defensive action on controlled systems

Owners and lawful operators may isolate, patch, filter, revoke credentials, restore backups, and take other proportionate defensive action on systems within their authority. They SHALL protect continuity, users, evidence, privacy, and third-party systems.

## 13.2 Third-party systems

Accessing, altering, disabling, redirecting, infiltrating, deceiving, or taking control of a third-party system is not ordinary defence. It requires SCPA-27 and applicable domestic, cross-border, criminal-procedure, sovereignty, armed-conflict, and human-rights authority.

“Neutralizing ransomware infrastructure,” “taking down a dark market,” or “countering an APT” does not reduce the requirement.

## 13.3 Attribution

Cyber attribution SHALL state confidence, evidence classes, alternative explanations, spoofing or false-flag risk, infrastructure ownership, operator identity uncertainty, state-control inference, and prohibited conclusions. Technical attribution is not legal attribution.

No offensive or third-party cyber action may be authorized solely by an AI attribution result, private-sector claim, intelligence fusion output, or internal supermajority.

## 13.4 Digital evidence

Digital evidence requires reproducible acquisition, integrity verification, tooling disclosure, error rates, chain of custody, explainability sufficient for challenge, and protection of unrelated data. Blockchain or cryptographic logging may support integrity but cannot make evidence “irrefutable.”

## 13.5 Service restoration and vulnerability handling

Cyber action SHALL include service restoration, vulnerability disclosure or retention rules, downstream notification, credential reset, victim support, and termination. Retaining a vulnerability for intelligence or operational use requires specific authority, risk assessment, independent oversight, and expiry.

# 14. Financial coercion, sanctions, asset recovery, and victim restitution

## 14.1 Distinct legal acts

The following are distinct and SHALL NOT be merged:

- evidentiary preservation;
- provisional asset restraint;
- sanctions listing;
- regulatory restriction;
- procurement exclusion;
- adjudicated forfeiture;
- tax or levy;
- victim restitution;
- property return;
- programme finance.

## 14.2 Sanctions

Sanctions require valid law, competent authority, defined target and conduct, evidence, notice or lawful exception, humanitarian and innocent-party safeguards, reasons, duration, review, delisting, correction, and remedy.

Non-participation, opt-out use, refusal to share data beyond law, political disagreement, or failure to adopt a GGF framework is not a sanctions basis.

## 14.3 Asset recovery

Recovered assets remain subject to ownership claims, victim priorities, creditor and innocent-party rights, evidentiary needs, appeal, and final adjudication. They SHALL NOT become operational budgets before lawful disposition.

Investigators, enforcers, prosecutors, adjudicators, and programme managers SHALL not receive incentives tied to volume of seizures, forfeitures, sanctions listings, or recovered value.

## 14.4 Essential continuity

Freezes and restrictions SHALL protect food, housing, health, wages, legal fees, humanitarian operations, dependent persons, public services, and lawful third-party transactions where law permits. Broad de-risking and automatic exclusion are non-conforming.

# 15. Cross-border cooperation, extradition, deportation, asylum, and non-refoulement

## 15.1 Mutual legal assistance

Cross-border assistance requires a defined request, competent authorities, legal basis, exact act, purpose, speciality, rights, data limits, refusal grounds, costs, execution authority, return or destruction, and remedy.

Joint teams remain subject to host law. Foreign participants do not carry home-state powers into another territory by agreement among agencies alone.

## 15.2 Extradition and criminal transfer

Extradition requires applicable law or treaty, identity, offence, evidentiary threshold, dual criminality where required, speciality, political-offence and discrimination safeguards, fair-trial and treatment assessment, counsel, reasons, judicial review, and appeal.

## 15.3 Deportation, expulsion, and removal

Removal is not an intelligence or law-enforcement shortcut. It requires migration law, status determination, individual notice and reasons, counsel, evidence access sufficient for challenge, family and child assessment, health and disability safeguards, non-refoulement, appeal, and safe execution.

A criminal allegation that cannot support prosecution does not automatically support expulsion. Nor may immigration action be used to evade criminal-procedure protections.

## 15.4 Protection and essential services

Access to emergency care, shelter, food, legal aid, education, survivor services, child protection, and other essential support SHALL not depend on biometric enrolment, universal digital identity, intelligence consent, cooperation with prosecution, or waiver of asylum rights.

Identity reconstruction and provisional standing should use the minimum information necessary and support low-documentation, offline, and community-attested routes where lawful.

## 15.5 Corridors, protected zones, and relocation

A humanitarian corridor, sanctuary arrangement, protected mobility zone, evacuation route, camp, reception area, or relocation programme requires host and territorial authority, affected-community participation, Indigenous authority where applicable, civilian character, non-discrimination, service continuity, data limits, exit, review, and protection from militarization or detention by another name.

---

# 16. Adjudication, enforcement, appeal, and remedy

## 16.1 Independent adjudication

Adjudicators SHALL be independent from intelligence collectors, investigators, operational commanders, arresting officers, asset managers, sanctions administrators, programme funders, mediators, and evaluators in the matter.

Internal security-council approval is not a warrant, judgment, appeal, or remedy.

## 16.2 Evidence and disclosure

Affected persons SHALL receive sufficient evidence and reasons for an effective challenge, subject to lawful protection of sources and methods. Where disclosure is limited, an independent body SHALL assess necessity and provide a protective procedure that preserves meaningful contestability.

Exculpatory and materially inconsistent information SHALL be preserved and disclosed under applicable law.

## 16.3 Enforcement of orders

Enforcers execute lawful orders and may not expand them. A judgment does not authorize foreign enforcement, armed deployment, or unrelated data access. A sanctions listing does not authorize seizure unless law separately provides it. A detention order does not authorize interrogation or involuntary treatment.

## 16.4 Appeals and urgent relief

Urgent review SHALL be available for detention, force, search, seizure, asset restraint, secrecy, protected sources, cyber disruption, sanctions, status loss, transfer, deportation, extradition, and non-refoulement.

The challenge mechanism SHALL be accessible to people in custody, outside the territory, with disabilities, without documents, without funds, or facing language barriers.

## 16.5 Wrongful action

Law and implementation SHALL provide release, cessation, correction, expungement, property and credential return, data recall, watchlist removal, compensation, restitution, medical and psychosocial care, apology where appropriate, misconduct investigation, institutional correction, and guarantees of non-repetition.

---

# 17. Restoration, rehabilitation, correctional action, and reintegration

## 17.1 Separation from guilt and coercion

Restorative, rehabilitative, correctional, educational, therapeutic, spiritual, or service programmes do not determine guilt and cannot be imposed by investigators, peacebuilders, intelligence bodies, asset-recovery programmes, or community councils without valid law and adjudication where coercive.

## 17.2 Survivor sovereignty

Survivor support is available independent of prosecution, testimony, immigration cooperation, restorative participation, reconciliation, or forgiveness. Survivors control contact and restorative pathways subject to third-party safety and valid law. Withdrawal does not remove material support or create adverse inference.

## 17.3 Prohibited content

No programme may require:

- developmental-stage classification;
- spiritual or contemplative practice;
- ideological conversion;
- confession beyond lawful process;
- forgiveness, apology, reconciliation, or relationship restoration;
- unpaid or underpaid labour;
- military, police, disaster, or community service as proof of worth;
- surrender of non-waivable rights;
- public shaming or Love Ledger reputation consequences.

## 17.4 Reintegration and records

Reintegration requires housing, health, livelihood, education, family, community safety, identity, and record-correction support. Completion metrics SHALL not become automated eligibility scores or public reputational rankings.

---

# 18. Data governance, identity, secrecy, and public communication

## 18.1 Purpose separation

Security, justice, migration, welfare, health, mediation, survivor, Indigenous, labour, and public-communication records SHALL use separated access, retention, and legal bases. A shared technical platform does not make purposes compatible.

## 18.2 Identity

No universal GGF identity, biometric registry, blockchain identity, Love Ledger enrolment, or global mobility passport is required by this Specification. Identity collection SHALL be function-specific, minimal, correctable, accessible offline, and separated from unrelated security or benefit systems.

## 18.3 Government and security access

Government access to data requires SCPA-01, SCPA-02, and the relevant compulsory-process or intelligence records. Emergency or public-interest claims do not create open-ended access.

## 18.4 Secrecy

Secrecy SHALL be specific, reviewable, time-bounded, and no broader than necessary. Classification cannot conceal illegality, rights violations, institutional embarrassment, environmental harm, or misconduct. Oversight and adjudicative bodies require protected access adequate to review legality.

## 18.5 Public communication

Security-facing public communication SHALL distinguish verified facts, allegations, estimates, operational needs, and legal findings. It SHALL include correction and expiry mechanisms and avoid propaganda, developmental persuasion, political narrative control, collective blame, or public punishment by accusation.

---

# 19. Special safeguards

## 19.1 Children and dependent persons

Best interests, family unity, independent representation, age-appropriate notice, disability support, education, health, placement review, and protection from detention apply. Children SHALL not be used as intelligence leverage, translation substitutes, or pressure on family members.

## 19.2 Disability and mental distress

Disability, neurodivergence, communication difference, psychosocial distress, or crisis behaviour is not dangerousness. Responders SHALL use accessible communication, supported decision-making, sensory and medical accommodations, and non-carceral alternatives. Involuntary health intervention requires its own law and safeguards.

## 19.3 Survivors and trafficked persons

Rescue SHALL not become detention, compulsory testimony, immigration punishment, or forced return. Support, safe housing, legal advice, health care, identity repair, family contact, and compensation remain distinct from criminal cooperation.

## 19.4 Indigenous nations

Territorial access, surveillance, data collection, evidence gathering, policing, military presence, border operation, resource seizure, and protected-knowledge requests require affected-nation analysis. Emergency rescue may proceed only under valid narrow authority and must not create continuing jurisdiction.

## 19.5 Workers and operators

Security personnel, community responders, cyber operators, interpreters, medical staff, contractors, converted-capability workers, and whistleblowers retain wages, safe staffing, bargaining, fatigue limits, professional autonomy, safe refusal, injury support, and complaint rights. Private employment does not reduce public-power safeguards.

## 19.6 Humanitarian and journalistic actors

Humanitarian, legal, medical, religious, peacebuilding, and journalistic roles require functional protection. Assistance, presence, contact, or confidential communication with a suspect group does not establish complicity without individualized evidence.

# 20. Emergency overlay

## 20.1 Controlling interface

Where a security or coercive action occurs during a declared or de facto emergency, the Emergency, Catastrophic-Risk, and Continuity Interface Specification v0.1 controls declaration, activation, command, review, renewal, termination, restoration, and compensation.

This Specification controls the action class and security-specific safeguards. Emergency activation does not merge powers.

## 20.2 Provisional action

A lawful provisional action MAY compress prior process where delay would create serious harm. It SHALL remain narrow, reversible where possible, documented promptly, independently reviewed, and subject to a fixed expiry no later than seventy-two hours. Detention, force, interception, cyber disruption, sanctions, and transfer remain subject to their stricter action-specific law.

## 20.3 Prohibited emergency shortcuts

Emergency labels SHALL NOT authorize:

- general political surveillance;
- detention without review;
- torture, disappearance, collective punishment, or forced confession;
- refoulement;
- propaganda or official narrative control;
- seizure or sanctions for non-participation;
- forced mediation, spirituality, rehabilitation, or labour;
- automatic transfer of Aegis capabilities into security use;
- indefinite retention or secret renewal.

## 20.4 After-action duties

Every emergency action requires termination, authority return, release or handback, data disposition, public correction, compensation for wrongful action, worker care, independent review, and a preserved dissent and uncertainty record.

# 21. Cross-framework conformance requirements

## 21.1 Shield successor

A Shield successor SHALL:

- narrow itself to cooperation, mutual legal assistance, cyber defence, victim protection, and separately constituted operational support;
- abolish any presumption that one council can define offences, classify threats, approve operations, command enforcement, impose sanctions, fund itself from recoveries, and oversee itself;
- replace mandatory global intelligence sharing with lawful, purpose-limited, reciprocal requests and protected-source rules;
- separate intelligence, investigation, cyber action, asset action, arrest, detention, prosecution, adjudication, and victim restitution;
- prohibit developmental, cultural, religious, political, and migration profiling as coercive predicates;
- adopt SCPA-01 through SCPA-44 as applicable.

## 21.2 Peace successor

A Peace successor SHALL:

- limit itself to voluntary prevention, mediation, ceasefire support, reconciliation, reconstruction, and non-coercive public advice;
- remove threat, developmental, spiritual, or values classification from security and justice consequences;
- protect mediation and spiritual disclosures from intelligence use;
- separate immediate-danger reporting from investigation referral;
- ensure refusal, failed mediation, or disagreement does not create adverse legal inference.

## 21.3 Kintsugi successor

A Kintsugi successor SHALL:

- preserve unarmed first response, survivor sovereignty, crisis sanctuary, support, and voluntary restoration;
- define the precise legal boundary of urgent intervention;
- place any SHRU or equivalent under separately constituted law, external review, force limits, medical duties, evidence rules, and remedy;
- prevent Community Safety Councils or Healing Justice bodies from combining administration, investigation, force, adjudication, and discipline;
- preserve support when survivors refuse reporting or restoration.

## 21.4 Migration successor

A Migration successor SHALL:

- distinguish service access, identity reconstruction, asylum, temporary protection, residence, travel documents, admission, relocation, detention, expulsion, extradition, return, and rematriation;
- remove automatic legal status or territorial consequence from model thresholds unless valid law independently creates it;
- reject compulsory biometric or Love Ledger enrolment for essential protection;
- establish non-refoulement, counsel, evidence, reasons, appeal, family, child, disability, detention-alternative, and status-continuity rules;
- firewall humanitarian and migration data from security systems.

## 21.5 Aegis conformance patch

Aegis SHALL adopt the SCPA-44 security-use reversion gate and map any request involving surveillance, border, detention, intelligence, targeting, raids, crowd control, offensive cyber, weapons, armed protection, or sanctions enforcement to this Specification before the capability is offered or deployed.

## 21.6 Justice interface

Justice Systems Framework v1.4 remains controlling for adjudication, warrants, evidence, interim orders, appeals, remedies, judgment recognition, and enforcement handoffs. This Specification adds security-action typing and records; it does not create a parallel tribunal.

# 22. Implementation, pilots, first seating, and non-activation

## 22.1 Permissible pre-constitutional work

Before lawful coercive institutions exist, actors MAY conduct:

- synthetic-data tabletop exercises;
- voluntary mediation and support;
- cyber hygiene on systems under their control;
- legal-aid and rights education;
- non-identifying capability mapping;
- policy drafting, training, and independent research;
- civilian Aegis conversion under existing exclusions.

## 22.2 Prohibited pilot actions

A pilot SHALL NOT:

- conduct real-person intelligence fusion or threat scoring;
- create watchlists with adverse consequences;
- search, intercept, seize, arrest, detain, deport, extradite, sanction, freeze, forfeit, or use force;
- undertake third-party cyber access or disruption;
- compel identity or biometric enrolment;
- create protected zones, corridors, tribunals, police units, or enforcement teams by framework declaration;
- use mediation, welfare, health, migration, or survivor data for security testing without valid law and ethics;
- fund itself from seized or frozen assets.

## 22.3 First-seating gate

Before a coercive institution acts, its constituting record SHALL show:

- valid law and jurisdiction;
- enumerated powers and prohibitions;
- independent authorization and adjudication routes;
- counsel, accessibility, language, child, survivor, Indigenous, labour, and data safeguards;
- command, custody, force, cyber, asset, and cross-border rules as applicable;
- complaints, misconduct investigation, appeal, and compensation;
- budget independence and no seizure incentive;
- records, audits, termination, transfer, and dissolution;
- technical blocks against unactivated powers.

## 22.4 Version and compatibility control

Every adopting framework SHALL pin the version and hash of this Specification and the constitutional instruments it relies on. A later version does not enter force through a generic “as amended” reference where it affects powers, safeguards, records, or rights. Compatibility review is required.

# Appendix B — Access classes

- **SCPA-PUBLIC:** public constitutional, legal, statistical, and non-sensitive action information.
- **SCPA-AFFECTED:** available to the affected person or authorized representative, subject to lawful redaction.
- **SCPA-REVIEW:** available to competent independent reviewers, courts, auditors, and counsel under protected conditions.
- **SCPA-OPERATIONAL:** purpose-limited operational access with role, time, and logging controls.
- **SCPA-PROTECTED:** source-identifying, survivor, child, medical, Indigenous-restricted, privileged, classified, or high-risk material subject to heightened law and non-transfer rules.

Access class does not determine admissibility, public-interest disclosure, or retention by itself.

# Appendix C — Handoff state model

A handoff SHALL use one of:

- `offered`;
- `received_not_assessed`;
- `deficient`;
- `partially_accepted`;
- `accepted`;
- `concurrent_responsibility`;
- `rejected`;
- `withdrawn`;
- `returned`;
- `closed`.

Silence is never acceptance. Acceptance transfers only the named function and material. Sender duties survive unless valid law and the accepted record expressly reassign them.

# Appendix D — Security-use reversion checklist

Before a civilian or converted capability supports a security-facing request, record:

- requested action classes;
- former and residual military/intelligence/police functions;
- weapons, targeting, surveillance, remote access, cyber, identity, and classified interfaces;
- operator, owner, host, technical, and legal authorities;
- technical segregation and independent verification;
- proposed evidence or intelligence collection;
- border, detention, transfer, or sanctions functions;
- worker and safe-refusal protections;
- public markings and mission representation;
- reversion status;
- re-qualification or permanent decommissioning requirements.

# Appendix E — Compound conformance test suite

At minimum, adopting frameworks SHALL test:

1. trafficking rescue where the survivor refuses prosecution;
2. ransomware defence that would require foreign-server disruption;
3. mass-violence warning based partly on spiritual or developmental classification;
4. protest incorrectly labelled a systemic threat;
5. asylum claim with secret security allegations;
6. undocumented child needing services without biometric enrolment;
7. search of an Indigenous territory after an external threat report;
8. Kintsugi response to immediate severe violence;
9. wrongful asset freeze affecting wages and medicines;
10. disputed cyber attribution involving a state and private infrastructure;
11. converted Aegis drone requested for border surveillance;
12. humanitarian corridor requested in contested territory;
13. undercover operation involving a journalist or lawyer;
14. detention labelled protective stabilization;
15. sanctions proposed because a state used an opt-out;
16. mediation data requested for an intelligence watchlist;
17. urgent network isolation that affects a hospital;
18. extradition to a jurisdiction presenting torture or unfair-trial risk;
19. use of seized assets to fund the investigating body;
20. operation closure with release, property return, watchlist removal, public correction, and compensation.

A passing result must demonstrate that the right outcome can be non-escalation, refusal, partial action, or dissolution—not merely successful intervention.

# Closing commitment

A safe security architecture is not one that promises benevolent control. It is one that can distinguish help from surveillance, warning from investigation, investigation from compulsion, protection from custody, custody from judgment, defence from counter-operation, status from suspicion, and restoration from forced conformity—and that can stop, correct, return, compensate, and dissolve when it is wrong.

> **The legitimacy of protection is demonstrated not by the breadth of power it can mobilize, but by the precision with which it refuses powers it has not lawfully earned.**
