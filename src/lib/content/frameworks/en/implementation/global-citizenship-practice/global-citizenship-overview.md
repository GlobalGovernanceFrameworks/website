# Global Citizenship Practice

*Expanding identity, responsibility, and action — one choice at a time*

---

## What this is

A practice rather than a position. It has four parts:

- **Identity** — belonging to a global community *alongside* your local and cultural identities, not instead of them.
- **Ethics** — ten values worth arguing about, offered below as a starting point rather than a settled list.
- **Action** — a rough progression from noticing to leading, in five levels.
- **Systems** — supporting structural changes, such as basic income, that make participation possible for people who currently cannot afford it.

> *It isn't about getting it right. It's about direction. Most choices nudge the world one way or the other, and noticing which is most of the work.*

---

## Ten values worth arguing about

These are not values that unite humanity — that would be a convenient thing to claim and it isn't true. They are ten that recur across many traditions, offered for you to test, translate, and disagree with.

| | Value | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| 🌱 | **Honour life** | Protect living beings and the systems that sustain them. |
| ❤️ | **Compassion** | Act to relieve suffering. |
| ⚖️ | **Justice** | Challenge exploitation; work toward equity. |
| 🔍 | **Seek truth** | Stay humble; question your own biases first. |
| 🗝️ | **Respect freedom** | Defend dignity and expression, including for people you disagree with. |
| 🌎 | **Care for Earth** | Steward resources for people not yet born. |
| 🕊️ | **Build peace** | Resolve conflict through dialogue where dialogue is possible. |
| ✨ | **Practise integrity** | Align actions with stated values, and notice when they diverge. |
| 🙏 | **Cultivate gratitude** | Appreciate what you did not build and did not earn. |
| 🧠 | **Grow in wisdom** | Learn from perspectives that unsettle you. |

Each of these is contested. "Justice" means materially different things to a libertarian and a socialist; "honour life" runs into hard cases the moment you ask about pests, pathogens, or livestock. The disagreements are the useful part.

## Five levels of practice

Not a ladder to climb. Most people move between these depending on the issue and the week.

| Level | What you do | Shift | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Awareness** | Learn how global systems work. | *This is connected to that.* | Trace where one thing you own came from. |
| **2. Understanding** | Look for causes, not just symptoms. | *This is structural, not accidental.* | Read the opposing case on an issue you care about. |
| **3. Empathy** | Connect to people the systems affect. | *This costs someone something.* | Talk with someone whose position differs from yours. |
| **4. Participation** | Act where you actually have leverage. | *My choices are small and not nothing.* | Change one habit; write to one representative. |
| **5. Leadership** | Work on the structures themselves. | *Systems can be redesigned.* | Organise something; start a pilot. |

## Why it might matter

**For you** — a sense of direction, some resilience against despair, and skills that travel across cultures.

**For where you live** — less polarisation, more people willing to act on shared problems.

**For the wider world** — the cooperation that climate, pandemics, and technology governance all require has to be built by someone, and it is built out of people who think this way.

None of this is measured. Treat it as a hypothesis about what helps, not a finding.

## Common objections

| Objection | A response |
|---|---|
| *"I'm overwhelmed."* | Pick one thing and do it for a month. Breadth comes later, if at all. |
| *"It's not my problem."* | Often true. Start with the version of the problem that lands on your street. |
| *"I lack resources."* | Most of this needs conversation and attention, not money. |
| *"This is just feeling good about yourself."* | Sometimes it is. The test is whether anything you do costs you something. |

## Where the circle is expanding

Three live arguments about who counts, included because they are unresolved:

**Ecosystems.** New Zealand's Whanganui River holds legal personhood, and Ecuador's constitution recognises rights of nature. Whether this is a durable legal innovation or a symbolic one is genuinely disputed.

**Future generations.** Wales has a Future Generations Commissioner; several countries have debated similar offices. The hard question is how anyone represents people who cannot instruct them.

**Artificial systems.** Whether AI systems could ever warrant moral consideration is contested, and this framework takes no position on it. It matters here only because the same reasoning that expands the circle to rivers gets invoked, and the two cases are not obviously alike.

---

## Starting this week

1. **Pick one value** from the ten and notice where you fall short of it. Just notice.
2. **Find one other person** to talk it through with. This does not work alone.
3. **Do one thing** that costs you something small — time, convenience, or a comfortable opinion.
