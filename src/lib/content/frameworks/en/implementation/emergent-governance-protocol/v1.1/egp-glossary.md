# EGP Glossary v1.1.1
## *Key Terms for the Emergent Governance Protocol*

This glossary is part of `EGP/1.1.1`. Terms describing authority, consent, activation, emergency action, protected domains, and closure inherit `GMEAIA/0.1`.

---

## Core and constitutional terms

**`adopt(experiment)`**  
The third public operation. It records an adoption claim. Effect depends on its label and a valid GMEAIA authority envelope.

**Activation**  
The separate event allowing an authorized action to begin. Authorization, deployment, funding, and activation are distinct.

**Adoption claim**  
A record stating that an actor reports acceptance of a proposal.

**Affected Indigenous authority**  
The specific affected nation or people and its own representatives, law, consent process, knowledge rules, and territorial authority.

**Affected-party map**  
A structured identification of people, communities, workers, residents, users, rights-holders, institutions, and affected outsiders.

**Assertion**  
A submitted statement or observation before validation.

**Authority effect**  
The consequence category of a record: none, advisory, claim-only, voluntary internal, or consequential reference.

**Authority envelope**  
The GMEAIA references for affected parties, consent or lawful basis, authority, authorization, activation, safeguards, review, expiry, rollback, remedy, and closure.

**Authority verification**  
A separate review establishing that a competent actor may make the specific decision within jurisdiction, scope, rights, and time.

**Capability token**  
A technical permission such as UCAN. It does not establish public office or legal jurisdiction.

**Claim-only**  
An effect label stating that authority and active effect are unverified.

**Closure**  
Ending temporary authority, returning powers, completing data disposition, addressing remedy, and assigning unresolved duties.

**Constitutional resolution**  
The result of checking whether a structurally valid record has valid authority, consent, scope, rights safeguards, and lifecycle requirements.

**Correction propagation**  
Updating dashboards, indexes, linked records, automated systems, and public views after correction or withdrawal.

**DID**  
Decentralized Identifier: a cryptographic identity reference, not proof of office, representation, jurisdiction, or consent.

**Effect label**  
A mandatory classification such as claim-only, voluntary internal, active bounded trial, suspended, closed, or enactment reference.

**EGP/1.1.1**  
The harmonized package consisting of the core, appendix, glossary, one-page summary, manifest, schema, example, validation, and checksums.

**Essential-service safeguard**  
A record protecting basic water, food, sanitation, shelter, healthcare, education, energy, income, and safety.

**Event transport**  
Movement of structured governance records without creating the authority described by them.

**Experiment**  
A bounded, monitored, reversible trial. Temporary does not automatically mean lawful.

**GMEAIA/0.1**  
The controlling interface for governance methods, experiments, adoption, activation, implementation, review, scaling, and closure.

**Governance action**  
A structured EGP event using sense, propose, or adopt.

**Identity proof**  
Evidence that an actor signed or submitted a record; not proof of entitlement to bind others.

**Immutable log**  
A tamper-evident history that must still support effective correction, restriction, and lawful deletion.

**Learning artifact**  
A bounded record of pilot lessons that excludes protected participants, evidence, and knowledge without authority and consent.

**Local authority**  
Authority of the most local effective actor within lawful scope—not unlimited power over outsiders or affected nations.

**Machine-readable**  
Structured for software while preserving effect labels, authority states, access classes, and corrections.

**Offline parity**  
Paper, oral, assisted, SMS, and disconnected records have equal dignity when substantively equivalent.

**Permanent enactment reference**  
An EGP record pointing to a lasting rule created through a separate lawful process.

**Protected domain**  
A field in which ordinary experimentation fails closed, including coercion, borders, essential services, title, high-risk technology, adjudication, emergency, and fiscal deprivation.

**Protected payload**  
Sensitive content stored with restricted access, purpose limitation, retention, correction, and deletion controls.

**`propose(solution)`**  
The second public operation. It registers a candidate response with alternatives, affected parties, risks, resources, tests, and required authority.

**Public metadata**  
Accountability information visible without exposing protected payloads.

**Readiness assessment**  
An advisory evaluation of capacity or conditions; not jurisdiction, eligibility, or political competence.

**Representation record**  
A record of who represents whom, by what source, scope, duration, conflicts, and revocation.

**Rollback**  
The planned safe reversal or unwinding of a trial.

**Scaling proposal**  
A new proposal to expand a pilot. Success does not authorize scaling.

**Schema validity**  
Confirmation that a record has required structure; not proof of constitutional validity.

**`sense(issue)`**  
The first public operation. It records an asserted observation, concern, opportunity, allegation, or request.

**Smart contract**  
Executable ledger code that may register bounded technical events but cannot manufacture public authority.

**Stress packet**  
A structured sense record with asserted scope, severity, urgency, evidence, affected-party claims, uncertainty, and routing.

**Sunset**  
Expiry of temporary authority. Continuing rights, remedies, payments, restoration, safety, and records duties persist.

**Technical deployment**  
Installation or availability of software; not authorization or activation.

**UCAN**  
User Controlled Authorization Network token: technical delegation, not public-law authorization.

**Validation**  
Separate review of a claim or record for evidence, provenance, method, and status.

**Voluntary internal**  
An authorized practice affecting consenting members within the actor’s lawful internal scope.

**Withdrawal**  
Ending consent or participation where permitted, without retaliation or loss of essential support.

## Related GGF terms

**Adaptive Universal Basic Income (AUBI)**  
A separate economic framework. EGP may transport proposals and records but cannot determine benefit eligibility, amount, issuance, withholding, or clawback.

**Bioregional Autonomous Zone (BAZ)**  
A governance form whose authority depends on its lawful and Indigenous constitutional basis. A BAZ identifier or EGP record does not create territorial jurisdiction.

**CDEE**  
The cross-domain emergency execution interface. EGP may transport referrals and mission references but cannot declare or command emergencies.

**CERGTA/0.1**  
The commons, ecological rights, guardianship, title, and resource-authority interface.

**ECRC**  
The emergency constitutional record and clocks profile.

**FPIC**  
Free, Prior, and Informed Consent as defined by the specific affected Indigenous nation and applicable law; not a generic checkbox or proxy approval.

**Global Commons Fund (GCF)**  
A separate funding institution. EGP proposals may request funding, but GCF authority and decisions remain external.

**SCPA**  
The security and coercive powers architecture. EGP cannot authorize search, seizure, detention, force, intelligence, sanctions, cyber disruption, or asset restraint.

- preserve `GMEAIA/0.1` authority-effect and lifecycle meanings;
