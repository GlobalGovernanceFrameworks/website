# EGP Glossary v1.2

Terms describing authority, consent, activation, handoff, protected domains, and closure inherit `GMEAIA/0.2.1`. EGP is not a source of jurisdiction and does not authorize action.

**Activation** — The separate event allowing an authorized action to begin. Deployment, funding, authorization, and activation are distinct.

**Adoption claim** — A record stating that an actor reports acceptance of a proposal. It may remain claim-only.

**Affected-party standing** — Standing to participate, consent where applicable, challenge, and receive remedy. It does not itself assign causal responsibility.

**Authority effect** — A record's effect classification: none, advisory, claim-only, voluntary internal, or consequential reference.

**Authority envelope** — References connecting an EGP event to authority, causal responsibility, consent, authorization, activation, implementation, safeguards, monitoring, challenge, expiry, remedy, and closure.

**Authority formation** — `retained`, `delegated`, `transferred`, `concurrent`, `jointly_constituted`, or `absent`.

**Capability token** — A technical permission. It is not public office or legal jurisdiction.

**Causal responsibility** — Responsibility mapped through causal control, jurisdiction, capacity, resources, dependencies, and continuing duties.

**Consequence profile** — `CP0`, `CP1`, `CP2`, or `CP3`, used to scale records to actual consequence.

**Constitutional gap** — A consequential condition for which no identified authority has sufficient lawful jurisdiction. It is not mere lack of capacity.

**Capacity limited** — A handoff state in which a recipient recognizes jurisdiction or duty but lacks sufficient practical capacity.

**Correction propagation** — Updating linked records, dashboards, indexes, automated systems, and decisions after correction or withdrawal.

**EGP/1.2** — The package comprising the core, appendix, glossary, quick start, schema, examples, migration notes, validation, manifest, and checksums.

**Effect label** — The adoption-state label such as claim-only, voluntary internal, authorized but inactive, active bounded trial, suspended, terminated, closed, or enactment reference.

**Event transport** — Movement of structured governance records without creating the authority described by them.

**Handoff** — A referral or transfer request whose recipient response, duty, clocks, custody, and closure are represented through `GMEAIA-08` or `GMEAIA-59`.

**Impossible mandate** — A duty assigned without sufficient authority, causal control, resources, or feasible means.

**Impossible pilot** — A pilot too bounded, under-resourced, or causally disconnected to test its claimed mechanism.

**Jointly constituted authority** — Authority created by a lawful relationship or compact among parties over a shared governance object.

**Minimum causal adequacy** — The minimum scope, duration, variables, dependencies, and implementation conditions needed to expose the claimed mechanism.

**Non-activation** — An explicit state that prevents a claim, prototype, simulation, or deployment from being mistaken for active authority.

**Omission** — Failure to provide a required response or, after acceptance, failure to perform within applicable clocks.

**Protected domain** — A domain in which ordinary experimentation is insufficient and a specialized interface is required.

**Response duty** — A duty to acknowledge, decide, refer, or act, whose source and legal effect must be disclosed.

**Safe state** — A separately authorized temporary configuration intended to limit harm. A referral cannot activate it by itself.

**Structural conformance** — Successful validation against the EGP schema. It does not establish substantive validity.

**Consequential compatibility** — Resolvable and valid required GMEAIA and specialized-interface references for a consequential event.

**Timeout** — Expiry of a response clock. It makes non-response visible but does not transfer authority.

**Unresolved-duty custody** — Named responsibility for continuing duties after pause, termination, transfer, or closure.
