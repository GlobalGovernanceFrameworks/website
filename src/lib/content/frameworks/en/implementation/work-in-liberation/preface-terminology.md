# Preface: Terminology and Intent

This framework employs specific analytical terminology that may appear unconventional in labor policy documents. This is intentional and serves multiple audiences simultaneously.

## Dual-Register Language

Throughout this document, you will encounter terms that carry weight from both labor movement history and critical analysis of how work systems maintain control hierarchies. "Work in Liberation" rather than "workforce development." "Anti-elite capture" rather than "governance best practices." "Survival freedom" rather than "income security." "Industrial Sunsetting" rather than "sector transition."

These are not rhetorical flourishes. They are design constraints. Each term names a specific structural condition that its polite equivalent obscures, and the framework's architecture depends on keeping that structural condition visible.

For readers approaching this as workforce policy, the translations are direct:

**Work in Liberation** translates to autonomous labor organization with material security. The implementation requirement is that AUBI baseline eliminates survival coercion from employment decisions. When someone can refuse a job without risking homelessness, the power dynamic between employer and worker changes fundamentally. Calling this "labor market reform" understates what is actually happening.

**Anti-elite capture** translates to prevention of rent extraction from labor value. The implementation requirement is Community Provider governance rather than corporate ownership. Community Providers cannot be sold to corporations, cannot issue stock, cannot have external investors extracting surplus value. These aren't aspirational guidelines—they are architectural requirements baked into the charter process.

**Survival freedom** translates to decoupling basic needs from employment. Layer 1 AUBI ensures no one must accept exploitative terms to eat, sleep indoors, or access healthcare. The framework models what happens to labor markets when this condition is met: the coercive component of "market wages" becomes visible and begins to dissolve.

**Work With Purpose** translates to intrinsically motivated contribution. The Love Ledger recognizes value beyond market metrics—the mentoring, the caregiving, the community building that wage systems render invisible because they cannot be efficiently extracted from.

**Industrial Sunsetting** translates to managed phase-out of ecologically harmful sectors. The implementation mechanisms are concrete: Just Transition Fund support, retraining through Community Providers, Ecocide enforcement under the Treaty for Our Only Home. The word "sunsetting" carries intention—these industries end, by design, on a timeline.

**Care economy** translates to formalization of previously invisible reproductive labor. Hearts payments ensure that childcare, eldercare, community maintenance, and ecological stewardship receive material recognition through community investment rather than remaining unpaid because they resist commodification.

**Planetary contribution** translates to work aligned with ecological regeneration. Green Job Score multipliers direct enhanced community investment toward restoration activities, creating material incentives for healing rather than extraction.

## Why Both Registers Matter

**For the labor organizer**: The framework names what wage systems obscure. Work under survival threat is coercion, not contract. "Liberation" is not hyperbole—it is the accurate description of what happens when people can refuse bad conditions without starving. Every previous labor reform that avoided naming this dynamic was eventually neutralized. "Work-life balance" became "flexible scheduling" (always employer-flexible). "Fair wages" became "competitive compensation" (race to the bottom). The framework studies these linguistic captures explicitly because understanding how reforms get co-opted is essential to designing reforms that resist co-optation.

**For the HR professional**: These principles translate to concrete talent management realities. AUBI-backed workers require genuine engagement, not compliance theater. Hearts-based compensation requires actual community value creation, not performance metrics designed to justify existing hierarchies. The framework specifies the mechanisms—this is not theory but operational design.

**For the economist**: The terminology challenges the assumption that labor markets clear at equilibrium. When baseline survival is guaranteed through AUBI Layer 1, "market wages" reveal their coercive component—the portion of compensation that exists not because the work is valuable but because the worker has no alternative. The framework models this explicitly and designs around it.

**For the policymaker**: Understanding the "liberation" framing distinguishes this from welfare-to-work programs. AUBI with Hearts is designed to *reduce* labor supply to exploitative employers, not increase it. This is a feature, not a bug. The framework creates conditions where employers must offer genuinely meaningful, fairly compensated work to attract contributors—because contributors have alternatives.

## Design Transparency

We maintain this dual-register approach rather than sanitizing the language for three reasons that go beyond preference.

**Historical clarity**: Every labor reform that avoided naming power dynamics was eventually captured by the interests it sought to constrain. The framework documents these patterns not as historical trivia but as active design threats. When we name "anti-elite capture" rather than "good governance," we are specifying what the governance must prevent, not just what it should aspire to.

**Predictive resistance modeling**: The analytical framework predicts specific counter-moves against each mechanism:

Framing AUBI recipients as "lazy" or "free riders" is the predictable response to unconditional income security. The Love Ledger directly counters this by making visible the enormous volume of contribution that wage systems ignore—the caregiving, volunteering, mentoring, and community building that people do when freed from survival anxiety.

Automating away Community Provider roles is the predictable corporate response to worker-controlled organizations gaining market share. Hearts validation requires human relationship and community verification, creating structural resistance to automation that bypasses human judgment about what constitutes genuine care.

Capturing Community Providers as contractors is the predictable platform economy response to democratic work organization. BAZ governance and charter requirements prevent this by ensuring Community Providers cannot be acquired, cannot have external equity investors, and maintain democratic internal governance as a condition of their charter.

These are not hypothetical concerns. They are based on documented patterns of how previous labor movements were undermined, and the framework's architecture is designed specifically to resist each one.

**Boundary maintenance**: "Work in Liberation" versus "Future of Work" is not a semantic preference. The latter accepts employment as the primary social relation and seeks to improve conditions within that frame. The former questions whether wage labor should determine access to material security, social recognition, or life meaning—and builds systems based on the answer that it should not.

## How to Read This Document

**If you are implementing**: The analytical language provides design constraints. "Anti-elite capture" means Community Providers cannot be sold to corporations, cannot issue stock, cannot have external investors extracting surplus value. These are architectural requirements, not aspirational statements.

**If you are transitioning from gig work**: The framework explains why Community Providers are structurally different from platform work. This is not better conditions within the same model—it is a different ownership and governance structure that cannot replicate the extraction dynamics of gig platforms because the extraction mechanisms have been architecturally removed.

**If you are evaluating pilots**: Both registers matter for assessment. Does AUBI actually enable refusal of bad work? Does the Love Ledger prevent informal care from being exploited? The analytical framework predicts what should change; the metrics track whether it does. If the predictions fail, the framework needs revision—not just better implementation.

**If you are forming policy**: Understanding the liberation framing helps distinguish this from welfare-to-work programs. AUBI with Hearts is designed to shift power from employers to workers by removing survival coercion as a labor market mechanism. Calling this "workforce optimization" would obscure the intent. Calling it "liberation" states it clearly.

## Translation Is Not Dilution

The companion technical specifications and implementation guides use organizational development terminology where appropriate—"workforce autonomy" rather than "liberation from wage coercion," "community investment" rather than "Hearts economy." This is strategic translation for institutional contexts where radical language triggers defensive rejection before the content can be evaluated.

Both registers describe the same work system architecture. Both are true. Both are necessary.

When you encounter "Work in Liberation" in the introduction, understand it as both a radical restructuring of work's social function—from extraction to contribution—and a series of concrete labor market interventions: material security through AUBI, distributed ownership through Community Providers, value recognition through Hearts and the Love Ledger, and ecological alignment through the Green Job Score.

The tension between these readings is productive. It is the work system design specification.
