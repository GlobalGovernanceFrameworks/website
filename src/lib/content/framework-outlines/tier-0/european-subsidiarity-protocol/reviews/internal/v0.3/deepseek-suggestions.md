## 1. Structural: The legal section is too early

Currently, Section 2 is "Legal compatibility statement." While EU readers scan for legality first, placing it this early creates a **defensive posture**. You're implicitly saying: *"This might be illegal—let me reassure you."*

**Suggestion:** Move legal compatibility to an **appendix or a short callout box**, and open instead with the **crisis + solution framing**. The executive summary already does this well—let it breathe before you get into legal defenses.

**Alternative:** Keep the legal anchor but reframe it as:

> "This protocol operates within existing EU law. For those seeking the precise legal basis, see Section X."

This signals competence without defensiveness.

---

## 2. Rhetorical: The "shadow-mode" framing can be sharpened

You've correctly positioned shadow-mode as zero-risk validation. But the phrase "shadow-mode" carries connotations of:
- Bypassing legitimate process
- Hidden agendas
- Unofficial parallel systems

**Suggestion:** Reframe as **"Parallel Diagnostic Phase"** or **"Non-Binding Analytical Pilot."** The function is identical, but the connotation shifts from "shadowy" to "rigorous testing."

---

## 3. Missing: A clear articulation of what *doesn't* change

One of the most powerful adoption tools is **reducing cognitive load** for decision-makers. Right now, the document focuses heavily on what the protocol *adds* or *changes*. 

**Suggestion:** Add a short section or callout box:

### What remains unchanged

| Domain | Status |
|--------|--------|
| EU treaties | Fully intact |
| ECB monetary authority | Unchanged; complementary currency is bounded and non-threatening |
| Single market | Preserved; all outputs remain interoperable |
| National sovereignty | Enhanced through precision subsidiarity |
| Existing structural funds | Repurposed, not eliminated |

This gives readers a **safe anchor**—they know what they're *not* losing.

## 4. The care-crisis example: Excellent, but needs a "without GSI" baseline

The demographic/care example is now your strongest concrete illustration. But to make the GSI's value *undeniable*, you need to show the **counterfactual**.

**Suggestion:** Add a short framing:

> **Without GSI:** A centralized directive on eldercare funding is applied uniformly across 27 member states. Regions with strong informal care networks receive redundant funding; regions with weak networks remain underserved. Political friction over migration quotas intensifies.
>
> **With GSI:** The index identifies regions where informal care capacity is high (measured through the Civic Value Registry) and routes resources toward recognition infrastructure rather than formal care hiring. Regions with low capacity receive different support. The same problem—care deficit—is solved at different scales without uniform policy triggering uniform backlash.

This before/after structure is **persuasion architecture**.

- Normalizes the mechanism
- Positions your protocol alongside *already-successful* EU innovations
