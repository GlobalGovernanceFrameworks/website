# DPI 2.0: The Subsidiarity Layer
### A Governance Routing Protocol for India Stack

**Version:** 0.1 (India Adaptation — Draft)
**Target:** iSPIRT Foundation, NITI Aayog, Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology (MeitY)
**Architectural Status:** Open Standard — DPI-Compatible

---

## Executive Summary

India has built the most successful Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) in the world:

- **Aadhaar:** Identity for 1.4 billion people
- **UPI:** Real-time payments at planetary scale (over 10 billion monthly transactions)
- **DEPA:** User-owned data empowerment
- **ONDC:** Open network for digital commerce

**This is the digital plumbing.**

What India lacks is the **governance routing layer**—the protocol that determines *where* decisions should be made, *how* resources should flow, and *which* scale (local, state, or national) is optimal for any given problem.

The result: a hyper-efficient digital infrastructure serving a **bottlenecked governance architecture**. Policy decisions centralize in New Delhi. State-level variety is averaged out. Local knowledge remains inaccessible to formal systems.

**DPI 2.0** adds the missing layer: a subsidiarity-native routing protocol that:

- **Diagnoses** which problems require national coordination, which require state-level autonomy, and which require local adaptation
- **Routes** resources to the optimal governance scale dynamically
- **Measures** informal economic capacity (the 85% of employment invisible to formal systems)
- **Enables** climate resilience, agricultural responsiveness, and regional equity without central bottleneck

This is not a replacement for India Stack. It is the **governance operating system** that runs on top of it.

## 1. The Structural Deficit: Bottlenecked Abundance

India possesses extraordinary digital infrastructure but faces a governance architecture that cannot fully utilize it.

| Layer | Status | Gap |
|-------|--------|-----|
| **Identity (Aadhaar)** | Complete | Identity without governance routing |
| **Payments (UPI)** | Complete | Transactions without allocative intelligence |
| **Data (DEPA)** | Complete | Data without subsidiarity-aware coordination |
| **Commerce (ONDC)** | Emerging | Open network without local adaptation |
| **Governance** | Centralized bottleneck | 28 states' variety averaged into New Delhi decisions |

**The problem is not digital.** The problem is **governance latency**—the inability to match governance scale to problem scale.

Consider three scenarios where this latency creates measurable harm:

### 1.1 Climate Resilience

A heat wave hits Rajasthan. A cyclone approaches Odisha. Floods devastate Kerala.

**Current system:** Resources route through New Delhi. Bureaucratic approval cycles delay response. Centralized allocation ignores local knowledge of vulnerable populations, informal networks, and adaptive capacity.

**DPI 2.0:** The Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI) identifies optimal routing for resources—some nationally coordinated (emergency supplies), some state-managed (infrastructure deployment), some locally directed (community-led relief using informal networks). Response time decreases from weeks to days. Lives are saved.

### 1.2 Agricultural Distress

A region in Maharashtra faces crop failure. Farmers need debt relief, input subsidies, or market access—but the optimal intervention depends on local soil conditions, market integration, and informal credit networks.

**Current system:** Centralized agricultural policies average across vastly different agro-ecological zones. The farmer in Vidarbha receives the same intervention as the farmer in Punjab, despite radically different needs.

**DPI 2.0:** The GSI identifies that Maharashtra requires state-level debt restructuring, but local-level input distribution. Resources route accordingly. The Averaging Problem—where national policy destroys local variety—is mathematically neutralized.

### 1.3 Regional Inequality

Tamil Nadu's human development indicators diverge from Bihar's. Uttar Pradesh's demographic profile differs from Kerala's.

**Current system:** Centrally allocated resources follow formulas that average across states, systematically underfunding high-need regions while overfunding low-need regions relative to actual requirements.

**DPI 2.0:** The GSI measures actual local capacity and need—including informal economic activity invisible to GDP metrics—and routes resources dynamically. Equity becomes mathematically precise rather than politically negotiated.

## 2. India Stack: The Foundation

DPI 2.0 is built *on top of* India Stack, not parallel to it.

| India Stack Layer | DPI 2.0 Integration |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Aadhaar (Identity)** | Edge nodes authenticated via Aadhaar; privacy preserved through zero-knowledge proofs |
| **UPI (Payments)** | Value transfer remains in UPI; DPI 2.0 adds reputational layer without processing transactions |
| **DEPA (Data)** | User-owned data principles extended to reputation; users control disclosure of trust proofs |
| **ONDC (Commerce)** | Reputation portability across open network; trust builds across platforms |
| **DigiLocker** | Storage for zero-knowledge reputation proofs; user-controlled access |

**Explicit boundary:** DPI 2.0 is a **Layer 2 Reputation Oracle**. It does not:

- Process financial transactions (remains in UPI)
- Store personal data centrally (remains user-owned via DEPA)
- Replace Aadhaar (authenticates but does not create parallel identity)
- Create new currency (strictly reputational)

## 3. Protocol Architecture: The Subsidiarity Layer

DPI 2.0 adds three capabilities to India Stack:

### 3.1. The Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI)

The GSI is a diagnostic algorithm that identifies the optimal governance scale for any given policy domain.

**Inputs:**
- Formal data (administrative boundaries, census, infrastructure)
- Informal data (local economic activity, trust networks, adaptive capacity)—measured through the Civic Value Registry (Section 3.2)

**Output:**
- "This problem requires national coordination" (e.g., monetary policy, defense)
- "This problem requires state-level autonomy" (e.g., agricultural extension, health infrastructure)
- "This problem requires local adaptation" (e.g., community resilience, informal sector support)

**Technical foundation:** Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety—the governance system must match the variety of the system it governs. The GSI mathematically identifies where centralized regulation destroys local variety and where local autonomy cannot achieve coordination benefits.

**Integration with NITI Aayog:** The GSI operationalizes NITI Aayog's mandate for cooperative and competitive federalism, replacing political negotiation with mathematical diagnosis.

### 3.2. The Civic Value Registry

The Indian informal economy employs approximately **85% of the workforce**—agricultural labor, street vendors, domestic workers, construction laborers, gig workers. These actors are invisible to formal systems, yet they constitute the majority of economic activity.

The Civic Value Registry measures this informal capacity:

- **What it measures:** Trust networks, community care capacity, local adaptive resilience, informal credit relationships
- **How it measures:** Voluntary participation via USSD (feature-phone native); zero-knowledge proofs protect individual privacy
- **What it enables:** Visibility for policy without surveillance; credit access for informal actors; climate resilience mapping

**Integration with DEPA:** Users control their reputation data under DEPA principles—they decide what to prove, to whom, and when.

**Integration with UPI:** The registry attaches to UPI transactions as a reputational layer, without processing value. A vendor using UPI for payments can build a verifiable reputation score that travels with them across the economy.

### 3.3. The Routing Protocol

The Routing Protocol translates GSI diagnosis into resource flows:

| Layer | Function | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **National** | Budget allocation, regulatory harmonization | Disaster relief funds released centrally |
| **State** | Program design, infrastructure deployment | Gujarat designs drought response within national framework |
| **District/Block** | Implementation, local adaptation | Village-level committees direct resources using local knowledge |
| **Community** | Trust-based coordination | Self-help groups (SHGs) activate informal care networks |

**Dynamic routing:** As conditions change (e.g., a drought intensifies, a cyclone approaches), the GSI re-diagnoses and the Routing Protocol adjusts—resources flow to the scale that can act most effectively.

**Integration with ONDC:** The Routing Protocol enables ONDC to support localized adaptation—allowing different pricing, inventory, and logistics rules for different regions while maintaining network interoperability.

## 4. Deployment Model: Enhanced Federalism

DPI 2.0 is designed for India's federal structure—respecting state autonomy while enabling national coordination.

### Phase 1: Pilot States (12–18 months)

| State | Rationale | Focus Domain |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| **Tamil Nadu** | High digital literacy; strong administrative capacity | Climate resilience, coastal adaptation |
| **Uttar Pradesh** | Large population; significant informal economy | Agricultural distress, informal sector visibility |
| **Kerala** | High social development; strong community networks | Care economy, aging population |

**Triangular Activation:**

| Leg | Role | Partner |
|-----|------|---------|
| **DPI Anchor** | Standards, integration | iSPIRT Foundation, MeitY |
| **State Government** | Policy authority, pilot deployment | State IT Departments, NITI Aayog |
| **Civic-Tech Builder** | USSD deployment, local capacity | EkStep Foundation, Wadhwani AI, local civic-tech ecosystem |

### Phase 2: Interstate Coordination (Years 2–3)

- GSI outputs shared between states
- Cross-state resource routing (e.g., labor migration patterns, supply chain coordination)
- Enhanced Cooperation (Article 20 TEU—adapted to Indian context) for coalitions of states pursuing shared protocols

### Phase 3: National Integration (Years 4–5)

- GSI integrated into NITI Aayog policy formulation
- Civic Value Registry data incorporated into national statistics
- Routing Protocol operational for major national programs

## 6. Privacy & Data Sovereignty

DPI 2.0 inherits India Stack's data sovereignty principles:

| Principle | Implementation |
|-----------|----------------|
| **User ownership** | Reputation data owned by the individual; controlled via DEPA |
| **Zero-knowledge proofs** | Users prove "I have Level 4 reliability" without revealing transaction history |
| **No central repository** | Raw data remains at the edge; central system holds only anonymized aggregates |
| **Voluntary participation** | No mandatory registration; formal actors can ignore protocol |
| **Consent architecture** | DEPA-based consent; users approve each data disclosure |

**Constitutional alignment:** Consistent with *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India* (2017) privacy ruling.

## 11. Conclusion: From Plumbing to Intelligence

India has built the digital pipes. Now it needs the **governance intelligence** to route resources through those pipes to the places they are most needed.

DPI 2.0 does not replace India Stack. It completes it—adding the missing layer that:

- Diagnoses the optimal scale for each governance problem
- Measures the informal economy invisible to formal systems
- Routes resources dynamically as conditions change
- Preserves state autonomy while enabling national coordination

This is not a foreign import. It is the next logical step in India's digital journey—built on Indian infrastructure, anchored in Indian constitutional principles, designed for Indian realities.

**The plumbing is ready. The intelligence is ready. It is time to route.**
