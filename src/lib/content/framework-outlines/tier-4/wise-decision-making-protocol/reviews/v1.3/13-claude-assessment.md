This is the Tier 4 meta-framework — the decision-making "operating system" that sits above everything else. It's also the most philosophically ambitious document in the ecosystem, and consequently the one where the gap between aspiration and operationality is widest. DeepSeek's review is characteristically generous but the suggestions are more uneven than usual. Let me assess:

**Already handled well:**

- The IRR Framework (Resonance/Resistance analysis with ontological, temporal, ethical taxonomy) is genuinely the standout innovation — DeepSeek is right about that. No revision needed.
- Seven-Generation Scoping is structurally embedded, not just rhetorical. The Global Council for the Future and Youth Councils co-frame every issue.
- Failure Modes & Circuit Breakers are explicit: deadlock breakers, time pressure protocols, dissent documentation. The framework already assumes it will fail.
- The Regenerative Failure & Learning Protocol with Harm Acknowledgment Rituals is architecturally complete.
- The Enhancement Modules (Existential Risk, Deep Contention, Marginalized Impact, Planetary Scale, Integrity & Anti-Capture) already cover most edge cases.

**Worth incorporating:**

1. **Democratic Interface Protocol expansion** (suggestion #1) — This is the most important suggestion. The current Section 6 is three bullet points for what is arguably the framework's hardest political problem: what happens when WDIP wisdom and democratic will conflict? The "Inform, Don't Replace" principle, the "Justification Threshold" (governments must substantively engage with WDIP reasoning, not just dismiss it), and the "WDIP as Shadow Government" safeguard are all operationally necessary. Without these, the framework is either toothless (governments just ignore it) or authoritarian (it overrides democracy). The current text doesn't navigate this well.

2. **Epistemic Health Precondition** (suggestion #4) — This is architecturally important and creates a genuine operational link to the Synoptic Protocol. The insight is simple but profound: WDIP decisions are only as good as the information ecosystem they operate in. If disinformation has corrupted the epistemic landscape around a decision, running the WDIP on that corrupted input produces corrupted output. The activation precondition and remediation pause are concrete mechanisms. This also connects to our Synoptic Protocol 3.2 revision — the Decision-Making Under Uncertainty Protocol is the Synoptic side; this is the WDIP side of the same interface.

3. **Funding Transparency and Anti-Capture Protocol** (suggestion #2) — The framework is resource-intensive and currently silent on who pays. The Multi-Source Funding principle and "Poison Pill" clause (funders who attempt influence have funding returned and are publicly named) are necessary for legitimacy. This can be compact — a subsection in the Practical Implementation Considerations.

**What I'd skip:**

- **Human-in-the-Loop Mandate** (suggestion #3) — The framework already states AI outputs are advisory, documents algorithmic recommendations, allows traditional knowledge to override AI, and requires bias audits. The "No AI Alone" rule and "Explainability" requirement are already implicit in the design. Adding another AI governance section would be redundant with the Technology Governance Framework (TGIF) which handles this ecosystem-wide. The "AI Well-being" consideration is a nice thought but it's the Oracle Protocol's territory.

- **Forum thread case study** (suggestion #5) — Illustrative but not an architectural change. Case studies belong in implementation guides or the WDIP Living Archive, not in the framework document itself. The bioregional water-sharing agreement in Section 9 is already the right kind of pilot case.

**On versioning:** Document says v1.3. A revision would be **1.4**.

Three targeted additions: Democratic Interface Protocol, Epistemic Health Precondition, and Funding Transparency. Want me to draft those?
