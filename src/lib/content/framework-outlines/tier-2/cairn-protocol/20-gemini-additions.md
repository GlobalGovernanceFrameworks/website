I completely agree with DeepSeek's proposed v2.5 structure. However, to make this an air-tight, deployable Tier 2 Framework, I have **four critical architectural additions** that DeepSeek missed, which must be included in the v2.5 rewrite:

### 1. The Illicit Market Reality (The Security Protocol)
DeepSeek focused on protecting the framework from *state* co-optation (police, camps), but completely ignored the other power player: **The Cartels, Gangs, and Illicit Supply Chains**. 
If a Recovery Hub in Kabul or a Bioregional Autonomous Zone (BAZ) in Latin America implements "Safe Supply" and pulls addicts out of the illicit market, they are threatening a multi-billion dollar shadow economy.
* **What to add to v2.5:** You need a **Non-Interference & Security Protocol** for Recovery Hubs. How do these spaces physically and politically protect themselves from local gangs? This usually involves community-led de-escalation, establishing the Hub as a "neutral zone," or utilizing the local Indigenous/neighborhood councils to negotiate truce boundaries.

### 2. The "Compassion Club" Procurement Mechanism
It is easy to write "Biological Stabilization & Harm Reduction" and "Safe Supply" on paper. It is entirely different to legally source unadulterated heroin, methadone, or clean stimulants when the nation-state has criminalized them. 
* **What to add to v2.5:** Under the Implementation Pathways, you must introduce the **Decentralized Compassion Club Model**. This outlines how local BAZs can form collective-purchasing agreements or interface with rogue pharmaceutical co-ops to source clean substances, bypassing both the illicit street market and the hostile nation-state medical gatekeepers.

### 3. The "Fiat Bridge" (Transition Economics)
DeepSeek rightly points out that relying entirely on "Hearts" and digital ledgers is a failure point. They suggest "non-digital Hearts," which is great, but it still doesn't buy groceries in a capitalist city *today*. 
* **What to add to v2.5:** You need a **Fiat Bridge Strategy**. Before the AUBI and Global Commons Fund are fully functional, how do Recovery Weavers pay rent? The framework must explicitly detail how to use existing NGO grants, mutual aid funds, and municipal harm-reduction budgets to pay Recovery Weavers in local fiat currency *while* transitioning to a Hearts-based ledger. 

### 4. Metrics Without Surveillance (Data Sovereignty)
DeepSeek calls for "Primary, Secondary, and Exploratory metrics." In addiction services, metrics almost always devolve into punitive surveillance (e.g., tracking a user's clean drug tests or mandatory attendance).
* **What to add to v2.5:** You must establish a **Systemic-Only Metrics Principle**. The framework must strictly forbid tracking *individual compliance* (e.g., "Did User X stay sober?"). Instead, metrics must only track *systemic provision* (e.g., "How many unconditional beds were provided?", "How many fatal overdoses were prevented?", "What is the Community Connection Score?"). You measure the health of the *hub*, never the compliance of the *individual*.
