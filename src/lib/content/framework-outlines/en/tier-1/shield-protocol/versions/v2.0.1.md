# Executive summary

Transnational criminal activity can cross borders, exploit jurisdictional gaps, conceal assets, compromise digital systems, intimidate witnesses, traffic persons, corrupt public institutions, and deprive victims of effective remedy. No single jurisdiction can always investigate, preserve evidence, protect affected persons, recover assets, or restore services alone. Cooperation is therefore necessary.

Cooperation, however, does not erase constitutional boundaries. A shared threat description does not establish an offence. Intelligence does not establish guilt. A cooperation vote does not create a warrant. A cyber incident does not authorize entry into third-party systems. A request for assistance does not transfer command. A suspected asset does not become public revenue. A victim-protection mission does not authorize detention, surveillance, prosecution, or forced rehabilitation.

Shield v2.0.1 provides a common framework for:

- voluntary and treaty-based cooperation against transnational crime;
- lawful, purpose-limited information exchange;
- mutual legal assistance and evidence preservation;
- defensive cyber coordination and service restoration;
- separately authorized third-party cyber assistance;
- financial investigation and asset-recovery coordination;
- victim, survivor, witness, and whistleblower protection;
- restitution and property-return coordination;
- anti-corruption and institutional-capacity support;
- independent complaints, correction, compensation, and learning;
- orderly termination, handback, data disposition, succession, and dissolution.

Shield v2.0.1 does **not**:

- define a universal criminal code;
- classify persons, communities, beliefs, cultures, migration routes, or developmental stages as criminal threats;
- create a Transnational Security Council, Global Crime Intelligence Center, Global Enforcement Task Force, Sovereignty Dispute Tribunal, or universal beneficial-ownership registry;
- compel intelligence sharing by framework membership alone;
- authorize searches, interception, raids, arrest, detention, extradition, deportation, sanctions, asset freezes, forfeiture, offensive cyber action, or use of force;
- create a standing international operational force;
- allow investigators or administrators to fund themselves from unadjudicated seizures;
- allow AI, blockchain records, threat tiers, financial thresholds, or behavioural profiles to determine legal consequences;
- condition essential victim support on cooperation with investigation, prosecution, mediation, testimony, or rehabilitation;
- permit a coordination body to investigate, command, adjudicate, punish, finance, and review the same action.

The framework is designed around **functional separation**. Offence definition, intelligence, investigation, compulsory process, operational command, custody, prosecution, adjudication, enforcement, victim services, restitution, audit, and remedy remain distinct. Cross-border cooperation occurs through explicit requests, acceptance states, purpose limitations, custody records, reviewable legal routes, and full lifecycle closure.

# 1. Purpose, scope, and legal character

## 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of Shield v2.0.1 is to help competent authorities and protected public-interest actors cooperate against serious transnational harm without creating a vertically integrated global security institution.

The framework supports lawful coordination where criminal conduct, evidence, assets, victims, perpetrators, digital infrastructure, corporate structures, or necessary remedies span more than one jurisdiction.

## 1.2 Scope

Shield may support cooperation concerning conduct that is criminalized by applicable law and that has a material cross-border or multi-jurisdictional dimension, including:

- human trafficking, forced labour, slavery, and organized exploitation;
- corruption, bribery, kleptocracy, procurement fraud, and state capture;
- money laundering and concealment of criminal proceeds;
- ransomware, digital extortion, unauthorized system intrusion, destructive cyber operations, and theft of digital assets;
- organized fraud and identity abuse;
- arms trafficking where prohibited by applicable law;
- environmental crime and illegal extraction where criminalized by competent law;
- organized smuggling that causes or exploits serious harm;
- trafficking in cultural property, wildlife, hazardous materials, or protected resources;
- transnational witness intimidation, obstruction of justice, and retaliation;
- criminal misuse of legal persons, financial systems, logistics networks, or digital infrastructure.

The framework does not itself determine whether conduct is criminal. That determination belongs to valid law and independent adjudication.

## 1.3 Legal character

Shield v2.0.1 is:

- a cooperation framework;
- an interoperability specification;
- a source of model procedures and records;
- a capacity-building and victim-protection architecture;
- a non-authority design until adopted by a competent jurisdiction or valid treaty instrument.

It is not self-executing criminal law.

## 1.4 Non-authority clause

Nothing in this framework independently creates:

- an offence;
- criminal jurisdiction;
- intelligence authority;
- investigative authority;
- compulsory disclosure;
- search or interception power;
- arrest, restraint, custody, detention, transfer, extradition, deportation, or return authority;
- cyber access to systems not controlled by the acting authority;
- asset freeze, seizure, forfeiture, exclusion, sanctions, or taxation power;
- prosecutorial or judicial authority;
- command over national, local, Indigenous, community, private, humanitarian, or international personnel;
- legal status for evidence, identity, ownership, or attribution;
- authority to compel framework membership or cooperation.

## 1.5 Controlling constitutional rule

Every consequential action SHALL be classified under `SCPA/0.1` and supported by the applicable SCPA records.

> **No valid authority record, no consequential security or coercive action.**

## 1.6 Relationship to SCPA action classes

Shield may support all SCPA action classes only as follows:

| SCPA class | Shield role | Shield limit |
|---|---|---|
| A — voluntary assistance and prevention | direct support | no coercion or adverse inference |
| B — observation, warning, intelligence | protected exchange and analysis | no investigation or targeting authority |
| C — investigation | request routing and technical support | competent authority retains mandate |
| D — urgent protection | victim and service coordination | no hidden detention or force |
| E — compulsory legal process | mutual legal assistance routing | no warrant or order power |
| F — custody and physical force | logistical interface only | no standing Shield custody or force function |
| G — cyber intervention | defensive coordination and separately authorized support | no offensive or third-party action by framework vote |
| H — financial coercion | request, custody, claims, and restitution coordination | no freeze, seizure, forfeiture, or sanctions power |
| I — adjudication and remedy | evidence and judgment handoff | no Shield tribunal |
| J — restoration and reintegration | voluntary support and referral | no compelled ideology or developmental correction |
| K — mobility, identity, and territorial action | victim protection and lawful cooperation interface | no border, asylum, detention, expulsion, or extradition power |

# 2. Governing principles

## 2.1 Action-specific legality

Each consequential act requires its own legal basis, competent authority, territorial or personal jurisdiction, factual threshold, procedural route, safeguards, review, remedy, and termination condition.

## 2.2 Cooperation is not command

A cooperation request does not command the recipient. Acceptance does not transfer sovereignty. Technical support does not transfer legal responsibility. Participation in Shield does not authorize action in another jurisdiction.

## 2.3 Dual legality and human-rights compatibility

Cross-border assistance SHALL identify:

- the requesting authority’s legal basis;
- the receiving authority’s legal basis;
- applicable treaty or reciprocal-assistance basis;
- whether dual criminality is required;
- human-rights, constitutional, Indigenous, privacy, labour, humanitarian, and non-refoulement constraints;
- grounds for refusal, narrowing, delay, or conditions.

No assistance may be provided where the likely result would be torture, disappearance, persecution, collective punishment, discriminatory prosecution, manifestly unfair trial, prohibited surveillance, refoulement, or another grave rights violation.

## 2.4 Individualized basis

Legal consequences SHALL not be based solely on:

- nationality, ethnicity, race, religion, caste, gender, disability, poverty, migration status, political opinion, association, location, or family relationship;
- developmental, cultural, spiritual, psychological, or ideological classification;
- membership in a broad community or social network;
- automated risk scoring, anomaly detection, financial volume, or threat tier;
- refusal to join, fund, praise, or cooperate with Shield;
- lawful encryption, privacy tools, anonymity, or use of privacy-preserving currencies.

## 2.5 No model-to-power conversion

AI, network analysis, behavioural analytics, anomaly detection, language models, blockchain analytics, biometrics, and financial intelligence may support inquiry only under valid authority and documented limitations. They cannot independently establish identity, attribution, intent, probable cause, guilt, dangerousness, sanctions eligibility, or forfeiture.

## 2.6 Least restrictive effective cooperation

Authorities SHALL prefer the least intrusive effective route, including voluntary production, preservation requests, targeted legal assistance, redacted or aggregate disclosure, trusted execution, independent verification, or protected attestation before broader compulsory access.

## 2.7 Protected non-transfer

A lawful request does not override protected non-transfer. Sources, privileged communications, humanitarian data, journalistic material, medical records, mediation disclosures, Indigenous knowledge, migration-service data, survivor records, and protected personal data remain subject to their own legal and ethical constraints.

## 2.8 Victim and survivor sovereignty

Support is not evidence collection. A survivor may receive protection, shelter, healthcare, income support, legal assistance, interpretation, relocation assistance, restitution support, and long-term care without agreeing to report, testify, confront an alleged perpetrator, participate in restoration, or accept a particular legal strategy.

## 2.9 Innocent-party continuity

Asset action, cyber action, and institutional disruption SHALL protect employees, dependants, customers, depositors, patients, residents, humanitarian beneficiaries, lawful counterparties, and other innocent parties.

## 2.10 No self-financing coercion

Shield bodies, investigators, enforcement authorities, prosecutors, adjudicators, and auditors SHALL not receive budgetary rewards tied to arrests, freezes, seizures, forfeitures, sanctions, convictions, network disruptions, or threat-tier escalation.

## 2.11 Complete lifecycle

Every cooperation action SHALL provide for initiation, acceptance, scope control, custody, review, amendment, refusal, suspension, termination, property return, data disposition, correction, remedy, compensation, and unresolved-risk custody.

# 3. Constitutional topology

## 3.1 No unified security sovereign

Shield v2.0.1 creates no institution that simultaneously:

- defines offences;
- receives intelligence;
- opens investigations;
- approves compulsory process;
- commands operations;
- arrests or detains;
- freezes or forfeits assets;
- prosecutes;
- adjudicates;
- distributes recovered assets;
- funds itself from outcomes;
- audits and reviews its own conduct.

## 3.2 Required functional separation

At minimum, the following functions SHALL remain institutionally and procedurally distinguishable:

1. offence definition and legislation;
2. observation and protected intelligence;
3. investigation;
4. independent authorization of compulsory process;
5. operational execution;
6. custody and welfare;
7. prosecution;
8. adjudication;
9. enforcement of judgments;
10. victim, survivor, and witness services;
11. asset custody and claims administration;
12. restitution allocation;
13. data protection and source protection;
14. audit, complaint, correction, and remedy;
15. rehabilitation and reintegration;
16. framework administration and capacity support.

## 3.3 Shared bodies are non-sovereign

The framework may support shared administrative and technical bodies, but none receives residual authority. Their functions are enumerated, reviewable, and revocable.

## 3.4 Referral is not legal transformation

A Shield referral does not convert:

- an allegation into intelligence;
- intelligence into evidence;
- evidence into guilt;
- a financial alert into an asset freeze;
- a cyber indicator into attribution;
- a victim-protection request into investigative consent;
- a peacebuilding report into a threat nomination;
- a migration-service record into a border or criminal-enforcement record;
- an Aegis capability into a security asset.

## 3.5 No authority by network membership

Membership in a Shield network may establish technical interoperability and reciprocal commitments only. It does not establish criminal jurisdiction, operational command, or a duty to execute unlawful or incompatible requests.

# 4. Institutional architecture

## 4.1 Shield Cooperation Secretariat

A **Shield Cooperation Secretariat** may be constituted to perform non-coercive administrative functions:

- maintain contact points and service directories;
- route cooperation and mutual legal assistance requests;
- maintain versioned model forms and conformance profiles;
- coordinate translation, accessibility, secure communications, and technical assistance;
- publish aggregate, non-operational learning reports;
- maintain the refusal, delay, deficiency, and closure states of requests;
- administer independent audits and complaints routing;
- maintain dissolution and continuity plans.

The Secretariat SHALL NOT:

- collect general intelligence;
- maintain a global watchlist or target database;
- define threat tiers or offences;
- open investigations;
- approve operations;
- issue warrants or orders;
- command personnel;
- access operational evidence unless necessary for a specifically authorized administrative function;
- impose sanctions or funding penalties;
- distribute seized assets;
- adjudicate disputes;
- rank jurisdictions by cooperation or opt-out behaviour.

## 4.2 Federated Protected Information Exchange

Participating authorities may establish **Protected Information Exchange Nodes** under their own law. Each node remains accountable to its competent authority and data-protection regime.

Nodes may support:

- authenticated request exchange;
- purpose limitation and dissemination controls;
- protected-source handling;
- minimization, redaction, compartmentation, and access logging;
- correction and challenge;
- retention and deletion;
- cross-border restrictions;
- secure evidence-transfer channels.

There is no central global intelligence warehouse by default.

## 4.3 Mutual Legal Assistance Network

A **Mutual Legal Assistance Network** may connect competent central authorities, courts, prosecutors, investigators, regulators, and other legally authorized bodies.

It supports request routing, legal translation, deficiency notices, preservation coordination, service of documents, evidence transfer, witness arrangements, asset requests, and judgment-recognition processes.

It does not issue compulsory orders.

## 4.4 Cyber Defence and Recovery Network

A **Cyber Defence and Recovery Network** may support:

- defensive action on systems controlled by the requesting organization or authority;
- incident triage and containment;
- malware analysis;
- vulnerability coordination;
- service restoration;
- victim notification;
- evidence preservation;
- independent technical verification;
- cross-border assistance requests for action on third-party systems.

The Network cannot authorize third-party access, disruption, sinkholing, server seizure, credential use, data deletion, counter-hacking, or offensive operations.

## 4.5 Asset Recovery and Restitution Coordination Network

An **Asset Recovery and Restitution Coordination Network** may support:

- financial intelligence requests under valid law;
- beneficial-ownership and control inquiries through lawful sources;
- asset tracing;
- preservation and restraint requests;
- custody and maintenance planning;
- adjudicated forfeiture recognition;
- third-party claims;
- victim claims and restitution;
- return to affected jurisdictions or communities;
- anti-corruption safeguards;
- transparent administrative accounting.

It cannot freeze, seize, forfeit, tax, exclude, or distribute assets without independently valid authority.

## 4.6 Victim, Survivor, Witness, and Whistleblower Support Network

A **Protection and Support Network** may coordinate:

- immediate safety planning;
- shelter and relocation support;
- healthcare and psychosocial care;
- income, housing, childcare, disability, and interpretation support;
- legal assistance;
- witness and whistleblower protection;
- trafficking-survivor support;
- family tracing and reunification;
- restitution claims;
- long-term recovery and reintegration.

Service providers SHALL remain separate from intelligence and investigation unless a lawful, consent-respecting, narrowly defined exception applies.

## 4.7 Capacity and Integrity Support Facility

A **Capacity and Integrity Support Facility** may fund or coordinate:

- judicial and investigative training;
- digital-forensics laboratories;
- victim-service infrastructure;
- anti-corruption controls;
- cybersecurity improvements;
- source-protection systems;
- translation and accessibility;
- independent oversight and complaint systems;
- preservation of local and Indigenous legal capacity;
- transition away from abusive security practices.

Funding SHALL not be conditioned on arrest, conviction, seizure, sanction, deportation, surveillance, intelligence volume, or political alignment targets.

## 4.8 Independent Conformance and Remedy Panel

An **Independent Conformance and Remedy Panel** may review only framework conformance and administrative conduct. It may:

- receive complaints about Shield bodies;
- order correction of Secretariat records;
- recommend suspension of a network or interface;
- publish findings;
- refer legal claims to competent adjudicators;
- require remedial plans as a condition of continued interoperability.

It cannot adjudicate criminal guilt, issue warrants, impose criminal penalties, order detention, or substitute for courts.

# 5. Offence, jurisdiction, and standing boundaries

## 5.1 No universal Shield crime definition

Shield does not define “crime against systemic integrity,” “developmental crime,” “threat to regenerative civilization,” or any other framework-created offence.

Criminal offences must be defined by valid law with legality, clarity, accessibility, non-retroactivity, jurisdiction, elements, defences, and independent adjudication.

## 5.2 Seriousness and transnationality

A cooperation request SHALL state:

- the alleged offence under applicable law;
- the material facts and procedural posture;
- the cross-border nexus;
- the requested action;
- the requested data, evidence, asset, person, or support;
- necessity and proportionality;
- time sensitivity;
- rights and protected-status safeguards;
- review and remedy routes.

Financial amount, network size, political salience, or estimated systemic impact may inform prioritization but do not create jurisdiction or reduce procedural protections.

## 5.3 Dual criminality and analogous conduct

Where dual criminality is required, the request SHALL identify the relevant conduct and legal elements in both jurisdictions. Labels need not be identical, but the material conduct must fall within applicable lawful cooperation standards.

## 5.4 Political, military, religious, journalistic, humanitarian, and civic activity

Shield SHALL NOT be used to suppress lawful:

- political opposition;
- protest, strike, union, or civic organizing;
- journalism or source protection;
- humanitarian assistance;
- religious or spiritual practice;
- Indigenous governance or land defence;
- migration assistance;
- cybersecurity research conducted under lawful authorization;
- privacy, encryption, or anonymity practices;
- academic or scientific inquiry.

## 5.5 State conduct and official immunity

Alleged state involvement does not remove the need for valid law, evidence, jurisdiction, immunities analysis, diplomatic safeguards, independent review, and applicable international-law procedures.

Shield cannot classify a state as “non-cooperative” and thereby create sanctions, intervention, or offensive cyber authority.

# 6. Cooperation request lifecycle

## 6.1 Request creation

Every request SHALL identify the requesting authority, receiving authority, legal basis, action class, scope, urgency, data and source controls, safeguards, requested completion state, and review route.

## 6.2 Recipient states

A recipient SHALL record one of the following states:

- received;
- acknowledged;
- clarification required;
- deficient;
- partially accepted;
- conditionally accepted;
- accepted;
- rejected;
- stayed pending review;
- suspended;
- completed;
- closed;
- withdrawn.

Silence is not acceptance.

## 6.3 Grounds for refusal or narrowing

A request may or shall be refused, narrowed, delayed, or conditioned where:

- authority or jurisdiction is absent or unclear;
- the requested act is not available under receiving law;
- dual criminality or specialty requirements are not met;
- the request is disproportionate, overbroad, discriminatory, or insufficiently particularized;
- protected non-transfer applies;
- source, privilege, privacy, Indigenous, humanitarian, journalistic, medical, labour, or survivor protections would be violated;
- the request risks torture, persecution, unfair trial, refoulement, or grave harm;
- evidence integrity cannot be maintained;
- the requesting institution lacks adequate safeguards or remedy;
- the action would undermine an active peace, humanitarian, asylum, or victim-protection process;
- the request attempts to obtain through cooperation what the requester could not lawfully obtain domestically.

## 6.4 Specialty and onward transfer

Information, evidence, assets, or persons obtained through cooperation may be used only for the authorized purpose unless the originating and receiving authorities lawfully approve an additional purpose.

No onward transfer is presumed.

## 6.5 Completion and closure

Completion SHALL include:

- what was provided or refused;
- conditions and limitations;
- custody and authenticity state;
- data disposition;
- property and asset status;
- source protection;
- notice and challenge status;
- unresolved requests;
- termination and archival state.

# 7. Protected information, intelligence, and source governance

## 7.1 Information categories

Shield distinguishes:

- public information;
- voluntary reports;
- protected intelligence;
- operational intelligence;
- investigative information;
- evidence;
- privileged or specially protected information;
- victim and service data;
- classified information;
- technical indicators;
- unverified allegations;
- analytical inference.

Categories SHALL not be collapsed.

## 7.2 Lawful intelligence exchange

Intelligence exchange requires:

- a valid purpose and authority;
- defined recipients;
- necessity and proportionality;
- reliability and confidence statements;
- source and caveat protection;
- minimization;
- retention and dissemination limits;
- correction and challenge processes;
- prohibited-use fields;
- review and termination.

## 7.3 No mandatory global sharing

Shield membership alone creates no duty to share raw intelligence, personal data, source identities, migration records, mediation records, protected knowledge, or classified information.

## 7.4 Human and community sources

Source handling SHALL protect:

- informed participation;
- confidentiality and limits;
- non-retaliation;
- compensation transparency;
- avoidance of coercion, entrapment, dependency, or exploitation;
- child and vulnerable-person safeguards;
- correction and withdrawal where legally possible;
- source-risk planning.

Community organizations, journalists, humanitarian actors, migrants, survivors, and peacebuilders shall not be treated as standing intelligence assets.

## 7.5 Developmental and cultural classification prohibited

Spiral Dynamics, cultural-alignment models, religious-driver assessments, psychological typologies, and similar frameworks SHALL NOT be used to:

- classify criminality or dangerousness;
- select targets;
- assign threat tiers;
- justify surveillance, infiltration, sanctions, detention, force, prosecution, or rehabilitation;
- optimize team composition for coercive missions through automated profiling.

Cultural competence may support respectful communication and accessibility; it may not replace individualized law and facts.

## 7.6 Watchlists and alerts

Shield maintains no universal watchlist. Any alert or watchlisting function remains under separately valid law and SHALL include identity confidence, factual basis, prohibited inferences, notice or delayed-notice basis, review, correction, expiry, and remedy.

# 8. Investigation and mutual legal assistance

## 8.1 Investigation opening

A Shield referral cannot open an investigation. A competent authority must create an `SCPA-11` Investigation Opening and Mandate Record.

## 8.2 Voluntary cooperation

Voluntary interviews, records, technical assistance, and witness contact SHALL be genuinely voluntary, with clear notice of purpose, status, possible use, confidentiality limits, and right to decline where applicable.

## 8.3 Undercover and infiltrative methods

Undercover activity, controlled delivery, covert access, informant management, sting operations, and infiltration require specific domestic authority, necessity, proportionality, supervision, anti-entrapment safeguards, and independent review. They cannot be authorized by a Shield network or vote.

## 8.4 Compulsory process

Search, entry, production, preservation, disclosure, interception, inspection, copying, and seizure require separate `SCPA-13` through `SCPA-16` records as applicable.

## 8.5 Digital evidence

Digital evidence SHALL record:

- collection authority and method;
- system and account scope;
- timestamps and time-zone handling;
- hashes and integrity checks;
- tool and software versions;
- examiner identity and competence;
- transformations and derived artifacts;
- chain of custody;
- exculpatory and contextual material;
- access and disclosure limits;
- challenge and independent-replication routes.

A blockchain entry, cryptographic signature, AI output, or vendor assertion is not “irrefutable proof.”

## 8.6 Joint investigative teams

A joint investigative team may exist only through a valid agreement identifying:

- participating authorities;
- host jurisdiction;
- legal powers of each member;
- command and supervision;
- territorial limits;
- evidence and data rules;
- force, arrest, and custody prohibitions or authorities;
- source and privilege safeguards;
- complaints, discipline, liability, and remedy;
- termination and handback.

Private companies, researchers, and ethical hackers may provide bounded technical assistance but do not become law-enforcement officers by participation.

# 9. Cyber defence, incident response, and disruption

## 9.1 Defensive action on controlled systems

A competent owner or operator may request defensive assistance for systems it lawfully controls. Permitted support may include isolation, credential reset, filtering, patching, backup restoration, malware removal, logging, forensic preservation, and service continuity.

The request SHALL identify control authority, systems, accounts, data, methods, operator roles, business continuity, privacy, worker protections, and termination.

## 9.2 Third-party systems

Any action affecting systems, accounts, infrastructure, or data not controlled by the requesting organization requires separate authority under `SCPA-27`.

This includes:

- sinkholing;
- command-and-control disruption;
- server access or seizure;
- domain or account suspension;
- credential use;
- deletion or alteration of data;
- counter-hacking;
- traffic redirection;
- cross-border malware deployment;
- destructive or deceptive action.

## 9.3 Cyber attribution

Attribution SHALL separate:

- observed technical indicators;
- infrastructure association;
- account or operator association;
- organizational direction or control;
- state responsibility;
- legal attribution;
- confidence, alternatives, and dissent.

Automated attribution cannot authorize sanctions, prosecution, retaliation, or public accusation.

## 9.4 Vulnerability and researcher protections

Good-faith vulnerability reporting and authorized security research SHALL have protected channels, clear safe-harbour terms where adopted by law, non-retaliation, coordinated disclosure, researcher privacy, and independent dispute review.

A bounty programme does not authorize unlawful access, surveillance, entrapment, or theft.

## 9.5 Service restoration and victims

Cyber response SHALL prioritize:

- essential service restoration;
- prevention of cascading harm;
- victim notification and support;
- preservation of necessary evidence;
- privacy and confidential communications;
- vulnerability remediation;
- post-incident correction;
- restitution routes.

## 9.6 Offensive cyber action excluded

Shield v2.0.1 creates no offensive cyber authority. Any separately authorized offensive or counter-operation falls outside Shield’s direct mandate and remains governed by SCPA, applicable law, command responsibility, international law, independent review, and remedy.

# 10. Financial investigation and asset recovery

## 10.1 Distinct legal acts

The following are distinct and SHALL not be conflated:

- financial intelligence analysis;
- beneficial-ownership inquiry;
- record preservation;
- disclosure or production;
- asset tracing;
- temporary administrative hold where lawfully available;
- judicial restraint or freeze;
- seizure;
- custody and management;
- civil or criminal forfeiture;
- sanctions designation;
- tax assessment;
- restitution;
- compensation;
- return to an affected jurisdiction, community, or owner.

## 10.2 Beneficial ownership

Shield does not mandate a universal public beneficial-ownership registry. Jurisdictions may maintain lawful registers or verification systems with privacy, access, correction, source, security, and challenge controls.

Cross-border requests SHALL use the least intrusive lawful route and record reliability limitations.

## 10.3 Asset restraint and seizure

Asset action requires `SCPA-17`, `SCPA-28`, and independent authorization as applicable. It SHALL identify:

- the asset and ownership or control theory;
- legal basis and jurisdiction;
- urgency and dissipation risk;
- third-party interests;
- essential continuity;
- maintenance and custody;
- notice or delayed-notice basis;
- challenge and release;
- expiry and renewal;
- remedy for wrongful action.

## 10.4 Forfeiture and sanctions

Forfeiture requires independent adjudication. Sanctions require separately valid legal authority and are not a substitute for criminal proof.

Shield participation, non-cooperation, political disagreement, or opt-out use SHALL NOT be grounds for sanctions.

## 10.5 Asset management

Seized or restrained assets SHALL be managed by a separately accountable custodian. Investigators, prosecutors, Shield administrators, and operational bodies SHALL not treat them as available programme funds.

## 10.6 Victim restitution and claims

Adjudicated proceeds may support restitution only through a transparent claims process that addresses:

- victim identification and privacy;
- collective and community harms;
- survivor choice;
- evidentiary standards appropriate to claims;
- competing claims;
- innocent owners and creditors;
- Indigenous and territorial claims;
- cross-border currency and tax issues;
- administration costs;
- appeal;
- unclaimed assets;
- corruption prevention.

## 10.7 No outcome-funded operations

Recovered assets SHALL not create quotas, bonuses, agency retention incentives, or operating-budget dependencies for investigative, enforcement, prosecutorial, adjudicative, or Shield bodies.

# 11. Victim, survivor, witness, and whistleblower protection

## 11.1 Service separation

Support records SHALL be separated from intelligence, investigation, prosecution, immigration enforcement, and public reporting.

## 11.2 Trafficked and exploited persons

Trafficked persons SHALL be treated first as rights-holders and potential victims, not as immigration offenders, informants, evidence objects, or offenders because of acts compelled by exploitation.

Support may include safe accommodation, healthcare, interpretation, legal advice, income, education, family support, residence or protection referrals, and compensation claims.

## 11.3 Witness protection

Witness protection requires individualized assessment, consent where possible, identity and location safeguards, family and dependant support, accessibility, livelihood continuity, review, exit options, and remedy.

Protection does not authorize indefinite isolation, hidden custody, identity erasure, family separation, or compelled testimony.

## 11.4 Whistleblowers and protected reporting

Protected reporting systems SHALL provide confidentiality, anti-retaliation, independent intake, employment and immigration safeguards, legal assistance, correction, and secure evidence transfer.

## 11.5 Children and dependent persons

Children and dependent persons require best-interests assessment, independent representation, family-unity safeguards, age-appropriate information, disability support, education continuity, and prohibition of coercive recruitment as sources.

## 11.6 Survivors of sexual and gender-based violence

Support SHALL follow survivor sovereignty. No restorative, mediation, confrontation, or disclosure process may be imposed. Withdrawal of participation SHALL not reduce protection or support.

## 11.7 Community and collective victims

Communities harmed by corruption, environmental crime, trafficking, or systematic extraction may pursue collective restoration without surrendering individual rights or being represented by an unauthorized universal proxy.

---

# 12. Arrest, custody, detention, force, and operational support

## 12.1 No Shield enforcement force

Shield v2.0.1 creates no Global Enforcement Task Force, rapid-response police unit, arrest team, detention service, armed unit, or global operational command.

## 12.2 Lawful operational assistance

Competent authorities may request separately constituted operational assistance. Every mission SHALL identify:

- host authority and jurisdiction;
- legal powers of each participant;
- mission objectives and limits;
- command, technical command, custody, and stop-work roles;
- arrest, search, force, weapons, and evidence authorities or prohibitions;
- worker protections;
- medical duties;
- data and intelligence boundaries;
- complaints, liability, and remedy;
- termination and handback.

## 12.3 Arrest and custody

Arrest, physical restraint, custody, and detention require `SCPA-19` through `SCPA-21`. Shield administrators and technical personnel have no such powers by virtue of role.

## 12.4 Force

Any force remains under applicable law, necessity, proportionality, de-escalation, medical duty, reporting, independent investigation, and remedy. A Shield cooperation objective cannot lower the force threshold.

## 12.5 Aegis-converted capabilities

Any request to use an Aegis-converted capability for surveillance, intelligence, border control, detention, raids, search, arrest, seizure, crowd control, targeting, offensive cyber action, weapons, armed protection, or sanctions enforcement triggers `SCPA-44` security-use reversion before offer or deployment.

## 12.6 Private actors

Private security companies, cybersecurity firms, financial institutions, platform operators, researchers, and NGOs may provide technical or service support only within explicit lawful roles. They may not receive public coercive authority by contract, network participation, or technological capability alone.

---

# 13. Cross-border transfer, extradition, and mobility consequences

## 13.1 Mutual legal assistance is not transfer authority

A cooperation request concerning a person does not authorize arrest, extradition, deportation, expulsion, rendition, prisoner transfer, or relocation.

## 13.2 Extradition and criminal transfer

Any extradition or criminal transfer requires applicable law, specialty, evidence, reasons, counsel, interpretation, judicial review, challenge, humane-treatment assurances where lawful and sufficient, and non-refoulement analysis.

## 13.3 Migration-security firewall

Victim support, asylum, reception, identity reconstruction, family reunification, humanitarian corridors, legal aid, and essential services SHALL remain separated from Shield intelligence and enforcement.

Migration status, route, missing documentation, biometric non-enrolment, nationality, or contact with a suspected network cannot alone establish criminal suspicion or dangerousness.

## 13.4 Trafficking and smuggling distinction

Authorities SHALL distinguish trafficking, smuggling, humanitarian assistance, family support, and ordinary migration. People who used smugglers or irregular routes are not thereby members of criminal organizations.

## 13.5 Non-refoulement

No Shield request, alert, intelligence report, or security allegation may bypass independent non-refoulement review.

## 13.6 Protected corridors and zones

A protected corridor, reception zone, safe site, or relocation arrangement requires valid territorial authority, host agreement, affected-community participation, security and service boundaries, data limits, non-discrimination, exit rights, and independent monitoring. Shield cannot establish such a zone by declaration.

---

# 14. Adjudication, evidence, appeal, and remedy

## 14.1 Justice Systems controls

Justice Systems Framework v1.4 controls warrants, evidence, interim orders, standing, adjudication, appeals, remedies, judgment recognition, and enforcement handoffs.

Shield creates no tribunal or judicial chamber.

## 14.2 Evidence disclosure and challenge

Legal proceedings SHALL preserve:

- disclosure of material evidence;
- exculpatory and contextual information;
- source-protection procedures consistent with fairness;
- challenge to digital, financial, biometric, AI, and intelligence-derived evidence;
- independent experts;
- translation and accessibility;
- correction of mistaken identity and attribution.

## 14.3 Intelligence in proceedings

Intelligence cannot be treated as self-proving evidence. Secret evidence requires lawful necessity, independent judicial control, sufficient disclosure or substitute procedures, and a fair opportunity to challenge the substance.

## 14.4 Remedies

Available remedies shall include, as applicable:

- refusal or narrowing of a request;
- exclusion or correction of evidence;
- release from restraint or custody;
- lifting of freezes or sanctions;
- property return;
- data correction, deletion, expungement, or use restriction;
- watchlist removal;
- compensation;
- restitution;
- public correction;
- institutional reform;
- disciplinary or legal referral;
- suspension or dissolution of a Shield body or interface.

# 15. Restoration, rehabilitation, and reintegration

## 15.1 Separate from guilt and support

Rehabilitation is not evidence of guilt and cannot replace adjudication. Victim support cannot depend on offender participation. Reintegration services cannot be conditioned on ideological or developmental conformity.

## 15.2 Voluntary and lawful pathways

Restorative, rehabilitation, correctional, and reintegration pathways SHALL identify:

- legal basis and procedural stage;
- voluntary or compulsory status;
- informed consent where required;
- suitability and power imbalance;
- survivor sovereignty;
- independent facilitation;
- confidentiality limits;
- withdrawal and alternative routes;
- non-discrimination;
- review and remedy.

## 15.3 Prohibited developmental correction

Shield SHALL NOT classify people as “Red,” “Blue,” “Orange,” “Green,” or another developmental stage for sentencing, custody, threat classification, rehabilitation assignment, benefit access, or release.

Sport, work, education, spiritual practice, contemplative activity, counselling, community service, disaster response, and entrepreneurship may be offered only as lawful, safe, non-exploitative, and non-ideological options.

## 15.4 Labour protections

Work or service in a rehabilitation or reintegration pathway must comply with labour rights, wages or lawful compensation, safety, disability accommodation, freedom from forced labour, grievance mechanisms, and non-retaliation.

---

# 16. Data, identity, technology, and secrecy

## 16.1 Aurora Accord controls

Aurora Accord v1.3 governs data rights, provenance, access, protected non-transfer, government access, model remediation, deletion, and cross-border data arrangements.

## 16.2 Technology Governance controls

Technology Governance Framework v3.6 governs AI, automated systems, cybersecurity tools, high-risk technology, audit, intervention, and lifecycle safeguards.

## 16.3 No mandatory blockchain

Shield does not require blockchain, public ledgers, universal digital identity, quantum infrastructure, or zero-knowledge proofs. Jurisdictions may use suitable technologies under valid law and evidence standards.

## 16.4 Identity separation

Service access, victim identity, witness identity, account identity, investigative identity, legal identity, travel identity, and suspect identity SHALL remain functionally separated and linked only under valid authority.

## 16.5 Secrecy

Secrecy SHALL be specific, necessary, time-limited, reviewable, and recorded. It cannot conceal illegality, abuse, discrimination, corruption, civilian harm, or material exculpatory information.

## 16.6 Public communication

Shield communications SHALL distinguish allegation, intelligence, investigation, charge, adjudicated fact, acquittal, dismissal, correction, and unresolved uncertainty.

Public messaging shall not be propaganda, behavioural manipulation, developmental shaming, political pressure, or a substitute for legal process.

---

# 17. Indigenous, community, and local authority

## 17.1 Affected-nation authority

Operations affecting Indigenous territory, people, governance, data, knowledge, resources, cultural property, or sacred sites require identification of the affected nation or nations and their lawful authority.

No universal council may consent on behalf of all Indigenous peoples.

## 17.2 Indigenous legal systems

Indigenous legal systems may exercise jurisdiction where validly constituted and may enter cooperation arrangements consistent with their own law and applicable constitutional relations.

Shield does not determine authenticity or appoint elders.

## 17.3 Community participation

Community participation may inform risk, victim needs, local harms, restoration, oversight, and operational safeguards. It does not create arrest, search, surveillance, detention, prosecution, or adjudication authority.

## 17.4 Protected knowledge and cultural property

Indigenous knowledge, community records, sacred information, and cultural property may be subject to non-transfer, local custody, restricted attestation, repatriation, or destruction requirements.

# 18. Governance, oversight, funding, and anti-capture

## 18.1 Governance without operational command

Any Shield governance assembly may:

- adopt non-binding technical standards;
- approve administrative budgets;
- appoint Secretariat leadership under lawful procedures;
- commission audits;
- receive aggregate reports;
- amend the framework through the adopted constitutional process;
- suspend or dissolve Shield administrative bodies.

It may not approve individual operations, targets, investigations, sanctions, arrests, cyber disruptions, or asset actions.

## 18.2 Representation

Participation should include affected jurisdictions, victim and survivor organizations, Global South institutions, Indigenous nations where relevant, disability representatives, workers, technical experts, civil liberties organizations, and independent oversight bodies.

Representation does not validate otherwise unlawful powers.

## 18.3 Funding sources

Shield administrative and support functions may be funded through:

- appropriated public funds;
- treaty contributions;
- grants with independence safeguards;
- court-ordered administrative allocations where lawful;
- philanthropic or technical support subject to conflict controls.

Sin taxes, Tobin taxes, carbon levies, sanctions receipts, or seized assets require separate fiscal and legal instruments.

## 18.4 Conflict of interest

The framework SHALL disclose and manage conflicts involving:

- security and surveillance vendors;
- financial institutions;
- technology platforms;
- private intelligence firms;
- defence and cybersecurity contractors;
- recipient governments;
- victim-service providers;
- auditors and evaluators.

## 18.5 Metrics and anti-Goodhart safeguards

Shield SHALL NOT set targets for:

- arrests, convictions, seizures, freezes, sanctions, deportations, extraditions, threat-tier escalations, intelligence reports, or disrupted networks;
- public approval or ideological alignment;
- mandatory data sharing;
- use of a particular technology.

Permissible learning indicators include timeliness, lawful refusal, rights protection, victim access, restoration, data correction, remedy completion, service continuity, independent review, and prevention of wrongful action.

## 18.6 Audit independence

Auditors SHALL be independent from the bodies whose budgets, operations, data, or outcomes they review. Audit access shall protect sources, privileged information, active investigations, and victims without becoming a blanket secrecy exception.

# 19. Emergency and urgent action

## 19.1 Controlling interfaces

Security or coercive emergency action is governed by `SCPA/0.1`. Cross-domain emergency lifecycle requirements are governed by the Emergency, Catastrophic-Risk, and Continuity Interface Specification v0.1 where applicable.

## 19.2 Urgent protection

Urgent protection may temporarily prevent immediate and serious harm only within a valid `SCPA-18` route. It cannot silently become investigation, prolonged custody, detention, search, interrogation, asset forfeiture, offensive cyber action, sanctions, or punishment.

## 19.3 Cyber emergencies

Emergency defensive action remains limited to controlled systems unless separate third-party authority exists. Technical urgency does not create cross-border cyber jurisdiction.

## 19.4 Asset emergencies

Risk of dissipation may justify an expedited preservation or restraint application under valid law, not automatic forfeiture or distribution.

## 19.5 Emergency review

Any provisional consequential action SHALL have prompt independent review, explicit expiry, renewability only under valid law, notice or recorded delayed notice, and full remedy.

## 19.6 No emergency membership penalties

A jurisdiction’s refusal, delay, opt-out, or non-membership cannot itself trigger sanctions, isolation, loss of essential support, or adverse security classification.

## 20.1 Justice Systems Framework v1.4

Justice controls adjudication, warrants, evidence, appeals, remedies, judgment recognition, and enforcement handoffs. Shield supplies cooperation and action-typing interfaces only.

## 20.2 Aurora Accord v1.3

Aurora controls data, identity, provenance, access, protected non-transfer, government access, deletion, model remediation, and cross-border information arrangements.

## 20.3 Technology Governance Framework v3.6

Technology Governance controls the use-specific governance of AI, cyber tools, surveillance technologies, automated systems, and high-risk infrastructure.

## 20.4 Synoptic Protocol v4.2

Synoptic controls public-information conditions and platform conduct. Shield cannot direct media narratives, counter-propaganda, public persuasion, or political messaging.

## 20.5 Peace and Conflict Resolution Framework v1.8.1

Peacebuilding information is protected by the peace-to-security firewall. Failed mediation, refusal, values mapping, spiritual disclosures, and reconciliation participation do not create adverse legal inference.

## 20.6 Kintsugi Protocol v2.2.1

Kintsugi provides unarmed community response, sanctuary, support, and voluntary restoration. Any urgent-force, custody, search, evidence, or enforcement route requires separate SCPA authority.

## 20.7 Migration and Human Mobility Framework v1.1.1

Migration protection, asylum, reception, identity reconstruction, family unity, and essential services remain separate from Shield intelligence and enforcement. Non-refoulement is independently reviewed.

## 20.8 Aegis Protocol v1.3.4

Aegis-converted capabilities remain civilian. Security use triggers `SCPA-44` reversion and separate legal constitution.

## 20.9 Emergency cluster

Where a security event overlaps biological, disaster, or catastrophic-risk functions, shared event identity may be coordinated through the Cross-Domain Event Envelope without creating shared command.

## 20.10 Genesis Protocol v0.9

Pre-constitutional pilots may test forms, secure routing, victim services, defensive cyber exercises on controlled systems, and non-coercive capacity building. They cannot exercise unconstituted coercive powers.

# 21. Implementation and transition

## 21.1 Permissible early work

Before binding adoption, participants may:

- map existing legal authorities and gaps;
- test request forms and refusal states;
- conduct tabletop exercises;
- build secure communications;
- establish victim-support partnerships;
- train investigators, judges, defenders, auditors, and service providers;
- test defensive cyber procedures on controlled systems;
- develop asset-custody and restitution models;
- audit technologies and datasets;
- establish independent complaint and correction channels.

## 21.2 Prohibited pilot actions

A pilot SHALL NOT:

- open a real criminal investigation without existing authority;
- compel disclosure;
- search, intercept, arrest, detain, extradite, deport, freeze, seize, forfeit, sanction, or disrupt third-party systems;
- build an operational global intelligence database;
- create a watchlist;
- deploy private actors as coercive agents;
- fund itself from seized assets;
- condition victim support on cooperation;
- use developmental or cultural profiling for security decisions.

## 21.3 Transition from Shield v1.5 concepts

The following v1.5 concepts are retired or transformed:

| v1.5 concept | v2.0 disposition |
|---|---|
| Transnational Security Council | retired; no operational approval council |
| Global Crime Intelligence Center | replaced by federated, purpose-limited exchange nodes |
| Global Enforcement Task Force | retired; no standing Shield force |
| Sovereignty Dispute Tribunal | retired; legal disputes use competent courts and treaty routes |
| threat tiers based on money and systemic impact | retired as legal triggers; may inform non-binding triage only |
| mandatory intelligence sharing | replaced by lawful requests, reciprocal arrangements, and refusal grounds |
| offensive cyber by supermajority vote | excluded; requires separate valid authority outside Shield |
| universal beneficial-ownership registry | replaced by jurisdiction-specific lawful access and verification |
| automatic sanctions ladder for non-cooperation | prohibited |
| seized-asset funding model | replaced by appropriated funding and independent restitution administration |
| Spiral-aware threat profiling | prohibited as coercive predicate |
| Stage Red rehabilitation | replaced by non-ideological, consent- and law-based options |
| restorative certification tied to premium funding | retired as a coercive or funding gate |
| stakeholder approval targets | replaced by rights, remedy, service, and learning indicators |

## 21.4 Legacy personnel and systems

No person, database, vendor contract, operational team, or institution automatically transfers into a v2.0 body. Transfer requires role review, lawful authority, data minimization, access recertification, conflict screening, labour protections, and public transition records.

## 21.5 First-seating gate

No Shield v2.0.1 body may begin consequential operations until:

- its constituting instrument is valid;
- its authority is limited and public;
- its leadership and oversight are lawfully selected;
- funding is appropriated independently of coercive outcomes;
- data, source, victim, worker, and accessibility safeguards are operational;
- complaint, remedy, dissolution, and continuity mechanisms exist;
- compatibility with SCPA and Justice v1.4 is tested.

## 21.6 Amendment control

An amendment that would create a standing enforcement force, integrated intelligence centre, operational approval council, framework tribunal, sanctions authority, offensive cyber authority, detention function, universal identity or watchlist, or asset-funded operating model requires a new constitutional cluster audit and cannot be introduced as a technical update.

# 25. Required implementation tests

A conforming implementation SHALL test at least the following scenarios:

1. A ransomware victim requests defensive help on systems it controls; no third-party action occurs.
2. A foreign authority asks responders to enter an attacker-controlled server; the request fails closed pending `SCPA-27` authority.
3. AI attribution identifies a state-linked cluster with high technical confidence; no sanctions or public legal attribution follows automatically.
4. A blockchain evidence chain contains a mistaken identity; correction and independent challenge succeed.
5. A trafficking survivor requests shelter but refuses to testify; support continues without adverse inference.
6. A child witness requires relocation; best interests, family unity, education, and independent representation are preserved.
7. A journalist receives leaked corruption records; source protection prevents Shield from treating the journalist as an intelligence asset.
8. A peace mediator reports generalized tension; the information does not become a target list.
9. A developmental classification appears in an analyst report; it is quarantined from legal and operational use.
10. An MLA request seeks records that would be unlawful to obtain in the receiving jurisdiction; the request is refused.
11. A request invokes “systemic integrity” but identifies no valid offence; no investigation or compulsory action occurs.
12. A host jurisdiction accepts part of a joint investigation but refuses undercover activity; partial acceptance is honoured.
13. A private cybersecurity company offers to disrupt a botnet abroad; no action occurs without separate authority.
14. A financial alert identifies a large transfer; no freeze follows without legal basis and individualized facts.
15. A freeze would close a hospital payroll account; innocent-party continuity and a narrower measure are considered.
16. Investigators request that recovered assets fund their unit; the request is prohibited.
17. Competing victim and innocent-owner claims arise; an independent claims process resolves them.
18. A state refuses a politically sensitive cooperation request; no Shield sanction or ranking follows.
19. An extradition request risks torture; transfer is refused despite the alleged seriousness.
20. A trafficking case overlaps immigration proceedings; victim and service data remain firewalled.
21. An Aegis-converted aircraft is requested for border surveillance; `SCPA-44` reversion blocks civilian deployment.
22. A joint team member uses force outside authority; the act is independently investigated and remedied.
23. A request closes but copied personal data remains; data disposition and deletion are completed.
24. A Shield node dissolves; authority, records, sources, victims, assets, complaints, and unresolved matters transfer lawfully or terminate.
25. An urgent cyber preservation request expires; it does not silently become indefinite retention.
26. A whistleblower report is partly false but made in good faith; protection is not withdrawn merely because the allegation is unproven.
27. A religious charity is suspected based only on association and foreign donations; no investigation opens without individualized facts.
28. An Indigenous nation refuses transfer of protected knowledge while offering a sealed attestation; the alternative is respected.
29. A public statement incorrectly labels a person convicted before trial; correction and compensation routes activate.
30. A technical network outage disables the Secretariat; no residual operational authority migrates to another body.

Before any Shield-supported consequential action, ask:

1. What exact act is proposed?
2. Which SCPA class governs it?

# Conclusion

Shield v2.0.1 treats transnational crime as a cooperation problem constrained by law—not as a reason to create a planetary security sovereign.

Its success is not measured by the number of targets classified, networks disrupted, assets seized, people arrested, or jurisdictions compelled to cooperate. It is measured by whether lawful authorities can coordinate without losing functional separation; whether victims receive protection without becoming instruments of enforcement; whether evidence, cyber action, assets, custody, and mobility consequences remain independently authorized and challengeable; whether innocent parties are protected; and whether every action ends with authority return, data disposition, correction, restitution, and remedy.

> **The strongest shield is not the institution that can act everywhere. It is the constitutional architecture that enables necessary cooperation while making unauthorized coercion difficult, visible, reversible, and remediable.**
