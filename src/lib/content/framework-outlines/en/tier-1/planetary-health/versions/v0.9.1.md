## 0. Executive summary

Planetary health is a coordination problem across human health, animal health, ecosystems, culture, infrastructure, and future generations. This framework provides a strategic forum for seeing those interactions, auditing cross-domain effects, enabling affected communities to challenge system-level conclusions, and issuing transparent recommendations to competent institutions.

It does not own ecological observation, recognize ecosystem rights, appoint guardians, bind other frameworks, condition essential funding, impose sanctions, declare emergencies, command specialized responders, adjudicate disputes, or enforce compliance.

> **Synthesis is not supremacy. Audit is not investigation. Recommendation is not command. A planetary view must not erase local authority, distributional harm, or uncertainty.**

### Core institutional split

- The **Ecological Intelligence Commons** maintains ecological methods and evidence.
- The **Planetary Health Synthesis Office (PHSO)** independently compiles the Biosphere Health Index and its modules.
- The **Planetary Health Council (PHC)** interprets published evidence, conducts advisory audits, and issues strategic recommendations.
- **Planetary Health Assemblies** and the **Future Generations Forum** challenge assumptions, distributions, and long-horizon effects.
- Competent legal, sectoral, financial, emergency, and operational authorities decide whether and how to act.

# 1. Planetary Health Charter

Planetary health governance is grounded in:

1. the interdependence of human, animal, ecosystem, and community health;
2. health and essential ecological conditions as public goods and commons;
3. prevention and precaution without automatic coercion;
4. epistemic justice and affected-nation authority;
5. intergenerational and interspecies responsibility;
6. distributional justice—averages cannot hide concentrated harm;
7. plurality of cultural and spiritual meaning without binding metaphysical doctrine;
8. subsidiarity and lawful role separation;
9. reversible, reviewable, and correctable governance;
10. protection of future agency rather than prescription of one future.

# 3. Role separation

## 3.1 Ecological Intelligence

EIOI owns ecological observation methods, ecological indicators, model registries, causal assessment, alerts, and ecological module handoffs. PHG cannot alter source evidence or direct a predetermined result.

## 3.2 Planetary Health Synthesis Office

PHSO is technically and administratively independent from PHC deliberation. It compiles modules under a published methodology and cannot recommend policy. Its governing charter protects professional independence, conflict disclosure, correction, open methods, low-tech participation, and protected data.

## 3.3 Planetary Health Council

PHC receives published evidence, conducts system-level audits, identifies interactions and blind spots, convenes dialogue, and issues recommendations. PHC cannot issue binding directives, sanctions, funding freezes, emergency orders, guardian appointments, rights decisions, or operational commands.

## 3.4 Assemblies and future generations

Planetary Health Assemblies and the Future Generations Forum have rights to information, explanation, challenge, reconsideration, dissent, and independent review. They do not possess automatic vetoes over persons, property, budgets, permits, or projects merely because a threshold is crossed.

## 3.5 Specialized frameworks

Public health, animal welfare, biodiversity, water, oceans, soil, technology, finance, justice, emergency, and other specialized frameworks retain their lawful roles. PHG coordinates; it does not absorb them.

# 4. Governance architecture

## 4.1 Planetary Health Council

PHC composition should include Earth-systems science, public health, animal health, Indigenous and affected-community representatives, ethics, youth, disability expertise, labour, and regional diversity. Members serve fixed terms, disclose conflicts, and may be recalled or removed under a published process.

PHC deliberations must identify which statements are scientific findings, normative judgments, scenario assumptions, strategic recommendations, or unresolved questions.

## 4.2 Planetary Health Synthesis Office

PHSO maintains:

- the module registry;
- the BHI methodology charter;
- versioned releases;
- uncertainty and dissent records;
- correction propagation;
- accessible public explanations;
- protected-data controls;
- historical reproducibility.

## 4.3 Planetary Health Assemblies

Assemblies are affected-community review bodies. They may require PHC and PHSO to explain methods, correct local representation, include missing evidence, publish dissent, or commission independent review. An assembly cannot speak for an affected Indigenous nation unless that nation expressly authorizes it.

## 4.4 Future Generations Forum

The Forum conducts long-horizon impact review. It may place a recommendation under reconsideration and require a published response. It may not claim to represent future persons as a sovereign constituency or create a general veto.

## 4.5 Independent Audit and Methodology Review Board

This board reviews conflicts, audit fairness, BHI methods, model governance, and complaints. It cannot decide the substantive policy response or exercise enforcement powers.

# 5. Biosphere Health Index constitution

## 5.1 Purpose

The BHI is a modular synthesis and navigation tool. It helps decision-makers see interactions among ecological integrity, human health, animal health, social conditions, and distributional burdens.

## 5.2 Non-purpose

The BHI is not:

- a replacement for GDP by legal fiat;
- a legal threshold for rights, vetoes, permits, sanctions, or funding;
- proof of causation, compliance, guilt, stewardship success, or community worth;
- a licence to compare culturally incommensurable values without explanation;
- a single optimization objective for civilization.

## 5.3 Module architecture

Each module retains its own custodian, method, version, units, uncertainty, and access rules. A composite release must show the modules and distributions. Non-derogable essential minimums and catastrophic localized harm cannot be averaged away.

## 5.4 Weighting

Weighting is an explicit normative choice. PHSO conducts a public process, publishes sensitivity analysis, records dissent, and keeps alternative views available. PHC cannot secretly or retroactively change weights.

## 5.5 Cultural and spiritual measures

LMCI, cultural vitality, sacred relationship, and spiritual well-being may be included only as voluntary, context-specific, non-diagnostic evidence. They cannot be used to rank communities, infer developmental stage, justify intervention, or condition rights and resources.

# 6. Planetary Health Audits

An audit identifies cross-framework effects and recommends correction. It is not an investigation, adjudication, or compliance order.

Every audit SHALL include:

- a published question and scope;
- source and legal character;
- conflict disclosures;
- evidence and access rules;
- affected-framework and affected-community notice;
- answer, correction, context, and dissent rights;
- distributional and affected-nation analysis;
- uncertainty and alternative explanations;
- draft review;
- final finding and recommendation separation;
- expiry, follow-up, correction, and closure.

PHC may request information. Compulsory inspection, confidential seizure, witness compulsion, or evidentiary investigation requires separate authority.

# 8. Rights, guardianship, and commons boundaries

PHC may recommend that a competent body open ecological-rights review. It cannot recognize a rights-holder, define a legal boundary, appoint or instruct guardians, designate sacredness, transfer title, establish a trust, revoke stewardship, or determine compensation.

The International Tribunal for the Rights of Nature, if lawfully constituted elsewhere, is not a PHG institution and receives no jurisdiction from a PHC referral alone.

---

# 9. Future generations and procedural challenge

The Future Generations Forum identifies irreversible risk, option foreclosure, closure debt, long time horizons, and distribution across generations. Its output is a reasoned challenge requiring response and reconsideration.

No numerical threshold creates an automatic youth veto. A lawful jurisdiction may separately enact veto or consent rights, but must identify scope, authority, standing, review, override, expiry, and remedy.

---

# 10. Indigenous health sovereignty and epistemic justice

Affected Indigenous nations retain authority over their health systems, Traditional Knowledge, sacred relations, territories, participation, consent, representation, and data.

Earth Council or global Indigenous representation may advise and audit process integrity. It cannot consent, waive rights, disclose knowledge, designate sacredness, or speak for another nation.

---

# 11. Emergency boundary

The former PHC “binding intervention authority,” 90-day emergency power, supermajority operational override, and automatic real-time data sharing are retired.

PHC may issue `PHG-24` and refer a possible emergency to CDEE/ECRC. Emergency authorities independently decide declaration, command, resource mobilization, action, renewal, termination, and remedy.

PHSO and PHC may share minimum-necessary information under `PHG-25`. Health, ecological, asylum, Indigenous, survivor, and protected data do not automatically migrate into crisis or security systems.

---

# 12. Funding and incentive firewall

PHC may identify funding needs, distributional injustice, and priority gaps. It may not allocate GCF resources, impose levies, freeze funding, direct Hearts or Leaves, or condition essential services on BHI performance.

Funding authorities must distinguish stewardship effort from ecological outcome, and local action from upstream, historical, climatic, or transboundary drivers. Communities must not be punished for inherited or externally caused harm.

---

# 13. Technology and precaution

PHC may identify health and biospheric concerns related to AI, biotechnology, synthetic biology, nanotechnology, or other technologies. Regulatory decisions, moratoria, deployment conditions, shutdown, containment, and enforcement belong to TGIF, PIS, justice, or other competent authorities.

No PHC subcommittee receives a general kill switch or containment mandate.

---

# 14. Health commons and essential minimums

PHG treats clean air, water, sanitation, soil, seeds, food, habitat, public health, and necessary care as shared foundations of flourishing. It may recommend protection but cannot itself nationalize, transfer, decommodify, allocate, ration, or restrict a resource.

Every downstream action must preserve essential minimums and non-deprivation under CERGTA-36 and CERGTA-38.

---

# 15. Education, dialogue, and public communication

Planetary Health Literacy, cultural dialogue, spiritual dialogue, citizen science, and professional training are voluntary, plural, accessible, and non-doctrinal. Public materials must distinguish aspiration, evidence, recommendation, adopted law, and operational authority.

A public ratification tracker, target, campaign, or endorsement count cannot activate legal power or preferential essential funding.

# 16. Cross-framework interfaces

- **EIOI:** source of ecological modules and alerts.
- **Global Health and pandemic frameworks:** source of human-health evidence and competent operational response.
- **Animal welfare:** source of animal-health and welfare evidence.
- **Biodiversity, Water, Oceans, Soil, and Hearthstone:** competent implementation frameworks receiving advisory findings.
- **CERGTA:** controls rights, guardianship, title, stewardship, allocation, restriction, finance, adjudication, and restoration routes.
- **CDEE/ECRC:** controls emergency identity and coordination.
- **SCPA:** controls coercive execution and security-facing action.
- **TGIF and Oracle:** control technology and digital-sentience questions; PHG supplies biospheric-health analysis only.
- **MOS:** supplies advisory ethical reflection, never legal status or scientific validation.

# 20. Constitutional readiness gates

1. Exact versions and hashes are pinned.
2. PHG states that it is strategic, coordinative, participatory, and advisory—not a rights, title, guardian, funding, emergency, investigation, adjudication, or enforcement authority.
3. Ecological observation and ecological methodology remain with EIOI and lawful source custodians.
4. The BHI is compiled by an independent synthesis office rather than by PHC decision-makers.
5. Every BHI release shows module scores, distributions, uncertainty, dissent, and method version.
6. No module may compensate for a non-derogable essential minimum or hide concentrated harm.
7. PHC cannot change weights or methods in order to obtain a preferred policy result.
8. Planetary Health Audits have scope, evidence rules, conflicts, answer rights, and closure.
9. An audit request is not a compulsory inspection or investigation.
10. An audit finding is separate from a strategic recommendation.
11. A strategic recommendation is separate from legal adoption or enforcement.
12. Rights review uses CERGTA-11/12 and lawful recognition remains CERGTA-13.
13. Future Generations review creates a duty to answer and reconsider, not an automatic veto.
14. Planetary Health Assemblies may challenge, correct, and seek independent review without acquiring general jurisdiction.
15. Affected Indigenous nations retain authority over their health systems, knowledge, territories, and consent.
16. Spiritual, cultural, LMCI, or social-cohesion measures cannot create adverse legal or funding consequences.
17. Funding recommendations cannot condition emergency or essential services on BHI improvement.
18. A BHI threshold may trigger review but not sanctions, trade restrictions, freezes, guardianship, or receivership.
19. Emergency alerts route to CDEE/ECRC; PHC cannot declare, command, renew, or override an emergency.
20. Crisis information sharing is minimum-necessary, purpose-limited, logged, and non-automatic.
21. Specialized frameworks retain operational authority and may reject PHC recommendations with reasons.
22. Technology ethics recommendations route to TGIF or another competent authority; PHC has no kill switch.
23. Corrections propagate to every known policy, funding, legal, and public recipient.
24. No treaty threshold, coalition count, future date, dashboard status, or public target self-activates authority.
25. Audits and methods have expiry, termination, data disposition, and unresolved-duty custody.
26. The role-separation matrix is incorporated and tested.

# 23. Success and evaluation

PHG success is measured by whether it:

- reveals cross-domain harms earlier;
- prevents one framework from exporting costs to another;
- represents distributions and affected communities honestly;
- improves the quality of recipient decisions;
- preserves local and Indigenous authority;
- records disagreement and uncertainty;
- corrects errors before they harden into law or finance;
- reduces fragmented governance without creating a planetary super-authority.

## A.2 Controlling rules

1. `CERGTA/0.1` controls the separation of observation, interpretation, rights review, legal recognition, guardianship, affected Indigenous-nation authority, sacred and biocultural designation, claims, title, tenure, stewardship, allocation, permitting, inspection, urgent protection, restrictions, finance, adjudication, restoration, and closure.
2. `EIPHR/0.1` controls the division among EIOI, the Planetary Health Synthesis Office, and the Planetary Health Council. EIOI supplies evidence; PHSO compiles modular synthesis; PHC interprets and recommends. None may create a downstream legal or financial consequence.
3. MOS is advisory only. Its Dynamic Rights Spectrum, philosophical language, developmental translations, structural-failure hypotheses, and guardianship metaphors never create legal status, classify persons, determine credibility, establish Indigenous consent, or activate consequential action.
4. Hearthstone controls the cluster interface for claims, competing claims, title, tenure, trust constitution, voluntary conversion, rematriation, compensation, debt, tenants, workers, registry correction, receivership, succession, and dissolution. Sectoral ecological frameworks may provide evidence or programme support but cannot decide those questions by implication.
5. Water, Biodiversity, Oceans, and Soil retain only the powers separately constituted in applicable law and recorded through the relevant CERGTA route. Framework membership, a council vote, a dashboard value, a target, a pilot, a future date, or a coalition size does not activate authority.
6. Emergency identity and coordination use CDEE and ECRC. Ecological urgency does not create emergency command, and an emergency declaration does not create ecological title, guardianship, permanent restrictions, receivership, or enforcement authority.
7. Search, seizure, restraint, custody, force, cyber action, border action, intelligence use, asset freezing, or analogous coercive execution requires separate SCPA authority. A commons or ecological claim may justify review; it does not authorize a raid.
8. The specific affected Indigenous nation or nations determine their representatives, applicable law, territorial and relational authority, consent process, internal plurality, protected knowledge, and whether to participate. Global or framework-created Indigenous bodies may support or audit but cannot consent or waive rights for another people.
9. Essential minimums for water, sanitation, health, disability support, food, shelter, rescue, crew welfare, and ecological survival remain protected from commercial, tariff, certification, funding, listing, or transition leverage.
10. Every handoff uses the canonical CERGTA states. Receipt, technical ingestion, silence, consultation, or partial use is not acceptance and never creates authority.
11. Every consequential route keeps its own legal basis, decision-maker, affected persons, evidence, access controls, clock, expiry, challenge, correction, remedy, termination, and data-disposition record.
12. Unknown, missing, stale, or incompatible versions fail closed for consequential action. Non-coercive care, emergency life-safety support, accessibility, explanation, and correction should continue wherever lawful and safe.

## A.3 Record inheritance and packaging

A local record may implement, extend, split, contain, import, or mark a CERGTA record not applicable. It may not erase the canonical identity, merge distinct powers, weaken an affected-person safeguard, or use a compound package to supply a missing legal basis.

The forty-field CERGTA header, access classification, handoff state, independent clocks, correction propagation, remedy, termination, authority return, and data disposition are inherited wherever the substantive base creates or transports a consequential record. Advisory and observation-only records must state their prohibited inferences.

## A.4 Registry and compatibility failure

The file `commons-ecological-authority-cluster-conformance-registry-v0.1.json` is the machine-readable release registry. It is evidence of the exact release set, not an authority record. A hash mismatch, missing component, unsupported local extension, or unresolved version conflict changes the compatibility state to `failed_closed`.

A compatibility failure blocks new consequential cross-framework handoffs. It does not by itself terminate independently valid local law, erase existing rights, interrupt essential services, or authorize emergency or coercive action.

## A.5 Closure

A cluster transaction closes only after all accepted handoffs, powers, services, persons, property, guardianship duties, claims, title or tenure effects, financial actions, emergency dependencies, coercive dependencies, corrections, remedies, clocks, credentials, records, and residual risks are resolved or assigned to a named lawful custodian without preserving expired power.
