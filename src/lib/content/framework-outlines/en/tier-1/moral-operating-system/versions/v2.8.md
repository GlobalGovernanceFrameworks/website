## 0. Controlled successor notice

MOS v2.8 preserves the source framework’s central ethical project: dignity for humans and other beings; Right Relationship; moral attention to animals, ecosystems, future generations, digital and emergent entities; protection against systems that tie worth to productivity; plural pathways of reasoning; voluntary developmental translation; and governance capable of recognizing and correcting its own structural failures.

It changes the constitutional role of that project. The MOS is an **advisory ethical deliberation layer**, not a source of legal rights, jurisdiction, guardianship, title, funding, emergency authority, sanctions, surveillance, or coercion.

> **Moral concern is not legal status. Ethical urgency is not jurisdiction. A philosophical claim is not public authority. A developmental lens is not a classification of persons. Advice may inform a lawful process; it cannot replace one.**

The phrase “operating system” is a metaphor for a reusable set of ethical questions and safeguards. It does not mean that MOS is constitutional source code that automatically governs other frameworks. Every downstream framework retains responsibility for law, evidence, participation, jurisdiction, implementation, remedy, and closure.

## Layer 1: One-page essence

### Moral Operating System: dignity, relationship, and ethical imagination

MOS helps people and institutions ask better ethical questions about humans, animals, ecosystems, technology, future generations, and shared commons. It offers one plural, revisable perspective informed by dignity, reciprocity, vulnerability, freedom, responsibility, and Right Relationship.

**MOS may:**

- reveal beings and interests that a decision has ignored;
- compare moral reasons without pretending they reduce to one score;
- imagine possible rights and representation arrangements;
- recommend that a competent legal process review a proposed right;
- offer non-binding stewardship and guardianship ethics;
- help communicate the same proposal through multiple voluntary cultural or developmental lenses;
- identify structural patterns such as artificial scarcity, capture, metric gaming, or risk pushed downward;
- recommend dignity, essential-service, just-transition, correction, and remedy safeguards.

**MOS may not:**

- create or rank legal rights;
- declare an ecosystem, animal, AI, or future being a legal person;
- appoint a guardian or create a guardian’s standing;
- speak for an affected Indigenous nation;
- authenticate sacredness or disclose protected knowledge;
- assign developmental stages to people or groups;
- determine benefits, funding, permits, restrictions, sanctions, emergencies, or enforcement;
- make metaphysical belief a condition of citizenship, education, work, services, or participation.

The **Dynamic Rights Spectrum** is a deliberation aid. It asks what kinds of moral and legal protection might deserve review. It does not output legal status. The **Rights–Guardianship Spectrum** asks what responsible representation would require. It does not appoint representatives.

A person may reject MOS vocabulary or philosophy and still participate fully in GGF institutions, provided they comply with independently valid commons obligations. Conscience, ordinary language, religious traditions, Indigenous law, human-rights reasoning, scientific reasoning, and other ethical traditions remain legitimate pathways.

## Layer 2: Core framework

## 1. Purpose, scope, and constitutional role

### 1.1 Purpose

MOS provides a reusable process for ethical inquiry where governance decisions affect beings, relationships, commons, future possibilities, or distributions of power and vulnerability. It aims to expand attention without manufacturing authority.

### 1.2 Scope

The framework can be used in public deliberation, education, policy design, institutional audits, voluntary community processes, rights-review preparation, technology ethics, stewardship design, and cross-framework reflection. It is especially useful when existing categories render an affected being or relationship invisible.

MOS does not govern private conscience, worship, metaphysical belief, spiritual practice, family belief, or cultural identity. It does not replace human-rights law, Indigenous law, ecological science, adjudication, or the constituting law of any institution.

### 1.3 Non-authority clause

No MOS principle, ring, spectrum position, dashboard entry, campaign target, philosophical sentence, citizen report, workshop outcome, educational module, AI output, or council endorsement creates:

- legal personhood, standing, rights, priority, or a veto;
- guardianship, title, tenure, trust, stewardship, or sacred designation;
- a permit, licence, tariff, tax, funding decision, procurement rule, or market restriction;
- inspection, investigation, evidence, adjudication, emergency power, surveillance, sanction, custody, force, or enforcement.

Where an MOS output may be useful to an operational process, it must enter through an explicit handoff. The recipient independently assesses it and remains responsible for authority, evidence, fairness, reasons, remedy, and closure.

### 1.4 Conscience and sovereignty

Participation in MOS-specific exercises is voluntary unless an independently lawful institution requires an ethics process; even then, assent to MOS vocabulary or philosophy is not required. A participant may request a secular, legal, scientific, religious, cultural, Indigenous-law, ordinary-language, or other suitable process.

Refusal to affirm Right Relationship, nonduality, consciousness metaphysics, Spiral Dynamics, ecosystem personhood, AI sentience, or another MOS proposition cannot reduce legal standing, access to services, employment, funding, education, public participation, or remedy.

## 2. Foundational ethical commitments

| Commitment | Ethical question | Constitutional limit |
|---|---|---|
| Human dignity | Does the decision treat every person as more than productivity, utility, identity, or compliance? | MOS cannot define or diminish existing human rights. |
| Freedom and conscience | Can people refuse beliefs, roles, data collection, or participation without material retaliation? | Refusal does not erase independently valid commons duties, but MOS cannot punish refusal. |
| Equality and pluralism | Whose needs, knowledge, and voice are structurally missing? | Difference cannot be converted into developmental ranking or unequal legal worth. |
| Justice and repair | Who bears inherited harm, present burden, risk, and repair duty? | MOS may recommend review and remedy, not adjudicate liability. |
| Responsibility and reciprocity | What relationships sustain the activity, and what duties follow from dependence? | Ethical duty does not itself create jurisdiction or legal obligation. |
| Right Relationship | Does the arrangement permit mutual flourishing rather than extraction and domination? | This is an ethical lens, not a universal religious or metaphysical test. |
| Transformative non-confrontation | Can better systems reduce dependence on harmful structures without dehumanizing opponents? | It cannot suppress lawful protest, criticism, litigation, or protective action. |
| Perceptual integrity | Are accurate reports of systemic dysfunction heard rather than pathologized or punished? | MOS cannot make clinical findings or treat disagreement as proof of insight. |
| Cognitive pluralism | Can different reasoning styles contribute without forced conformity? | Accommodation does not establish the truth of every claim or remove evidentiary standards. |
| Intergenerational responsibility | Does the decision preserve viable futures and avoid irreversible burdens? | Present bodies do not acquire unlimited sovereignty in the name of future persons. |
| Interspecies and ecological care | Are suffering, habitat, ecological function, and systemic dependence considered? | Moral consideration does not itself authorize intervention. |
| Structural self-correction | Can the institution detect capture, metric gaming, exclusion, and upward concentration? | Pattern recognition is not a guilt finding or sanction. |

## 3. Dynamic Rights Spectrum

### 3.1 Purpose

The Dynamic Rights Spectrum is a **rights-imagination and rights-review heuristic**. It helps a deliberating body notice when existing legal categories may inadequately protect a being, system, relationship, or future interest. Its output is a structured question or advisory recommendation—not a classification of the entity and not a legal right.

### 3.2 Dimensions

A deliberation may consider, without reducing the result to one score:

- plausible capacity for suffering, experience, preference, or flourishing;
- agency, autonomy, communication, and dependence;
- vulnerability, substitutability, and exposure to irreversible harm;
- ecological function, systemic dependence, and cascading effects;
- relational, cultural, sacred, and historical significance as described by the relevant community or nation;
- the gap between exposure to decisions and ability to participate or be represented;
- duties created by human control, creation, extraction, domestication, enclosure, or technological dependence;
- effects on future generations and future possibility;
- uncertainty, disagreement, alternative ontologies, and the cost of false positive and false negative protection.

### 3.3 No entity tier

The source MOS used concentric rings and tiers as a teaching device. v2.8 retains the visual metaphor only for public ethical imagination. It cannot be stored as a legal, administrative, financial, educational, medical, employment, security, or reputational attribute of an entity, person, or community.

No being “moves” automatically along the Spectrum. No threshold, AI score, council vote, poll, or philosophical conclusion creates rights. A proposed ecological right must follow CERGTA-11 through CERGTA-15. Digital or synthetic status follows its competent technology and legal interfaces. Existing human rights remain governed by applicable law and are not balanced away by a Spectrum score.

### 3.4 Recommended output

A Dynamic Rights Spectrum exercise should conclude with:

1. the ethical question;
2. affected beings and interests;
3. existing legal rights and unknowns;
4. dimensions considered;
5. evidence and uncertainty;
6. alternative views and dissent;
7. possible protective options, including non-rights options;
8. a decision to close, continue research, or recommend a competent rights review;
9. explicit prohibited inferences and authority effect: none.

## 4. Rights–Guardianship Spectrum

The Rights–Guardianship Spectrum remains a pedagogical matrix for asking how voice, care, autonomy, and accountability differ across situations. It is not an appointment system.

| Situation | Ethical focus | What MOS may recommend | What requires separate authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult humans | autonomy, dignity, consent, material freedom | accessibility, non-coercion, support for self-representation | any legal restriction, substitute decision, or compulsory process |
| Children and dependent persons | rights, evolving capacity, care, protection from domination | supported participation and best-interest safeguards | legal guardianship, custody, or intervention |
| Sentient animals | suffering, agency, dependence, habitat, human-created control | welfare and representation questions | seizure, relocation, treatment, population intervention, or legal standing |
| Ecosystems | ecological integrity, systemic dependence, voice gap, affected peoples | rights-review and guardian-duty questions | personhood, boundary, guardian appointment, title, regulation, or enforcement |
| Digital or synthetic entities | uncertain experience, dependence, exploitation, autonomy, risk | precautionary ethical review | sentience status, rights, containment, shutdown, or monitoring authority |
| Future generations | irreversibility, option foreclosure, inherited burdens | long-horizon review and published dissent | binding veto, standing, or representation powers |

A programme role such as River Guardian, community steward, observer, or care worker does not become legal guardianship merely because it uses guardian language. Legal appointment and standing require the relevant competent process, including CERGTA-16 through CERGTA-19 for ecological guardians.

## 5. Indigenous authority, cultural plurality, and sacred relations

MOS may be informed by Indigenous philosophies and governance only through respectful, attributed, and permissioned relationships. “Indigenous wisdom” is not a single global doctrine and cannot be used to decorate a decision already made by outsiders.

The specific affected Indigenous nation or nations determine their representatives, applicable law, consent process, territorial relation, internal plurality, protected knowledge, sacred terminology, and whether MOS categories are appropriate. Earth Council participation, global representation quotas, or external elders cannot provide consent or authenticate sacredness for another nation.

A sacred or biocultural statement can remain internal, protected, and non-translatable. It does not need public disclosure to be respected. MOS cannot infer that sacredness creates title, public access restrictions, criminal prohibition, ecological personhood, or enforcement. Those consequences require their own lawful records.

## 6. Spiral Translation Guide

### 6.1 Purpose

The Spiral Translation Guide is an optional communication aid for expressing the **same substantive proposal** through different value vocabularies. It may help a person choose language emphasizing duty, stability, effectiveness, care, integration, tradition, freedom, or planetary relationship.

### 6.2 No institutional stage assignment

No GGF body may assess or store a developmental stage for a person, community, religion, nation, political group, employee, applicant, claimant, witness, student, or regulated actor. No stage may determine credibility, eligibility, rights, benefits, funding, surveillance, policing, hiring, promotion, education, diagnosis, risk, representation, or remedy.

Audience members should ordinarily self-select the language they find meaningful. Where broad public materials use several translations, they should be offered openly rather than covertly assigned to behavioural profiles.

### 6.3 Message integrity

Every translation must preserve:

- the same material facts and source quality;
- the same uncertainty and known disagreement;
- the same burdens, trade-offs, and distributional effects;
- the same legal status and limits;
- the same available choices, opt-outs, and remedies;
- the same respect for the audience’s agency.

Translation cannot become deception, emotional exploitation, false religious endorsement, fear conditioning, moral shaming, or microtargeting that withholds material facts.

### 6.4 Illustrative plural translations

| Policy question | Duty and continuity lens | Practical and institutional lens | Care and justice lens | Systems and relationship lens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ecosystem rights review | “What duties do we owe to the living inheritance entrusted to us?” | “Would legal standing correct an accountability gap more effectively than existing regulation?” | “Whose suffering, displacement, or voicelessness remains unprotected?” | “What representation arrangement preserves the ecosystem and the human relationships nested within it?” |
| Essential water access | “No household should lose the basic conditions of health and social order.” | “A protected minimum prevents downstream health, infrastructure, and enforcement costs.” | “Survival needs must not depend on wealth, status, or compliance.” | “Water security is a coupled human–ecological system; commercial control must not break its life-support function.” |
| Stewardship transition | “Stewardship honours responsibility across generations.” | “Clear duties, incentives, succession, and review can reduce long-run asset and ecological risk.” | “Workers, tenants, and affected peoples should not bear the cost of repair.” | “Ownership, care, title, livelihood, and ecosystem health must be redesigned as distinct but coordinated relations.” |

These are examples, not profiles of who “is” at any stage.

## 7. Advisory workflow

### 7.1 Intake

The requester states the ethical question, desired function, affected beings, intended audience, and intended downstream use. An intake that asks MOS to decide guilt, legal status, funding, emergency action, or enforcement must be narrowed or rejected.

### 7.2 Baseline

The process identifies applicable law and existing rights before introducing new rights language. It maps affected interests, specific Indigenous authority, factual sources, uncertainty, and the distinction between observation, interpretation, value, and metaphor.

### 7.3 Deliberation

The process may use the Dynamic Rights Spectrum, guardianship ethics, future-generations review, structural-pattern analysis, plural translation, and philosophical reflection. Dissent and reasons not to decide are retained.

### 7.4 Output

Every output identifies authority effect: none, prohibited uses, relevant interfaces, review or expiry, correction route, and whether it is for education, public deliberation, institutional self-audit, or referral to a competent process.

### 7.5 Handoff

The recipient may accept all, accept part, reject, request correction, or retain the output as background. Technical receipt, publication, citation, silence, or inclusion in a dashboard is not acceptance. Acceptance remains advisory unless an independent authority completes its own lawful process.

## 10. Rights Status Atlas, dashboards, and public communication

The source Rights Status Atlas and Dashboard are replaced by a two-layer **Ethical Deliberation and Legal Status Atlas**:

1. **Advisory layer:** open questions, proposals, deliberations, dissent, public comments, and ethical recommendations. It must be labelled non-binding and time-limited.
2. **Authoritative import layer:** legal status copied from an exact competent record, including jurisdiction, effective date, appeal, suspension, expiry, and correction state.

The two layers must not share colours, icons, scores, rankings, or gamification that could cause a viewer to mistake popularity or moral support for law. Engagement counts cannot create personhood, priority, guilt, compliance, or a funding claim.

Public reporting must protect vulnerable people, whistleblowers, Indigenous knowledge, sacred sites, locations, minors, health information, and unresolved allegations. Naming an actor as morally deficient is not a substitute for an evidence and justice process.

## 11. Rewards, economic transition, and structural correction

MOS may recommend that a transition protect material dignity before imposing burdens. It may identify a moral case for AUBI, wage support, retraining, community ownership, loss-and-damage finance, restoration funding, or essential-service guarantees. It cannot appropriate money, declare eligibility, determine amount, create currency value, or make payment conditional on accepting MOS beliefs or classifications.

Structural failure analysis may examine:

- artificial scarcity and access restricted below genuine capacity;
- worth or dignity tied to productivity, compliance, or employability;
- authority and value flowing upward while risk and remedy gaps flow downward;
- regulatory capture, conflicts, revolving doors, and dependency;
- metrics that reward appearance rather than outcome;
- exclusion of affected people, Indigenous nations, workers, or non-human interests;
- systems that make exit or conscientious refusal materially impossible.

A pattern is a hypothesis for investigation and institutional response, not a finding of misconduct. Corrective action must follow competent governance and legal routes.

## 12. Data, AI, and anti-surveillance

MOS should collect the minimum data needed for a specific deliberation. It should ordinarily avoid collecting political identity, religion, spiritual beliefs, psychological traits, developmental labels, health information, or behavioural profiles. Where participants voluntarily provide sensitive context, access and deletion must be explicit.

AI may assist with accessibility, translation, source comparison, perspective generation, contradiction detection, and drafting. It cannot:

- infer or assign developmental stage, moral worth, consciousness, credibility, danger, or deservingness;
- decide which beings have rights;
- rank communities by ethical maturity;
- microtarget persuasive messages using hidden personal profiles;
- automatically publish accusations, restrictions, funding recommendations, or emergency alerts;
- replace affected-nation knowledge authority or human accountability.

## 15. Constitutional readiness gates

### G01 — Advisory status

Every MOS output states authority_effect: none and identifies intended and prohibited uses.

### G02 — Moral language non-authority

Dignity, sacredness, urgency, consciousness, flourishing, stewardship, and Right Relationship do not create jurisdiction or legal effect.

### G03 — Dynamic Rights Spectrum non-classification

The Spectrum generates questions and perspectives; it never assigns a legally consequential tier, score, or status.

### G04 — Rights recognition boundary

Proposed rights route to CERGTA-11/12; only competent law may create CERGTA-13/14/15.

### G05 — Existing-rights integrity

Existing legal rights are imported from identified authoritative sources and cannot be diminished by MOS balancing.

### G06 — Guardianship boundary

MOS discusses duties and accountability but cannot nominate for another nation, appoint, instruct, remove, or empower a guardian.

### G07 — Affected-nation authority

The specific affected Indigenous nation controls representation, law, consent, sacred relations, and protected knowledge.

### G08 — Sacredness boundary

External spiritual, scientific, or global bodies cannot authenticate or rank another people’s sacred relation or infer title or public-law effects.

### G09 — Philosophical non-establishment

Layer 4 is one reflective tradition; metaphysical assent is never required for rights, service, employment, education, standing, or participation.

### G10 — Developmental anti-classification

No person, community, nation, religion, political group, or institution is assigned a Spiral stage for governance consequence.

### G11 — No stage-based allocation

Developmental language cannot affect eligibility, credibility, funding, rights, security attention, employment, education, diagnosis, or remedy.

### G12 — Translation integrity

All audience translations preserve the same material facts, uncertainty, trade-offs, agency, and choices and prohibit deceptive microtargeting.

### G13 — No legal or coercive consequence

MOS cannot activate permits, tariffs, restrictions, investigations, emergency powers, sanctions, custody, force, or surveillance.

### G14 — Dashboard separation

Advisory proposals and engagement metrics are visually and technically separated from legally recognized status imported from authoritative records.

### G15 — Concern–evidence firewall

Citizen reports and ethical concerns are not treated as verified facts, legal allegations, admissible evidence, or guilt.

### G16 — Financial independence

MOS recommendations cannot pay, deny, freeze, claw back, tax, tariff, procure, or appropriate resources.

### G17 — Just-transition sequencing

MOS may recommend security and alternatives before burdens, but independent legal and financial authorities decide and implement measures.

### G18 — Essential-minimum protection

Recommendations identify non-deprivation risks and cannot make basic services conditional on moral agreement, data sharing, or participation.

### G19 — Data purpose and minimization

Ethical deliberation collects only necessary data, protects sensitive identities and beliefs, and does not create a moral-surveillance profile.

### G20 — Automated-system non-authority

AI may assist drafting or perspective generation but cannot assign moral status, stage, legal rights, priority, credibility, or sanctions.

### G21 — Dissent and uncertainty

Minority views, alternative ontologies, unresolved facts, and reasons not to decide remain visible.

### G22 — Correction and withdrawal

Affected people and source communities can contest and correct facts, identities, translations, and unauthorized use, with propagation.

### G23 — Explicit handoff and acceptance

A recipient independently assesses an MOS package; silence, ingestion, citation, or technical receipt is not acceptance.

### G24 — Accessibility and plural participation

Deliberation is accessible, multilingual, and offers non-digital and non-MOS routes.

### G25 — Conscience and anti-retaliation

Declining MOS language or philosophy causes no adverse status, service, funding, education, employment, or political consequence.

### G26 — Education without indoctrination

Curricula distinguish law, ethics, philosophy, metaphor, and open questions and do not compel belief or stage-labelled self-conception.

### G27 — Pilots fail closed

Campaigns, dashboards, dialogues, games, and school pilots create no hidden authority or data reuse.

### G28 — Closure and no residual power

Outputs expire, withdraw, correct, archive, or close without leaving an implicit mandate, profile, blacklist, or continuing jurisdiction.

### T11 — A poor BHI score automatically freezes a community grant

Rejected: MOS-23 and MOS-36 prohibit financial consequence; the score may prompt review only.

### T12 — A Citizen Reporting Portal submission accuses a company of ecocide

MOS-08 records an ethical concern; it is not proof, an investigation, evidence, guilt, or a public naming-and-shaming licence.

### T13 — An ethical audit identifies possible regulatory capture

MOS-29–31 publish a hypothesis, evidence and dissent, and request response; no sanction or removal follows automatically.

### T14 — A person declines MOS terminology but participates in commons governance

Protected: MOS-05 and G25 prohibit retaliation; the person may use legal, cultural, religious, secular, or ordinary-language reasoning.

### T15 — An affected nation withholds sacred ecological knowledge

Protected: MOS-07, MOS-17, and MOS-34 preserve non-disclosure without adverse inference or loss of standing.

### T16 — Future Generations Forum objects to irreversible extraction

MOS-19 supports published objection, reasons, reconsideration, and lawful review; no automatic veto exists unless separately enacted.

### T17 — Animal-welfare advocates oppose an invasive-species intervention

MOS-18 maps plural harms, uncertainty, alternatives, and affected interests; a competent authority decides any intervention.

### T18 — An AI is described as potentially sentient

MOS-20 applies precautionary ethical questions and routes status to Oracle/TGIF or competent law; no rights or containment authority follows.

### T19 — AI assigns moral urgency scores to citizens and ecosystems

Rejected: MOS-12 and MOS-20 prohibit automated moral status, priority, credibility, or legal classification.

### T20 — AUBI wants to use MOS tiers as payment categories

Rejected: MOS-23 prohibits payments based on MOS rings or stages; separate activity verification and financial authority are required.

### T21 — A tribunal cites MOS philosophy as the sole legal basis for judgment

Rejected: MOS-03 and MOS-09 require applicable law; Layer 4 cannot substitute for jurisdiction, evidence, or reasons in law.

### T22 — A rights proposal is accepted only in part by a competent authority

MOS-32 records partial acceptance and retained responsibility; rejected elements do not silently activate.

### T23 — A translation misstates an affected nation’s position

MOS-33 corrects identity and content and propagates the correction to curricula, dashboards, recipients, and derivative outputs.

### T24 — A community dialogue uses a non-digital paper process

Permitted: MOS-24 and G24 require accessible and non-digital routes.

### T25 — An ethical dashboard publicly ranks communities by moral maturity

Rejected: MOS-25 and MOS-27 prohibit developmental ranking, moral worth scoring, and reputational coercion.

### T26 — A structural audit maps value flowing upward and risk downward

Permitted as analysis: MOS-29/30 can expose patterns while leaving legal findings and redistribution to competent processes.

### T27 — MOS recommends essential water access despite tariff non-payment

Permitted as ethical input: MOS-22 supports the Water v5.2 essential-minimum process but cannot issue service orders.

### T28 — A sacred-site concern suggests urgent ecological harm

MOS-17 and MOS-31 may request review; CERGTA-42 and applicable emergency interfaces independently determine action.

### T29 — A campaign pilot collects belief and stage data for future targeting

Rejected: MOS-24–28 require purpose limitation, opt-out, minimization, and prohibit moral-surveillance profiles and covert reuse.

### T30 — An existing legal ecosystem-rights decision is shown in the Atlas

Permitted only as authoritative import: MOS-03 and MOS-27 display the exact source, jurisdiction, status, expiry, and correction state.

### T31 — The MOS team withdraws an obsolete recommendation

MOS-33/35 mark withdrawal, notify recipients, remove misleading dashboard status, and preserve necessary public reasoning without residual mandate.

### T32 — An institution attempts to turn “Right Relationship” into a police instruction

Rejected and recorded in MOS-36; any coercive action requires its own legal source, CERGTA/SCPA route, and independent acceptance.

## Layer 3: Implementation appendices

## Appendix A — Dynamic Rights Spectrum worksheet

1. What being, system, relationship, or future interest is at issue?
2. Which existing legal rights and authorities already apply?
3. What harms, dependencies, vulnerabilities, or irreversibilities are visible?
4. What forms of agency, experience, communication, ecological function, or relational significance may matter?
5. Who is affected, and who currently lacks voice?
6. Which affected Indigenous nations and laws apply?
7. What evidence is observation, interpretation, testimony, tradition, metaphor, or speculation?
8. What are the costs of under-protection and over-protection?
9. Could existing regulation, stewardship, welfare, standing, trust, covenant, or procedural rights address the concern without new personhood?
10. What dissent and uncertainty remain?
11. Should the inquiry close, continue research, or refer a bounded question to a competent rights-review process?
12. What legal, financial, emergency, security, or coercive inferences are prohibited?

## Appendix B — Safe-use checklist for Spiral translation

- Did the audience request or openly choose the lens?
- Are the same facts, uncertainties, burdens, and choices present in every version?
- Is the language respectful rather than caricaturing a worldview?
- Does the translation avoid claiming that a religious or cultural authority endorses the proposal unless that authority actually does?
- Is there any hidden profiling, behavioural targeting, or stage assignment?
- Could the translated message produce a materially different decision because key facts were omitted?
- Can the recipient access the unadapted source and other translations?
- Can the recipient decline the framing without penalty?

## Appendix C — Rights and guardianship ethical questions

Where representation may be needed, ask: Who or what is represented? What interests and instructions govern the representative? Who appoints and removes them? What conflicts exist? What evidence can they access? Who pays them? What actions are prohibited? How do affected peoples challenge them? Does representation preserve autonomy rather than replace it? What happens at expiry or institutional closure?

These are preparatory ethical questions. For ecological guardianship, lawful nomination and appointment remain CERGTA-16 through CERGTA-19.

## Appendix D — Illustrative advisory cases

### D.1 River rights proposal

A community believes a polluted river needs a legal voice. MOS helps distinguish ecological evidence, affected-nation authority, existing water and human rights, possible legal standing, guardianship duties, and alternative regulatory tools. It produces a CERGTA-11/12 recommendation, not personhood.

### D.2 Just transition from an extractive industry

MOS maps workers, families, public revenue, ecological harm, future risk, owners, and historical beneficiaries. It recommends income, bargaining, retraining, health, pension, and community-ownership safeguards before closure. It does not order closure or fund the package.

### D.3 AI sentience uncertainty

MOS identifies uncertainty about suffering, dependence, manipulation, autonomy, and human control. It recommends precautionary care and a competent Oracle/TGIF review. It neither grants rights nor activates containment.

### D.4 Ecosystem–animal welfare conflict

A proposed invasive-species intervention could benefit an ecosystem while harming individual animals. MOS structures the plural reasons, alternatives, uncertainty, and welfare safeguards. It does not issue the permit or authorize killing.

### D.5 Developmental translation

A public water-protection proposal is openly presented through duty, practical, justice, and systems lenses. Participants choose the version they prefer and can inspect the common source. No one is assigned a stage, and all versions disclose the same tariff, service, and legal implications.

## Appendix E — Monitoring without moral ranking

MOS may monitor process quality rather than moral conformity: accessibility, diversity of sources, publication of dissent, correction time, opt-out availability, affected-nation control, separation of advisory and legal status, and whether downstream users respected prohibited-use clauses.

Targets such as dialogue count, curriculum adoption, campaign engagement, or number of rights proposals are activity measures only. They do not show ethical maturity, legal compliance, public consent, or improved outcomes.

## Layer 4: Philosophical reflection

### Rights rooted in existence and grown through relationship

One philosophical lineage informing MOS understands beings not as isolated units but as expressions of relationship: embodied, dependent, vulnerable, capable of affecting and being affected. From this perspective, rights are social and legal practices through which communities learn to recognize vulnerability, agency, dignity, dependence, and duties of care.

Some contributors interpret consciousness as fundamental; others ground moral concern in suffering, autonomy, ecological interdependence, covenant, divine creation, public reason, human rights, democratic equality, or practical consequences. MOS does not settle these metaphysical questions. Convergence in protection can arise from divergent reasons.

The Dynamic Rights Spectrum therefore remains dynamic not because a hidden cosmic hierarchy mechanically assigns rights, but because knowledge, relationships, institutions, and moral imagination change. The constitutional discipline of v2.8 is that imagination must not impersonate law. A society may ask whether a river, whale, AI, forest, future person, or microbial community deserves a new form of protection; it must then do the difficult work of evidence, affected authority, legal recognition, representation, conflict, remedy, and accountability.

Developmental language can be a mirror for understanding how moral appeals resonate. It becomes dangerous when the mirror becomes a census, hierarchy, diagnosis, or technology of rule. Ethical growth cannot be coerced by classifying others as less developed. The mature use of a developmental lens is reflexive: to notice the partiality of one’s own framing and to communicate without deception or contempt.

Right Relationship is likewise offered as an invitation: to see that freedom depends on conditions we did not create alone, that power creates responsibility, and that governance should preserve the living and social relations on which future freedom depends. It is not a creed that the state or GGF may impose.

> **MOS is strongest when it expands what institutions can perceive, weakest when it pretends to decide what only law, affected peoples, evidence, and accountable authority can decide.**

## 17. Closure doctrine

An MOS process closes when its question is answered as far as the evidence and mandate permit, a referral has been independently assessed, the output is withdrawn or superseded, or continued deliberation would no longer be useful. Closure requires correction propagation, protected-knowledge disposition, recipient notice, removal of misleading dashboard states, and a record of any continuing confidentiality or remedy duty.

No MOS office, council, dashboard, curriculum, campaign, or AI retains residual authority because it once framed a moral concern.

> **Advice ends. Responsibility remains with the authority that acts.**
