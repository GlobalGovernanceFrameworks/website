## Executive constitutional summary

The Aegis Protocol supports the voluntary and lawfully constituted transition of military, intelligence, defence-industrial, aerospace, cyber, logistical, scientific, and emergency capabilities toward civilian public purposes. Its proper objects are people, skills, facilities, budgets, supply chains, research programmes, satellites, vessels, aircraft, communications systems, engineering capacity, medical logistics, and other assets that may be converted without creating a new global military authority.

The valuable intuition of the predecessor remains: security transitions are more durable when they preserve legitimate defence needs, protect workers and regions, reduce destabilizing capability gaps, and offer credible civilian missions rather than demanding abrupt abolition. Asteroid observation, space-weather monitoring, disaster logistics, environmental observation, infrastructure resilience, search and rescue, public-interest research, demining, ecological restoration, and peaceful exploration may create practical grounds for cooperation among institutions that otherwise remain strategic rivals.

The predecessor also moved beyond voluntary transition into sanctions for non-participation, surveillance of political resistance, a general Earth Defense Force, covert information links, public-psychology campaigns, and pathways for global enforcement. v1.3 removes those functions.

This Protocol does **not** create an Earth Defense Force, defensive alliance, intelligence service, sanctions authority, global procurement sovereign, emergency command, technology-licensing authority, or power to redirect national military budgets. It does not classify non-participants as threats. It does not authorize military deployments, covert action, offensive cyber operations, surveillance, compulsory disarmament, facility seizure, treaty enforcement, or intervention in domestic political debate.

> **Transition becomes legitimate through consent, law, labour protection, mission separation, and verifiable conversion—not through pressure to join a planetary security identity.**

The framework separates nine functions that the predecessor partly combined:

1. voluntary participation and lawful compact formation;
2. capability and dependency assessment;
3. personnel, community, and regional transition;
4. civilian mission design;
5. dual-use conversion and custody;
6. finance, procurement, and industrial policy;
7. verification, declassification, and whistleblower protection;
8. action-specific emergency re-tasking under valid authority;
9. adjudication, remedy, termination, and learning.

No body controls the full chain.

# 1. Purpose, status, and non-authority

## 1.1 Purpose

The Aegis Protocol provides interoperable governance interfaces for:

- voluntary reduction or redirection of military and security expenditure;
- civilian conversion of defence facilities, production lines, research programmes, and logistical systems;
- transition of personnel, contractors, families, and defence-dependent regions;
- peaceful scientific and environmental missions;
- multinational confidence-building projects;
- declassification and public-interest release of information where lawful and safe;
- dual-use technology custody and purpose limitation;
- civilian disaster, health, infrastructure, and space-hazard assistance;
- transparent finance, procurement, inspection, and after-action learning;
- transfer, decommissioning, return, or destruction of converted assets.

## 1.2 Status

This document is a framework and constitutional interface. Participation may be created only through a valid domestic law, treaty, compact, public-finance instrument, labour agreement, procurement contract, facility charter, research agreement, Indigenous agreement, humanitarian arrangement, or other lawful constituting instrument.

Templates in this Protocol do not become binding merely because a GGF body, funder, platform, standards network, participating state, or international organization publishes or endorses them.

## 1.3 Non-authority clause

This Protocol does not itself:

- create or command armed forces;
- establish a collective-defence obligation;
- authorize military, intelligence, police, border, cyber, or covert operations;
- authorize sanctions, embargoes, trade restrictions, asset freezes, patent revocation, market exclusion, or use of force;
- establish offences, liability, courts, inspections, warrants, compulsory disclosure, or enforcement jurisdiction;
- compel a state, Indigenous nation, community, company, worker, veteran, researcher, contractor, or institution to participate;
- limit military expenditure, redirect a budget, impose a financial-transaction tax, issue debt, or allocate the Global Commons Fund;
- transfer command to the Planetary Immune System, Meta-Governance, a Global Security and Exploration Trust, an Earth Defense Force, or another framework-created body;
- classify refusal, criticism, lobbying, protest, journalism, or non-participation as sabotage, hostility, extremism, misinformation, or a transnational threat;
- condition AUBI Layer 1, essential services, justice, asylum, humanitarian assistance, healthcare, employment rights, pensions, or rescue on Aegis participation.

> **No valid constituting instrument, no Aegis authority.**

## 1.4 No transition-to-jurisdiction conversion

A programme does not acquire authority because it successfully converts assets, reduces expenditure, attracts major powers, creates jobs, earns public trust, prevents hazards, or operates across borders. Demonstrated usefulness may justify renewal, replication, procurement, or proposed legislation. It does not itself create jurisdiction.

# 2. Constitutional position and deferred security matters

## 2.1 A transition framework, not a security constitution

Aegis governs capability transition and civilian conversion. It does not determine the lawful structure of collective defence, intelligence cooperation, arms control enforcement, peacekeeping, border security, sanctions, military command, or coercive intervention.

## 2.2 Deferred cluster

The following matters remain outside v1.3 pending a separate security-and-coercion cluster review involving the Shield Protocol, Peace and Conflict Resolution frameworks, migration and asylum frameworks, Kintsugi or transitional-justice frameworks, Justice Systems v1.4, the Treaty, and relevant humanitarian and security law:

- armed collective defence;
- peace enforcement;
- sanctions and coercive economic measures;
- intelligence collection and covert operations;
- cyber operations against adversaries;
- military detention;
- weapons authorization and rules of engagement;
- command responsibility for armed deployment;
- designation of hostile or transnational actors;
- compulsory disarmament;
- security-sector intervention in non-participating jurisdictions.

## 2.3 Immediate exclusion controls

Until those matters are separately constituted, no Aegis agreement may be interpreted to supply them by implication. A converted asset retains no latent Aegis mandate for armed or covert use.

## 2.4 Relation to the emergency interface

The Emergency, Catastrophic-Risk, and Continuity Interface Specification controls any emergency use of Aegis-supported civilian capabilities. Catastrophic severity may justify urgency but does not transfer command or create residual authority.

## 2.5 Relation to PIS v2.2.2

PIS v2.2.2 may request information, exercises, dependency mapping, readiness analysis, or offers of assistance. It cannot command Aegis assets, personnel, facilities, finance, or mission partnerships. Re-tasking requires the lawful command authority of the asset, an action-specific activation record, and the applicable emergency review.

# 3. Governing principles

1. **Voluntary, lawful transition:** Participation rests on valid public authority, institutional consent, and applicable labour and contractual rights.
2. **Civilian purpose:** Converted capability must have a defined civilian public-purpose mission and lawful operator.
3. **Mission separation:** Space hazards, disaster response, environmental monitoring, health logistics, infrastructure resilience, research, and armed security do not share one residual command.
4. **No security conditionality:** Benefits and essential services are not conditioned on political alignment or membership.
5. **Worker and community justice:** Personnel, contractors, families, and defence-dependent regions are rights-holders, not adjustment variables.
6. **Dual-use realism:** Civilian branding does not erase surveillance, weapons, cyber, intelligence, or coercive capacity.
7. **Civilian control and purpose limitation:** Custody, access, software, data, command, and secondary use must be bounded.
8. **Indigenous authority and non-appropriation:** Projects affecting Indigenous lands, knowledge, data, sacred relations, or peoples require affected-nation interfaces.
9. **Plural participation:** States and institutions may join specific missions without adopting a common ideology or comprehensive security identity.
10. **Transparency with lawful secrecy:** Public accountability is essential, but operational, personal, security, protected, and Indigenous information require layered access.
11. **Reversibility and decommissioning:** Conversion must address return, destruction, demilitarization, software removal, data disposition, and residual capability.
12. **No propaganda governance:** Public communication explains programmes and evidence; it does not manipulate populations or classify dissent.
13. **No automatic economic coercion:** Finance supports transition but does not punish non-participation.
14. **Learning without mandatory scaling:** Pilots may remain local, fail, stop, fork, or dissolve.

# 4. Scope, objects, and exclusions

## 4.1 Capability objects

Aegis may address:

- personnel, skills, credentials, pensions, and professional communities;
- bases, shipyards, airfields, laboratories, factories, warehouses, hospitals, training sites, and communications facilities;
- vessels, aircraft, vehicles, satellites, sensors, launch systems, computing systems, manufacturing equipment, and logistics networks;
- research programmes, standards, software, models, patents, licences, datasets, and technical documentation;
- procurement contracts, supplier networks, maintenance capacity, and regional economic dependencies;
- emergency stockpiles, transport corridors, field hospitals, engineering units, mapping systems, and search-and-rescue capacity.

## 4.2 Civilian mission domains

Possible civilian missions include:

- asteroid and near-Earth-object observation;
- space-weather monitoring and civil warning;
- space-debris tracking and non-destructive remediation;
- disaster logistics, engineering, communications, and search and rescue;
- environmental and climate observation;
- wildfire, flood, drought, earthquake, and industrial-hazard support;
- public-health logistics and laboratory support under Global Health v2.2.2 and Aethelred v1.2.2;
- infrastructure inspection, emergency repair, and continuity;
- humanitarian transport and supply-chain support;
- demining and explosive-remnant removal under applicable law;
- ecological restoration and pollution remediation;
- peaceful scientific exploration and shared research infrastructure.

## 4.3 Excluded activities

Aegis does not authorize:

- weapons development or deployment;
- offensive surveillance or targeting;
- intelligence operations against political actors;
- coercive border, policing, detention, or migration functions;
- influence operations, psychological operations, counter-propaganda, or covert media support;
- autonomous use of force;
- armed response to attacks on Aegis assets;
- military procurement disguised as civilian conversion;
- general-purpose security data collection;
- compulsory budget or industrial conversion.

## 4.4 Mixed missions

Where a project combines civilian and security purposes, each purpose must have a separate constituting instrument, command chain, data boundary, procurement basis, and legal review. A civilian Aegis charter cannot legalize the security component.

# 5. Participation, standing, and constituting instruments

## 5.1 Eligible participants

Participants may include:

- states and their competent ministries or agencies;
- regional and municipal authorities;
- Indigenous nations and their own institutions;
- public enterprises;
- defence and civilian firms;
- trade unions and professional associations;
- universities and research institutes;
- humanitarian and disaster-response organizations;
- public-interest foundations and finance facilities;
- international organizations acting within their mandates.

## 5.2 Mission-specific participation

Participation should be modular. A participant may join an asteroid-observation partnership, shipyard-conversion programme, veteran transition compact, or environmental satellite project without joining unrelated missions.

## 5.3 Constituting instrument minimums

Each Aegis programme requires a **Participation and Authority Charter** identifying:

- participating legal persons and authorities;
- lawful basis and territory;
- mission and excluded uses;
- assets, personnel, finance, data, and technology involved;
- decision rights and command boundaries;
- procurement, labour, safety, and environmental duties;
- Indigenous, community, and cross-border interfaces;
- audit, complaint, appeal, and remedy;
- duration, withdrawal, transfer, and dissolution.

## 5.4 No representation by category

Youth, Indigenous peoples, veterans, workers, scientists, affected communities, and civil society are not represented merely because a percentage of seats is reserved for a universal category. Representation must identify the constituency, selection authority, mandate, accountability, and limits.

## 5.5 Withdrawal

A participant may withdraw according to the constituting instrument, subject to lawful notice, safety, worker, environmental, data, asset, and financial obligations. Withdrawal is not itself a violation or hostile act.

# 6. Voluntary transition compacts

## 6.1 Purpose

A **Security Capability Transition Compact** records a participant’s voluntary commitments to reduce, redirect, convert, retire, or share specified capabilities.

## 6.2 Permitted commitments

A compact may include:

- budget redirection established by the participant’s lawful fiscal process;
- facility conversion;
- procurement transition;
- research redirection;
- civilian mission contributions;
- personnel retraining and placement;
- declassification schedules;
- inspection or confidence-building measures;
- environmental remediation;
- reparative or community investment adopted through valid public processes.

## 6.3 Prohibited conditions

A compact may not make access to humanitarian assistance, basic public data, essential medicines, emergency rescue, AUBI Layer 1, justice, or protected civic rights conditional on a military-budget pledge.

## 6.4 No universal pledge percentages

The Protocol does not prescribe a universal percentage of military expenditure for transition. Commitments must reflect security context, fiscal law, capability needs, alliance obligations, worker impacts, regional dependence, and public authorization.

## 6.5 Confidence-building without bloc formation

Compacts should permit verification and cooperation without requiring membership in a geopolitical bloc. Shared civilian projects must not secretly create mutual-defence obligations.

# 7. Capability, dependency, and transition assessment

## 7.1 Capability Transition Profile

Before conversion, participants prepare a multidimensional profile covering:

- current mission and lawful owner;
- personnel and skills;
- facility and asset condition;
- supply-chain and maintenance dependencies;
- classified, personal, protected, and Indigenous information;
- weapons, surveillance, cyber, and coercive functions;
- environmental liabilities;
- community and regional economic dependence;
- alternative civilian uses;
- conversion cost, time, and reversibility;
- safety, proliferation, and re-militarization risk;
- decommissioning or disposal options.

## 7.2 No single transition score

These dimensions must not be collapsed into one “peacefulness,” alignment, conversion, or regenerative score that determines finance, market access, or political legitimacy.

## 7.3 Dependency mapping

Programmes must map dependencies on:

- proprietary components and vendors;
- foreign maintenance and software keys;
- energy, communications, transport, and data systems;
- specialized workers and security clearances;
- export controls and licences;
- classified supply chains;
- local land, housing, and public services;
- strategic materials and environmental permits.

## 7.4 Counterfactual assessment

The profile must compare conversion with continued use, retirement, decommissioning, sale, transfer, mothballing, and no action. Conversion is not automatically preferable.

## 7.5 Independent challenge

Material assumptions should be reviewed by independent technical, labour, fiscal, environmental, community, and security reviewers. Dissent and unresolved uncertainty must be recorded.

# 8. Civilian mission chartering

## 8.1 Mission Charter

Each civilian mission requires a **Civilian Mission Charter** specifying:

- public-purpose objective;
- constituting authority;
- participants and operators;
- territory and host authority;
- assets and personnel;
- command and professional responsibility;
- permitted and prohibited functions;
- data and technology boundaries;
- funding and procurement;
- safety and environmental safeguards;
- review, termination, and handback.

## 8.2 No general Earth Defense Force

The predecessor’s Earth Defense Force combined cosmic threats, climate response, ecological protection, pandemic prevention, exploration, and military capability. v1.3 replaces it with separately constituted mission partnerships. No common label or funding vehicle creates general command.

## 8.3 Possible mission partnerships

Participants may form, among others:

- a Near-Earth Object Observation Partnership;
- a Space Weather Civil Warning Network;
- a Disaster Airlift and Engineering Compact;
- an Environmental Observation and Restoration Partnership;
- a Humanitarian Maritime Logistics Network;
- a Public Health Logistics Compact;
- a Civil Infrastructure Emergency Repair Network;
- a Peaceful Exploration and Shared Science Consortium.

Each remains legally and operationally separate.

## 8.4 Host authority

Deployment into a territory requires a valid host request, treaty basis, humanitarian mandate, or other lawful authority. A multinational mission’s internal charter cannot substitute for territorial permission.

## 8.5 Civilian identifiers and status

Where appropriate, converted assets and personnel should use distinct civilian registration, markings, communications, uniforms, insurance, and command. Such identifiers do not themselves determine legal status.

# 9. Asteroid, space-weather, and space-debris missions

## 9.1 Observation and warning

Aegis may support civilian observation, orbit calculation, data exchange, warning, exercises, and public communication for near-Earth objects, solar storms, and space debris.

## 9.2 Intervention authority

A mission to deflect, disrupt, capture, or otherwise alter a celestial object requires a separately valid space and emergency instrument defining scientific evidence, decision authority, liability, launch authorization, environmental effects, planetary defence coordination, and termination.

## 9.3 No weapons presumption

Military launch, tracking, or interception technology does not become authorized for planetary-defence use merely because it might be technically useful. Conversion, custody, software, command, and non-targeting safeguards must be independently established.

## 9.4 Open and protected information

Orbital and warning information should be openly shared where safe. Security-sensitive, proprietary, personal, or dual-use details may require layered access under Aurora v1.3 and Technology Governance v3.6.

## 9.5 Peaceful-purpose assurance

Participants must document how the mission avoids missile-warning manipulation, covert targeting, intelligence exploitation, destabilizing tests, and strategic ambiguity.

---

# 10. Disaster, humanitarian, health, and infrastructure missions

## 10.1 Disaster capability

Converted transport, engineering, medical, communications, mapping, search-and-rescue, and logistics assets may support the Living Shield v4.4.2 through requested assistance and mission-specific charters.

## 10.2 Humanitarian principles

Humanitarian missions must preserve humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence where applicable, do-no-harm, protection from exploitation, and separation from intelligence or political objectives.

## 10.3 Health missions

Health logistics, field laboratories, medical transport, stockpile management, and manufacturing support operate under Global Health v2.2.2, Aethelred v1.2.2, professional law, host authority, and the emergency interface. Aegis does not authorize isolation, quarantine, vaccination, sample collection, or countermeasure allocation.

## 10.4 Infrastructure missions

Engineering and repair units may support energy, water, transport, communications, shelter, and public facilities through lawful operator requests. They do not inherit regulatory control over the infrastructure they repair.

## 10.5 No intelligence collection through assistance

Assistance missions may collect only information necessary for their defined purpose. They may not use disaster, health, or infrastructure access for political, military, commercial, or intelligence collection.

# 11. Environmental observation and ecological restoration

## 11.1 Conversion opportunities

Satellites, vessels, aircraft, drones, sensors, laboratories, engineering units, and logistics systems may support:

- deforestation and wildfire observation;
- pollution and spill detection;
- ocean and coastal monitoring;
- biodiversity surveys;
- ecosystem restoration;
- hazardous-material remediation;
- climate and weather observation;
- enforcement support where separately authorized by competent authorities.

## 11.2 Observation is not enforcement

A sensor alert, satellite image, model output, or Aegis report may support investigation. It does not itself create land, environmental, criminal, customs, or sanctions jurisdiction.

## 11.3 Indigenous and territorial authority

Projects affecting Indigenous territories, sacred places, ecological knowledge, or community-controlled lands require affected-nation and territorial interfaces. Observation may be prohibited, limited, locally held, or disclosed only through protected attestation.

## 11.4 Environmental liabilities

Conversion plans must address contamination, unexploded ordnance, fuels, hazardous materials, emissions, waste, habitat damage, water use, and long-term remediation.

## 11.5 Restoration is not compensation by default

An ecological project cannot substitute for reparations, cleanup liability, worker claims, land return, or community compensation unless the affected parties and competent authorities validly agree.

# 12. Dual-use conversion lifecycle

## 12.1 Lifecycle stages

Each converted capability follows a documented lifecycle:

1. identification;
2. authority and ownership review;
3. risk and dependency assessment;
4. mission selection;
5. demilitarization or separation design;
6. technical conversion;
7. independent verification;
8. civilian commissioning;
9. operation and monitoring;
10. incident response;
11. transfer, renewal, decommissioning, return, or destruction.

## 12.2 Residual-capability analysis

Programmes must identify residual:

- weapons functionality;
- targeting capacity;
- surveillance reach;
- cyber access;
- classified interfaces;
- export-controlled components;
- remote-control pathways;
- military software and data;
- manufacturer or state override keys;
- supply-chain dependence.

## 12.3 Conversion is not renaming

Changing the operator, logo, mission statement, or procurement category does not establish civilian conversion. Technical, legal, organizational, and data separation must be demonstrable.

## 12.4 Re-militarization controls

The constituting instrument must specify whether, by whom, and under what law an asset may be returned to security use. Where return is prohibited, physical, software, contractual, inspection, and disposal controls must support that prohibition.

## 12.5 Incident and recall

Converted assets require incident reporting, containment, suspension, recall, correction, and remedy arrangements proportional to their residual capability and public dependence.

# 13. Custody, command separation, and access control

## 13.1 Custody record

Every converted asset requires a **Custody and Command Separation Record** identifying owner, operator, physical custodian, software administrator, data controller, maintenance authority, funder, and lawful command.

## 13.2 Separation requirements

Depending on risk, separation may require:

- distinct legal entity or programme;
- civilian chain of command;
- separate facilities or network segments;
- removal of weapons and targeting systems;
- replacement of military software;
- access-control changes;
- independent key management;
- data segregation and deletion;
- external inspection;
- different procurement and personnel rules.

## 13.3 Remote access

Remote administration, manufacturer access, state override, and emergency-control functions must be disclosed to competent reviewers. Hidden access paths are prohibited.

## 13.4 Command ambiguity

No person may be subject to conflicting civilian and military orders for the same mission without an explicit conflict rule and lawful priority. Personnel must have a protected route to refuse an unlawful or unsafe order.

## 13.5 Asset handback

At mission end, assets must be returned, transferred, decommissioned, or destroyed according to a verified handback record. Temporary civilian custody does not silently change ownership or strategic status.

# 14. Technology, cyber, data, and intelligence safeguards

## 14.1 Technology governance

Technology design, assessment, procurement, operation, and decommissioning follow Technology Governance v3.6. Aegis does not create a Global Technology Council or compulsory technology certification.

## 14.2 Data governance

Personal, operational, environmental, genetic, community, Indigenous, classified, and inferred data follow Aurora v1.3 and applicable law. Aegis does not create a universal security or participation identity.

## 14.3 Political surveillance prohibition

Aegis programmes may not monitor:

- opposition to military conversion;
- lobbying or advocacy as such;
- media sentiment;
- ideological affiliation;
- public approval;
- protest, journalism, or organizing;
- “resistance” to Aegis as a generic risk indicator.

Lawful investigation of corruption, fraud, espionage, threats, or other offences requires the appropriate legal basis, competent authority, evidence safeguards, and judicial process.

## 14.4 Cyber boundaries

Converted cyber capability may be used for defensive system hardening, incident recovery, testing with authorization, and civilian infrastructure protection. Offensive access, persistent intrusion, retaliation, disruption, and intelligence collection remain outside this Protocol.

## 14.5 AI and automated systems

AI may assist logistics, maintenance, anomaly detection, simulation, translation, and planning. It may not independently authorize deployment, classify political threats, select targets, allocate force, determine guilt, or create command authority.

## 14.6 Classified information

Classification must have a valid legal basis, defined custodian, review date, access rules, and declassification pathway. “Security” may not be used to hide corruption, illegality, environmental harm, labour abuse, or programme failure.

# 15. Finance and the federated transition-facility model

## 15.1 No self-created global trust

v1.3 does not establish a single Global Security and Exploration Trust with authority over participating budgets. Participants may create or join separately constituted transition funds, development banks, public trusts, grant facilities, pension-backed vehicles, or project-finance arrangements.

## 15.2 Transition Finance Facility Charter

Each facility must identify:

- constituting law or agreement;
- contributors and beneficiaries;
- custody and investment mandate;
- eligible missions and exclusions;
- procurement and conflict rules;
- worker, regional, Indigenous, environmental, and reparative allocations;
- risk, guarantee, and loss allocation;
- audit, complaint, and appeal;
- withdrawal, succession, insolvency, and dissolution.

## 15.3 No automatic taxes or levies

Financial-transaction taxes, military-budget contributions, peace bonds, procurement preferences, tax credits, or other revenue measures require valid fiscal and legislative authority. Aegis may provide design options but cannot impose them.

## 15.4 No participation dividends for political alignment

Finance may compensate contributions, fund projects, purchase services, and support transition. It must not create superior access to essential public goods solely because a participant adopts the preferred security narrative or pledge level.

## 15.5 Reparative finance

Security reparations, environmental remediation, worker claims, land restoration, and community investment require claimant standing, evidence, affected-party participation, lawful custody, remedy design, appeal, and non-substitution safeguards. A fixed global percentage does not by itself establish justice.

# 16. Procurement, contractors, and industrial conversion

## 16.1 Public-purpose procurement

Procurement may support civilian conversion through transparent, competitive, cooperative, public-enterprise, or mission-specific methods established by competent authorities.

## 16.2 Contractor eligibility

Eligibility criteria should address:

- legal compliance;
- beneficial ownership;
- corruption and conflict controls;
- labour and human-rights performance;
- environmental liabilities;
- technical capability;
- security and data safeguards;
- supply-chain resilience;
- decommissioning and remediation.

## 16.3 No guaranteed market reservations

Defence contractors do not receive automatic civilian market shares, tax benefits, or procurement priority as a reward for partial conversion. Transitional support must be justified against alternatives and protected from capture.

## 16.4 Conversion additionality

Public funding should distinguish genuine conversion from relabeling existing civilian work, shifting harmful activity elsewhere, or using civilian contracts to subsidize military capacity.

## 16.5 Small suppliers and regional diversity

Transition plans should prevent concentration in incumbent prime contractors and support worker-owned firms, public enterprises, cooperatives, local suppliers, repair ecosystems, and Global South capacity where lawful and effective.

## 16.6 Private military and armed actors

Private military contractors and non-state armed groups do not become lawful civilian actors merely by joining an Aegis programme. Demobilization, disarmament, reintegration, criminal accountability, security regulation, and conflict resolution belong to separately valid processes.

# 17. Personnel, labour, and professional transition

## 17.1 Rights-bearing transition

Personnel include military members, civil servants, intelligence and cyber staff, contractors, researchers, manufacturing workers, maintainers, cleaners, logistics workers, and others affected by conversion.

## 17.2 AUBI protection

AUBI Layer 1 remains unconditional. Transition work, retraining, reserve commitments, dangerous duties, and professional services require ordinary wages, pensions, benefits, insurance, expenses, collective bargaining, and employment rights.

## 17.3 Personnel Transition Agreement

Each programme must address:

- employment status and employer;
- voluntary and commanded participation under applicable law;
- role, credentials, and scope;
- wages, pension, seniority, benefits, and leave;
- retraining and education;
- disability, injury, trauma, and mental-health support;
- housing, relocation, family, and caregiving;
- security clearances and confidentiality;
- conscientious objection and safe refusal;
- grievance, union, whistleblower, and appeal rights;
- demobilization and post-programme placement.

## 17.4 No mandatory consciousness programme

Mindfulness, reflective practice, leadership education, trauma support, or values dialogue may be offered voluntarily. Employment, command, promotion, benefits, or programme access may not depend on participation in spiritual, psychological, developmental, or “shadow work” programmes.

## 17.5 Professional integrity

Engineers, scientists, health workers, pilots, mariners, cyber specialists, and other professionals retain duties to law, safety, evidence, and their professional standards. A mission charter cannot require them to conceal material risk.

# 18. Veterans, families, and defence-dependent communities

## 18.1 Broader transition constituency

Conversion affects not only institutions and employees but also veterans, reservists, families, schools, housing markets, small businesses, municipalities, public services, and cultural identities.

## 18.2 Regional Transition Plan

A plan should assess:

- employment and supplier dependence;
- municipal revenue;
- housing and land;
- schools, health, transport, and care systems;
- contamination and remediation;
- veteran and family services;
- small-business and cooperative opportunities;
- alternative public and private investment;
- distributional and demographic effects;
- long-term maintenance capacity.

## 18.3 No model-to-policy automation

Economic models may inform planning but do not establish projected dividends, job creation, or GDP gains as facts. Assumptions, distributions, uncertainty, alternatives, and failed scenarios must be visible.

## 18.4 Community participation

Affected communities require accessible information, meaningful participation, complaint, and remedy. Consultation does not substitute for legal authority, labour bargaining, land rights, or Indigenous consent.

## 18.5 Anti-abandonment duty

A participant should not close or convert facilities while externalizing contamination, pension deficits, unemployment, housing collapse, or infrastructure liabilities onto the host community.

# 19. Research, education, and peaceful exploration

## 19.1 Shared research

Aegis may support civilian research infrastructures in astronomy, climate, ecology, engineering, materials, communications, public health, logistics, and other peaceful fields.

## 19.2 Research independence

Funding bodies may define mission scope but must not predetermine scientific conclusions, suppress safety findings, or use research as public-relations proof of transition success.

## 19.3 Dual-use publication

Publication and access decisions require case-specific review of scientific benefit, security risk, public accountability, Indigenous restrictions, proprietary claims, and foreseeable misuse. Neither universal openness nor indefinite secrecy is presumed.

## 19.4 Education and retraining

Curricula should be plural, professionally grounded, accessible, and voluntary where they concern ideology or worldview. “Planetary guardianship” may be an invitation or narrative, not a required identity.

## 19.5 Exploration boundaries

SETI, exoplanet research, deep-space science, and other exploration remain scientific programmes. Claims involving extraterrestrial contact, planetary defence, space resources, or strategic competition require their own evidence and legal frameworks.

# 20. Declassification and public-interest release

## 20.1 Declassification Review Record

Programmes should establish lawful, independent review of information that may be released for:

- scientific and historical research;
- environmental and health protection;
- accountability and justice;
- technical conversion;
- public understanding;
- worker and community claims.

## 20.2 Protected interests

Review must protect legitimate operational security, personal privacy, sources, vulnerable persons, Indigenous knowledge, active investigations, and non-proliferation interests.

## 20.3 No automatic blockchain publication

Declassification decisions, sensitive records, and protected material must not be placed on immutable public ledgers by default. Integrity, custody, correction, retention, sealing, and deletion follow Aurora v1.3.

## 20.4 Wrongdoing exception

Classification and confidentiality should not prevent lawful reporting, investigation, adjudication, or remedy for corruption, unlawful surveillance, environmental harm, human-rights violations, safety failures, or other wrongdoing.

## 20.5 Historical record and institutional memory

Released and protected records require durable archival arrangements, provenance, context, correction, access rules, and succession planning.

# 21. Inspection, verification, and confidence building

## 21.1 Verification purposes

Verification may confirm:

- budget or procurement commitments;
- facility conversion;
- removal or separation of specified functions;
- asset custody;
- civilian mission performance;
- environmental remediation;
- declassification compliance;
- worker and community obligations.

## 21.2 Valid inspection authority

Inspection requires consent, treaty authority, regulatory jurisdiction, contract, warrant, or another valid basis. Aegis does not create a global right to inspect states, firms, bases, laboratories, networks, or closed societies.

## 21.3 Independent verification network

Participants may recognize plural accredited inspectors, auditors, technical bodies, labour monitors, environmental reviewers, and public institutions. One Aegis body should not monopolize certification.

## 21.4 Evidence boundaries

Inspectors must distinguish factual observation, technical opinion, legal conclusion, and remedy recommendation. Their report does not itself impose sanctions or adjudicate liability.

## 21.5 Confidence-building measures

Possible measures include:

- reciprocal observation;
- shared civilian exercises;
- notification of conversion milestones;
- jointly maintained inventories;
- protected facility visits;
- data escrow;
- incident hotlines;
- third-party verification;
- reversible pilot projects.

## 21.6 Verification failure

A failed or disputed inspection may suspend the relevant voluntary benefit or trigger review under the constituting instrument. It does not authorize general sanctions, covert collection, or force.

# 22. Whistleblowing, complaints, and protected disclosure

## 22.1 Protected channels

Programmes must provide confidential and, where lawful, anonymous channels for workers, contractors, researchers, communities, and officials to report:

- corruption and misuse;
- hidden military functions;
- safety or environmental risks;
- unlawful surveillance;
- labour abuse;
- data misuse;
- false certification;
- retaliation;
- mission creep.

## 22.2 Independence

Channels should be institutionally separate from the programme management implicated in the complaint and connected to competent labour, regulatory, oversight, ombuds, inspector, or justice bodies.

## 22.3 No covert political network

Protected disclosure is not a licence to run deniable cells, covert influence operations, clandestine political monitoring, or unauthorized intelligence collection.

## 22.4 Retaliation protection

Good-faith reporters require employment, legal, physical, digital, family, migration, and psychological protection appropriate to risk.

## 22.5 False or disputed reports

A report that is unsubstantiated does not by itself establish bad faith. Deliberate fabrication may be addressed through valid law and due process.

# 23. Indigenous peoples, territories, and knowledge

## 23.1 Recognition, not creation

Aegis recognizes Indigenous peoples and authorities as defined by them and applicable law. It does not create a universal Indigenous council, media network, representative percentage, or veto proxy.

## 23.2 Indigenous Capability Transition Interface

Where a programme affects Indigenous lands, airspace, waters, resources, cultural heritage, knowledge, data, burial sites, sacred relations, employment, or security, an affected-nation interface must identify:

- the people or peoples affected;
- authorities recognized by them;
- applicable law and treaty rights;
- consent, participation, refusal, or non-engagement;
- land, knowledge, data, benefit, and custody conditions;
- protected non-transfer and non-publication;
- emergency and withdrawal arrangements;
- remedy, return, deletion, restoration, and compensation.

## 23.3 No cultural appropriation for legitimacy

Indigenous cosmologies, symbols, narratives, or representatives may not be used to market a project or confer moral legitimacy without valid authorization.

## 23.4 Security history and reparative claims

Projects involving lands or peoples harmed by military activity must preserve claims for cleanup, return, disclosure, apology, compensation, and other remedies. Participation in a new project does not waive historical claims unless explicitly and lawfully agreed.

## 23.5 Protected non-participation

An Indigenous nation may decline an Aegis mission, data exchange, monitoring arrangement, or public narrative without being classified as obstructive, anti-science, anti-peace, or a security risk.

# 24. Public communication and epistemic safeguards

## 24.1 Lawful communication functions

Aegis institutions may:

- explain programme purposes, terms, evidence, costs, risks, and results;
- publish accessible records and corrections;
- support public deliberation;
- communicate emergencies within valid mission roles;
- provide multilingual and disability-accessible information.

## 24.2 Prohibited influence functions

They may not:

- run propaganda or psychological operations;
- develop regionally tailored covert persuasion campaigns;
- classify publics by developmental stage or political susceptibility;
- manipulate fear, patriotism, spirituality, identity, or trauma;
- fund nominally independent media to manufacture support;
- treat criticism as sabotage or disinformation;
- use Synoptic institutions as an alignment or counter-propaganda service.

## 24.3 Relationship to Synoptic v4.2

Information-system accountability, public-interest media, source protection, crisis communication, correction, and platform governance follow Synoptic v4.2. Media findings do not create Aegis enforcement power.

## 24.4 Public deliberation

Major transition commitments should support accessible deliberation that includes workers, host communities, veterans, scientists, Indigenous peoples, affected industries, public-finance authorities, and critics.

## 24.5 No belief-change metrics

Trust, approval, “security identity,” support for planetary guardianship, reduced dissent, or narrative adoption are not programme success metrics.

# 25. Governance, conflicts, and anti-capture safeguards

## 25.1 Separated programme functions

Each programme should separate, as appropriate:

- governing board;
- executive management;
- scientific and technical advice;
- procurement;
- finance and investment;
- audit and inspection;
- labour and community participation;
- complaint and whistleblower channels;
- justice referral.

## 25.2 Conflict of interest

Rules should cover current and former military, intelligence, public-office, contractor, finance, consulting, technology, and programme relationships.

## 25.3 Revolving door

No retiring commander, official, executive, or contractor receives an automatic Aegis leadership role or “golden parachute.” Appointments require open qualifications, conflict review, cooling-off rules, and accountable selection.

## 25.4 Contributor power

Financial or asset contributions do not automatically determine command, board control, procurement shares, data access, or project location.

## 25.5 Major-power dominance

Rotating leadership among major powers does not cure dominance if contribution, veto, technology control, classification, or supply-chain dependency remain concentrated. Governance design must document these dependencies and protect smaller participants.

## 25.6 Public and worker oversight

Oversight must have access to relevant information, independent resources, protected advice, and response rights. Advisory participation without decision or challenge rights should not be represented as co-governance.

# 26. Emergency re-tasking and crisis interruption

## 26.1 No framework override

PIS, Meta-Governance, Living Shield, Global Health, Aethelred, or another framework cannot override normal Aegis functions by declaration alone.

## 26.2 Asset Re-tasking Record

Temporary re-tasking of a civilian-converted asset requires:

- initiating event and state estimate;
- competent activating authority;
- asset owner, operator, custodian, and lawful command;
- requested mission and territory;
- host authority;
- permitted and prohibited functions;
- personnel, labour, safety, and insurance;
- data, secrecy, and intelligence limits;
- weapons and coercion exclusion or separate lawful basis;
- start, expiry, review, termination, and handback;
- compensation and wrongful-action route.

## 26.3 Review architecture

Provisional emergency use follows the applicable 72-hour review, 30-day renewal, and extraordinary review beyond 90 cumulative days. Repeated mission extensions cannot create permanent command or ownership.

## 26.4 Crisis interruption of transition

A transition programme may pause where lawfully required for safety, defence readiness, fiscal continuity, worker protection, or asset integrity. The pause must identify authority, scope, consequences, review, and restart or termination conditions.

## 26.5 No general stand-down order

Aegis does not authorize Meta-Governance or another GGF body to issue a general stand-down order to states, militaries, firms, or personnel.

## 26.6 Return of authority

At mission end, command, credentials, access, finance, assets, and data return according to the constituting instrument and termination record. Emergency performance does not transfer permanent authority.


## 26.7 Cross-domain event and Living Shield assistance

Aegis adopts the Emergency Cross-Domain Event Envelope v0.1 (`CDEE/0.1`) for compound-event identity and the Living Shield–Aegis Civilian Converted-Capability Assistance Addendum v0.1 (`LS-AE-CCAA/0.1`) for disaster and continuity missions using converted or temporarily re-tasked capabilities.

> **Aegis–Living Shield assistance interface.** An Aegis-converted or temporarily re-tasked capability accepted for a Living Shield mission shall conform to `LS-AE-CCAA/0.1`. Aegis retains only the conversion, excluded-use, custody, safety, labour, data, and handback functions lawfully assigned to it; it does not acquire disaster command or territorial authority. The host mission objective, asset operation, professional command, and authority lifecycle remain separately recorded.

An unresolved conversion defect blocks the affected capability. It does not compel whole-mission acceptance, whole-event rejection, or transfer of technical command to the coordination steward.

# 27. Armed and security capability boundary

## 27.1 Separate legal basis

Any armed, coercive, intelligence, police, border, detention, or offensive cyber function requires a separate legal basis outside Aegis v1.3.2.

## 27.2 No implied collective defence

An attack on an Aegis facility, vessel, satellite, network, or personnel does not automatically trigger collective defence. Protection and response follow applicable domestic, international, alliance, maritime, space, humanitarian, and security law.

## 27.3 Weapons and force

Converted missions should remove or segregate weapons where feasible. Any retained weapon, armed escort, force-protection unit, or security function requires explicit authority, command, rules, accountability, and review outside the civilian mission charter.

## 27.4 Intelligence separation

Military and intelligence institutions may contribute technical knowledge or lawfully releasable information. Aegis does not authorize tasking, collection, analysis, dissemination, or covert action.

## 27.5 Future amendment gate

No amendment may add general EDF command, sanctions, intelligence, or armed enforcement until the security-and-coercion cluster has been audited and the relevant powers separately ratified.

# 28. Justice, evidence, liability, and remedy

## 28.1 Justice separation

Justice Systems v1.4 controls standing, jurisdiction, applicable law, evidence, due process, remedies, appeal, recognition, and enforcement. Aegis bodies do not prosecute, adjudicate, or execute judicial remedies.

## 28.2 Possible claims

Claims may involve:

- labour and pension rights;
- procurement corruption;
- environmental contamination;
- data or surveillance misuse;
- Indigenous land and knowledge rights;
- misrepresentation of conversion;
- contractor fraud;
- wrongful declassification or secrecy;
- unsafe deployment;
- injury or death;
- unlawful re-militarization;
- breach of finance or transition commitments.

## 28.3 Evidence custody

Programmes must preserve relevant records without turning auditors or programme managers into investigators with unlimited access. Evidence handoff requires provenance, access controls, privilege, source protection, and lawful process.

## 28.4 Remedies

Depending on law and harm, remedies may include correction, reinstatement, wages, pension restoration, compensation, cleanup, land return, data deletion, access restriction, contract rescission, procurement exclusion, injunction, asset return, decommissioning, apology, or institutional reform.

## 28.5 No sanctions for non-participation

Refusal to join Aegis, a transition fund, mission partnership, inspection programme, or budget pledge is not itself a wrong and cannot support a remedy or sanction.

A lawful conclusion may be that a conversion is unsafe, too expensive, strategically destabilizing, captured by incumbents, environmentally harmful, or inferior to closure and direct transition support.

# 31. Pilots, implementation, and Genesis alignment

## 31.1 Pilot purpose

Pilots test whether specific capability transitions are lawful, safe, useful, economically viable, rights-preserving, and transferable. They do not prove the legitimacy of a global Aegis institution.

## 31.2 Suggested pilot classes

Possible pilots include:

- civilianizing a satellite dataset and ground segment;
- retraining and placing a defined workforce cohort;
- remediating and reusing a contaminated facility;
- establishing a bilateral asteroid-observation project;
- converting a military logistics warehouse into a civilian emergency hub;
- testing a declassification and archival process.

## 31.3 Pilot authorization

## 31.5 Scaling gate

## 31.6 Non-scaling and dissolution

A pilot or programme may stop without replacement. Assets, staff, finance, data, liabilities, records, and claims must still be resolved.

# 33. Required operational records

## Record inheritance and conformance

1. **Specialization does not replace the canonical set.**  
   This framework's domain record list specializes but does not replace the records defined by the Emergency, Catastrophic-Risk, and Continuity Interface Specification and the Emergency and Catastrophic-Risk Cluster Record Profile. Every canonical record remains required whenever its applicability trigger occurs.

2. **Canonical identity.**  
   Every domain record shall identify the canonical `ECRC-##` record or records it `implements`, `extends`, `splits`, `contains`, or `imports`. A domain label may differ from the canonical title, but the canonical identifier, schema version, minimum common header, legal effect, access protections, and lifecycle obligations remain traceable.

3. **Direct import.**  
   A canonical record not mapped to a domain specialization is imported directly when triggered. Absence from a domain appendix, implementation guide, software form, dashboard, or local registry does not make the canonical record optional.

4. **Non-applicability.**  
   A record may be marked `not_applicable` only through a reasoned, event- or action-specific determination identifying: the trigger tested; the deciding function; the evidence and legal basis; the reason the trigger cannot arise or has not arisen; the records that would become required if conditions change; and the review or expiry condition. `Not applicable` does not mean unknown, inconvenient, deferred, or unavailable.

5. **Split records.**  
   Where several domain records jointly implement one canonical record, the framework shall declare a `splits` relationship, identify every component, allocate each required canonical field, designate the function that verifies completeness, and preserve one traceable lifecycle. No component inherits the authority or legal effect of another by implication.

6. **Containing packages.**  
   A domain package may contain several canonical records only when each canonical subrecord remains separately identifiable, reviewable, correctable, exportable, and closable, with its own responsible authority, legal effect, timestamp, expiry, and access class. The Investigation and Evidence Handoff Record (`ECRC-24`) and the Adjudication and Enforcement Handoff Record (`ECRC-25`) shall remain distinct even when displayed in one user interface or transmitted in one package.

7. **No record-to-power conversion.**  
   A record name, canonical identifier, completed form, model output, dashboard state, validation result, digital signature, institutional endorsement, or reference to another framework does not create authority. Consequential action requires a valid Emergency Authority Provenance Record (`ECRC-06`) and every other record required by the applicable activation package.

8. **Shared event identity without shared command.**  
   Records participating in a compound or cross-domain event shall carry the same `cluster_event_id` and identify their responsible function, authority, handoff state, evidence or data custodian, review time, and closure state. Shared identity coordinates information; it does not merge jurisdictions, transfer command, or establish a common legal classification.

9. **Handoff acceptance and retained duties.**  
   A handoff is effective only when the receiving function records acceptance, rejection, deficiency, or concurrent responsibility. A handoff transfers only the responsibility expressly named. It does not erase the sender's retained duties, source obligations, correction duties, evidence-preservation duties, or responsibility for prior acts.

10. **Lifecycle completeness.**  
    Any activation package that can restrict rights, deploy personnel or assets, process emergency data, spend extraordinary funds, or continue beyond a provisional period shall also represent renewal, termination, authority and asset restoration, emergency-data disposition, wrongful-action remedy, after-action review, institutional sunset or continuation, and unresolved risk or dissent. A package is not closed merely because a declaration or mission has ended.

11. **Access and protected material.**  
    Canonical identity does not require universal publication. Public summaries, protected annexes, restricted operational records, protected sovereignty or source records, and sealed adjudicative or security material shall follow the access classes in `ECRC/0.1`. Indigenous authority, non-engagement, protected knowledge, source protection, privacy, security, correction, retention, deletion, return, repatriation, and non-transfer obligations survive transport between frameworks.

12. **Correction, supersession, and integrity.**  
    Record identifiers remain immutable. Corrections, withdrawals, supersession, invalidation, and disputes shall preserve the prior state, reason, responsible actor, time, and linked replacement. Hashes and automated validation may support integrity but do not prove authenticity, accuracy, legal validity, or authority.

13. **Precedence and conflict.**  
    Where a domain record conflicts with the canonical profile, the rule that better preserves valid authority provenance, functional separation, rights, protected knowledge, temporal limits, restoration, and review controls for cluster interoperability controls. A domain framework may impose stronger safeguards. It may not weaken the canonical safeguards through renaming, omission, aggregation, software implementation, or local practice.

14. **Version pinning.**  
    This framework conforms only to the exact profile and interface versions identified in its compatibility manifest. A later framework, profile, schema, or interface version is not substituted automatically. Material changes fail closed until a successful compatibility and scenario test is recorded.

15. **Conformance appendix.**  
    This framework shall publish a complete crosswalk for `ECRC-01` through `ECRC-33`, showing for each canonical record: domain record name; inheritance relationship; applicability rule; responsible function; default access class; required linked records; lifecycle closure; and any current gap. Conformance is not established by a general statement that another framework controls.

16. **Automated systems.**  
    Automated systems may check schema, completeness, links, expiry, consistency, signatures, and access rules. They may not determine whether an authority is legally valid, whether evidence is credible, whether a person is liable or guilty, whether an Indigenous authority has consented, or whether an emergency power should be exercised or renewed.

17. **Failure rule.**  
    Missing, invalid, expired, incompatible, or unresolved authority and lifecycle records block the affected consequential action. They do not transfer authority to a coordinating body, observatory, emergency council, court, fund, platform, model, or residual command.

> **Framework conformance statement:** The Aegis Protocol v1.3.2 adopts Emergency Record Inheritance Clause v0.1 and conforms to Emergency and Catastrophic-Risk Cluster Record Profile v0.1 only to the extent shown in its complete ECRC-01–ECRC-33 crosswalk. Unmapped records are imported directly when triggered. Recorded gaps block the affected consequential action until resolved.

# 34. Constitutional readiness gates

The Aegis Protocol is not ready for binding implementation unless the relevant programme passes these gates:

1. **No framework-created force:** no Earth Defense Force or general command is created by the framework.
2. **No PIS override:** catastrophic-risk institutions cannot take command by declaration.
3. **No sanctions for refusal:** non-participation is not punishable.
4. **No spending-threshold coercion:** military expenditure does not automatically trigger sanctions or enforcement.
5. **No propaganda apparatus:** no psychological, covert, memetic, or counter-propaganda operation.
6. **No political surveillance:** criticism, lobbying, protest, journalism, and resistance are not generic monitoring targets.
7. **No covert Synoptic link:** public-information frameworks do not supply secret operations.
8. **No AUBI conditionality:** Layer 1 remains unconditional.
9. **No automatic elite pathway:** former commanders and executives receive no guaranteed roles.
10. **No capability-by-renaming:** civilian status requires demonstrable conversion and command separation.
11. **No mixed-mission ambiguity:** civilian and security purposes have separate authority.
12. **No hidden remote access:** override and administration pathways are disclosed to competent reviewers.
13. **No general inspection right:** access rests on valid consent or law.
14. **No auditor-to-sanction conversion:** findings do not themselves impose penalties.
15. **No public ledger mandate:** security and personal records are not immutably public by default.
16. **No Indigenous proxy:** affected peoples define their own authority and interfaces.
17. **No cultural appropriation:** narratives and knowledge are not used without authorization.
18. **No contributor command:** money or assets do not automatically buy control.
19. **No mandatory worldview training:** employment and benefits do not depend on spiritual or developmental programmes.
20. **No universal transition percentage:** fiscal commitments are lawfully chosen and context-specific.
21. **No essential-good leverage:** rescue, medicine, justice, and public information are not participation rewards.
22. **No automatic armed response:** attacks on programme assets do not trigger force through Aegis.
23. **No permanent emergency conversion:** re-tasking expires and authority returns.
24. **Failure may stop scaling:** a programme may narrow, close, decommission, or dissolve.

# 36. Conclusion

Aegis is strongest when it stops trying to become a better global military hierarchy and instead becomes a disciplined architecture for reducing dependence on militarized capability.

The world contains immense stores of logistics, engineering, aerospace, medical, scientific, manufacturing, communications, and organizational capacity inside security institutions. Some of that capacity can serve disaster resilience, environmental repair, public health, infrastructure continuity, asteroid observation, peaceful research, and shared exploration. But conversion is not achieved by changing the story told about the institution. It requires lawful consent, technical separation, civilian command, worker justice, data and technology boundaries, independent verification, mission-specific authority, and the ability to return or dismantle power.

> **The shield is not a new force above existing forces. It is the lawful conversion of capability so that fewer human and material systems depend on force at all.**
