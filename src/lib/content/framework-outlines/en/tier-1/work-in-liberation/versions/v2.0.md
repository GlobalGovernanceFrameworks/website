## Revision Note

Version 2.0 preserves v1.9's emancipatory purpose and principal mechanisms while changing the constitutional order among them.

The prior edition correctly argued that people cannot freely choose work while refusal threatens survival. It did not yet fully constrain the social, administrative, algorithmic, reputational, and emergency pressures that can replace wage coercion after baseline security is established.

This edition therefore begins with a stricter test:

> **Work is liberated only when participation, refusal, privacy, exit, and return are all materially and socially credible.**

The framework organizes voluntary work. It does not define human worth, impose a duty to demonstrate usefulness, or claim authority over the whole of personal, familial, cultural, spiritual, or relational life.

# Preface: Terminology and Intent

## Dual-Register Language

Work in Liberation deliberately speaks in both emancipatory and operational language.

| Emancipatory term | Institutional translation | Design requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Work in Liberation** | Autonomous work organization under material security | AUBI Layer 1 remains unconditional and refusal-compatible |
| **Survival freedom** | Decoupling essential needs from employment | No work condition attaches to baseline income or essential services |
| **Work With Purpose** | Freely chosen contribution with perceived value | Institutions offer opportunities but do not define a person's purpose |
| **Anti-capture** | Prevention of rent extraction and authority concentration | Worker governance, asset locks, transparent finance, independent appeal |
| **Fluid project assembly** | Time-bounded, cross-provider team formation | Participant-controlled availability, compensation, data, and exit |
| **Need broadcasting** | Accountable publication of a problem seeking contributors | A broadcast is an invitation, not an assignment or proof of need |
| **Industrial Sunsetting** | Managed phase-out of ecologically harmful production | Workers and communities receive security, voice, replacement capacity, and repair |
| **Planetary contribution** | Work serving ecological and shared-commons outcomes | Ecological evidence, Indigenous authority, safety, and slow-variable review |

## Intellectual Honesty

The framework retains the word **liberation** because many apparently voluntary labor arrangements remain structured by survival dependency, unequal bargaining power, inaccessible exit, and social status.

It also recognizes that liberation language can itself become coercive. A community may praise visible regenerative contribution so strongly that ordinary life, privacy, rest, disability, contemplation, and non-participation become morally suspect. A platform may replace the résumé with a more invasive contribution history. A crisis body may replace the employer's command with an institutional call to serve.

The framework therefore treats its own moral narrative as a possible source of capture.

## Accountable Coordination Anchor

No actor should simultaneously control:

- which needs are recognized;
- who is matched to them;
- work validation;
- payment;
- provider chartering;
- worker discipline;
- programme evaluation;
- and appeal.

Where several functions share the same person, platform, funding body, or authority, the dependency must be declared and counterbalanced through secondary review, worker representation, independent observation, or external appeal.

# Executive Summary

Work in Liberation provides the organizational infrastructure through which people may contribute to care, culture, community resilience, creativity, and planetary regeneration after AUBI has made material survival independent of employment.

Its purpose is not to maximize labor-force participation. It is to make better forms of organized work possible while protecting life outside work.

The framework rests on nine commitments:

1. **Unconditional dignity:** A person's rights and material security do not depend on contribution.
2. **Credible refusal:** A participant may decline, pause, withdraw, or remain undiscoverable without penalty.
3. **Worker authority:** Community Providers are governed substantially by the people who perform the work.
4. **Need before role:** Communities broadcast accountable needs rather than force people into static occupational containers.
5. **Purpose-limited matching:** Matching helps people find opportunities without creating a generalized employability or moral reputation score.
6. **Multiple commissioning channels:** Public goods, ecology, prevention, care, and crisis capacity are not forced to survive on individual transaction demand.
7. **Evidence proportional to claim:** Proof that work occurred is not automatically proof that it helped, caused an outcome, or deserves a specific reward.
8. **Independent observation:** Programme activity is separated from evidence of liberation and flourishing.
9. **Freedom beyond work:** Rest, care, relationships, study, art, contemplation, and unstructured life are legitimate ends, not residual time awaiting productive use.

The core constitutional test is:

> **Could a person decline every formal opportunity, keep private contributions unrecorded, retain full material security and civic standing, and later return without penalty?**

If not, work has been reorganized but not liberated.

# 1. Purpose, Scope, and Constitutional Freedoms

## 1.1 Purpose

Work in Liberation governs:

- Community Provider and Community Work Team charters;
- worker and participant rights;
- work governance and professional standards;
- flexible and fluid participation;
- need broadcasting and project assembly;
- Skills Resonance Matching;
- compensation and work-validation interfaces;
- public-good and ecological commissioning;
- Community Weaver professional roles;
- crisis work safeguards;
- Industrial Sunsetting and just transition;
- work-system observation and evaluation.

## 1.2 Boundaries

The framework does not:

- define AUBI eligibility, Layer 1 entitlement, or LMCI-M;
- issue Hearts or Leaves;
- govern Treasury reserves, redemption, fraud, or ICTL settlement;
- replace local BAZ authority over service priorities;
- make all care or contribution monetizable;
- create a universal duty to work;
- authorize military service, forced labor, or compulsory civic service;
- determine Indigenous knowledge validity outside community-governed protocols;
- convert private life into institutional evidence.

## 1.3 Constitutional Freedoms

Every person has the right to:

- material security independent of work participation;
- decline any ordinary work opportunity;
- pause or leave a provider or project;
- return later without reputational penalty;
- remain absent from matching systems;
- keep informal contributions unrecorded;
- choose ordinary, commercial, artistic, familial, contemplative, or unstructured life;
- refuse public recognition;
- refuse unsafe or conscience-violating tasks;
- know and challenge consequential decisions about their work records or access;
- organize with other workers;
- use an advocate or representative;
- access essential services regardless of contribution history.

## 1.4 No Adverse Inference from Non-Participation

Low, irregular, or absent formal participation does not establish:

- laziness;
- lack of meaning;
- low social value;
- incapacity;
- fraud;
- weak community connection;
- refusal of reciprocity;
- or policy failure.

Possible explanations include privacy, health, recovery, disability, informal care, sufficient private life, inaccessible opportunities, discrimination, distrust, ordinary preference, or a deliberate choice not to organize life through work.

## 1.5 Purpose Is Not Assigned

Institutions may create conditions for purpose-seeking. They may not define the purpose of a person or require that a life be publicly legible as meaningful.

**Work With Purpose** is therefore an invitation and design aspiration, not a status category.

# 2. Participation Modes and the Right Not to Contribute

## 2.1 Private and Unrecorded Life

The following ordinarily remain outside institutional observation:

- household and family life;
- friendship and intimate relationships;
- spiritual practice;
- private study and creativity;
- rest and recovery;
- ordinary mutual aid;
- informal cultural participation;
- unstructured time.

A participant may voluntarily disclose bounded evidence for a specific purpose, but no comprehensive inventory is required.

## 2.2 Voluntary Love Ledger Recognition

The Love Ledger provides non-monetized, voluntary recognition of informal care, gratitude, reciprocity, and community life.

Participation:

- is optional;
- is not required to rebut claims of laziness;
- does not create a default work credential;
- does not feed LMCI-M or LMCI-A mechanically;
- does not determine AUBI, Hearts, fiat, employment access, or civic standing;
- may remain local, private, pseudonymous, or non-digital where culturally appropriate;
- is subject to correction, contextualization, and purpose-limited deletion.

## 2.3 Formal Community Provider Participation

A person joins a chartered Community Provider under explicit terms concerning:

- role;
- governance;
- compensation;
- schedule;
- safety;
- data;
- validation;
- complaint;
- and exit.

Formal provider participation is organized work even where it is meaningful, communal, or intrinsically motivated.

## 2.4 Time-Limited Project Participation

A person may join one bounded project or need broadcast without becoming a permanent provider member or creating a generalized occupational identity.

The project must disclose:

- duration;
- compensation;
- authority;
- required evidence;
- data retention;
- safety;
- exit;
- and whether completion creates any portable credential.

## 2.5 Public or Collective Service

A BAZ, compact, or authorized commons body may commission services serving collective needs. Such work is publicly funded and governed rather than made dependent on individual purchases.

Public commissioning does not make participation compulsory.

## 2.6 Emergency Participation

Emergency work is voluntary by default and uses the safeguards in §13.

A separate constitutional framework would be required to authorize any compulsory civic duty. Work in Liberation does not create such authority.

## 2.7 Entry, Pause, Withdrawal, and Return

Participants may:

- enter at a level suited to current capacity;
- pause without disclosing intimate reasons;
- withdraw from a project or provider under disclosed safety and handover rules;
- retain earned compensation;
- retain claimant-controlled credentials;
- return later without a penalty based solely on the pause or withdrawal.

Providers may require reasonable notice for safety-critical tasks, but may not use handover obligations to trap participants indefinitely.

# 3. Community Providers and Worker Governance

## 3.1 Community Work Teams

Community Work Teams are locally governed forms of Community Provider performing care, cultural, technical, educational, repair, ecological, civic, and resilience work.

They may be:

- worker cooperatives;
- community trusts;
- public-community partnerships;
- Indigenous-governed entities;
- nonprofit providers;
- time-limited project organizations;
- federations of smaller teams.

## 3.2 Provider Charter Standard

Every charter specifies:

1. mission and permitted activities;
2. ownership and asset lock;
3. worker membership and standing;
4. governance method;
5. leadership selection and removal;
6. compensation and pay ratios;
7. financial transparency;
8. safety and safeguarding;
9. working-time rules;
10. disability and caregiving accommodation;
11. anti-discrimination;
12. cultural and Indigenous authority;
13. data and privacy;
14. intellectual, cultural, and ecological knowledge rights;
15. conflicts of interest;
16. external contracting;
17. complaint and whistleblower channels;
18. independent review and appeal;
19. exit and dissolution;
20. treatment of accumulated assets and records.

## 3.3 Anti-Capture Requirements

A Community Provider may not, by default:

- issue conventional equity giving external investors control;
- distribute surplus to passive outside owners;
- be sold without worker and community authorization;
- transfer public or commons assets into private ownership;
- bind workers through debt or repayment clauses that undermine exit;
- use exclusive platform access to prevent worker portability;
- condition AUBI or public rights on provider membership.

External financing may receive bounded repayment or stewardship returns without governance capture.

## 3.4 Worker Governance

Workers have meaningful authority over:

- operational leadership;
- scheduling;
- compensation allocation;
- safety;
- work methods;
- admission and role design;
- internal discipline;
- financial oversight;
- provider strategy.

No universal voting method is prescribed. Providers may use one-person-one-vote, consent, sociocracy, sortition, delegated authority, or bounded quadratic methods where understandable and rights-compatible.

## 3.5 BAZ Chartering and Separation

BAZ Councils charter and monitor local providers under Work in Liberation standards.

The BAZ body that designs or funds a provider should not be the sole body that:

- audits its success;
- adjudicates worker complaints;
- validates contested contribution claims;
- or determines sanctions.

Independent worker representation, secondary validation, and external appeal are required for consequential disputes.

## 3.6 Provider Recognition Across BAZs

Cross-BAZ recognition follows Nested Economies through a Cross-Scale Interface Contract or provider-recognition compact.

Recognition may transfer a bounded credential without transferring all worker, recipient, cultural, or project evidence.

## 3.7 Cultural Adaptation and Intergenerational Leadership

BAZs adapt work organization to local cultural rhythms, languages, reciprocal traditions, disability practices, and Indigenous governance. Adaptation may change schedules, governance, recognition, team composition, and acceptable evidence, but may not weaken material, safety, refusal, privacy, or appeal rights.

Youth and elders may hold substantive leadership and mentorship roles without being reduced to symbolic representation. The inherited pilot target of at least 20% youth leadership may be retained locally as a benchmark where youth assemblies support it, but no universal quota substitutes for actual authority.

Elder and cultural mentorship may be:

- formal provider work compensated under published terms;
- voluntary Love Ledger recognition;
- protected, unrecorded cultural transmission;
- or a community-governed role outside the formal system.

Traditional or sacred knowledge is not made portable merely because it contributes to work quality.

# 4. Worker Bill of Rights

Every worker or formal project participant has the right to:

## 4.1 Material and Contractual Clarity

- know the commissioning and funding source;
- receive plain-language terms before participation;
- know whether compensation is Hearts, fiat, Leaves, expenses, LMCI-A bonus, or a combination;
- know the schedule, duration, and expected hidden labor;
- receive timely payment and expense reimbursement;
- inspect provider financial rules relevant to compensation.

## 4.2 Governance and Organization

- elect or remove leadership under the charter;
- organize independently;
- form worker councils or unions;
- use an advocate;
- participate in decisions materially affecting work conditions;
- challenge charter or standard changes.

## 4.3 Safety and Refusal

- refuse unsafe, unlawful, culturally harmful, or conscience-violating tasks;
- receive training and protective equipment;
- stop work where imminent harm is reasonably suspected;
- report hazards without retaliation;
- receive trauma and recovery support where relevant.

## 4.4 Time and Rest

- schedule predictability proportionate to the service;
- rest and recovery;
- recognition of travel, preparation, administration, on-call time, emotional debrief, and mandatory training;
- limits on repeated emergency or overtime demands;
- accommodation for disability, health, caregiving, prayer, and cultural rhythms.

## 4.5 Data and Epistemic Rights

- know what records are created;
- access and correct records;
- supply context and contradictory evidence;
- receive an explanation of matching, validation, discipline, or access decisions;
- obtain accountable human review;
- appeal outside the original decision chain;
- restrict reuse of records beyond their original purpose;
- refuse Love Ledger participation.

## 4.6 Exit and Restoration

- leave without losing earned compensation;
- take claimant-controlled credentials and work products where lawful;
- protect cultural and personal data;
- correct downstream records after a successful appeal;
- receive restoration where an erroneous decision caused material or reputational harm.

# 5. Flexible Time, Rest, and Hidden Labor

## 5.1 Default Protective Ceiling

The inherited **15-hour rule** is retained as a pilot default of **15 hours per week**, averaged over a four-week period, for routinely requested or incentivized participation in Community Provider and Work in Liberation projects.

It is:

- a protection against institutional overreach;
- not a minimum;
- not a measure of citizenship;
- not an expectation of every person;
- not a limit on private unrecorded life;
- not automatically applicable to conventional employment outside the framework.

## 5.2 Time Included

The ceiling includes work reasonably required by the role:

- direct activity;
- preparation;
- travel where substantial;
- documentation;
- meetings;
- mandatory training;
- on-call time proportionate to restriction;
- emotional debrief and safeguarding processes;
- handover.

Providers may not externalize required labor by labelling it voluntary.

## 5.3 Voluntary Additional Participation

A participant may freely choose additional hours where:

- consent is explicit and renewable;
- AUBI and material access are not driving overwork;
- compensation and safety remain adequate;
- the provider does not normalize chronic excess;
- caregiving and health effects are considered;
- refusal of extra hours has no penalty.

Repeated excess hours trigger a provider-capacity review.

## 5.4 Local Adaptation

BAZs may propose a different ceiling for a defined service class, but must publish:

- the reason;
- worker consultation;
- hidden-labor treatment;
- health and care impacts;
- voluntariness evidence;
- review and sunset.

A higher ceiling cannot be justified solely by unmet demand.

## 5.5 No Minimum Contribution

A person contributing zero formal hours retains all baseline rights, public services, civic standing, and future eligibility.

## 5.6 Workload and Burnout Monitoring

Providers monitor:

- requested versus accepted hours;
- voluntary excess;
- unpaid time;
- cancellations and withdrawals;
- burnout;
- injury;
- caregiving conflict;
- turnover;
- chronic vacancies;
- emergency frequency.

These are provider-capacity signals, not worker-motivation scores.

# 6. Need Broadcasting and Collective Priority Setting

## 6.1 Purpose

Need broadcasting replaces a narrow job-first process with an accountable description of a problem, affected condition, or desired public outcome.

It allows people with different capabilities and availability to assemble around the need without first fitting a fixed occupational identity.

## 6.2 A Broadcast Is an Invitation

A need broadcast is not:

- a command;
- proof that the broadcaster's formulation is correct;
- evidence that a specific person should respond;
- authority to contact undiscoverable people;
- justification for lowering compensation or safety;
- proof of community consent.

## 6.3 Who May Broadcast

Possible broadcasters include:

- affected residents or participant groups;
- BAZ Councils;
- chartered Community Providers;
- Indigenous authorities;
- public agencies;
- voluntary BAZ compacts;
- authorized ecological or commons bodies;
- crisis authorities within their mandate.

The broadcast identifies the source and authority.

## 6.4 Need Broadcast Contract

Every institutionally promoted or funded broadcast specifies:

1. broadcaster and authority;
2. affected group;
3. affected-group account and consent where applicable;
4. problem or need;
5. evidence, missingness, and uncertainty;
6. competing formulations;
7. urgency and irreversibility;
8. classification: individual service, public good, ecological, solidarity, infrastructure, or crisis;
9. task scope and excluded tasks;
10. resources and funding;
11. compensation and expenses;
12. expected time, including hidden labor;
13. hazards and safeguarding;
14. required and learnable capabilities;
15. accessibility;
16. data collected;
17. discoverability and matching rules;
18. outcome measures;
19. stop and reassessment conditions;
20. complaint and challenge pathway.

## 6.5 Need Prioritization

Prioritization considers separately:

- severity;
- number and identity of people affected;
- rights;
- urgency;
- irreversibility;
- ecological significance;
- neglected populations;
- distributional effects;
- feasibility;
- existing capacity;
- alternatives;
- affected-party preference.

No single popularity, transaction, or dashboard score decides priority.

## 6.6 Contested Needs

Where affected parties reject the broadcaster's framing, the broadcast records the disagreement and may be:

- revised;
- split into alternatives;
- paused;
- tested through a reversible pilot;
- or declined.

## 6.7 Unfilled Needs

An unfilled need triggers diagnosis of:

- compensation;
- safety;
- schedule;
- skill availability;
- equipment;
- accessibility;
- trust;
- project design;
- authority;
- affected-party support;
- provider and Weaver capacity.

It does not justify shaming, surveillance, or adverse inference against non-participants.

## 6.8 Critical and Unpopular Work Protocol

For essential work that remains unfilled, the commissioning body must test, in order:

1. improve conditions and safety;
2. increase compensation or provide stable salaried capacity;
3. redesign or reduce the task;
4. provide training and equipment;
5. rotate burdens fairly among consenting workers;
6. automate harmful or repetitive components where rights-compatible;
7. recruit across a wider voluntary network;
8. build permanent public-service capacity.

Compulsory assignment is not authorized by this framework.

# 7. Skills Resonance Matching and Discoverability Rights

## 7.1 Purpose

Skills Resonance Matching helps people discover opportunities that fit their chosen capabilities, interests, constraints, and availability.

It does not determine who a person is or what they owe the community.

## 7.2 Claimant-Controlled Matching Profile

Participants choose whether to create a profile and control:

- disclosed skills;
- credentials;
- interests;
- availability;
- location or remote range;
- accessibility needs;
- desired task types;
- excluded task types;
- contact permissions;
- cross-BAZ portability;
- expiry;
- historical evidence allowed.

## 7.3 Love Ledger Exclusion by Default

Love Ledger records are not used for matching by default.

A participant may deliberately convert a specific experience into a bounded self-controlled capability statement without transferring the relational record itself.

Example:

- permitted: “experienced in organizing meal support for groups of 20”;
- prohibited by default: exposing the underlying recipients, gratitude records, frequency, or social network.

## 7.4 No General Reputation Score

The matching system may not create a generalized score for:

- reliability;
- helpfulness;
- employability;
- community spirit;
- social value;
- trustworthiness;
- moral deservingness.

Purpose-specific credentials remain distinguishable from reputation.

## 7.5 No Adverse Inference from Sparse Records

Sparse or absent records may reflect privacy, new arrival, disability, care, illness, discrimination, inaccessible technology, or simple preference.

The system provides self-declared and learning pathways rather than automatically ranking sparse profiles lower.

## 7.6 Explainability and Correction

For each proposed match, the participant may see:

- which fields produced the match;
- why a filter excluded or included them;
- uncertainty;
- whether a human changed the result;
- what data source and model version were used;
- how to correct the profile;
- how to opt out.

## 7.7 Prohibited Uses

Matching data may not be used without separate authority for:

- AUBI eligibility;
- fraud scoring;
- creditworthiness;
- policing;
- migration control;
- insurance;
- political targeting;
- compulsory mobilisation;
- generalized social ranking.

## 7.8 Evaluation

Matching is evaluated through separate measures:

- participant-rated fit;
- voluntary acceptance;
- correction rate;
- withdrawal and mismatch;
- subgroup access;
- accessibility;
- repeat contact pressure;
- harmful or unsafe matches;
- data misuse;
- participant autonomy after matching.

There is no single “match accuracy” score with an assumed objective ground truth.

# 8. Compensation, LMCI-A, and Proof of Care

## 8.1 Distinct Economic Flows

The framework separates:

1. **provider operating revenue** — funds service delivery and organizational costs;
2. **worker compensation** — payment for labor under provider or project rules;
3. **expenses and materials** — reimbursement or direct procurement;
4. **AUBI Layer 2 bonus** — contribution-level recognition under LMCI-A and Proof of Care;
5. **Leaves** — ecological records and reward instruments under AUBI, Financial Systems, FLP, and ecological protocols;
6. **Love Ledger recognition** — non-monetized gratitude and narrative;
7. **public commissioning funds** — collective payment for public goods;
8. **Treasury redemption** — provider conversion for authorized external costs.

No flow silently substitutes for another.

## 8.2 Fair Compensation Standard

Provider compensation rules address:

- compensation floor;
- pay ratios;
- unpaid and delayed time;
- preparation and travel;
- emotional and care labor;
- risk and inconvenience;
- equipment;
- disability accommodation;
- caregiving constraints;
- transparent allocation among workers, operations, and reserves;
- portable earned rights at exit.

## 8.3 Provider Revenue Is Not Worker Pay

A provider receiving Hearts does not by itself establish that workers were fairly paid.

Providers publish worker-accessible allocation rules and material variances.

## 8.4 Proof of Care Evidence Layers

Work in Liberation inherits AUBI's distinction among:

1. **Proof of Activity:** the work occurred;
2. **Recipient or Community Acceptance:** it was received as represented, where confirmation is appropriate and safe;
3. **Proof of Outcome:** a relevant condition changed;
4. **Proof of Additionality:** the change is attributable to the contribution rather than ordinary background change;
5. **Uncertainty and Adverse Effects:** disagreement, limits, displacement, or harm.

Not every claim requires all five layers. The claim must state which layer is being asserted.

## 8.5 Activity Compensation and Outcome Recognition

Competent work may receive stable compensation even when long-term outcomes remain uncertain or outside worker control.

Outcome-based rewards are used cautiously where:

- the outcome is sufficiently observable;
- attribution is credible;
- workers do not bear uncontrollable risk;
- the metric cannot be cheaply manipulated;
- adverse effects are monitored;
- affected communities participate in interpretation.

## 8.6 Ecological Work

Ecological work may combine:

- stable operating compensation;
- safety and skill recognition;
- Leaves for verified ecological claims;
- team-level long-term outcome recognition.

Weather, migration, ecological lag, actions by other actors, and method changes remain viable explanations for outcomes.

## 8.7 Challenge and Appeal

Workers and providers may challenge:

- activity records;
- recipient evidence;
- outcome attribution;
- multiplier application;
- compensation allocation;
- anomaly flags;
- withheld payment.

Community Weavers may attest evidence but cannot solely adjudicate a contested high-value or adverse claim.

# 9. Commissioning Public, Ecological, Solidarity, and Essential Work

## 9.1 Individual Service Purchase

Participants may spend Hearts with providers for directly chosen services such as:

- childcare;
- repair;
- tutoring;
- meals;
- transport assistance;
- personal support.

## 9.2 Participatory Public Commissioning

BAZs may commission collectively valued services through transparent budgets and participatory priority setting.

Examples:

- public-space maintenance;
- preventive health;
- accessibility;
- mediation;
- emergency preparation;
- community infrastructure.

## 9.3 Commons and Ecological Commissioning

BAZs, compacts, ecological authorities, Indigenous authorities, or the Global Commons Fund may commission work benefiting shared and future interests.

Examples:

- watershed restoration;
- biodiversity monitoring;
- soil recovery;
- habitat connectivity;
- cultural-landscape stewardship;
- long-term ecological observation.

## 9.4 Solidarity and Reparative Commissioning

Work may be funded where:

- affected people lack purchasing power;
- a community bears a stewardship burden;
- prior extraction created a reparative obligation;
- transition costs are uneven;
- shared infrastructure is needed.

The Solidarity Protocol in Nested Economies governs cross-BAZ transfers.

## 9.5 Crisis Commissioning

Crisis work follows §13 and Financial Systems's protected-minimum and liquidity rules.

## 9.6 Funding Model Must Match Service Type

A provider is not judged financially unsustainable merely because a public, preventive, seasonal, ecological, or reserve service does not earn sufficient individual transaction revenue.

Evaluation asks whether the commissioning model matches the service and whether funding is reliable, equitable, and accountable.

## 9.7 Demand Does Not Equal Need

Individual spending observes preference, access, awareness, and ability to pay. It does not fully observe rights, prevention, ecological value, future benefit, or people absent from the market.

## 9.8 Funding Portfolio

Depending on service type, authorized funding may include:

- participant Hearts spending;
- BAZ participatory budgets;
- Global Commons Fund grants;
- solidarity and reparative transfers;
- Automation Tax and Resource Tax revenues;
- commons dividends;
- public procurement;
- philanthropic or cooperative finance without governance capture;
- fair external contracts;
- transition and crisis funds.

A funding source does not acquire authority over worker governance, cultural knowledge, or programme evaluation merely by financing the work. Financial flows follow AUBI, Financial Systems, Nested Economies, and applicable Treaty rules.

## 9.9 Regenerative Market Interfaces

Community Providers may trade regenerative goods and services through BAZ markets and Gaian Trade interfaces. Hearts settlement follows Financial Systems, and cross-BAZ recognition follows Nested Economies.

Leaves are not treated as a general fungible trading currency. Ecological claims remain linked to provenance, ecological evidence, Indigenous authority, and slow-variable review.

# 10. Informal Contribution and the Love Ledger Boundary

## 10.1 Purpose

The Love Ledger recognizes informal relational value without absorbing it into the formal work or financial system.

## 10.2 Permitted Uses

Love Ledger records may:

- express gratitude;
- preserve voluntary community stories;
- support cultural reflection;
- generate hypotheses about unmet needs;
- help a participant articulate a self-controlled capability claim;
- identify a desire for support at the participant's request.

## 10.3 Prohibited Default Uses

Love Ledger records do not, by default:

- rank people;
- match people to work;
- establish professional competence;
- prove moral worth;
- determine AUBI;
- determine LMCI-M or LMCI-A;
- evaluate provider productivity;
- support fraud findings;
- determine civic standing;
- become public work histories.

## 10.4 Informal Care Burden Sentinel

Where informal care creates exhaustion, coercion, health effects, lost opportunity, material deprivation, or need for respite, the participant may use the claimant-controlled AUBI Informal Care Burden Sentinel.

The Sentinel records burden and need rather than ranking the relationship or contribution.

## 10.5 Recognition Ceremonies

Community Renewal ceremonies and other public recognition are optional.

A person may:

- decline attendance;
- decline public naming;
- choose private recognition;
- withdraw a record;
- participate without receiving a comparative rank.

## 10.6 No Need to Demonstrate Usefulness

AUBI recipients do not need to make informal contribution visible to answer accusations of laziness. Baseline rights do not depend on proving social usefulness.

# 11. Community Weaver Profession and Role Separation

## 11.1 Purpose

Community Weavers support access, translation, provider development, team formation, and community interpretation.

They are facilitators and witnesses, not presumed neutral or omniscient authorities.

## 11.2 Permitted Functions

Weavers may:

- explain Work in Liberation and AUBI options;
- support accessible onboarding;
- help groups form or charter providers;
- facilitate need formulation;
- help participants articulate skills and constraints;
- originate or attest evidence;
- support Love Ledger use where voluntarily requested;
- identify unmet need and system barriers;
- help participants use complaint and appeal pathways;
- translate between local and institutional language.

## 11.3 Prohibited Sole-Source Functions

A Weaver may not alone:

- validate a claim they helped construct;
- adjudicate a complaint against a provider they helped establish;
- determine fraud;
- certify programme success;
- judge cultural legitimacy without delegated community authority;
- assign a participant reputation;
- determine whether refusal was acceptable;
- control both matching and eligibility;
- serve as the only observer of worker satisfaction or coercion.

## 11.4 Secondary Review

Secondary review is mandatory for:

- adverse decisions;
- disputed evidence;
- high-value contribution claims;
- provider suspension or closure;
- alleged retaliation;
- structural programme evaluation;
- cultural or ecological claims with cross-community consequences.

## 11.5 Worker-Controlled Channels

Each BAZ provides access to:

- worker advocate or ombuds;
- confidential complaint route;
- independent validator;
- provider audit capacity;
- appeal outside the original decision chain;
- anti-retaliation protection.

## 11.6 Capacity Monitoring

The Work Liberation Conditions Dashboard tracks:

- Weaver caseload;
- supervision;
- turnover;
- translation and accessibility capacity;
- validation backlog;
- complaint response;
- secondary-review availability;
- community and worker evaluations;
- source dependencies.

Numerical Weaver targets do not override training and supervision capacity.

# 12. Governance and Decision Rights

## 12.1 Fractal Labor Parliament

The **Fractal Labor Parliament (FLP)** is the Meta-Governance coordination body for shared work principles.

It:

- sets minimum labor, safety, care, and provider-governance standards;
- proposes Green Job Score ranges and ecological work categories;
- supports cross-BAZ credential and profession standards;
- convenes worker, Indigenous, youth, care, technical, and ecological expertise;
- publishes Observation Contracts for consequential work-standard changes;
- cannot adjudicate its own alleged capture or rights violations.

The inherited composition target—40% Indigenous representatives, 30% workers, 20% youth, and 10% technical or domain experts—remains an illustrative default subject to Meta-Governance harmonization, transparent selection, term limits, conflicts rules, and accessibility.

## 12.2 BAZ Councils

BAZ Councils:

- charter local Community Providers;
- set local service priorities;
- adapt shared standards to local context without weakening rights floors;
- support participatory commissioning;
- maintain local complaint and worker-advocacy capacity;
- submit evidence and counter-models;
- may not condition AUBI on contribution.

## 12.3 Provider Worker Governance

Provider members govern ordinary operations, compensation allocation, leadership, scheduling, and work methods within charter and rights constraints.

## 12.4 Social Resilience Council and Treasuries

The SRC and Hearts Treasuries exercise only the financial functions assigned by Financial Systems.

They do not:

- define work's social worth;
- adjudicate ordinary labor disputes;
- control provider leadership;
- convert Love Ledger records into labor surveillance.

## 12.5 Digital Justice Tribunal and Independent Appeals

The Tribunal or other authorized independent bodies hear:

- systemic rights violations;
- cross-BAZ labor disputes;
- provider capture;
- retaliation;
- contested sanctions;
- serious data misuse;
- enforcement-adjacent cases.

## 12.6 Earth Council and Slow Variables

Earth Council and LEKH processes protect Indigenous sovereignty and long-term ecological evidence.

Changes to:

- Green Job Score ranges;
- ecological eligibility categories;
- Industrial Sunsetting schedules;
- long-term ecological work standards

follow the Slow-Variable Decision Protocol.

## 12.7 Decision-Rights Matrix

| Decision | Primary authority | Required review or challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Charter a local Community Provider | BAZ Council | Work in Liberation standards; worker and community appeal |
| Set provider labor and safety standards | FLP | Worker participation; BAZ adaptation; NARP where structural |
| Set local service priorities | BAZ community and Council | Nested Economies subsidiarity and participation rules |
| Set compensation allocation | Provider worker governance | FLP floor; financial transparency; worker appeal |
| Validate LMCI-A claim | AUBI Proof of Care process | Secondary review; claimant challenge |
| Process Hearts payment or redemption | Treasury under Financial Systems | Financial rights and appeal |
| Set Green Job Score range | FLP proposal | SVDC, LEKH, ecological governance |
| Match participant to opportunity | Participant-controlled matching system | Explanation, correction, opt-out |
| Resolve ordinary labor dispute | Independent local process | Tribunal escalation where necessary |
| Commission public-good work | BAZ or authorized compact | Participatory process and State Estimate Record |
| Activate voluntary crisis mobilisation | Authorized crisis body | §13 safeguards, independent monitoring, sunset |
| Impose sanction | Tribunal or lawful authority | Evidence, notice, proportionality, appeal |
| Evaluate population flourishing | Independent LMCI-M process | No provider, matching, or Love Ledger activity proxy |

## 12.8 Observation and State Estimates

Consequential decisions require the AUBI Observation Contract and State Estimate Record architecture, adapted to work governance.

This includes:

- provider chartering or closure;
- need prioritization;
- matching-system changes;
- adverse worker action;
- ecological valuation;
- crisis mobilisation;
- Industrial Sunsetting;
- major work-standard revisions.

# 13. Crisis Work and Emergency Mobilisation

## 13.1 Voluntary Default

A crisis does not automatically remove:

- refusal;
- informed consent;
- safety;
- compensation;
- disability accommodation;
- caregiving constraints;
- conscientious objection;
- rest;
- withdrawal;
- appeal.

## 13.2 Authorized Mobilisation

A formally authorized crisis body may:

- publish urgent need broadcasts;
- accelerate recruitment and training;
- commission essential providers;
- redirect discretionary project funding;
- request temporary inter-BAZ assistance;
- coordinate essential-provider liquidity under Financial Systems.

It may not simply reassign individuals or providers outside lawful and chartered authority.

## 13.3 Emergency Work Mobilisation Record

Every mobilisation states:

1. lawful declaration;
2. crisis condition and evidence;
3. authorized body;
4. affected area and population;
5. essential task;
6. why ordinary capacity is insufficient;
7. recruitment method;
8. voluntariness safeguards;
9. compensation and expenses;
10. insurance, equipment, and safety;
11. training;
12. maximum duration and hours;
13. disability and caregiving accommodation;
14. refusal, withdrawal, and conscientious objection;
15. data collected;
16. independent observer;
17. reassessment date;
18. demobilisation plan;
19. restoration and after-action review.

## 13.4 Repeated Emergency as Capacity Failure

Repeated crisis mobilisation for the same service triggers review of:

- permanent staffing;
- infrastructure;
- public commissioning;
- prevention;
- funding;
- leadership;
- safety;
- supply chains.

Fast deployment is not evidence of resilience where ordinary capacity is chronically neglected.

## 13.5 Compulsory Civic Service

Any compulsory service requires a separate constitutional basis establishing necessity, proportionality, democratic authority, exemptions, duration, compensation, and judicial review.

No such power is implied by membership in AUBI, a BAZ, a provider, the Love Ledger, or the matching system.

## 13.6 Protected AUBI Minimum

Crisis work does not condition or replace AUBI Layer 1. Financial crisis measures remain subject to Financial Systems's protected-minimum rules.

# 14. Industrial Sunsetting and Just Transition

## 14.1 Purpose

Industrial Sunsetting phases out activities whose ecological, health, rights, or commons harms cannot be reduced to an acceptable level.

The framework distinguishes harmful systems from the people whose livelihoods and communities currently depend on them.

## 14.2 Sunsetting State Estimate

Before a major phase-out, publish:

- harmful condition;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- affected ecosystems and communities;
- rights and Treaty basis;
- alternative pathways;
- affected workers;
- transferable capabilities;
- regional fiscal dependence;
- critical products currently supplied;
- replacement capacity;
- transition-fund adequacy;
- geographic and household effects;
- Indigenous and long-embedded evidence;
- sequencing;
- outcome measures;
- review and revision conditions.

## 14.3 Worker and Community Transition Bodies

Affected workers and communities establish representative transition bodies with authority over:

- retraining design;
- replacement work;
- relocation or place-based alternatives;
- use of transition funds;
- transfer of useful infrastructure;
- pension and income protection;
- remediation work;
- local economic diversification.

## 14.4 Sequencing

A default sequence is:

1. stop subsidy and expansion of harmful activity;
2. establish worker and community transition governance;
3. secure incomes, benefits, and services;
4. fund training and replacement capacity;
5. transfer useful assets and skills;
6. phase down operations;
7. remediate ecological and social damage;
8. evaluate worker, community, and ecosystem outcomes.

The sequence may change where continued operation creates imminent harm, but the burden may not be shifted silently onto workers.

## 14.5 Transition Pathways

### Gig Worker Transition

Workers may participate in legacy and Community Provider systems during transition. AUBI protects refusal and experimentation. Matching remains opt-in and claimant-controlled.

### Cooperative Conversion

Businesses may convert to Community Providers through:

- worker buyout or stewardship transfer;
- asset lock;
- governance redesign;
- financing without external control;
- provider chartering;
- Hearts acceptance and Treasury interfaces;
- labor-rights audit.

### Fair Labor Contracts

Corporations and public bodies may contract Community Providers under terms protecting:

- worker governance;
- pay and safety;
- BAZ authority;
- Indigenous rights;
- data limits;
- anti-dependency;
- independent dispute resolution.

A premium to shared funds may be used where governed transparently, but does not substitute for fair worker compensation.

## 14.6 Success

Transition success is evaluated through:

- income stability;
- material security;
- quality and durability of new work;
- worker autonomy;
- health;
- geographic displacement;
- community fiscal resilience;
- skill use;
- worker consent;
- unpaid transition burden;
- ecological recovery.

Training completion alone is not success.

# 15. Planetary Mobilisations and Enforcement-Adjacent Work

## 15.1 Scope

Large-scale transnational work programmes may address shared commons challenges through chartered providers and voluntary corps.

Examples include:

- marine stewardship;
- ecosystem restoration;
- disaster prevention;
- public-health mobilisation;
- peacebuilding;
- infrastructure repair.

## 15.2 Blue Stewards

The Marine Stewardship Corps remains a flagship model for:

- ecological observation;
- protected-area presence;
- restoration;
- debris and ghost-net removal;
- evidence support to lawful authorities.

Blue Stewards are civilian ecological workers and witnesses, not combatants.

## 15.3 Enforcement Boundary

Work programmes may observe and preserve evidence. They do not automatically determine guilt or impose sanctions.

Sensor or witness evidence may trigger:

- evidence preservation;
- alert;
- narrowly bounded temporary containment under lawful authority;
- investigation.

Permanent financial, trade, licensing, or blacklist sanctions require an authorized adjudicative process with notice, evidence, proportionality, and appeal.

## 15.4 Dedicated Evidence Pipeline

Enforcement evidence is not published through the Love Ledger.

A dedicated system maintains:

- chain of custody;
- source provenance;
- sensor reliability;
- method and model version;
- human review;
- uncertainty;
- protected and safety-sensitive handling;
- affected-party notice;
- Tribunal admissibility;
- correction and appeal.

## 15.5 Worker Protection

Enforcement-adjacent workers receive:

- hazard classification;
- training;
- no-combat rules;
- withdrawal rights;
- trauma support;
- insurance;
- rescue capacity;
- whistleblower protection;
- no compensation incentive to seek confrontation;
- no personal liability for lawful refusal.

## 15.6 Activity and Outcome

Patrol hours, samples, inspections, and restoration actions may support operating compensation.

Claims of biodiversity recovery, deterrence, or ecological additionality require separate evidence and uncertainty.

## 15.7 Framework Placement

The relevant Treaty, Justice, Ocean, Technology, Financial, and ecological frameworks govern law, sanctions, sensors, and ecological methodology. Work in Liberation governs worker rights, provider organization, compensation, and participation.

# 16. Technology, Data, and Algorithmic Rights

## 16.1 Community Work Technology

Tools may support:

- team formation;
- schedules;
- need broadcasts;
- matching;
- service records;
- payments;
- accessibility;
- complaints;
- portable credentials.

No advanced platform is required for small-scale implementation. Paper, SMS, telephone, and trusted in-person pathways remain available.

## 16.2 Data Separation

The following systems remain logically and access-controlled as distinct:

- matching profiles;
- provider work records;
- LMCI-A contribution evidence;
- Hearts financial records;
- Layer 3 needs evidence;
- Love Ledger Narrative Feed;
- LMCI-M population research;
- worker complaints and health records;
- enforcement evidence.

## 16.3 Data Minimization

Collect only what is necessary for the stated decision.

A provider does not need a participant's full AUBI, health, relational, migration, or Love Ledger history merely to offer a bounded role.

## 16.4 Algorithmic Limits

AI may:

- suggest matches;
- identify schedule conflicts;
- summarize need broadcasts;
- flag inconsistent records;
- support scenario analysis.

AI may not independently:

- assign moral or social worth;
- impose discipline;
- determine fraud;
- compel work;
- alter AUBI;
- infer cultural sincerity;
- create a generalized worker reputation;
- issue final adverse findings.

## 16.5 Model and Sensor Changes

Changes to matching, validation, scheduling, risk, provider evaluation, or dashboard methods are governance changes.

Material changes require:

- public versioning;
- affected-party testing;
- subgroup analysis;
- error and appeal review;
- rollback;
- NARP where structurally consequential.

## 16.6 Observation Independence Map

Each BAZ maps whether observation channels share:

- data source;
- platform;
- Community Weaver;
- provider;
- funding body;
- algorithm;
- governance authority;
- incentive.

Nominally separate channels are not counted as independent where these dependencies are material.

## 16.7 Graceful Degradation

During technical failure:

- AUBI and essential services continue through fallback channels;
- workers retain complaint and payment access;
- matching becomes manual or pauses;
- adverse decisions based on unreconciled records are suspended;
- providers preserve minimal safe operations;
- no one is presumed inactive or non-compliant because the platform failed.

- subgroup divergence;

An activity indicator moving in the desired direction does not certify liberation.

# 18. Implementation and Pilot Gates

## 18.1 Stage 1 — Autonomy Foundation

Before organized work programmes scale, the community establishes:

- functioning AUBI or equivalent material-security protection;
- clear non-conditionality;
- accessible refusal and complaint information;
- distinction between private, informal, and formal contribution;
- low-tech access.

Without material security, the programme should not describe participation as liberated.

## 18.2 Stage 2 — Initial Community Providers

Pilot providers establish:

- charter;
- worker governance;
- compensation clarity;
- safety;
- complaint and appeal;
- provider financial records;
- limited need broadcasts;
- no generalized matching score.

## 18.3 Stage 3 — Need Broadcasting and Commissioning

The BAZ tests:

- Need Broadcast Contracts;
- participatory public commissioning;
- individual service purchase;
- public-good and ecological funding;
- unfilled-need diagnosis;
- critical-work protocol.

## 18.4 Stage 4 — Matching and Fluid Assembly

Only after data rights and correction function does the BAZ introduce claimant-controlled Skills Resonance Matching.

Graduation requires:

- voluntary profiles;
- explainability;
- opt-out;
- no Love Ledger default use;
- subgroup testing;
- complaint and rollback;
- no adverse inference from sparse records.

## 18.5 Stage 5 — Full Provider Network

A mature local network requires:

- provider diversity;
- worker ombuds;
- secondary validation;
- Community Weaver supervision;
- workload and hidden-labor monitoring;
- multiple commissioning channels;
- administrative-capacity dashboard;
- Observation Independence Map.

## 18.6 Stage 6 — Cross-BAZ Recognition

Provider and credential interfaces follow Nested Economies.

No interface transfers more evidence than necessary.

## 18.7 Stage 7 — Planetary and Transition Mobilisations

Large programmes require:

- lawful cross-framework mandate;
- work and safety standards;
- dedicated evidence systems;
- financing;
- affected-community participation;
- enforcement separation;
- independent evaluation.

## 18.8 Minimum Viable Work in Liberation

A small pilot should prioritize:

1. unconditional material security;
2. one worker-governed provider;
3. clear compensation and refusal rights;
4. one accountable need broadcast;
5. low-tech access;
6. independent complaint and secondary review.

It should defer:

- generalized AI matching;
- cross-BAZ credentials;
- public dashboards of individual activity;
- ecological outcome bonuses without evidence capacity;
- planetary mobilisations;
- enforcement-adjacent work.

## 18.9 Pilot Failure

A pilot may fail because of:

- inadequate material security;
- provider capture;
- poor compensation;
- weak need formulation;
- inaccessible participation;
- insufficient skills;
- social pressure;
- platform failure;
- Community Weaver overload;
- mismatch between funding and service type.

Failure is documented without forcing a singular diagnosis or blaming participants.

## 18.10 Pilot Portfolio

A balanced pilot portfolio may include:

- an Indigenous-led BAZ testing cultural sovereignty, protected knowledge, and local provider governance;
- a welfare-state municipality testing compatibility with existing labor, social-insurance, union, and public-service institutions;
- a lower-resource or infrastructure-constrained community testing low-tech access, leapfrogging, and external-dependency risks.

These are pilot archetypes, not claims that any named community has consented or that the same design fits every context. Site selection requires FPIC where applicable, local invitation, administrative capacity, independent evaluation, and the right to withdraw.

## 18.11 Twenty-Five-Year Transition Horizon

The inherited 25-year horizon is retained as a planning frame rather than a fixed promise.

### Phase I — Local proof and rights protection (approximately years 0–5)

- establish material-security compatibility;
- pilot worker-governed providers;
- test need broadcasts and commissioning;
- build refusal, complaint, and data rights;
- publish failures.

### Phase II — Network formation and legacy transition (approximately years 5–15)

- expand provider diversity;
- support gig and cooperative transition;
- develop cross-BAZ recognition;
- establish public-good and ecological commissioning;
- test Industrial Sunsetting.

### Phase III — Mature coordination and planetary capacity (approximately years 15–25)

- operate interoperable provider networks;
- maintain large-scale commons mobilisations;
- evaluate whether work has actually become more voluntary, safe, and worker-governed;
- devolve or end institutions that no longer justify their authority.

Progression depends on rights, capacity, and outcome gates rather than adoption percentages alone.

# 20. Irreducible Design Tensions

## 20.1 Recognition versus Moral Pressure

Recognition may strengthen belonging while creating conformity and status hierarchy. Participation and publicity remain voluntary.

## 20.2 Matching versus Privacy

Matching can widen access while converting life into data. Profiles remain claimant-controlled and purpose-limited.

## 20.3 Fluidity versus Security

Fluid projects can increase autonomy while weakening continuity, income predictability, and responsibility. Provider charters and public commissioning supply stable capacity where needed.

## 20.4 Local Governance versus Local Capture

BAZ control can increase legitimacy while enabling local hierarchy, discrimination, or retaliation. Rights floors, worker organization, secondary review, and external appeal remain necessary.

## 20.5 Meaningful Work versus Freedom from Work

A society can value meaningful contribution without making visible purpose a civic expectation. Freedom beyond work is part of liberation.

## 20.6 Ecological Outcomes versus Worker-Controlled Risk

Outcome incentives may support regeneration while shifting ecological uncertainty onto workers. Stable compensation and careful additionality protect workers.

## 20.7 Crisis Speed versus Voluntariness

Fast response can save lives while normalizing command. Mobilisation remains bounded, transparent, rights-protecting, and temporary.

## 20.8 Observation versus Surveillance

Work systems need evidence about safety, access, pay, and outcome. Combined work, identity, financial, relational, health, and matching data can become a surveillance architecture. Pipeline separation and protected unobservability are constitutional constraints.

# Conclusion

Work in Liberation begins from a proposition that conventional labor policy rarely accepts:

> **A person must be materially free not to work before organized contribution can become meaningfully voluntary.**

Version 2.0 adds the second half of that proposition:

> **Material freedom is not sufficient if institutions recreate coercion through reputation, visibility, matching, moral expectation, provider dependency, or emergency command.**

The framework therefore does not seek maximum participation. It seeks a condition in which people can:

- refuse exploitative work;
- form worker-governed providers;
- respond to real needs;
- move fluidly among projects;
- be fairly compensated;
- protect private and informal life;
- challenge institutional interpretations;
- rest;
- leave;
- and return.

Its governing principles are:

> **Work is liberated only when participation, refusal, privacy, exit, and return are all materially and socially credible.**

> **A need broadcast is an accountable invitation to collective attention—not a command, a diagnosis, or proof that a particular person should respond.**

> **The system may help people find opportunities; it may not convert their lives into a generalized record of employability, reliability, or moral worth.**

> **More work, more transactions, more logged care, more rapid matching, and more visible contribution do not by themselves demonstrate greater freedom or flourishing.**

# Appendix I: Glossary

- **AUBI:** Adaptive Universal Basic Income.
- **BAZ:** Bioregional Autonomous Zone.
- **Community Provider:** Chartered organization delivering formal services under worker, BAZ, AUBI, and financial rules.
- **Community Work Team:** Worker-governed form of Community Provider organized around care, culture, technical, civic, or ecological work.
- **Community Weaver:** Facilitator supporting access, provider formation, evidence, and translation without sole-source validation or adjudication.
- **FLP:** Fractal Labor Parliament, the Meta-Governance coordination body for shared work principles.
- **Hearts:** Formal regenerative currency governed by AUBI and Financial Systems.
- **Leaves:** Ecological records and reward instruments governed by AUBI, Financial Systems, FLP, and ecological protocols.
- **Love Ledger:** Voluntary, non-monetized recognition system for informal relational contribution.
- **LMCI-M:** Independent population-level Love, Meaning, and Connection measurement.
- **LMCI-A:** Contribution-level allocation process for AUBI Layer 2.
- **Need Broadcast:** Accountable invitation describing a problem, resources, rights, and opportunity for voluntary participation.
- **Skills Resonance Matching:** Opt-in, claimant-controlled opportunity matching without generalized reputation scoring.
- **Proof of Care:** Evidence architecture separating activity, acceptance, outcome, additionality, uncertainty, and adverse effects.
- **Industrial Sunsetting:** Rights-protecting phase-out of structurally harmful production with worker, community, and ecological transition.
- **Work With Purpose:** Freely chosen contribution experienced as meaningful; not an institutional status or duty.
- **Work Liberation Conditions Dashboard:** Multidimensional monitoring of material freedom, autonomy, worker authority, conditions, distribution, capacity, burden, matching rights, outcomes, and freedom beyond work.
