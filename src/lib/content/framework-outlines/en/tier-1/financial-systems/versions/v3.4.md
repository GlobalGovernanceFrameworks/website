# Preface: Terminology and Intent

## Dual-Register Language

This framework uses both structural and conventional financial language. Terms such as **extractive control patterns**, **scarcity-debt-coercion dynamics**, **capture resistance**, and **coordination technology** translate into operational requirements:

| Structural term | Financial-systems translation | Implementation requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Extractive control pattern | Rent-seeking capture with network effects, asymmetric transparency, prohibitive exit, and self-perpetuation | Revocable authority, symmetric transparency, protected exit, self-limiting functions, independent audit |
| Artificial scarcity | Supply manipulation or arbitrary constraint used to create dependence | Transparent issuance, published constraints, diagnostic review, prohibition on discretionary scarcity engineering |
| Worth coercion | Linking human dignity or basic security to institutionally approved productivity | Unconditional AUBI Layer 1; separation of Hearts allocation from Love Ledger recognition |
| Hierarchy without accountability | Centralized monetary or validation authority without challenge or reversal | Distributed operational authority, multi-party authorization, BAZ standing, tribunal review, reformation triggers |
| Capture-pattern detection | Institutional and protocol hardening against capture | Observation Independence Maps, adversarial audit, conflict disclosure, exit and devolution rights |
| Coordination technology | Financial infrastructure that enables exchange without claiming authority over total human value | Interoperable standards, purpose limitation, protected unobservability, local autonomy |

## Accountable Coordination Anchor

Financial systems create authority. The Social Resilience Council sets common standards, the Hearts Treasury manages liquidity and redemption, the Fractal Labor Parliament sets valuation standards, and the ICTL translates among economic systems.

Every such body is subject to the four-criteria **Accountable Coordination Audit**:

1. **Revocable authority** — authority can be withdrawn or reformed;
2. **Symmetric transparency** — institutions are more visible to participants than participants are to institutions;
3. **Protected exit** — leaving or challenging the system does not destroy access to basic rights;
4. **Self-limiting function** — the institution cannot indefinitely expand its own scope without constitutional review.

## Scope and Limits

This framework is the technical and operational manual for the GGF’s regenerative financial architecture. It specifies how Hearts, Leaves, the Love Ledger, reserve systems, translation mechanisms, and financial governance operate.

It does not:

- define AUBI eligibility or payout entitlement;
- define labor standards or Community Provider charters;
- determine Indigenous knowledge validity outside community-governed protocols;
- create sovereign-debt law;
- replace banking, securities, tax, or payment-system law in participating jurisdictions;
- claim that all value is monetizable;
- claim that an indicator uniquely identifies its cause.

# 1. Purpose and Constitutional Principles

## 1.1 Financial Systems as Channels for Right Relationship

Financial systems are the arteries of a regenerative economy. They channel resources, trust, obligations, and value among people, communities, institutions, and ecosystems. Their purpose is not transaction maximization or financial expansion for its own sake. Their purpose is to support **Right Relationship**: material security, local autonomy, reciprocal care, ecological restoration, cultural vitality, and accountable coordination.

The framework is designed against three recurring failures:

- **artificial scarcity**, where control over supply is used to create dependency;
- **worth coercion**, where dignity and security are made conditional on approved productivity;
- **hierarchical capture**, where issuance, validation, or conversion authority becomes self-protecting and unchallengeable.

## 1.2 Financial Constitutional Rules

The following rules apply to every consequential financial decision:

> **A financial indicator is an alert signal, not a diagnosis.**

> **A threshold may authorize temporary, reversible containment. It does not by itself authorize a causal diagnosis, punitive finding, permanent rule change, or uniform intervention.**

> **No uniform intervention may be selected where materially different latent states remain compatible with the evidence and require conflicting responses.**

> **An intervention’s effect on the financial indicator that justified it does not prove that the underlying human, distributive, relational, or ecological condition improved.**

> **Basic security may not be used as collateral for institutional compliance.**

## 1.3 Operating Objectives

The financial architecture seeks to maintain:

1. **material reliability** — AUBI obligations and essential Community Provider functions remain usable;
2. **sufficient circulation** — Hearts can move where useful without making high velocity an end in itself;
3. **credible redemption** — valid redemption operates predictably within disclosed liquidity constraints;
4. **local autonomy** — BAZs retain meaningful authority over local implementation;
5. **interoperability** — different economic systems can exchange without erasing their distinctions;
6. **distributional fairness** — rules are evaluated across subgroups, not only through aggregates;
7. **ecological integrity** — Leaves and ecological finance remain tied to evidence, sovereignty, and slow-variable review;
8. **epistemic humility** — uncertainty, disagreement, and unresolved causation remain visible;
9. **capture resistance** — no actor controls issuance, validation, conversion, and adjudication together;
10. **protected unobservability** — the system does not make all relationships legible merely because data collection is technically possible.

# 2. Financial Instruments and Information Boundaries

## 2.1 Hearts

**Hearts** are the GGF’s formal currency for care, community services, cultural work, and related regenerative economic activity. They are:

- issued through authorized AUBI and Community Provider pathways;
- accepted by participating providers and institutions;
- redeemable under the Hearts Treasury protocol;
- subject to transparent issuance, reserve, and conversion rules;
- designed for circulation rather than speculative accumulation;
- not a measure of a person’s inherent worth.

Hearts may use bounded demurrage where justified under §6. Demurrage is a monetary mechanism, not a moral judgment on holders.

## 2.2 Leaves

**Leaves** are non-fungible ecological records and reward instruments for verified restoration, stewardship, and ecosystem recovery. Their monetary or translation treatment is governed separately from Hearts because ecological value:

- manifests across longer timescales;
- depends on place, baseline, additionality, permanence, and leakage;
- often relies on Indigenous and long-embedded knowledge;
- cannot be reduced to one universal hourly rate.

Leaves are exempt from ordinary Hearts demurrage. Their issuance and conversion are subject to ecological evidence, Fractal Labor Parliament standards, Slow-Variable Decision Protocol review, and Earth Council authority.

## 2.3 Love Ledger

The **Love Ledger** is a parallel, non-monetized recognition system for informal care, gratitude, relationship, and contribution that should not be converted into transactional claims.

Its **Gratitude Tokens**:

- have no exchange rate;
- cannot be redeemed through the Hearts Treasury;
- cannot determine AUBI eligibility;
- cannot become a generalized reputation score;
- cannot be required for access to services;
- remain under individual and community data sovereignty.

The Love Ledger may provide a voluntary **Narrative Feed** for community reflection. It is not an input to LMCI-M, LMCI-A, ICTL rates, fraud scores, or creditworthiness unless a separate, consented, purpose-limited protocol explicitly authorizes a narrowly defined use.

## 2.4 LMCI and Operational Data Separation

The framework maintains four distinct information systems:

| System | Purpose | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| **LMCI-M** | Independent population-level flourishing estimate | Governance diagnosis, long-horizon evaluation, distributional review |
| **LMCI-A** | Contribution-level allocation and validation | AUBI Layer 2 scoring and provider-level allocation |
| **Financial operational telemetry** | Transactions, issuance, reserves, redemption, settlement, capacity | Financial operations and diagnostics |
| **Love Ledger Narrative Feed** | Voluntary qualitative recognition and relational reflection | Community learning without conversion into financial score |

These systems may be displayed side by side but may not be silently merged. LMCI-M does not mechanically determine an exchange rate. Operational activity does not constitute evidence of flourishing merely because it increased.

## 2.5 Data Ethics and Privacy

Financial transparency applies primarily to institutions, algorithms, aggregate flows, and public authority—not to rendering individuals permanently visible.

Required protections include:

- data minimization and purpose limitation;
- participant-controlled identity and consent;
- Indigenous and community data sovereignty;
- separation of identity, eligibility, transaction, contribution, health, migration, and relational data;
- privacy-preserving aggregate reporting;
- offline and low-technology access;
- correction and deletion where compatible with necessary audit obligations;
- explicit retention periods;
- public versioning of algorithms and schemas;
- prohibition on sharing AUBI enrollment information with immigration or ordinary law-enforcement systems absent a specific lawful and rights-compatible basis.

Public ledgers may expose institutional and aggregate proofs without exposing personal transactional histories.

# 3. Financial Decision Observability and Epistemic Governance

## 3.1 Scope

The AUBI Decision Observability Protocol applies directly to:

- Hearts issuance and suspension;
- reserve and liquidity policy;
- redemption terms and queues;
- demurrage introduction or adjustment;
- ICTL rates, bands, caps, and circuit breakers;
- fraud investigations, holds, and sanctions;
- Bridge Protocol migration;
- Heartstarter funding and dependency review;
- crisis reallocations;
- Treasury resolution;
- AI or sensor changes affecting financial decisions.

## 3.2 Observation Contracts

Each consequential decision class must have a published **Observation Contract** specifying:

1. the decision and authorized body;
2. the latent condition to be inferred;
3. admissible observation channels;
4. data owner and observer;
5. collection and sampling method;
6. cadence, latency, and data age;
7. known missingness and excluded populations;
8. shared-source, algorithmic, funding, and incentive dependencies;
9. estimator or interpretive method;
10. alternative states compatible with the observations;
11. uncertainty and disagreement representation;
12. threshold and authorized containment action;
13. prohibited causal or punitive inferences;
14. challenge, correction, appeal, and review rights;
15. privacy, sacredness, and sovereignty restrictions;
16. independent outcome measures;
17. reassessment, reversal, and sunset conditions;
18. protocol and framework versions.

## 3.3 State Estimate Records

Every consequential policy adjustment or adverse financial action must produce a **State Estimate Record** stating:

- estimated condition and as-of date;
- supporting and contradicting evidence;
- viable alternative explanations;
- uncertainty and missingness;
- source dependencies and provenance warnings;
- affected subgroups and their accounts;
- selected containment or intervention;
- why the action remains acceptable under uncertainty;
- intervention preconditions and order;
- independent expected outcomes;
- reassessment date;
- reversal or escalation condition.

Where the evidence does not distinguish among conflicting responses, the official finding is:

> **Cause unresolved — containment only; additional diagnosis required.**

## 3.4 Joint States and Disaggregation

A BAZ can contain several simultaneous conditions. Aggregate financial signals must therefore be disaggregated where lawful and proportionate by:

- geography;
- wallet class;
- balance distribution;
- provider category;
- household versus institutional wallet;
- first use, recirculation, and redemption;
- accessibility channel;
- relevant affected groups.

Disaggregation must not become unrestricted demographic surveillance. Collection requires a stated decision purpose and minimum necessary granularity.

## 3.5 Observation Independence

The Social Resilience Council, BAZ Councils, and Hearts Treasuries maintain **Observation Independence Maps** showing whether nominally different channels share:

- the same observer;
- the same transaction platform;
- the same funding source;
- the same governance body;
- the same algorithm;
- the same Community Weaver network;
- the same provider reports;
- the same incentives.

Correlated channels do not count as independent confirmation.

## 3.6 Sensor-Change Governance

Changes to the following are governance changes rather than neutral maintenance:

- price or reserve feeds;
- fraud models;
- anomaly thresholds;
- active-user definitions;
- wallet classifications;
- missing-data treatment;
- balance aggregation;
- LMCI interfaces;
- ecological evidence rules;
- human-versus-algorithmic validation;
- dashboard presentation;
- reliability flags.

Material sensor changes follow NARP. Changes affecting slow ecological evidence also require Slow-Variable Decision Protocol review.

## 3.7 Individual Epistemic Rights

Any person or provider affected by identity, access, transaction, validation, fraud, suspension, conversion, or sanction decisions has the right to:

1. know that a decision is being made;
2. inspect evidence, model output, and protocol version;
3. correct factual errors;
4. submit contextual evidence;
5. challenge human or algorithmic interpretation;
6. receive a plain-language rationale;
7. obtain timely human review and appeal;
8. know reassessment and reversal conditions;
9. retain protected AUBI Layer 1 access while disputed funds are isolated, unless a tribunal finds a direct and exceptional legal basis otherwise;
10. use accessible offline pathways.

Fundamental review may not depend on staking Hearts.

# 4. Bridge Protocol: From Social Trust to Cryptographic Sovereignty

## 4.1 Phase 1 — Ledger of Action

**Objective:** Establish useful, equitable, trusted circulation with minimal technical friction.

**Architecture:** Trusted-node or simple shared-ledger systems appropriate to local conditions.

**Validation:** Social acknowledgment, provider records, participant confirmation, and Community Weaver support.

Phase 1 does **not** aim to maximize velocity. It seeks to establish that:

- real needs are being met;
- services are accessible;
- participants understand the system;
- disputes can be corrected;
- data can be reconciled;
- social validation does not exclude isolated or lower-status participants;
- the local economy is not being forced to monetize protected relationships.

### Phase 1 safeguards

- no generalized reputation score;
- alternative validation for newcomers and socially isolated participants;
- secondary review for disputed transactions;
- confidential correction and anti-retaliation pathways;
- offline reconciliation;
- protocol-version capture;
- source-dependence mapping;
- minimal metadata necessary for later migration.

## 4.2 Fractal Seed Schema

Phase 1 records intended for later migration may include:

- transaction identifier;
- timestamp;
- broad location or service area where necessary;
- contribution or service category;
- parties’ consented identifiers;
- validation method;
- dispute status;
- correction history;
- protocol version;
- provenance and reliability flags.

Sensitive geolocation, health, migration, relational, or cultural data must not be collected merely because it could be useful later.

## 4.3 Phase 2 — Ledger of Record

**Objective:** Increase sovereignty, interoperability, integrity, and participant control once social-scale coordination is no longer sufficient.

**Architecture:** Agent-centric or appropriately distributed ledger infrastructure governed under Technology Governance standards.

**Migration object:**

- verified transaction records;
- claimant-controlled identity credentials;
- consent and data-use permissions;
- unresolved disputes;
- corrections;
- protocol versions;
- provenance.

The migration does **not** transfer one portable reputation score. Contribution history may be represented only for a specific authorized purpose, with correction and appeal rights.

## 4.4 Phase 2 Readiness

A BAZ may migrate only when AUBI Stage 4 and Phase 2 criteria are satisfied, including:

- sufficient active participation and operating history;
- stable reserve compliance;
- functioning correction and appeal;
- published Observation Contracts;
- State Estimate Record capability;
- administrative-capacity monitoring;
- missing-population and access monitoring;
- Observation Independence Map;
- data-schema compatibility;
- no unresolved high-severity observation, exclusion, rights, or capacity failure.

Network size alone does not authorize migration.

## 4.5 Sovereignty Ceremony

The migration ceremony confirms:

- participant control of credentials;
- what data moves and what does not;
- correction of legacy errors;
- deletion or archival rights;
- local governance approval;
- Indigenous and community data-sovereignty requirements;
- fallback if migration fails.

# 5. Heartstarter and Reserve Capitalization

## 5.1 Purpose

Heartstarter supplies matched seed capital so that low-wealth communities can establish Proto-Treasuries without needing to self-capitalize from scarcity.

## 5.2 Mechanics

- The Global Commons Fund may match fiat, grant, or verified in-kind reserve contributions at 1:1, subject to an approved per-BAZ ceiling.
- The inherited illustrative ceiling is the equivalent of six months of projected Layer 1 payments for the initial enrolled population, capped at USD 250,000 equivalent during Phase 1.
- Funds are released in tranches tied to AUBI bootstrapping stages.
- Match capital is ring-fenced for reserve and authorized infrastructure purposes.
- Failure to progress does not automatically trigger clawback; funds support redesign or orderly wind-down.

## 5.3 Reserve Hierarchy

The framework distinguishes four levels:

| Level | Illustrative threshold | Meaning | Default consequence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Normal operating target** | 50% or higher liquid-adjusted reserve ratio | Desired resilience under ordinary conditions | Treasury manages toward target |
| **Diagnostic intervention band** | 30–50% | Reserves remain above the issuance floor but require cause analysis and recovery plan | No automatic shutdown; publish State Estimate Record |
| **Hard issuance floor** | 30% | Minimum fiat-equivalent liquid-adjusted reserves relative to outstanding Hearts | Suspend new uncommitted issuance after data validation |
| **Emergency floor** | 20% | Temporary crisis exception | Maximum 90 days; heightened reporting, protected minimums, restoration plan |

These values are pilot parameters subject to NARP, stress testing, and Slow-Variable review where long-term fiat dependency is affected.

## 5.4 Liquid-Adjusted Reserve Ratio

Reserve assets are not counted equally. The reserve report distinguishes:

1. immediately available fiat or equivalent settlement assets;
2. committed and legally reliable liquidity facilities;
3. short-duration, low-risk assets subject to haircut;
4. illiquid regenerative assets;
5. volatile ecological or market-linked instruments.

The constitutional reserve ratio uses liquid-adjusted value, not nominal face value. Illiquid regenerative assets may support long-term balance-sheet resilience but may not be counted as if immediately redeemable.

## 5.5 Reserve-Breach Protocol

When the hard floor is breached:

1. validate reserve and outstanding-supply data;
2. isolate technical or accounting errors;
3. preserve committed AUBI Layer 1 and essential-provider obligations;
4. suspend new uncommitted issuance;
5. distinguish liquidity stress from insolvency;
6. determine leading and alternative causes;
7. activate the relevant liquidity, supply-chain, trust, fraud, or resolution response;
8. publish a State Estimate Record;
9. reassess within a specified short horizon.

The breach does not itself prove over-issuance.

## 5.6 Heartstarter Dependency

The SRC monitors the share of reserve funding derived from:

- Global Commons Fund support;
- local levies;
- automation and resource taxes;
- Community Provider flows;
- other authorized local sources.

A BAZ remaining above 50% GCF-dependent 24 months after formalization is flagged for review, not automatic sanction. Possible causes include local fiscal weakness, crisis, structural external-input dependence, or an unrealistic transition schedule.

# 6. Hearts Issuance, Circulation, and Demurrage

## 6.1 Issuance Authority

Hearts issuance is distributed across authorized AUBI, BAZ, Treasury, and Global Commons Fund pathways. No actor may unilaterally change supply rules outside published authority.

Every issuance class states:

- legal and governance basis;
- purpose;
- amount or formula;
- reserve treatment;
- recipient class;
- expiration or demurrage treatment;
- data and validation chain;
- review and appeal;
- expected human and economic outcomes.

## 6.2 Circulation Metrics

The dashboard reports separately:

- **use velocity** — eligible purchases and service exchange relative to average eligible stock;
- **recirculation velocity** — continued circulation after first use;
- **redemption rate** — exit into fiat through the Treasury;
- **active-participant share**;
- **90-day dormancy**;
- **balance concentration**;
- **dormancy concentration**;
- **failed and declined transactions**;
- **offline reconciliation gap**.

Initial issuance, self-transfers, wash transactions, reversals, administrative migrations, and pure conversion events do not count as useful circulation.

## 6.3 Low Hearts Velocity

The AUBI Low Hearts Velocity Diagnostic governs low circulation.

Low velocity may indicate:

- provider shortage;
- provider-capacity constraint;
- access mismatch;
- low quality or relevance;
- insufficient Hearts acceptance;
- distrust;
- balance concentration;
- seasonality;
- informal substitution;
- recording failure;
- fiat/Hearts composition mismatch;
- benign sufficiency.

No change to issuance, demurrage, currency composition, or provider recruitment follows from aggregate velocity alone.

## 6.4 Demurrage

Hearts may use bounded demurrage, with an inherited illustrative rate of 0.5% monthly, only where the Observation Contract identifies a specific monetary objective and the diagnostic excludes conflicting causes.

Demurrage may be considered where evidence supports strategic hoarding or persistent concentration that impairs useful circulation. It may not be introduced or increased merely because:

- velocity is low;
- participants save;
- formal transactions decline;
- the administration wants more activity;
- informal provision is strong;
- material sufficiency reduces consumption.

### Demurrage safeguards

- protected minimum or small-balance exemption;
- notice and plain-language explanation;
- accessibility and hardship assessment;
- subgroup impact analysis;
- explicit sunset and reversal condition;
- independent monitoring of trust, material security, fiat burden, and provider access;
- no claim that increased velocity proves success.

## 6.5 Issuance Adjustment

Issuance decisions distinguish among:

- liquidity shortage;
- real service-capacity shortage;
- inflation;
- confidence loss;
- reserve stress;
- concentrated balances;
- external-input shock;
- data failure.

Where several conditions coexist, interventions are sequenced. For example, expanding issuance before restoring essential Hearts acceptance may worsen accumulation without improving material access.

# 7. Governance and Decision Rights

## 7.1 Social Resilience Council

The **Social Resilience Council (SRC)**:

- oversees financial-system health and equitable Hearts/Leaves coordination;
- sets common standards and annual ICTL bands;
- manages Global Commons Fund financial interfaces;
- coordinates crisis response;
- publishes financial Observation Contracts;
- maintains system-wide Observation Independence Maps;
- cannot adjudicate its own alleged capture or rights violations.

The SRC is governed by AUBI §§4.1–4.5 and remains subject to Accountable Coordination Audit, BAZ reformation triggers, Earth Council authority, Office of the Adversary review, and Meta-Governance oversight.

## 7.2 Fractal Labor Parliament

The **Fractal Labor Parliament (FLP)**:

- sets care-work and ecological-work valuation standards;
- proposes Green Job Score ranges;
- participates in ICTL decisions affecting labor valuation;
- cannot ratify Slow-Variable Decision Category changes without required LEKH quorum and SRC process;
- does not determine individual fraud or AUBI eligibility.

## 7.3 BAZ Councils

BAZ Councils:

- govern local Hearts operations within common constitutional standards;
- charter Community Providers under the relevant frameworks;
- submit data, objections, counter-models, and State Estimate Records;
- adapt implementation to local culture and access needs;
- may not waive individual epistemic rights or protected AUBI minimums;
- may choose not to formalize or scale a local Hearts economy.

## 7.4 Hearts Treasuries and Credit-Unions

Hearts Treasuries and partner credit unions:

- maintain reserves and liquidity;
- process authorized redemption;
- report assets, liabilities, queues, and outages;
- execute but do not unilaterally determine monetary policy;
- are independently audited;
- must separate operational, risk, and compliance functions where scale permits.

## 7.5 Earth Council and LEKH Quorum

Earth Council and LEKH processes govern:

- ecological integrity;
- Indigenous sovereignty;
- slow-variable reserve and exchange decisions;
- Leaves valuation and ecological evidence;
- contested Slow-Variable classifications.

## 7.6 Digital Justice Tribunal

The Tribunal:

- adjudicates sanctions, contested freezes, rights violations, and cross-BAZ disputes;
- does not rely on algorithmic flags as findings of guilt;
- provides accessible review without mandatory Hearts staking;
- may order correction, restitution, release of funds, or institutional reform.

## 7.7 Decision-Rights Matrix

| Decision | Propose | Diagnose/review | Authorize | Challenge/appeal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Routine issuance within approved rule | Treasury/BAZ | Treasury risk + BAZ review | Published rule | SRC audit / Tribunal rights review |
| Change issuance formula | SRC/BAZ | Independent financial review | NARP; LEKH if applicable | BAZ objection; Meta-Governance; Tribunal |
| Change demurrage | SRC/BAZ | Low-velocity and distribution diagnostic | NARP | Affected participants, BAZs, Tribunal |
| Change reserve bands | SRC/Treasury | Independent liquidity and slow-variable review | Tier B NARP; LEKH where required | BAZs, Earth Council, Meta-Governance |
| Routine ICTL movement within band | SRC + FLP | ICTL State Estimate Record | Tier A with safeguards | BAZ reclassification petition |
| Structural ICTL change | SRC/FLP | Independent modelling and local counter-models | Tier B NARP | BAZ objection, Earth Council, arbitration |
| Temporary account hold | Treasury/authorized investigator | Human review | Narrow, time-limited protocol | Immediate review and Tribunal appeal |
| Fraud sanction or forfeiture | Investigator | Independent evidence review | Tribunal | Tribunal appellate pathway |
| Crisis exception | PIS/SRC | Independent crisis evidence and subgroup analysis | Time-limited constitutional process | Earth Council, Tribunal, after-action review |

# 8. Hearts Treasury: Liquidity, Redemption, and Resolution

## 8.1 Purpose

The Hearts Treasury is a clearing and liquidity institution. It:

- processes valid provider redemption;
- maintains reserves;
- coordinates settlement;
- supplies transparent liquidity information;
- supports local credit-union integration;
- executes approved issuance and conversion rules;
- enters resolution when ordinary promises cannot be maintained.

## 8.2 Redemption Service Standard

Under normal operating conditions, the Treasury aims to fulfil 100% of valid redemption requests within 48 hours.

This is a **service standard**, not an unconditional claim that all outstanding Hearts can be redeemed simultaneously from fractional reserves.

The Treasury publishes:

- eligible redeemers;
- eligible purposes;
- normal processing window;
- daily capacity;
- current queue;
- reserve and liquidity status;
- emergency facility availability;
- any temporary restrictions and their legal basis.

## 8.3 Redemption Eligibility

Chartered Community Providers have the primary formal redemption pathway for external operating costs. Additional participant redemption pathways, where permitted, must be explicitly defined and must not undermine Layer 1 usability or local autonomy.

Redemption cannot be denied on arbitrary political, cultural, or ideological grounds.

## 8.4 Redemption-Pressure Diagnostic

High redemption relative to issuance can result from:

- excessive issuance;
- external-input dependence;
- insufficient local supply chains;
- low Hearts acceptance;
- confidence loss;
- seasonality;
- emergency demand;
- arbitrage;
- provider concentration;
- expected rule changes;
- reporting failure;
- legitimate bridge-currency use.

High redemption is therefore a liquidity and confidence alert, not proof of inflation.

The diagnostic examines:

- redeemer type and concentration;
- external-input category;
- local acceptance;
- trust and expectations;
- exchange differential;
- reserve source;
- seasonal pattern;
- data integrity;
- subgroup material effects.

## 8.5 Liquidity Facilities

Before entering resolution, the Treasury may use authorized facilities such as:

- ring-fenced liquid reserves;
- precommitted GCF liquidity;
- partner credit-union lines;
- temporary inter-Treasury support;
- scheduled redemption queues;
- narrowly targeted issuance suspension.

Every facility has published limits, costs, priority rules, and sunset conditions.

## 8.6 Stress Queuing and Priority

During verified liquidity stress:

1. protected Layer 1 and essential service continuity are preserved;
2. small and essential providers receive priority over large discretionary conversion;
3. related-wallet splitting cannot evade limits;
4. queues remain transparent without exposing private identity;
5. participants receive estimated timing and appeal rights;
6. temporary restrictions expire automatically unless renewed through the proper process.

## 8.7 Treasury Resolution

A formal resolution process begins when the Treasury cannot maintain ordinary redemption after authorized liquidity facilities or when solvency is materially uncertain.

Resolution requires:

- independent asset and liability verification;
- distinction between liquidity and insolvency;
- protection of committed basic-security obligations;
- loss-allocation rules established before crisis;
- anti-insider preference;
- continuity plan for essential providers;
- public State Estimate Record;
- Tribunal and Meta-Governance oversight;
- restoration, restructuring, merger, devolution, or orderly wind-down pathway.

Lowering the reserve threshold does not by itself resolve an underlying loss.

# 9. Validation, Fraud, and Participant Protection

## 9.1 Anomaly Detection

AI and statistical systems may flag:

- unusual accrual;
- duplicate transactions;
- coordinated trading;
- implausible ecological metadata;
- wash activity;
- validator conflicts;
- source-provenance anomalies.

A flag initiates review. It is not a finding of fraud.

## 9.2 Comparison Groups

Anomaly thresholds must use context-appropriate comparison groups. Accrual 50% above a community average may reflect:

- provider scale;
- crisis response;
- high local need;
- delayed batch reporting;
- legitimate ecological performance;
- category mismatch;
- data error;
- fraud.

The framework therefore prohibits adverse action based solely on deviation from an average.

## 9.3 Proportionate Holds

Where immediate containment is necessary:

- isolate only the disputed increment where technically feasible;
- preserve AUBI Layer 1 and undisputed balances;
- notify the affected party;
- state the evidence and protocol version;
- set a short review deadline;
- provide human review and appeal;
- automatically release the hold if the deadline lapses without justified renewal.

## 9.4 Fraud Determination

A fraud finding requires:

- admissible evidence;
- provenance review;
- consideration of alternative explanations;
- human adjudication;
- opportunity to respond;
- proportionality;
- Tribunal authority for forfeiture or major sanction;
- restoration if the finding is reversed.

## 9.5 Cultural and Spiritual Evidence

Community-approved protocols may verify that a claimed cultural activity occurred and met agreed conditions. Officials and algorithms may not judge the sincerity, spirituality, or inner worth of a participant.

## 9.6 Anti-Capture Review

Proposals are evaluated for structural effects rather than forbidden vocabulary. Relevant indicators include:

- concentration of authority;
- asymmetric transparency;
- restrictions on exit;
- self-extension of institutional scope;
- means-testing of unconditional security;
- artificial constraints without public purpose;
- unequal burden shifting;
- conversion of recognition into coercive score.

Language analysis may support human review but cannot determine capture by itself.

# 10. Crisis Controls and Protected Minimums

## 10.1 Crisis Authority

During a formally declared crisis, the SRC and Planetary Immune System may temporarily:

- lower the reserve floor to the authorized emergency level;
- pause or limit certain conversions;
- redirect discretionary project issuance;
- deploy stabilization reserves;
- accelerate essential-provider liquidity;
- implement temporary Tier B measures before the full NARP window.

## 10.2 Protected Minimum

Crisis authority does not automatically permit bypassing or diverting AUBI Layer 1.

Protected baseline obligations may be altered only under a separately authorized constitutional emergency standard that specifies:

- evidence threshold;
- necessity;
- least-harm alternative;
- duration;
- material minimum that remains protected;
- affected-party rights;
- restoration and restitution;
- independent review.

## 10.3 Emergency Constraints

Emergency financial changes:

- last no more than 90 days without ordinary ratification;
- require public reports at 30 and 60 days;
- expire automatically;
- cannot be renewed indefinitely through relabelling;
- preserve pre-intervention data;
- state independent outcome measures;
- prohibit permanent sensor or model changes without NARP;
- undergo after-action review.

## 10.4 Crisis Observation

Crisis actions can change participation, reporting, trust, provider supply, and data quality. Recovery is therefore evaluated through independent measures, not only through restored reserves, lower redemption, or lower transaction volume.

# 11. Inter-Currency Translation Layer

## 11.1 Purpose

The **Inter-Currency Translation Layer (ICTL)** enables conversion among Hearts, Leaves, fiat, and approved local or external systems while preserving local autonomy and value integrity.

It is a constitutional compression point: local economic meanings and conditions are translated into common rates and rules. The ICTL must therefore make visible what it preserves, what it aggregates, and what it discards.

## 11.2 Objective Hierarchy

ICTL decisions follow this hierarchy:

1. protect material access and basic security;
2. preserve local and Indigenous autonomy;
3. maintain settlement integrity and prevent extractive arbitrage;
4. support useful cross-BAZ exchange;
5. minimize avoidable volatility and slippage;
6. preserve ecological and labor valuation constraints;
7. avoid using low flourishing as a reason to worsen a community’s terms.

No single scalar “optimal rate” captures all objectives.

## 11.3 Inputs

Permitted inputs may include:

- Treasury redemption terms;
- settlement costs;
- liquidity and volume;
- local price and access information;
- provider and supply-chain conditions;
- ecological valuation standards;
- care-work valuation standards;
- seasonal and regional conditions;
- distributional analysis;
- LMCI-M as contextual population evidence, not as a mechanical rate variable.

LMCI-A and Love Ledger activity may not be silently treated as LMCI-M or as market price.

## 11.4 Settlement Accuracy

The inherited target of 99% “conversion accuracy” is redefined as **settlement accuracy**:

- the displayed approved rate is applied correctly;
- rounding and fees match published rules;
- settlement completes to the intended account;
- unauthorized slippage remains below the approved limit.

Settlement accuracy does not prove the normative rate is socially or ecologically correct.

## 11.5 Change Classification

### Tier A — Routine adjustments

Tier A may include:

- rate movements within approved bands;
- seasonal or regional weights within an unchanged method;
- non-material technical parameter updates.

A proposed Tier A change becomes Tier B when it:

- materially affects access to essentials;
- changes subgroup outcomes beyond the published threshold;
- changes data, model, or methodology;
- alters effective redemption terms;
- affects Indigenous sovereignty or slow variables;
- accumulates with prior changes beyond a rolling structural threshold;
- receives qualifying BAZ reclassification petitions.

### Tier B — Structural changes

Tier B includes:

- changing band ceilings or floors;
- adding or removing currency categories;
- changing rate methodology;
- changing LMCI interfaces;
- changing capital controls or circuit breakers;
- changing ecological or care valuation logic;
- changing reserve or liquidity treatment;
- changing observation architecture;
- creating sovereign-debt conversion methodology.

## 11.6 Pre-Approved Bands

The inherited illustrative annual bands remain pilot parameters:

- Hearts/fiat: ±15% from annual baseline;
- care-act Hearts conversion: ±20%;
- Leaves/Hearts: ±25%;
- Green Job Score: 1.0x–1.5x.

These ranges do not make every internal movement routine. Distributional and cumulative effects can trigger Tier B treatment.

## 11.7 Exchange-Differential Circuit Breaker

A verified exchange differential above an inherited illustrative 5% threshold may trigger a temporary hold or limit.

The hold does not establish arbitrage or attack. The diagnostic examines:

- price-feed integrity;
- market depth;
- settlement timing;
- legitimate conversion cost;
- liquidity shortage;
- emergency risk premium;
- local controls;
- coordinated manipulation;
- band miscalibration.

Penalties require adjudication.

## 11.8 Volume Caps and Capital Controls

The inherited illustrative conversion cap of 10% of daily issuance may be used as a temporary anti-attack parameter, subject to:

- related-wallet aggregation;
- essential-provider exemptions;
- hardship and material-access review;
- public rationale;
- expiry;
- NARP for structural continuation.

“Hearts are non-exportable” means that Hearts are designed for approved ecosystem use rather than unrestricted speculative export. It does not prevent lawful participant exit, redemption, or locally authorized cross-system exchange.

# 12. Notice and Adaptive Response Protocol

## 12.1 Day 0 Publication

Every Tier B proposal is published with:

- full proposal and plain-language summary;
- decision purpose and authority;
- Observation Contract;
- leading and alternative State Estimates;
- methodology, data, and protocol versions;
- projected impacts for at least five BAZ archetypes;
- subgroup and affected-BAZ analysis;
- uncertainty and tail scenarios;
- LEKH determination where applicable;
- proposed outcome measures;
- reversal and sunset conditions;
- known source dependencies;
- independent dissent or Office of the Adversary review where available.

## 12.2 Notice Window

The standard Tier B notice window is 90 days. During the window, BAZs and affected parties may:

- endorse;
- object with reasons;
- propose modifications;
- submit local data;
- submit counter-models;
- challenge assumptions;
- request clarification;
- petition for additional Slow-Variable or rights review.

Material amendments restart the window.

## 12.3 Standing

The framework distinguishes:

- **voting standing** — formal active BAZs;
- **affected-party standing** — any BAZ materially affected by the change;
- **transition standing** — Stage 2–3 BAZs whose future operation would be affected;
- **protected community standing** — Indigenous, mobile, displaced, and other communities with relevant rights or exposure;
- **individual/provider evidence standing** — persons and providers may submit evidence and rights claims.

The active-BAZ definition cannot erase communities made inactive by administrative overload, exclusion, or observation failure.

## 12.4 Objection and Ratification

- If 30% or more of active BAZs object, the proposal is suspended and returned for revision.
- Two suspensions trigger Meta-Governance arbitration before a third submission.
- A change proceeds only after SRC ratification, applicable LEKH quorum, and unresolved constitutional challenges are addressed.
- Fewer than 30% objections do not make the underlying model true; the SRC still bears responsibility for evidence and outcomes.

## 12.5 Tier Reclassification

BAZ Councils may petition to reclassify a Tier A proposal as Tier B. The inherited automatic threshold of 15% of active BAZs is retained. Affected-party and protected-community petitions receive substantive review even when the numeric threshold is not reached.

## 12.6 Latency Dashboard

Latency is decomposed into:

1. **acknowledgement latency** — signal to formal receipt;
2. **triage latency** — receipt to assigned pathway;
3. **diagnostic latency** — triage to State Estimate;
4. **decision latency** — estimate to decision;
5. **implementation latency** — decision to effect;
6. **appeal latency** — challenge to resolution;
7. **outcome-review latency** — implementation to evaluated result.

The inherited ceilings of 180 days for Tier A and 365 days for Tier B apply to decision completion unless the State Estimate Record explains why additional diagnosis is necessary.

A justified **no change** or **cause unresolved** decision can close the response requirement. Speed does not require a rate adjustment.

## 12.7 Devolution and Reabsorption

Three or more unjustified ceiling breaches within 12 months trigger authority review and possible devolution to BAZ-cluster level.

Reabsorption requires evidence of:

- improved timeliness;
- acceptable diagnostic accuracy;
- lower rights and subgroup harm;
- effective appeals;
- restored trust;
- independent audit;
- affected-BAZ participation.

# 13. Automatic Controls and Intervention Sequencing

## 13.1 Permitted Automatic Containment

Automatic controls are most defensible where they are:

- temporary;
- reversible;
- narrow;
- non-punitive;
- evidence-preserving;
- followed by mandatory diagnosis.

Examples include:

- suspending new uncommitted issuance after a verified hard-floor breach;
- pausing ICTL conversion after verified feed divergence;
- isolating a compromised node;
- requiring secondary approval during an anomaly spike.

## 13.2 Actions Requiring Human and Governance Review

The following cannot be automatic:

- fraud finding;
- forfeiture;
- permanent account freeze;
- stronger demurrage;
- structural issuance contraction;
- reserve-band change;
- permanent capital control;
- material exchange-method change;
- redirection of protected Layer 1;
- generalized reputation downgrade;
- permanent community-wide pause.

## 13.3 Sequencing Rules

Where conditions interact, the State Estimate Record specifies order. Examples:

- repair transaction records before redistributing apparently concentrated balances;
- expand access before interpreting centralized provider capacity as sufficient;
- improve provider quality before rapid recruitment;
- restore essential Hearts usability before expecting trust repair to persist;
- validate price feeds before penalizing suspected arbitrage;
- distinguish liquidity from insolvency before changing reserve rules.

## 13.4 Reversible Diagnostic Probes

Where uncertainty is material, the framework prefers bounded probes such as:

- temporary acceptance expansion in one essential category;
- limited liquidity support;
- targeted technical repair;
- geographically bounded provider-capacity increase;
- temporary fee waiver;
- independent participant sample.

Probe outcomes are evaluated against competing hypotheses. A probe does not become permanent merely because it moved its target indicator.

# 14. Monitoring, Learning, and Stress Testing

## 14.1 Financial Operations Dashboard

The dashboard distinguishes:

### Monetary state
- outstanding Hearts;
- issuance by class;
- use and recirculation velocity;
- redemption rate;
- inflation and price-access indicators;
- reserve ratio by asset-liquidity class;
- conversion volumes and settlement accuracy.

### Distribution and access
- balance concentration;
- dormant-balance concentration;
- essential acceptance;
- fiat-essential burden;
- failed transactions;
- geographic and accessibility gaps;
- provider capacity and waiting time.

### Administrative capacity
- Treasury queues;
- validation backlog;
- Community Weaver workload;
- investigation and appeal delay;
- provider onboarding delay;
- offline reconciliation gap;
- technical incidents;
- unresolved data conflicts.

### Reliability
- data age;
- provenance warnings;
- source dependencies;
- sensor and model versions;
- missingness;
- unresolved hypotheses.

## 14.2 Learning System

Pilot data may inform model and algorithm updates, but learning systems must use:

- public versioning;
- pre-deployment evaluation;
- holdout or external validation where feasible;
- subgroup impact review;
- rollback capability;
- human and community review;
- NARP for material changes;
- no self-modifying production model outside authorized governance.

“Improved conversion efficiency” is not sufficient if material access, trust, distribution, or ecological integrity worsens.

## 14.3 Required Stress Tests

At minimum, annual stress testing covers:

1. reserve and liquidity shock;
2. confidence run;
3. external-input dependence;
4. low-velocity indistinguishable states;
5. correlated sensor failure;
6. strategic reporting and coalition manipulation;
7. non-additive interaction and intervention order;
8. rapid adoption and administrative overload;
9. price-feed and oracle failure;
10. fraud false positives;
11. ICTL arbitrage and legitimate differential;
12. emergency-authority misuse;
13. social-validation capture;
14. Treasury resolution;
15. Indigenous or community withdrawal;
16. re-emergence of worth coercion or artificial scarcity.

## 14.4 Core Failure-Mode Revisions

### Redemption pressure
Redemption above 80% of new issuance for three months is an inherited illustrative alert. It does not establish hyperinflation. The Treasury runs the §8.4 diagnostic before structural contraction.

### Accrual anomaly
Accrual above 50% of a comparison group for two months is an inherited illustrative review trigger. It cannot independently authorize a fraud finding or full freeze.

### Rapid adoption
Transaction growth above 30% for three months is an inherited illustrative capacity alert. The system measures validation workload, error, delay, appeals, and access before automating additional validation.

### Indigenous participation decline
A 20% decline over two quarters is an inherited illustrative attention trigger. The affected community determines whether the cause is harm, consent withdrawal, seasonality, mobility, data error, or another condition.

### Capture-pattern re-emergence
Means-testing of the unconditional baseline, coercive contribution hierarchy, arbitrary scarcity, or conversion of the Love Ledger into a productivity score triggers constitutional review.

## 14.5 Reporting

Stress-test assumptions, code where possible, results, failures, and unresolved findings are published in the GGF Failure Library. Synthetic success does not constitute empirical validation.

# 15. Framework Interfaces

## 15.1 AUBI Framework

**AUBI owns:**

- payout entitlement;
- Layer 1–3 design;
- LMCI-M and LMCI-A definitions;
- material-security and Hearts-usability tests;
- low-velocity diagnostic;
- Decision Observability Protocol;
- individual epistemic rights.

**Financial Systems owns:**

- issuance execution;
- reserve and liquidity mechanics;
- Treasury operation;
- settlement;
- ICTL;
- financial containment;
- financial stress testing.

No financial-system rule may override AUBI’s unconditional baseline or pipeline separation without a higher constitutional process.

## 15.2 Nested Economies

Nested Economies defines local economic sovereignty, BAZ operations, and cross-level coordination. Financial Systems supplies common transaction, Treasury, reserve, and translation protocols.

Local variation is preserved unless common interoperability or rights protections require a shared standard.

## 15.3 Work in Liberation

Work in Liberation defines Community Providers, labor standards, need broadcasting, and work governance.

Financial Systems processes Hearts and Leaves flows but does not define the social worth of work. LMCI-A and provider validation remain purpose-limited. Love Ledger recognition is not converted into labor surveillance.

## 15.4 Indigenous and Traditional Knowledge Governance

Indigenous communities control:

- use of traditional knowledge;
- culturally specific validation;
- data access and publication;
- withdrawal of consent;
- ecological interpretation under relevant protocols.

Financial translation does not create ownership over knowledge or sacred practice.

## 15.5 Technology Governance

Technology Governance specifies:

- cybersecurity;
- identity;
- cryptography;
- ledger architecture;
- AI governance;
- technical audit;
- incident response;
- data portability.

Financial Systems specifies the financial purpose and constitutional constraints those technologies must serve.

## 15.6 Meta-Governance and Justice

Meta-Governance coordinates overlapping authority. The Digital Justice Tribunal adjudicates rights, sanctions, and cross-framework disputes. Earth Council protects ecological integrity and Indigenous sovereignty.

## 15.7 Existential Risk and PIS

PIS may trigger time-limited emergency financial coordination under §10. It does not possess an unlimited power to bypass AUBI or permanently alter financial architecture.

## 15.8 Sovereign Debt Transformation

The surviving v3.3 source proposed debt service through verified ecological restoration, care infrastructure, or planetary-health outcomes. The mechanism is too consequential and under-specified to remain operational in this framework.

Revision 3.4 therefore defers sovereign-debt conversion to a dedicated protocol or framework covering:

- legal authority;
- debtor and affected-community consent;
- baseline and additionality;
- ecological permanence and leakage;
- creditor treatment;
- Indigenous land rights;
- verification independence;
- distributional effects;
- currency and default risk;
- dispute resolution;
- NARP and Slow-Variable review.

Financial Systems may supply settlement infrastructure only after that protocol is adopted.

# 16. Key User Pathways

## 16.1 Credit-Union or Treasury Manager

1. Obtain an authorized Treasury charter.
2. Integrate with approved settlement and identity standards.
3. Establish reserve accounts and liquidity reporting.
4. Publish operational rules, eligible redemption, queue policy, and appeal contacts.
5. Receive Heartstarter or GCF capitalization where authorized.
6. Begin processing redemption under the 50/30/20 reserve hierarchy.
7. Maintain Observation Contracts, State Estimate Records, and incident reporting.
8. Enter resolution rather than conceal insolvency or redefine the threshold.

## 16.2 BAZ Council

1. Complete the AUBI bootstrapping pathway.
2. Establish local access, correction, and low-velocity diagnostic channels.
3. Maintain practical provider and Treasury capacity.
4. Submit local evidence and counter-models to ICTL processes.
5. Use Tier reclassification and objection rights where necessary.
6. Protect non-participants and informal provision from forced monetization.
7. Decide whether scaling remains locally desirable.

## 16.3 Community Provider

1. Obtain a charter through the appropriate BAZ process.
2. Accept Hearts under published terms.
3. Maintain service and transaction records proportionate to purpose.
4. Request Treasury redemption for authorized external costs.
5. Receive notice and review rights for disputed transactions or holds.
6. Avoid wash activity, coercive bundling, or conversion evasion.

## 16.4 Participant

1. Receive AUBI and access Hearts through approved channels.
2. Use digital or offline payment options.
3. Inspect fees, demurrage, conversion, and privacy rules.
4. Correct transaction errors.
5. Challenge adverse decisions without staking Hearts.
6. Retain Layer 1 protection during disputes.
7. Decline Love Ledger participation or unnecessary data collection.

## 16.5 SRC or FLP Decision-Maker

1. Identify the authorized decision.
2. Use the published Observation Contract.
3. produce a State Estimate Record.
4. preserve alternative hypotheses and subgroup effects.
5. choose containment separately from long-term intervention.
6. specify intervention order.
7. publish independent outcome criteria.
8. use NARP where material.
9. reverse or adapt when outcomes falsify the decision.

# 17. Irreducible Design Tensions

## 17.1 Liquidity vs. Local Retention

Reliable redemption builds trust but can drain local circulation. Restricting redemption may preserve reserves while making Hearts coercive. The framework contains rather than eliminates this tension through transparent rules, liquidity facilities, local supply-chain development, and protected exit.

## 17.2 Transparency vs. Privacy

Financial integrity requires auditability, while participant dignity requires privacy. Institutional and aggregate transparency must exceed personal exposure.

## 17.3 Interoperability vs. Local Meaning

Common rates enable exchange but compress local context. NARP, local counter-models, protected categories, and Indigenous sovereignty limit that compression.

## 17.4 Speed vs. Diagnosis

Delayed response can cause harm, but rapid action can misdiagnose the state. Latency is therefore decomposed, and temporary containment is separated from permanent intervention.

## 17.5 Automation vs. Contestability

Automatic controls can stabilize a system faster than human governance, but they can also hide value choices and reproduce error at scale. Automation remains narrow, reversible, versioned, and challengeable.

## 17.6 Observation vs. Surveillance

Financial adaptation needs evidence. Combined identity, transaction, contribution, migration, health, and relational data can produce a surveillance architecture. Purpose limitation, pipeline separation, protected unobservability, and epistemic rights are constitutional constraints, not optional privacy features.

# Conclusion: Finance as Accountable Coordination

Financial Systems v3.4 preserves the core ambition of the earlier framework: finance should support care, ecological restoration, local sovereignty, and material freedom rather than scarcity, shame, and extractive hierarchy.

The reconstruction sharpens what that ambition requires operationally.

A resilient financial system is not one with the most controls. It is one that knows:

- what each control can safely do;
- what the triggering indicator does and does not reveal;
- when several causes coexist;
- when action must be sequenced;
- when the evidence supports only temporary containment;
- when a person or community may challenge the system’s interpretation;
- when moving the financial metric has failed to improve life;
- when authority must devolve, reverse, or end.

The governing principle is therefore:

> **Observe the financial condition, preserve uncertainty, contain immediate risk, diagnose before permanent intervention, protect basic security, and evaluate the human and ecological outcome rather than the trigger alone.**
