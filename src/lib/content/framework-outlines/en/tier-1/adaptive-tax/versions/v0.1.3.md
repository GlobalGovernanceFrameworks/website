# Executive constitutional summary

The GGF economic architecture already defines material security through AUBI, regenerative financial infrastructure through Financial Systems, cross-scale economic relations through Nested Economies, worker and provider governance through Work in Liberation, and several domain-specific fiscal proposals across the wider corpus.

What it has lacked is the framework that two Tier 1 economic documents already expect to exist: a common architecture for **revenue and fiscal instruments**.

The Adaptive Tax Framework fills that gap.

It does not prescribe one universal tax system. It does not make wealth taxation, carbon taxation, land-value taxation, automation taxation, corporate taxation, or any other instrument mandatory. It does not levy a tax by publication. It does not create a global revenue service. It does not turn economic indicators into self-executing tax rules.

Instead it supplies the shared fiscal grammar needed to answer, for each proposed instrument:

- Who has authority to enact it?
- What fiscal or public-purpose objective is it meant to serve?
- What event, stock, flow, rent, externality, or capacity is the legal base?
- Who is liable, and through what jurisdictional nexus?
- What evidence can lawfully and reliably observe that base?
- Who actually bears the economic burden after behavioural responses?
- What avoidance, migration, substitution, or liquidity responses are plausible?
- How is the obligation assessed, collected, challenged, corrected, and ended?
- How does the instrument interact with AUBI, Hearts, Leaves, land, enterprises, inheritance, commons, and cross-border systems?
- What evidence would justify revising, suspending, replacing, or abandoning it?

The word **adaptive** has a bounded meaning in this framework:

> **Adaptive taxation means a fiscal system capable of learning from observed outcomes and revising its instruments through constituted authority. It does not mean that a dashboard or indicator may autonomously change a tax base, rate, liability, or legal status.**

Four rules govern the framework:

> **No tax exists merely because a framework names it.**

> **A fiscal indicator is an alert signal, not a tax instruction.**

> **A tax system should observe economic facts through records constituted for fiscal or financial purposes rather than cannibalizing records created to recognize care, ecological contribution, civic participation, culture, or relationship.**

> **Conformance, collection, or revenue growth does not prove that a fiscal instrument improved welfare, fairness, resilience, legitimacy, or ecological outcomes.**

# 1. Purpose, scope, and boundaries

## 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of the Adaptive Tax Framework is to provide constitutional, administrative, observational, and implementation architecture for lawful public revenue systems that can:

- finance public goods, social protection, ecological restoration, and institutional functions;
- distribute contribution obligations in a publicly defensible way;
- reduce systematic tax advantages created by differences in legal form, realization, mobility, or information control;
- internalize externalities and recover rents where a competent authority lawfully chooses to do so;
- coordinate fiscal systems across borders without silently creating a fiscal sovereign above them;
- preserve material security, due process, privacy, Indigenous authority, and local fiscal autonomy;
- learn from incidence, avoidance, administrative failure, economic adaptation, and unintended effects;
- narrow, replace, or terminate instruments that fail their stated purpose.

## 1.2 Primary role

Adaptive Tax owns the **architecture of fiscal instruments**.

It may define common design requirements for:

- taxes;
- levies;
- duties;
- royalties;
- public charges;
- assessed contributions;
- tax credits and rebates;
- tax expenditures;
- fiscal backstops;
- coordinated minimum-contribution rules;
- revenue-sharing arrangements;
- lawful information and administration interfaces supporting those instruments.

It does not decide the full fiscal policy of every jurisdiction.

## 1.3 Matters outside direct scope

Adaptive Tax does not itself govern:

- AUBI eligibility, payment layers, or contribution recognition;
- Hearts or Leaves issuance, reserves, redemption, or settlement;
- labour standards, provider charters, or the social meaning of contribution;
- municipal land-use policy or Community Land Trust design;
- voluntary property conversion or stewardship title;
- social wealth fund governance or universal capital endowments;
- competition law or general market-concentration remedies;
- criminal forfeiture, sanctions, reparations adjudication, or compulsory acquisition;
- Indigenous internal taxation, title, law, or representation;
- expenditure appropriation merely because revenue was collected;
- the substantive legality of a tax in a jurisdiction without the relevant legal analysis.

## 1.4 No residual fiscal jurisdiction

A fiscal problem does not become an Adaptive Tax competence because:

- existing tax systems are ineffective;
- inequality is severe;
- a proposed tax appears economically efficient;
- a cross-border actor is difficult to tax;
- a public service needs funding;
- an indicator shows avoidance or concentration;
- a GGF body possesses better data.

Authority remains with the competent jurisdiction unless validly delegated, coordinated, jointly constituted, or conferred through the applicable constitutional process.

## 1.5 Treaty boundary

Treaty for Our Only Home Article 18 permits Parties to negotiate and implement common revenue instruments, but the Treaty does not levy them merely by naming them. Its currently enumerated protocol classes are carbon and pollution charges, aviation and shipping levies, financial-transaction levies, digital-services contributions, extractive-resource and commons-use charges, assessed Party contributions, voluntary contributions and bonds, and recovery of penalties and restitution.

Private personal wealth taxation is not among those enumerated Treaty revenue classes. Wealth and inequality may inform assessed **Party** contributions, but a Treaty-level personal wealth or minimum-wealth contribution would require a separately valid competence rather than being implied from Article 18.

Domestic jurisdictions remain free to enact instruments within their own lawful competence.

# 2. What “adaptive” means

## 2.1 Adaptation is institutional learning

Adaptive taxation is not autonomous taxation.

The framework recognizes four forms of adaptation.

### Diagnostic adaptation

Evidence changes the official understanding of:

- who bears the tax;
- whether the intended base is actually observed;
- whether avoidance has migrated;
- whether investment, work, prices, rents, ownership, or migration changed;
- whether administration excludes or overburdens particular groups;
- whether the instrument is achieving its stated objective.

Diagnostic adaptation changes the **state estimate**, not the law by itself.

### Administrative adaptation

A competent administration may alter procedures already within its delegated authority, such as:

- audit methods;
- staffing;
- filing processes;
- valuation practice;
- payment plans;
- information requests;
- service channels;
- error-correction processes.

Administrative adaptation may not silently change the legal base, rate, liable class, jurisdiction, or rights of appeal.

### Policy adaptation

A competent legislature, Treaty body, public authority, or other lawfully constituted decision-maker may revise:

- bases;
- rates;
- thresholds;
- credits;
- exemptions;
- timing;
- intergovernmental allocation;
- instrument choice;
- sunset and renewal.

Evidence informs this decision. It does not make the decision.

### Bounded formulaic adaptation

A law may define in advance a formula that adjusts a parameter in response to a specified observable condition.

Such a rule is conforming only where the constituting instrument defines:

- the variable;
- source and update cadence;
- adjustment function;
- upper and lower bounds;
- effective date;
- treatment of missing or disputed data;
- review and correction;
- override or suspension authority;
- sunset or renewal.

A formula may execute a previously enacted rule. It may not infer a new causal theory or tax competence from the data it observes.

A formula that **reduces, suspends, rebates, or defers** an already enacted fiscal burden may operate automatically where the constituting instrument has defined the trigger, scope, floor, duration, restoration rule, and review. Protective relief is not constitutionally identical to automatic punitive escalation: a temporary reduction of an existing burden can preserve access or continuity while remaining reversible.

Automatic **increases, maximum-rate escalation, new liability, adverse classification, or penalty** require the same prior enactment plus stricter evidentiary, correction, notice, and appeal safeguards proportionate to the adverse consequence. A framework indicator alone may authorize neither direction.

## 2.2 Fiscal indicators are not tax instructions

Revenue shortfall, inequality, inflation, unemployment, asset-price growth, emissions, transaction velocity, land vacancy, automation, migration, or any other indicator may justify review.

No indicator by itself establishes:

- tax liability;
- tax evasion;
- taxable ownership;
- wealth;
- beneficial control;
- ability to pay;
- an optimal rate;
- an emergency tax;
- retroactive liability.

## 2.3 Adaptation has a burden of explanation

Every material adaptation shall state:

1. what changed in the observed condition;
2. what alternative explanations remain plausible;
3. what part of the instrument is proposed to change;
4. who possesses authority to change it;
5. who is expected to bear the revised burden;
6. what independent outcomes will test the change;
7. what would reverse or end it.

# 3. Fiscal constitutional architecture

## 3.1 No self-authorizing tax

Every compulsory fiscal obligation requires an independently valid constituting instrument.

At minimum, that instrument shall identify:

- authority;
- jurisdiction;
- liable person or entity;
- legal base or taxable event;
- rate, schedule, or calculation method;
- exemptions, deductions, and credits;
- assessment authority;
- collection method;
- payment timing;
- records and information powers;
- challenge and appeal;
- penalties or enforcement authority where applicable;
- review, amendment, and expiry.

A framework reference, model result, coalition declaration, target, public dashboard, or GGF recommendation is not a substitute.

## 3.2 Decision-chain separation

Consequential fiscal systems should distinguish:

1. **policy formation** — proposes objectives and options;
2. **lawmaking or adoption** — creates the obligation;
3. **observation and valuation** — establishes relevant facts and estimates;
4. **assessment** — applies the law to a case;
5. **collection and settlement** — receives payment and records satisfaction;
6. **audit and investigation** — examines compliance;
7. **adjudication** — resolves contested law, fact, valuation, and procedure;
8. **allocation** — decides how revenue is used;
9. **outcome evaluation** — assesses the instrument independently of collection success.

The same institution may perform several functions where existing constitutional arrangements permit it. Concentration of functions shall be visible and reviewable.

No institution should both define the tax base, control the only observation channel, adjudicate its own disputed assessment, and benefit institutionally from maximizing the resulting revenue without external checks.

## 3.3 Tax authority is not expenditure authority

Collection does not determine appropriation.

Revenue may be routed to:

- general public budgets;
- AUBI financing;
- universal services;
- ecological restoration;
- affected communities;
- social or shared-capital funds;
- the Global Commons Fund;
- debt reduction;
- other lawful purposes.

The authority and distribution rule for each destination must be separately established.

## 3.4 Rights and protected continuity

Fiscal enforcement and reform shall preserve, subject to valid law and proportionate necessity:

- access to basic material security;
- essential care and services;
- worker wages and pensions;
- dependants’ interests;
- access to appeal and representation;
- records needed for correction and refund;
- Indigenous and community data control;
- continuity of essential enterprises and infrastructure where collection can be structured without defeating liability.

Protected continuity is not a general exemption from tax. It is a constraint on how obligations are administered and enforced.

# 4. Phase 0 — Use authority that already exists

## 4.1 Purpose

Adaptive Tax begins before any new tax is enacted.

A jurisdiction should first determine what it can lawfully do now, what its current system already observes, where revenue and fairness failures actually arise, and whether a new instrument is superior to repairing an existing one.

Phase 0 is the default starting point for municipalities, BAZs, states, voluntary coalitions, Treaty-development groups, and other actors that do not yet possess the authority assumed by a proposed future mechanism.

## 4.2 Fiscal Authority and Capacity Map

A Phase 0 process shall map:

- constitutional and statutory taxing powers;
- intergovernmental tax-sharing rules;
- existing major tax bases and rates;
- exemptions, credits, deductions, and tax expenditures;
- existing information and third-party reporting systems;
- valuation capacity;
- audit and collection capacity;
- legal aid, objection, and appeal systems;
- privacy and data limits;
- Indigenous fiscal and territorial interfaces;
- cross-border dependencies;
- fiscal transfers and grants;
- administrative cost and staffing;
- populations and economic activity poorly represented by existing systems.

## 4.3 Existing-system repair before instrument multiplication

Before proposing a new tax, the jurisdiction should test whether the stated objective could be achieved more effectively through:

- improved enforcement of existing law;
- removal or redesign of an exemption;
- better third-party reporting;
- simplified filing;
- corrected valuation;
- a different credit or rebate;
- intergovernmental transfer reform;
- lawful procurement conditions;
- better benefit take-up or expenditure design;
- a non-tax regulatory instrument;
- a land, commons, or pricing instrument already within local authority;
- a voluntary compact where compulsion is unnecessary.

A new tax should not be used to compensate for an administrative failure that the new tax would inherit.

## 4.4 Today-compatible actions

Without creating new fiscal authority, Phase 0 may support:

- tax-expenditure inventories;
- incidence studies;
- anonymous or synthetic modelling;
- public-data simulations of new bases;
- valuation exercises;
- taxpayer-service redesign;
- model legislation;
- appeal and correction design;
- administrative-capacity building;
- cross-jurisdiction information standards that remain voluntary until lawfully adopted;
- municipal land-value or vacancy instruments where existing law permits them;
- procurement and subsidy conditions within current public authority;
- fiscal stress tests;
- public deliberation over objectives and trade-offs.

## 4.5 What Phase 0 may not do

Phase 0 does not permit an actor to:

- collect an unauthorized tax;
- threaten future penalties for refusing a voluntary pilot;
- treat modelled liability as debt;
- create a private blacklist of hypothetical taxpayers;
- demand data it could not lawfully demand in the real system;
- condition AUBI Layer 1 or essential services on participation;
- represent a simulation as enacted law;
- turn a municipal, BAZ, or coalition pilot into implied Treaty authority.

## 4.6 Minimum viable fiscal architecture

If a jurisdiction implements only the minimum useful subset of this framework, it should establish:

1. a Fiscal Authority and Capacity Map;
2. an Instrument Design Record for every new or materially revised instrument;
3. an incidence and administrative-burden assessment;
4. purpose-limited fiscal observation compliant with CIS/0.2.4;
5. notice, correction, and independent appeal;
6. a scheduled outcome and adaptation review;
7. an explicit sunset, continuation, or replacement decision.

# 5. Fiscal objectives and instrument portfolio

## 5.1 Distinguish the objective before choosing the base

A tax may serve one or more purposes:

- general revenue;
- redistribution;
- social-insurance financing;
- rent capture;
- externality pricing;
- macroeconomic stabilization;
- behavioural incentive;
- public-investment return;
- intergovernmental solidarity;
- commons stewardship;
- administrative simplification or base protection.

An instrument shall not be described as successful merely because it performs one purpose while failing another that was also claimed.

## 5.2 Instrument families

Adaptive Tax may support design and comparison of the following instrument families where lawfully available.

### Earned-income and payroll instruments

Possible bases include wages, salaries, self-employment income, payroll, or employer-side labour costs.

Required review includes:

- burden on low and middle incomes;
- labour-form arbitrage;
- employee/contractor boundaries;
- interaction with AUBI and social insurance;
- payroll-base erosion through automation or outsourcing.

### Corporate and business-income instruments

Possible bases include corporate profits, distributed profits, excess returns, cash flow, or sector-specific bases.

Required review includes:

- entity choice;
- profit shifting;
- debt/equity bias;
- loss treatment;
- related-party transactions;
- economic incidence across owners, workers, consumers, and locations.

### Capital-income and gains instruments

Possible bases include dividends, interest, rents, realized gains, accrued gains, or other returns to capital.

Required review includes:

- realization incentives;
- loss symmetry;
- inflation treatment;
- deferral;
- borrowing against assets;
- entity retention;
- valuation and liquidity.

### Inheritance and gift instruments

These govern transfers across persons and generations and are specified further in §8.

### Property, land, and wealth instruments

Possible bases include land value, real property, selected asset classes, or net wealth.

Adaptive Tax provides fiscal-design and administration rules. Land-use transformation, Community Land Trusts, and municipal anti-speculation policy remain with the competent territorial framework, including Bioregional Polis where adopted.

### Consumption instruments

Possible bases include value added, retail sales, excise, or selected consumption categories.

Required review includes:

- regressivity;
- necessities;
- household composition;
- informal-economy effects;
- border and platform collection;
- rebate or credit architecture.

### Environmental, resource, and commons instruments

Possible bases include emissions, pollution, extraction, depletion, congestion, resource access, or commons use.

Required review includes ecological authority, affected-community standing, incidence, measurement quality, and the difference between paying a charge and acquiring permission to cause harm.

### Financial-transaction and financial-sector instruments

Possible bases include transactions, balance-sheet measures, sector rents, or systemic-risk-related charges.

Required review includes liquidity, market substitution, jurisdictional migration, financial stability, and collection at settlement points.

### Digital-services and platform instruments

Possible bases include revenue, user-market participation, digital presence, advertising, or other legally defined digital-economic connections.

Required review includes nexus, double taxation, cross-border allocation, platform pass-through, and data-purpose limitations.

### Automation-related instruments

Automation-related fiscal proposals may seek to:

- replace eroding payroll revenue;
- capture extraordinary rents;
- finance worker transition;
- recover public investment in enabling technologies;
- internalize displacement or concentration effects.

The framework does **not** adopt the legacy AUBI proposal of a universal 5–25% automation tax triggered by fixed productivity or labour-displacement thresholds. Productivity gain, causal displacement, substitution, price effects, and counterfactual employment are difficult to observe and may be jointly caused by several factors.

An automation-related tax remains a candidate instrument requiring an explicit base that can be observed and administered without pretending that one indicator proves displacement.

### Trade, customs, and border fiscal instruments

Possible instruments include tariffs, customs duties, border charges, temporary tariff exemptions, fee reductions, and other fiscal conditions attached to cross-border exchange.

The domain framework may define the trade-policy objective, product or activity classification, ecological or labour standard, provenance requirement, Regenerative Trade Zone condition, or transition pathway. Adaptive Tax governs the fiscal design where that policy is implemented through a compulsory charge, credit, rebate, exemption, or revenue transfer.

Required review includes:

- lawful customs, trade, compact, or Treaty authority;
- the taxable import, export, transaction, access event, or other legal base;
- classification and evidentiary rules;
- rate schedule and any maximum or default rate;
- interaction with trade obligations and non-member rights;
- pass-through to workers, producers, consumers, and affected regions;
- correction where provenance or classification data are missing or wrong;
- collection and settlement currency;
- revenue destination and transition-fund handoff;
- expiry, review, and appeal.

A traceability gap, certification score, ecological indicator, or logistics alert does not by itself create a tariff. A lawfully enacted schedule may apply a previously defined consequence to a verified legal fact, but uncertainty or missing data shall not be converted by framework logic into an unreviewable punitive maximum.

An enacted schedule may also define **automatic relief**, such as temporary suspension or reduction of staple-goods tariffs when a lawfully specified food-access or emergency condition is met. Such relief remains bounded formulaic adaptation under §2.1: the trigger, evidence source, duration, protected objective, restoration rule, and review must be enacted in advance. The permissibility of automatic relief does not create a reciprocal power to impose automatic punitive escalation.

### Credits, rebates, and tax expenditures

A credit or exemption is a fiscal instrument and shall be evaluated as such.

It shall state:

- objective;
- beneficiary;
- fiscal cost;
- distribution;
- behavioural hypothesis;
- duration;
- interaction with direct expenditure;
- evidence required for continuation.

A tax incentive is not presumptively superior to a transparent grant merely because it is delivered through the tax system.

## 5.3 Instrument Design Record

Every proposed or materially revised instrument shall state:

- legal authority and adoption state;
- objective or objectives;
- comparator policy;
- liable class;
- base and taxable event;
- territorial, residence, source, or other nexus;
- rate or calculation;
- thresholds;
- deductions, exemptions, credits, and rebates;
- timing;
- information sources;
- valuation method where relevant;
- expected statutory incidence;
- expected economic incidence;
- behavioural responses;
- administrative cost;
- liquidity risks;
- cross-border interactions;
- challenge and appeal;
- revenue destination or handoff;
- evaluation measures;
- review and sunset.

# 6. Fiscal observability and base integrity

## 6.1 Legal base and economic condition are different objects

A policy concern may involve a latent condition such as:

- economic capacity;
- rent extraction;
- pollution;
- market power;
- inherited advantage;
- tax avoidance;
- land speculation.

Tax liability nevertheless arises from the **legal base defined by the constituting instrument**, not directly from the analyst’s preferred economic concept.

The observation architecture shall therefore distinguish:

1. the policy condition motivating the instrument;
2. the legally taxable fact;
3. the observations used to establish that fact;
4. uncertainty and disputes;
5. independent outcomes used to evaluate the policy objective.

## 6.2 Fiscal Observation Contract

A consequential fiscal instrument shall maintain an Observation Contract consistent with CIS/0.2.4.

It shall identify, as applicable:

- decision or assessment supported;
- legally relevant condition;
- observations used and unavailable;
- original constituting purpose of each material data source;
- whether each use is primary, compatible secondary, or consequential secondary use;
- data authority and access basis;
- source and system dependencies;
- missing populations and economic activity;
- valuation uncertainty;
- timing and lag;
- alternative explanations;
- correction and challenge;
- independent outcome measures.

## 6.3 Recognition-record firewall

Care, ecological, civic, cultural, relational, and analogous recognition records shall not become default fiscal sensors.

Adaptive Tax shall comply with CIS/0.2.4 §10.4.

Where recognized activity lawfully creates a financial asset, payment, entitlement, liability, ownership interest, settlement event, or other consequential operational fact, the responsible domain systems shall create a purpose-appropriate financial or operational record at creation.

A later tax process should use that purpose-appropriate record rather than reconstructing the economic event from:

- Love Ledger narratives;
- Proof of Care material beyond what the fiscal law independently requires;
- contribution histories;
- gratitude records;
- ecological narratives;
- civic participation records.

A missing financial record is an interface defect. It is not permission for unrestricted reconstruction from recognition data.

## 6.4 Beneficial ownership and control

Where a tax legally depends on ownership, control, related-party status, or economic interest, the implementation shall define the relevant concept rather than relying on the word “owner” alone.

Possible legally relevant distinctions include:

- registered ownership;
- beneficial ownership;
- voting control;
- appointment rights;
- trust beneficial interests;
- partnership interests;
- related-party debt;
- options and derivatives;
- rights to income or disposal.

Control without legal ownership may matter for some instruments and not others. The tax law must say when.

## 6.5 Third-party information

Third-party reporting may reduce the difference between actors who cannot conceal a taxable flow and actors who can choose when or where it becomes visible.

A reporting obligation shall specify:

- reporter;
- legal basis;
- data fields;
- frequency;
- correction;
- confidentiality;
- retention;
- cross-border transfer;
- penalties where lawfully applicable;
- treatment of conflicting reports.

More data is not automatically better administration.

## 6.6 Valuation under uncertainty

Where the base includes non-traded or illiquid assets, the system shall publish the permissible methods and the treatment of uncertainty.

Methods may include:

- market value;
- comparable transactions;
- financing-round value;
- capitalization of income;
- formulaic value;
- independent appraisal;
- valuation bands or corridors.

The implementation shall state:

- valuation date;
- smoothing;
- control and minority treatment;
- debt treatment;
- dispute process;
- events requiring revaluation.

False precision is not fiscal fairness.

# 7. Minimum Effective Contribution Floor

## 7.1 Status

The Minimum Effective Contribution Floor is a **candidate high-threshold backstop**, not a universal requirement of the Adaptive Tax Framework.

A jurisdiction may reject it and still conform to Adaptive Tax if its alternative fiscal architecture satisfies the framework’s authority, observability, fairness, administration, and evaluation requirements.

## 7.2 Purpose

The instrument addresses a specific failure mode: persons with very large economic capacity may face low recognized personal tax liability because gains remain unrealized, profits remain inside controlled entities, consumption is financed through borrowing, or legal form and jurisdiction alter where income appears.

The policy objective is not simply “tax wealth.” It is to prevent control over realization, entity form, financing, or residence from creating a systematic effective-contribution advantage unavailable to ordinary earned income.

## 7.3 Reference architecture

A jurisdiction may define a high-threshold contribution floor using a rule of the following form:

`additional contribution = max(0, reference-rate × adjusted reference wealth − qualifying personal taxes already paid)`

The rule means the floor operates as a **top-up**, not necessarily as an additive tax on top of every existing liability.

The exact threshold, reference rate, qualifying taxes, base, and credits require enactment. This framework sets none of them.

## 7.4 Required design choices

An enacted floor shall define:

- covered persons;
- high-threshold entry rule;
- residence, citizenship, domicile, source, or other nexus;
- adjusted reference wealth;
- excluded assets or interests;
- liabilities deductible from the base;
- beneficial-ownership and trust rules;
- private-company valuation;
- treatment of pensions, ordinary homes, farms, small enterprises, and dependants;
- qualifying tax credits;
- foreign-tax credits;
- treatment of corporate taxes attributable to controlled entities, if any;
- valuation date and smoothing;
- liquidity and instalment rules;
- exit and trailing-liability rules where lawfully used;
- appeals and correction;
- review and sunset.

## 7.5 No treaty competence by implication

Treaty Article 18 does not currently confer a general Treaty power to impose this personal floor.

Treaty-level adoption would require a valid new competence or other constitutional route. Domestic or subnational jurisdictions may act only within their own legal authority.

## 7.6 Liquidity

Large economic capacity can coexist with low immediate cash liquidity.

An enacted floor may therefore permit:

- instalments;
- secured deferral;
- payment at a defined liquidity event;
- payment in accepted marketable assets;
- another legally defined arrangement.

Illiquidity is a payment-design problem unless the constituting law makes it a base or exemption rule. It should not become an automatic permanent exemption.

## 7.7 Comparator requirement

Before adoption, the floor shall be compared against plausible alternatives such as:

- capital-gains reform;
- accrual or mark-to-market treatment;
- realization-at-death;
- inheritance and gift reform;
- controlled-entity attribution;
- corporate-distribution rules;
- land or rent taxation;
- improved enforcement of existing taxes;
- combinations of these.

The floor should not be adopted merely because it has an intuitively appealing headline rate.

# 8. Inheritance, gifts, trusts, and intergenerational transfer

## 8.1 Fiscal ownership

Adaptive Tax owns the **tax architecture** of inheritance, gifts, and related intergenerational transfers.

The proposed Shared Capital and Social Inheritance Protocol owns the separate question of whether public revenue or shared assets finance universal capital endowments or other broadly distributed ownership claims.

Hearthstone continues to govern voluntary bequests and separately lawful transitions into stewardship structures.

## 8.2 Design options

A jurisdiction may use:

- estate-based taxation;
- recipient-based inheritance taxation;
- lifetime gift aggregation;
- realization or deemed-realization rules;
- trust and foundation attribution;
- generation-skipping rules;
- hybrid systems.

Adaptive Tax does not prescribe one universal model.

## 8.3 Required questions

The design shall state:

- whether liability follows the estate, donor, recipient, asset, or a combination;
- treatment of prior gifts;
- thresholds and relationship-based exemptions;
- treatment of dependants and disability-related trusts;
- valuation date;
- business and farm continuity;
- liquidity;
- residence and situs;
- trusts and foundations;
- charitable and public-benefit transfers;
- cross-border credits;
- Indigenous and communal-property boundaries;
- appeal and correction.

## 8.4 Anti-fragmentation

Where the objective includes limiting dynastic concentration, the law should test avoidance through:

- repeated sub-threshold gifts;
- family-controlled trusts;
- foundations retaining founder control;
- disguised loans;
- generation-skipping vehicles;
- entity transfers rather than direct asset transfers;
- cross-border relocation of the transferor, recipient, trust, or asset.

Anti-avoidance must remain lawfully defined and challengeable.

## 8.5 Indigenous and communal property

The existence of a succession tax does not authorize a jurisdiction to individualize, value, transfer, or tax Indigenous communal, sacred, customary, or protected property contrary to the applicable authority and law.

The tax system shall not treat non-market or collective tenure as a loophole merely because it does not resemble individually alienable property.

## 8.6 Death, taxpayer continuity, and succession interface

Where a tax instrument uses realization at death, estate taxation, inheritance taxation, beneficiary/recipient rules, or another death-dependent fiscal consequence, the tax administration must rely on a death/status event established by a competent external authority or another source valid under applicable law.

Adaptive Tax:

- does not register death;
- does not infer death from non-filing, account dormancy, cessation of transactions, disappearance, non-response, or missing telemetry;
- does not determine heirs, probate, family status, legal capacity, citizenship, migration status, or inheritance entitlement;
- may create a tax-estate, executor, liable-estate, recipient, or successor-taxpayer record only where valid tax/succession law authorizes it;
- uses purpose-limited taxpayer/estate identifiers and CIS/0.2.4 for any cross-domain same-person continuity assertion;
- treats such linkage as non-transitive by default and preserves source records;
- provides correction for mistaken merge, mistaken split, duplicate, collision, and status error.

An externally established death does not automatically erase:

- tax liabilities already lawfully accrued;
- refunds or credits due;
- pending valuation or assessment challenges;
- overcollection remedies;
- confidentiality duties;
- records required for lawful audit, appeal, estate administration, or succession;
- unresolved intergovernmental or revenue-handoff duties.

Who ultimately bears, receives, succeeds to, or may challenge those fiscal positions is determined by the competent tax, estate, succession, family, trust, or judicial authority—not by this framework merely because it defines fiscal architecture.

# 9. Land, environmental, commons, digital, and automation interfaces

## 9.1 Land and property

Bioregional Polis already contains local policy proposals involving land-value taxation, vacancy taxation, anti-speculation surcharges, Community Land Trusts, and rights of first refusal.

Adaptive Tax does not duplicate those urban-policy mechanisms.

Where a jurisdiction adopts a land or vacancy tax, Adaptive Tax supplies the fiscal questions:

- legal authority;
- base;
- valuation;
- liable party;
- exemptions;
- incidence;
- billing and collection;
- appeal;
- data;
- interaction with rents, tenants, development, and other taxes;
- review.

The currently published numerical Polis rates remain policy proposals of that framework, not defaults created by Adaptive Tax.

## 9.2 Environmental and commons charges

Environmental and commons instruments shall distinguish:

- **measurement of harm or use**;
- **legal authority to charge**;
- **permission to undertake the activity**;
- **allocation of the resulting revenue**.

Payment of a carbon, extraction, pollution, water, spectrum, shipping, or other commons-related charge does not by itself legalize an otherwise prohibited activity or extinguish restitution, restoration, Indigenous, or affected-community claims.

## 9.3 Treaty revenue classes

Where Treaty authority is validly activated, Adaptive Tax may provide implementation architecture for the Article 18 revenue classes:

- carbon and pollution charges;
- aviation and shipping levies;
- financial-transaction levies;
- digital-services contributions;
- extractive-resource and commons-use charges;
- assessed Party contributions;
- voluntary contributions and bonds;
- recovery of penalties and restitution.

Each still requires the base, rate, collector, use, exemptions, audit, review, and distributional/cross-border assessment required by the Treaty.

## 9.4 Digital services

Digital-service taxation requires a defined economic and jurisdictional connection.

User presence, data, advertising, sales, local market access, or digital establishment may be relevant only where the law makes them so.

The existence of user or community data does not grant the tax authority unrestricted access to the underlying data. Tax liability should be established from purpose-appropriate fiscal and business records wherever feasible.

## 9.5 Automation

Automation is not a taxable fact until law defines one.

A credible instrument should prefer observable bases such as:

- realized business returns;
- excess rents;
- payroll-base changes under a defined formula;
- deployment or licensing events;
- public subsidy or procurement conditions;
- other legally specified measures.

A model-estimated number of “jobs displaced by AI” should not directly generate tax liability where multiple causal explanations remain plausible.

## 9.6 Trade and logistics fiscal interface

Gaian Trade defines trade-policy objects such as Regenerative Trade Zones, Digital Product Passports, planetary-boundary tariffs, and trade-transition conditions. Global Supply Chains & Logistics operationalizes physical logistics, provenance, hubs, and transport networks. Neither framework acquires general fiscal authority from those functions.

Where a trade or logistics rule creates a compulsory tariff, duty, tax credit, rebate, exemption, or fiscal earmark:

1. the competent authority and constituting instrument shall be identified;
2. the legal base and rate schedule shall be enacted or otherwise validly adopted before assessment;
3. a domain indicator may establish evidence relevant to the enacted rule but shall not invent the fiscal consequence;
4. missing, disputed, or broken provenance should ordinarily trigger correction, inspection, a lawful customs hold, or another pre-authorized evidentiary treatment rather than a framework-created maximum penalty;
5. any bounded automatic application shall satisfy §2.1's formulaic-adaptation requirements; automatic relief may use proportionate pre-enacted triggers, while automatic punitive escalation requires stricter adverse-consequence safeguards, correction, notice, and appeal;
6. Hearts or Leaves denomination, valuation, or tax-credit treatment requires an explicit Adaptive Tax–Financial Systems interface and shall not create a universal fiat peg by example;
7. earmarked tariff or duty revenue shall use `ATX-08` and shall not give the receiving fund authority over the tax base or trade classification.

The currently published GSCL rule that broken or absent Digital Product Passport provenance automatically applies a maximum Dark Chain Tariff is therefore a legacy non-conforming proposal unless and until a competent authority expressly enacts the relevant legal classification, consequence, evidentiary standard, and challenge route. The same review applies to fixed Hearts/Leaves tax-credit examples, acoustic or environmental shipping taxes, and circular-logistics tax breaks.

By contrast, a pre-enacted rule that temporarily **suspends or reduces** an existing tariff to protect essential food access or another protected minimum may be conforming where it satisfies §2.1 and CIS automatic-control limits. Relief must still be bounded, reversible, observable, and reviewable; emergency or humanitarian purpose does not permit silent creation of a new fiscal competence.

# 10. Hearts, Leaves, and regenerative-currency tax interface

## 10.1 Purpose

Hearts and Leaves create a fiscal-interface question that AUBI and Financial Systems do not themselves resolve.

Financial Systems governs issuance, balances, transactions, reserve, redemption, and settlement. Adaptive Tax governs whether and how a participating jurisdiction treats relevant financial events for tax purposes.

Neither framework may silently repurpose Love Ledger or other recognition records to answer the other’s question.

## 10.2 Required explicit classification

A jurisdiction using Hearts shall expressly define which, if any, event is fiscally relevant:

- issuance;
- receipt as provider or business revenue;
- transfer;
- exchange for goods or services;
- redemption into fiat;
- appreciation or depreciation where legally recognized;
- another event.

Leaving the classification unresolved is not neutral. It creates compliance uncertainty and may unintentionally turn care/community participation into a tax-accounting burden.

## 10.3 No duplicate taxation of the same economic value

Where several events in the Hearts lifecycle are legally relevant, the system shall prevent the same underlying value from being fully taxed repeatedly without a stated justification.

The treatment shall specify basis, credits, timing, and prior inclusions.

## 10.4 De minimis and household-use rule

Participating jurisdictions should provide a meaningful de minimis rule or simplified treatment for small household and community Hearts use where ordinary participation would otherwise create disproportionate recordkeeping or fiat tax obligations.

The threshold and design remain matters for the competent jurisdiction.

A de minimis rule is an administrative and proportionality safeguard, not a declaration that care has no economic value.

## 10.5 Liquidity alignment

A tax system should not systematically create immediate fiat liabilities from non-fiat Hearts events where the taxpayer lacks a practical fiat realization channel.

Possible lawful approaches include:

- taxation at redemption;
- payment in Hearts where separately authorized;
- deferral until a liquidity event;
- fiat-equivalent withholding by institutional payers;
- de minimis exemption;
- another explicit rule.

Adaptive Tax does not choose among these universally.

## 10.6 Household and institutional distinction

The tax treatment of occasional household/community use need not equal the treatment of:

- large Community Providers;
- employers;
- commercial enterprises;
- professional traders;
- institutional Hearts balances;
- large redemption flows.

The distinction shall be based on lawfully defined scale or role rather than a moral ranking of contribution.

## 10.7 Financial record at creation

Where AUBI, Work in Liberation, or another contribution pathway causes Hearts to be lawfully issued, Financial Systems shall ensure that a purpose-appropriate financial issuance or transaction record exists for the resulting asset or payment.

The fiscal system should use that record rather than importing the recognition history that preceded it.

Cross-framework allocation of this record-creation duty shall follow CIS §18.1 where unclear.

## 10.8 Leaves

Leaves are governed by Financial Systems as non-fungible ecological records and reward instruments, not as a second general-purpose currency.

A jurisdiction proposing tax treatment of a Leaf-related event shall first define:

- whether the Leaf constitutes an asset, reward, credential, claim, or other legally relevant object in that jurisdiction;
- valuation method, if value is legally required;
- taxable event;
- liquidity;
- ecological-evidence boundary;
- treatment of transfer or conversion.

Ecological evidence and protected knowledge shall not become general fiscal profiling data merely because a Leaf exists.

# 11. Cross-border taxation and fiscal coordination

## 11.1 Subsidiarity and nexus

Cross-border coordination does not erase the need for jurisdictional nexus.

An instrument shall state the legally relevant connection, which may include:

- residence;
- domicile;
- citizenship where applicable;
- source;
- asset location;
- permanent establishment;
- market participation;
- resource use;
- Treaty membership;
- another lawful connection.

The framework does not declare one nexus universally superior.

## 11.2 Double taxation and double non-taxation

Cross-border coordination should identify:

- competing claims;
- tax credits;
- exemptions;
- apportionment;
- source/residence priority;
- treaty interaction;
- timing mismatches;
- entity classification mismatches.

Avoiding double taxation should not create deliberate double non-taxation.

## 11.3 Information exchange

Cross-border information exchange shall be:

- lawfully authorized;
- purpose-limited;
- field-limited;
- secure;
- correctable;
- subject to retention and onward-transfer rules;
- compatible with protected non-transfer and Indigenous authority.

Participation in AUBI, Love Ledger, care, cultural, or ecological recognition systems is not a general-purpose international tax-information channel.

## 11.4 Mobility and exit

Lawful movement shall not be treated as wrongdoing.

A jurisdiction may use trailing residence rules, exit taxation, deemed realization, or similar instruments only where valid law defines:

- the nexus;
- covered assets or gains;
- reference date;
- duration;
- credits;
- payment timing;
- treatment of return;
- appeal.

A policy forecast shall model both post-enactment migration and anticipatory restructuring or departure where material.

## 11.5 Non-participants

A Treaty protocol or fiscal compact does not bind non-participants merely because coordination would be easier if they joined.

Participating jurisdictions may regulate persons, entities, assets, transactions, and access within their lawful jurisdiction. External effects require their own legal basis and challenge route.

## 11.6 Joint fiscal arrangements

Where a taxable condition exists only through relations among jurisdictions, parties may jointly constitute limited fiscal arrangements for:

- shared infrastructure;
- cross-border commons;
- coordinated minimum bases;
- revenue apportionment;
- joint administration;
- information exchange.

The compact shall define contribution, authority, allocation, exit, dispute, and closure. Joint administration does not create residual fiscal sovereignty.

# 12. Administration, assessment, and taxpayer rights

## 12.1 Administrative usability

A tax that is theoretically efficient but administratively unusable is not implementation-ready.

Every instrument shall assess:

- taxpayer population;
- filing frequency;
- information availability;
- valuation burden;
- language and accessibility;
- low-tech and offline access;
- staff and systems;
- expected dispute volume;
- collection cost;
- small-taxpayer burden.

## 12.2 Assessment record

A consequential assessment shall identify:

- taxpayer or liable entity through a purpose-appropriate fiscal reference; no universal cross-domain identifier is required;
- legal authority;
- period;
- base;
- observations and valuations;
- calculation;
- credits and prior payments;
- amount due or refundable;
- due date;
- correction process;
- appeal route;
- relevant uncertainty where material.

Where taxpayer continuity depends on recognizing that two records concern the same person or entity across systems, the administration shall record the specific authority, purpose, minimum disclosed fields, correction route, and expiry/unlink condition under CIS/0.2.4. A lawful tax-to-tax or tax-to-estate linkage does not authorize access to unrelated housing, migration, health, work, Love Ledger, justice, or civic records.

## 12.3 Automated systems

Automation may support:

- arithmetic;
- matching;
- anomaly detection;
- valuation comparison;
- triage;
- document classification;
- simulation.

An automated score shall not independently establish fraud, beneficial ownership, intent, legal residence, contested valuation, or final adverse liability where human or adjudicative judgment is required.

## 12.4 Audit selection

Risk-based audit selection shall disclose the permitted factors and prohibited data classes at an appropriate level of detail.

Recognition records shall not become default fraud or audit-risk signals.

A person’s refusal to participate in Love Ledger, AUBI contribution recognition, civic activity, or ecological recognition shall not increase tax-enforcement suspicion by itself.

## 12.5 Challenge, correction, and refund

Every material tax system shall provide, consistent with applicable law:

- notice;
- reasons;
- access to relevant evidence;
- correction of factual errors;
- valuation challenge;
- administrative review;
- independent appeal;
- refund or credit for overcollection;
- time limits;
- remedy for unlawful disclosure or collection where applicable.

## 12.6 Enforcement boundary

Adaptive Tax specifies no police, seizure, criminal, border, or coercive enforcement power.

Those powers require separate law and the relevant justice, security, and constitutional safeguards.

Assessment is not guilt. Non-payment is not automatically fraud. Fraud is not established by an algorithmic discrepancy.

# 13. Incidence, distribution, and economic response

## 13.1 Statutory liability is not economic incidence

The person who remits a tax may not bear its final economic burden.

Evaluation should consider possible effects on:

- workers;
- owners;
- consumers;
- tenants;
- suppliers;
- creditors;
- land values;
- investment;
- wages;
- prices;
- migration;
- informal activity;
- affected regions and communities.

## 13.2 Distributional reporting

A material reform should report, where feasible:

- household income and wealth groups;
- business size;
- geography;
- relevant demographic or accessibility effects using lawful and proportionate data;
- workers versus owners;
- renters versus landowners;
- current versus future generations;
- affected Indigenous and territorial communities where applicable.

Distributional analysis shall not require unnecessary personal visibility.

## 13.3 Behavioural response

Revenue estimates shall identify assumptions about:

- avoidance;
- evasion;
- timing;
- realization;
- consumption;
- investment;
- saving;
- labour supply;
- entity formation;
- prices;
- migration;
- cross-border restructuring.

Static arithmetic shall not be presented as collected revenue when behavioural response is material.

## 13.4 Avoidance migration

Closing one route may move planning into another.

Stress tests should examine substitution among:

- personal and corporate income;
- wages and dividends;
- debt and equity;
- realization and retention;
- gifts and trusts;
- domestic and foreign entities;
- cash and in-kind benefit;
- ownership and contractual control;
- taxed and exempt asset classes.

## 13.5 Compliance legitimacy

Compliance depends partly on whether taxpayers perceive rules as understandable, consistently applied, challengeable, and difficult to game through privileged access to legal or financial engineering.

Legitimacy is an outcome to observe, not a reason to suspend due process for unpopular taxpayers.

# 14. Revenue use and framework handoffs

## 14.1 Revenue does not self-allocate

An instrument shall identify the lawful revenue destination or the authority that later appropriates it.

Adaptive Tax may support handoffs to:

- ordinary public budgets;
- AUBI financing;
- universal services;
- ecological restoration;
- local and affected-community funds;
- the Global Commons Fund;
- Shared Capital and Social Inheritance institutions;
- other lawful public purposes.

## 14.2 AUBI

AUBI remains unconditional according to its own framework.

Adaptive Tax may provide revenue used to finance AUBI. It may not make AUBI Layer 1 conditional on:

- tax participation beyond generally applicable law;
- Love Ledger participation;
- employment;
- care contribution;
- political support;
- use of Hearts;
- tax-compliance scoring beyond any separately lawful eligibility rule that AUBI itself constitutionally permits.

## 14.3 Shared Capital and Social Inheritance

If a jurisdiction dedicates fiscal revenue or received assets to a social wealth fund, community fund, citizen-capital account, or social inheritance programme, the receiving institution is governed by the Shared Capital and Social Inheritance Protocol or another valid public-capital framework.

Adaptive Tax governs the fiscal transfer into that system, not the fund’s investment mandate, voting rights, beneficiary governance, or distribution policy.

## 14.4 Hearthstone

A tax liability does not automatically trigger stewardship conversion.

Where law permits payment through asset transfer, public purchase, negotiated transition, or another property mechanism, Hearthstone may support the transition only within its own authority and consent rules.

## 14.5 Bioregional Polis

Bioregional Polis may propose municipal land and anti-speculation instruments. Adaptive Tax supplies common fiscal-design, observability, incidence, administration, and appeal standards where those instruments are lawfully adopted.

## 14.6 Financial Systems

Financial Systems executes Hearts and other GGF financial operations. Adaptive Tax defines fiscal treatment where a competent jurisdiction adopts it.

Neither framework may redefine the other’s authority through technical implementation.

## 14.7 Nested Economies

Nested Economies governs the political and constitutional conditions under which relatively autonomous economies coordinate.

Adaptive Tax governs the fiscal content of tax compacts, credits, apportionment, and revenue-sharing arrangements.

## 14.8 Trade and transition-fund revenue

Gaian Trade, GSCL, Work in Liberation, or another domain framework may identify a Just Transition Fund, Global Commons Fund, community fund, or other destination for tariff, duty, levy, royalty, or related revenue.

That domain objective does not self-appropriate the revenue. `ATX-08` shall identify:

- the authority collecting the revenue;
- the lawful appropriation or earmark;
- the receiving institution;
- conditions on use;
- audit and correction;
- whether the earmark expires with the originating instrument.

A certification body, logistics council, or trade administrator shall not increase its own funding merely by changing the classification or indicator that determines the charge.

# 15. Fiscal adaptation cycle

## 15.1 Lifecycle

A material fiscal instrument should move through the following learning loop:

1. **Purpose** — state the problem and objective.
2. **Authority** — establish the lawful decision-maker and jurisdiction.
3. **Design** — define base, rate, liability, administration, rights, and comparator.
4. **Observe** — establish the policy condition and legally relevant facts through purpose-appropriate records.
5. **Implement** — collect and administer under the enacted rule.
6. **Evaluate** — measure incidence, behaviour, administration, revenue, rights, and independent outcomes.
7. **Interpret** — preserve alternative explanations and uncertainty.
8. **Decide** — retain, revise, narrow, suspend, replace, or terminate through competent authority.
9. **Close or renew** — handle records, outstanding liabilities, refunds, transitions, and future review.

## 15.2 Adaptation Review

A scheduled Adaptation Review shall state:

- original objective;
- current authority;
- revenue and cost;
- distributional outcomes;
- avoidance and migration;
- administrative burden;
- rights and data incidents;
- economic and ecological outcomes relevant to the objective;
- changes in the base or economy;
- alternative policies now available;
- recommendation;
- competent decision-maker;
- next review or sunset.

## 15.3 No self-validation

The following are not sufficient evidence of success:

- more revenue after raising the rate;
- fewer reported violations after reducing audit coverage;
- lower measured avoidance after narrowing the measurement definition;
- lower vacancy after reclassifying vacant properties;
- lower taxable wealth after taxpayers change legal form;
- higher Hearts tax receipts after requiring more financial reporting.

Independent outcomes are required.

## 15.4 Emergency fiscal measures

A fiscal emergency does not create general fiscal sovereignty.

Temporary measures shall identify:

- emergency authority;
- underlying decision class;
- duration;
- base and liable class;
- protected minimums;
- review;
- expiry;
- transition to ordinary law if the condition persists.

Permanent fiscal policy shall not be maintained through repeated emergency renewal merely because ordinary enactment is politically difficult.

# 16. Stress testing and falsification

## 16.1 Instrument stress tests

Before consequential adoption or major revision, a material instrument should be tested against at least the relevant subset of the following.

### Authority failure

Can the full liability and collection chain be traced to valid authority?

### Base observability failure

Can the legally relevant base be observed without pretending a proxy uniquely identifies it?

### Recognition-record contamination

Does the system rely on care, ecological, civic, cultural, or relational recognition data that should instead be replaced by purpose-appropriate fiscal or financial records?

### Incidence reversal

Could the burden fall substantially on a different group than the statutory taxpayer?

### Avoidance migration

Does closing the intended route shift the tax base into another legal form, asset class, entity, or jurisdiction?

### Administrative overload

Can taxpayers and the administration comply at realistic staffing, technology, valuation, and appeal capacity?

### Valuation disagreement

Does the system remain workable under a plausible range of private-asset or property values?

### Liquidity stress

Can the obligation be met without converting every illiquid liability into either forced destruction or indefinite exemption?

### Migration and entry effects

Does the model include departure, non-arrival, restructuring, and anticipatory response where relevant?

### Cross-border mismatch

Can the design avoid both double taxation and deliberate double non-taxation?

### Distributional reversal

Could a nominally progressive or corrective instrument become regressive after price, wage, rent, or benefit responses?

### Capture and selective enforcement

Could powerful actors obtain exemptions, under-valuation, weak audit, privileged rulings, or political allocation of enforcement?

### Technology failure

Can filing, payment, records, challenge, and essential continuity function during cyberattack, outage, identity failure, or vendor collapse?

### Comparator failure

Is there a simpler, more lawful, cheaper, or less intrusive instrument that achieves the objective at least as well?

## 16.2 Framework-level falsification

Evidence should justify narrowing, replacing, or not scaling parts of Adaptive Tax where:

- the adaptation process adds administrative churn without improving decisions;
- information requirements create surveillance burdens disproportionate to revenue or fairness gains;
- a proposed base cannot be observed with acceptable error;
- cross-border coordination costs exceed the externality it addresses;
- a high-threshold contribution floor performs materially worse than simpler capital-income, inheritance, or realization reforms;
- formulaic adaptation repeatedly changes policy in the wrong direction because the observed state is ambiguous;
- local fiscal autonomy can achieve equal or better results without a common standard;
- purpose-specific frameworks already govern the issue more effectively.

Conformance to this framework is not evidence against those findings.

# 17. Implementation pathway

## 17.1 Phase 0 — Fiscal map and non-coercive preparation

Actors use authority already available to:

- map fiscal competence and capacity;
- inventory current instruments and tax expenditures;
- model incidence and avoidance;
- build valuation, correction, and appeal capacity;
- test data interfaces with synthetic, anonymized, public, or otherwise lawfully available information;
- draft legislation and compacts;
- compare alternatives;
- correct existing administrative failures.

No new compulsory liability is created by this phase.

## 17.2 Phase 1 — Domestic or local enactment

A competent jurisdiction may enact instruments within its existing authority.

Priority should be given to instruments whose:

- authority is clear;
- base can be observed;
- administration is feasible;
- taxpayer rights can be protected;
- incidence can be evaluated;
- failure can be reversed.

A Phase 1 package may include reforms to existing income, capital, inheritance, land, consumption, environmental, corporate, or other domestic taxes. It does not require adoption of every instrument in this framework.

## 17.3 Phase 2 — Bilateral and regional coordination

Jurisdictions may coordinate:

- definitions;
- credits;
- residence and source rules;
- information exchange;
- valuation standards;
- beneficial-ownership interfaces;
- apportionment;
- minimum bases or contribution rules;
- shared administration.

The coordination instrument shall preserve non-participant status, exit, dispute resolution, and fiscal autonomy beyond the shared problem.

## 17.4 Phase 3 — Treaty-compatible common revenue

After Treaty entry into force and relevant protocol activation, Parties may implement the revenue classes validly authorized under Article 18 and any future competences lawfully added to the Treaty.

Treaty implementation does not erase domestic administration or automatically centralize collection. The protocol should use the lowest effective collection and administration layer compatible with the common objective.

## 17.5 No inevitable progression

Phase 0 is not a promise of Phase 3.

A successful local or national system may remain local. A bilateral compact may remain regional. A proposed common tax may fail the subsidiarity, observability, legal, or comparator test and never scale.

# 18. Framework interfaces

## 18.1 Constitutional Interface Specification

CIS/0.2.4 controls:

- authority boundaries;
- subsidiarity;
- decision-chain separation;
- observation;
- recognition-record purpose limitation;
- person-continuity and purpose-limited attestation;
- non-transitive linkage and correction/collision handling;
- external-status ingestion without civil-registration authority;
- automatic controls;
- protected continuity;
- conformance and migration.

Adaptive Tax shall not create a fiscal exception to CIS merely because fiscal administration benefits from additional data.

## 18.2 Treaty for Our Only Home

The Treaty governs:

- Treaty fiscal competence;
- common revenue protocols;
- assessed Party contributions;
- GCF authority;
- non-member effects;
- Treaty-level adoption and enforcement.

Adaptive Tax provides implementation architecture within those powers. It does not enlarge them.

## 18.3 AUBI

AUBI governs:

- Layer 1–3 entitlement and support;
- LMCI architecture;
- contribution recognition;
- AUBI-specific epistemic rights.

Adaptive Tax governs revenue architecture and fiscal treatment. Legacy tax percentages and fiscal incentives presently embedded in AUBI should be reviewed for migration rather than assumed canonical.

## 18.4 Financial Systems

Financial Systems governs:

- Hearts and Leaves;
- issuance execution;
- Treasury operations;
- reserve and redemption;
- financial transaction records;
- ICTL;
- financial observability.

Adaptive Tax governs fiscal liability and treatment where lawfully adopted.

## 18.5 Work in Liberation

Work in Liberation governs workers, Community Providers, labour standards, and contribution pathways.

Adaptive Tax may tax legally relevant wages, enterprise revenue, payments, or other financial events under valid law. It may not convert recognition of work or care into a generalized tax profile.

## 18.6 Nested Economies

Nested Economies governs:

- fiscal and economic subsidiarity context;
- cross-scale compacts;
- bargaining and holdout;
- protected non-convertibility;
- material exit.

Adaptive Tax supplies the tax-specific content of a compact.

## 18.7 Bioregional Polis

Bioregional Polis governs municipal transition, land commons, zoning, CLTs, rights of first refusal, and local anti-speculation policy.

Adaptive Tax governs tax-design requirements for fiscal instruments adopted through that pathway.

## 18.8 Hearthstone

Hearthstone governs voluntary and separately lawful transitions of land, enterprises, infrastructure, and capital into stewardship forms.

Adaptive Tax governs tax consequences and fiscal incentives only where lawfully enacted. Tax policy shall not silently convert a voluntary Hearthstone pathway into compulsory transfer.

## 18.9 Shared Capital and Social Inheritance

The proposed sibling protocol governs:

- plural public and community capital funds;
- collective ownership mandates;
- beneficiary rights;
- social inheritance and universal capital endowments.

Adaptive Tax may provide revenue or assets to such institutions. It does not govern their ownership or investment authority.

## 18.10 Meta-Governance

Meta-Governance may coordinate cross-domain and cross-jurisdiction fiscal interfaces among authorities that already possess standing.

It does not become a revenue ministry or acquire taxation power because several jurisdictions disagree.

## 18.11 Justice and data governance

Justice frameworks govern adjudication, remedy, lawful enforcement, and rights disputes. Justice does not create a tax merely because a fiscal dispute enters adjudication.

Aurora and Technology Governance govern data security, identity, privacy, algorithms, and technical infrastructure. Their current architectures already reject self-authorized taxes or compulsory levies and require an independently valid fiscal basis.

Adaptive Tax specifies the fiscal purpose those systems serve and the data boundaries they must preserve. It is therefore the destination for fiscal instrument design that older technology or data frameworks routed to non-fiscal homes.

## 18.12 Gaian Trade

Gaian Trade governs the substantive trade-policy architecture, including Regenerative Trade Zones, Digital Product Passports, planetary-boundary trade conditions, the Right to Opacity, and transition relationships among trading systems.

Adaptive Tax governs the fiscal grammar of tariffs, duties, rebates, exemptions, fiscal fee reductions, and revenue handoffs used to implement those policies where lawfully adopted.

A Gaian Trade certification, score, status, or traceability result does not by itself create fiscal liability. If a competent authority has enacted a schedule that uses a defined trade classification as a legal input, the resulting charge remains subject to fiscal authority, evidence, incidence, correction, appeal, and revenue-handoff rules.

Hearts-denominated tariffs, certification-linked fee reductions, temporary tariff exemptions, and tariff revenue directed to transition funds are legacy proposals requiring this interface rather than automatic inheritance into Adaptive Tax.

Gaian Trade's **Symmetrical Imbalance Penalty** requires separate constitutional treatment. As currently described, it is an inter-state fiscal charge linked to persistent trade surplus or imbalance and directs revenue to the Global Commons Fund. It is not plainly one of the common-revenue classes enumerated in Treaty Article 18.2, nor is it the assessed Party-contribution formula described in Article 18.3. Adaptive Tax therefore does not treat current Treaty Article 18 as sufficient authority for a Treaty-level imbalance levy. A jurisdiction may consider an analogous domestic measure within its own lawful trade and tax competence; participating authorities may create a valid compact where applicable; or the Treaty may acquire the competence through a properly constituted protocol or amendment. The trade-position indicator itself creates no liability.

Gaian Trade's Food Sovereignty Circuit Breaker illustrates the opposite direction of automation: a pre-enacted temporary tariff **relief** rule may be permissible where it protects essential access and satisfies §2.1's bounded formulaic-adaptation requirements. That does not imply that the same indicator may automatically create or maximize a punitive tariff.

## 18.13 Global Supply Chains & Logistics

GSCL governs operational logistics, BTRHs, supply-chain traceability, transport, resilience, and implementation of Gaian Trade at the physical-infrastructure level.

GSCL observations may supply lawfully admissible evidence to a customs or tax process. They do not create a tax rate or fiscal penalty merely because a logistics rule labels a chain dark, non-compliant, noisy, inefficient, or high-risk.

The following current GSCL fiscal proposals require migration or conformance review under Adaptive Tax:

- automatic maximum Dark Chain Tariffs following missing or broken provenance;
- fixed tax-credit conversion examples for Leaves or Hearts;
- attribution of Resource and Environmental Taxes and Financial Transaction Taxes to Financial Systems, which governs financial machinery rather than general tax law;
- the Acoustic Tax and other shipping-related fiscal charges;
- circular-logistics tax breaks and their sunset rules;
- fiscal revenue or credits routed to Just Transition or other domain funds.

Where retained, they shall be rewritten as lawfully authorized fiscal instruments or as clearly labelled illustrative policy options using the records and safeguards of this framework.

For Resource and Environmental Taxes and Financial Transaction Taxes, the principal defect is **ownership attribution**, not necessarily the instrument family. Treaty Article 18.2 already contemplates relevant common-revenue classes under separately adopted protocols. Where GSCL relies on such revenue, the fiscal instrument should point to Adaptive Tax and the competent domestic, compact, or Treaty revenue authority; Financial Systems may provide settlement or Treasury machinery where separately authorized but does not own the tax.

# 20. Legacy fiscal-content migration

Several existing GGF documents contain fiscal proposals that predate Adaptive Tax as an actual framework.

Their presence should be treated as **legacy domain proposals**, not as automatically adopted Adaptive Tax policy.

## 20.1 AUBI fiscal proposals

Review:

- automation-tax percentages and trigger thresholds;
- resource-tax and land-value-capture references;
- tax incentives for Hearts/Leaves adoption.

The legacy 5–25% automation-tax schedule is not inherited by this framework. Any future automation-related instrument must satisfy §5.2 and the ordinary Instrument Design Record.

## 20.2 Work in Liberation funding bridge

Work in Liberation v2.0 currently lists “Automation Tax and Resource Tax revenues” among the possible sources for public, ecological, solidarity, and essential-work commissioning.

Adaptive Tax v0.1.1 does not treat that phrase as a dependency on one specific Automation Tax or Resource Tax design. Until Work in Liberation is revised, it should be read as a legacy example of **revenue from separately lawful fiscal instruments** within a diversified funding portfolio.

A future Work in Liberation revision should replace the named legacy taxes with a reference to lawfully adopted Adaptive Tax revenues and other authorized funding sources. Rejecting or redesigning one automation-tax proposal therefore does not itself defund Work in Liberation.

## 20.3 Bioregional Polis and Pathfinder

Review:

- Bioregional Polis land-value, vacancy, non-resident, anti-speculation, and other fixed fiscal rates;
- Pathfinder / Regenerative Enterprise tax incentives and tax-linked transition benefits.

Domain policy objectives may remain local to those frameworks; fiscal design, authority, incidence, and adaptation should conform here.

## 20.4 Gaian Trade and GSCL

Review and disposition:

- **Gaian Trade tariffs, fee reductions, exemptions, and earmarks:** retain only through an explicit Adaptive Tax–Gaian Trade interface identifying fiscal authority, classification, rate schedule, settlement, incidence, challenge, and `ATX-08` revenue handoff.
- **Gaian Trade Symmetrical Imbalance Penalty:** treat as a distinct constitutional-gap item. A Treaty-level inter-state charge on trade position is not presently treated as authorized merely by Treaty Article 18.2 or the Party-contribution formula in Article 18.3; it requires independently valid domestic authority, a competent compact, or new Treaty competence.
- **Gaian Trade Food Sovereignty Circuit Breaker:** review as a candidate bounded automatic-relief rule rather than as punitive indicator-to-tax automation. If retained, the enacted trigger, evidence source, duration, restoration rule, and review must satisfy §2.1.
- **GSCL automatic Dark Chain Tariffs:** remove the framework-created maximum consequence; retain only as an enacted customs/tariff schedule with evidentiary, correction, and appeal rules.
- **GSCL Leaves/Hearts tax-credit conversions or implied fiat pegs:** remove fixed illustrative pegs unless a competent fiscal rule and Financial Systems interface explicitly create the valuation for the stated purpose.
- **GSCL Resource and Environmental Taxes and Financial Transaction Taxes:** correct the attribution from Financial Systems to the competent fiscal authority under Adaptive Tax. The underlying instrument families may remain where lawfully adopted, including under applicable Treaty Article 18.2 protocols; Financial Systems may only provide authorized settlement or Treasury functions.
- **GSCL Acoustic Tax and other shipping fiscal instruments:** retain only as separately authorized fiscal proposals with defined base, rate, collector, incidence, review, and trade-law interface.
- **GSCL circular-logistics tax breaks and sunset rules:** treat as tax expenditures under §5.2 and evaluate against direct-spending comparators and explicit sunset evidence.

The priority correction remains the **indicator-to-tax path**: a traceability, compliance, ecological, or logistics signal may support a lawfully defined classification, review, customs hold, relief trigger, or other authorized process, but framework logic shall not autonomously impose a maximum punitive fiscal consequence. Pre-enacted automatic relief may be permissible under §2.1 where it is bounded, reversible, and reviewable.

## 20.5 Digital Commons orphaned instruments

Digital Commons v3.0 records two fiscal instruments as migrated to “Aurora Accord / Financial Systems”:

- **Automation Dividend Tax**;
- **Data Exploitation Tax**.

Those destinations are no longer adequate owners of the fiscal function: Aurora governs data authority and requires a separate lawful finance basis, while Financial Systems governs financial machinery rather than general tax law.

Adaptive Tax therefore accepts both as **migration candidates**, not adopted policies.

- An Automation Dividend Tax, if retained, belongs to the automation-related instrument family and must define an observable legal base rather than infer displacement from a single metric.
- A Data Exploitation Tax, if retained, belongs to the digital-services, rent, externality, or another explicitly selected fiscal family. Aurora supplies the data-rights and lawful-use constraints; Adaptive Tax supplies fiscal authority, base, incidence, administration, and review.

## 20.6 Current conformance precedents

Current Technology Governance, Aurora, and Justice architectures should not be treated as unresolved tax migration targets merely because older documents routed fiscal ideas toward them.

Their current direction is the model Adaptive Tax expects from domain frameworks:

- the domain framework may identify harms, activities, public purposes, or funding needs;
- it does not self-authorize a tax or compulsory levy;
- fiscal implementation requires separate lawful authority and the tax-specific architecture of this framework;
- justice adjudicates valid claims and remedies rather than manufacturing fiscal competence.

The remaining migration object is therefore the **legacy fiscal proposal**, not the current domain framework's authority model.

## 20.7 Migration disposition

For each legacy proposal, the migration shall decide whether to:

- retain it in the domain framework as an illustrative policy option;
- move its fiscal architecture into Adaptive Tax;
- replace fixed numbers with a derivation or local calibration rule;
- mark it inactive pending authority or evidence;
- remove it as duplicative or constitutionally unsupported.

Migration shall not imply that a proposal becomes law merely because it has been moved to the tax framework.

# 22. Conformance and falsification gates

A fiscal mechanism claiming conformance to Adaptive Tax should be able to answer:

1. **Authority:** What valid instrument creates the obligation?
2. **Jurisdiction:** Why is this taxpayer, asset, activity, or transaction within scope?
3. **Purpose:** What outcome is the instrument meant to improve?
4. **Base:** What legally taxable fact is being measured?
5. **Observability:** Can that fact be established without using an ambiguous proxy as if it were the fact itself?
6. **Data purpose:** Are fiscal records constituted for fiscal/financial use, or is the system repurposing recognition data?
7. **Incidence:** Who is expected to bear the economic burden?
8. **Alternatives:** What simpler or less intrusive comparator could achieve the same purpose?
9. **Administration:** Can the system actually value, assess, collect, correct, and adjudicate the tax?
10. **Liquidity:** Can payment be made without turning illiquidity into either destructive collection or permanent exemption?
11. **Cross-border effects:** How are mobility, credits, apportionment, non-participants, and restructuring handled?
12. **Rights:** What notice, correction, appeal, privacy, and protected-continuity rules apply?
13. **Adaptation:** Who may revise the instrument, on what evidence, and through what authority?
14. **Automation:** What, if anything, may change automatically, and what are the enacted bounds?
15. **Revenue handoff:** Who decides how the revenue is used?
16. **Independent outcome:** What evidence beyond revenue collection tests whether the objective improved?
17. **Failure:** What result would justify narrowing, suspending, replacing, or ending the instrument?
18. **Closure:** What happens to outstanding liabilities, refunds, data, and intergovernmental arrangements when the instrument ends?
19. **Person continuity:** Where identity, death, estate, recipient, or successor matching matters, what purpose-limited identifier or external status source is used; how are non-transitivity, merge/split/collision correction, challenge, and surviving liabilities/refunds handled?

A mechanism that cannot answer the first six questions is not ready for consequential implementation.

For trade and logistics fiscal instruments, conformance additionally fails where a framework-generated traceability, certification, ecological, trade-position, or compliance indicator directly imposes a punitive tariff or tax rate without an independently enacted fiscal rule, evidentiary standard, correction path, and competent collector. A pre-enacted automatic **relief** rule does not fail merely because an indicator activates it, provided the rule satisfies §2.1's bounded formulaic-adaptation requirements and applicable CIS protections.

# 23. Closing doctrine

Taxation is one of the clearest places where governance becomes materially real.

It can finance shared capacity, reduce privilege, price harms, return rents, and make public commitments possible. It can also become opaque, extractive, administratively cruel, economically self-defeating, or quietly sovereign over domains it was never authorized to govern.

The GGF therefore should not define a “good tax system” as one that maximizes revenue or equalizes every outcome.

A conforming fiscal system is one in which:

- authority is traceable;
- the base is legally defined and observable;
- economic burden is examined rather than assumed;
- powerful actors cannot gain systematic advantage merely by choosing a less observable legal form;
- ordinary participation does not require total financial legibility;
- care and relational recognition are not converted into fiscal surveillance;
- cross-border coordination does not erase jurisdiction;
- adaptation occurs through lawful learning rather than automatic policy drift;
- taxpayers can challenge error;
- public revenue does not purchase unaccountable institutional expansion;
- failed instruments can be abandoned.

The governing rule is:

> **Observe the fiscal fact, not the whole person. Tax through constituted authority, not through technical visibility. Adapt through evidence and law, not through indicator reflex.**

And the implementation rule is:

> **Start with the authority and administrative capacity that exist on Monday. Add new fiscal power only when the objective, base, evidence, incidence, rights, and lawful decision route can be named before collection begins.**
