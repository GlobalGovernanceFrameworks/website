**Framework ID:** `IAF/1.1`  
**Source successor:** *GGF Implementation & Adaptation Framework Outline v1.0*  
**Functional tier:** Cross-cutting implementation and methods layer; **not** a constitutional operating system  
**Status:** Controlled successor for conformance adoption and adversarial review  
**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Controlling interface:** `GMEAIA/0.1`  
**Event transport:** `EGP/1.1`  
**Emergency interfaces:** `ECRC/0.1` and `CDEE/0.1`  
**Ethical advisory input:** `MOS/2.8.1`

# Executive summary

The GGF needs an operating manual that helps communities, institutions, networks, and public bodies translate broad frameworks into context-sensitive practice. The source framework supplies valuable ideas: trust-building before assessment, low-tech pathways, a Journey–Tool Matrix, culturally adapted pilots, Community Weavers, learning reports, a Failure Library, and reversible scaling.

Its source architecture also lets implementation machinery acquire hidden authority. A readiness index can classify a community; an Ethics Gate and a generic Indigenous representative can approve adaptation; a Quick Start stack can appear to activate crisis command; facilitator certification can become a gate; a dashboard can become a scaling trigger; and the Catalyst Team can become the practical arbiter of what counts as valid implementation.

v1.1 keeps the implementation capacity while separating it from the authority of the action being implemented.

> **Context is not competence. Assessment is not jurisdiction. A tool is not a mandate. Ethical review is not approval. Cultural advice is not affected-nation consent. Adoption is not activation. A pilot is not public law. Success is not authority to scale. An urgent signal is not an emergency declaration. Support is not command.**

The framework's role is now precise:

- help an implementation principal understand conditions;
- identify affected parties, rights, authority, and dependencies;
- offer tools and local adaptation options;
- support deliberation, consent, authorization, and activation through the correct interfaces;
- monitor, learn, repair, close, and share bounded lessons;
- never manufacture the authority of the substantive decision.

## Constitutional result

The framework is a **support, selection, documentation, and learning system**. It is not:

- a legislature, regulator, court, fund, or emergency command;
- a source of BAZ status, institutional standing, FPIC, public office, or jurisdiction;
- a body that may classify communities as competent or unready;
- an approval mechanism for local cultural adaptation;
- an authority to activate a pilot, crisis stack, PIS function, or MGCC command;
- a power to condition essential support on adoption of GGF methods or values;
- a route for trade, security, or geopolitical pressure.

The source section titled **Hard Backstop Strategy** is therefore retired from this framework. Trade, security, illicit-finance, cyber, diplomatic, and coercive strategies belong to their controlling substantive frameworks and cannot be invoked by an implementation manual.

# 1. Purpose, scope, and non-authority

## 1.1 Purpose

`IAF/1.1` provides a reusable pathway for implementing a framework, programme, tool, institutional reform, or local initiative while preserving local law, cultural form, rights, affected-nation authority, domain-specific safeguards, and the distinction between experimentation and public power.

It supports:

- engagement and trust-building;
- context and capacity profiling;
- affected-party and power mapping;
- pathway and tool selection;
- cultural adaptation and local forks;
- advisory ethics and rights review;
- pilot design and implementation planning;
- monitoring, evaluation, and learning;
- funding and participation support;
- scaling proposals and permanent-adoption referrals;
- closure, remedy, and public learning.

## 1.2 Scope

The framework may be used by:

- Indigenous nations and communities that choose it;
- municipalities, public agencies, and public-service bodies;
- cooperatives, associations, networks, and civil-society organizations;
- international and regional institutions;
- GGF frameworks and pilot programmes;
- facilitators, technical teams, funders, and evaluators acting within bounded mandates.

The framework does not determine whether any of those actors possess the authority required for the underlying action. That question is resolved through `IAF-28` and `GMEAIA-20`, plus the controlling domain interface.

## 1.3 Interface, not grant of power

Publication, translation, training, certification, funding, adoption by a Community of Practice, or inclusion in a GGF repository does not activate `IAF/1.1` in any jurisdiction.

A participating actor must separately establish:

- its constituting instrument;
- authority to request and use implementation support;
- authority for each consequential pilot or permanent action;
- applicable rights and public-law constraints;
- affected Indigenous authority;
- domain-specific interfaces;
- funding and procurement authority;
- review and remedy.

## 1.4 Governing doctrine

> **The framework can recommend how to proceed. It cannot decide that an actor may proceed.**

## 1.5 No upward inference

The following do not create authority:

- a readiness or trust score;
- an Autonomy Statement;
- a completed Tool Stack;
- an Ethics Review;
- a facilitator certificate;
- an Indigenous participation quota;
- an EGP adoption record;
- a grant or AUBI payment;
- an AI recommendation;
- a positive dashboard;
- a successful pilot;
- a target year or implementation tier.

# 3. Core principles

## 3.1 Subsidiarity with jurisdiction

Decisions should be made at the most local effective level **that actually possesses the relevant authority and capacity**. Locality does not erase nonmembers, minorities, affected Indigenous nations, workers, users, transboundary effects, or applicable law.

## 3.2 Anti-colonial by affected authority

Anti-colonial design requires more than external cultural sensitivity. It requires:

- the affected people naming themselves;
- control over representatives and knowledge;
- non-engagement without penalty;
- recognition of internal plurality;
- refusal of generic proxies;
- non-extractive data and learning;
- material support independent of methodological conformity.

## 3.3 Minimum viable implementation within rights

Begin with the smallest reversible action that can produce useful learning. “Minimum viable” does not mean minimum evidence, minimum consent, or minimum rights.

## 3.4 Low-tech first with equal standing

Every digital process must have an accessible analog, oral, assisted, SMS, paper, or offline route where feasible. Non-digital records receive equal substantive standing and remedy.

## 3.5 Modular and interoperable, not jurisdictionless

Tools may be combined like modules, but each module retains its own authority effect, domain interface, data rules, prerequisites, and closure duties. Interoperability does not merge jurisdictions.

## 3.6 Accountability metadata, protected payloads

Non-sensitive purposes, responsible actors, authority states, funding, review dates, and results should be transparent. Personal, worker, survivor, health, child, security, sacred, protected Indigenous, whistleblower, and legally sealed material remains bounded.

## 3.7 Ethical prudence without hidden veto

Ethics and rights review should reveal harm, uncertainty, capture, exclusion, and missing safeguards. It does not itself grant or deny authority.

## 3.8 Reversibility and continuing duties

Temporary powers and trials expire. Rights, wages, debts, pensions, compensation, records, restoration, safety, and unresolved claims continue until discharged.

## 3.9 Support without dependency or conformity

Facilitation, translation, capacity building, independent advice, and participation support must not depend on accepting a recommended tool, GGF philosophy, or favourable evaluation.

## 3.10 Learning without compulsory exposure

Failure and adaptation should be documented, but participation in an implementation process is not consent to permanent publication, storytelling, research, or AI training.

# 4. Institutional roles and separation of duties

## 4.1 Implementation principal

The principal is the community, institution, network, or lawful authority requesting or accepting implementation support. It owns the decision to seek support and any lawful internal tool-selection decision. It does not gain new jurisdiction from the framework.

## 4.2 GGF Catalyst Team

The Catalyst Team may:

- maintain tools and compatibility information;
- provide non-binding technical assistance;
- train and support facilitators;
- synthesize voluntarily shared learning;
- identify missing interfaces and safety concerns;
- help prepare funding applications and records.

It may not:

- approve or reject local adaptations;
- designate representatives;
- certify FPIC;
- declare a community ready or unready;
- activate pilots or emergency functions;
- condition essential support on conformity;
- command domain authorities;
- adjudicate complaints concerning its own conduct.

## 4.3 Community Weavers and facilitators

A Community Weaver is a facilitator, translator, recorder, and systems navigator within a defined mandate. Certification demonstrates one route to competence, not exclusive eligibility. A community may appoint a locally qualified equivalent.

Facilitators cannot convert process influence into decision authority. They must disclose conflicts, preserve dissent, protect data, submit to complaint and removal, and avoid dependency creation.

## 4.4 Assessors and advisory reviewers

Context assessors, ethics reviewers, cultural advisers, and epistemic reviewers produce advisory records. They must be independent from material funding or approval decisions where feasible and must disclose uncertainty, dissent, limitations, and conflicts.

## 4.5 Funders and vendors

Funders decide only within their lawful funding authority. Vendors provide contracted services only. Neither acquires governance authority, access to protected knowledge, or rights over community data merely through financing or technical infrastructure.

## 4.6 Community of Practice and learning custodians

The Community of Practice may compare methods and publish bounded patterns. The Failure Library or Story Bank custodian manages consent, protection, withdrawal, access, and correction. Neither ranks communities or uses learning records as a sanction or eligibility system.

# 5. The three-stage pathway, constitutionally separated

The familiar three-stage pathway remains, preceded by optional Stage 0. The stages organize support; they do not create a ladder of political maturity or authority.

## Stage 0 — Requested trust-building and pre-assessment

Stage 0 is used when a principal requests relationship-building or when an outreach offer is accepted. It may include listening sessions, shared meals, story circles, community art, informal network awareness, accessibility planning, and seed support.

Stage 0 shall not:

- secretly score the community;
- select representatives;
- map political vulnerabilities for outside strategy;
- collect protected stories for later publication;
- imply consent to Stage 1;
- condition support on continued engagement.

Outputs are `IAF-06` through `IAF-10`, with protected content minimized.

## Stage 1 — Context and implementation conditions: “Where are we?”

Stage 1 creates a multidimensional **Implementation Conditions Profile**, not a competence score.

Required dimensions include:

- existing mandate and decision processes;
- affected parties and affected Indigenous authority;
- resources, time, finance, technology, and maintenance;
- accessibility and language;
- conflict, safety, capture, and retaliation;
- relevant evidence, uncertainty, and dissent;
- protected domains and external dependencies;
- desired support and self-identified strengths.

The output may recommend support intensity and sequencing. It cannot determine whether a community deserves support or may govern itself.

### Retirement of the single GGF Readiness Index

The Low/Medium/High index is retired as a universal classification. A local actor may use a simple self-assessment for planning, but external systems must not treat it as authoritative or connect it automatically to grants, intervention, recognition, or rights.

## Stage 1.5 — Urgent support and Quick Start

Urgency changes the pace of support, not the source of authority.

The Quick Start route may:

- perform rapid listening and needs clarification;
- identify pre-existing local emergency or rescue authority;
- provide non-consequential templates and logistics;
- create an EGP `sense()` event;
- prepare a CDEE referral;
- support an already accepted ECRC/CDEE mission;
- document an immediate low-risk voluntary action.

It may not:

- declare a crisis;
- activate PIS, MGCC, or Crisis Command;
- compel data sharing;
- deploy external teams without acceptance;
- override affected-nation authority;
- renew emergency powers;
- turn a 48-hour diagnostic target into legal permission.

## Stage 2 — Journey and Tool Stack selection: “What path fits?”

Stage 2 compares possible journeys and tools. Each resource card must state:

- purpose and intended users;
- authority effect;
- protected-domain flags;
- prerequisites and controlling interfaces;
- time, people, cost, and maintenance;
- accessibility and low-tech equivalents;
- data, vendor, and security effects;
- risks, failure modes, exit, and remedy;
- version and compatibility.

The local principal may choose, adapt, fork, combine, postpone, or reject tools within its lawful scope.

### Coherence and autonomy

The “non-negotiable core” is not ideological conformity. It is the minimum conformance needed to avoid falsely claiming GGF compatibility:

- accurate authority effect;
- rights and protected-domain safeguards;
- affected Indigenous authority;
- consent and refusal;
- accessibility and data protection;
- review, expiry, remedy, and closure;
- exact version and interface records.

The adaptable periphery includes language, ceremony, facilitation, sequencing, technology, record form, and locally chosen tools.

### Cultural adaptation review

A cultural or Indigenous adviser may identify concerns or recommendations. No generic representative has a dual key over an affected nation or local lawful process. Binding consent and instructions come from the affected nation through `IAF-09` and `IAF-27`.

### Ethics review

The former Ethics Gate becomes an **Ethics, Harm, Rights, and Anti-Capture Advisory Review**. It may conclude:

- no identified concern;
- concern requiring response;
- missing information;
- protected-domain route required;
- strong recommendation to pause;
- dissent or unresolved conflict.

It cannot label the pilot legally approved or rejected.

## Stage 3 — Pilot, learn, close, and propose scale: “How do we proceed?”

Stage 3 contains distinct constitutional events:

1. pilot proposal;
2. affected-party and consent records;
3. authority verification;
4. trial authorization;
5. EGP adoption registration;
6. operational activation;
7. implementation;
8. monitoring and incident response;
9. midpoint and final review;
10. termination and closure;
11. learning artifact;
12. optional scaling proposal.

### Pilot complexity bands

The original four tiers are retained only as planning bands:

| Band | Description | Authority consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single low-risk tool | None; still requires appropriate internal authority and consent |
| 2 | Multi-tool pilot | None; cumulative dependency review required |
| 3 | Full local stack | None; public or protected-domain effects require specialized authority |
| 4 | Multi-community or multi-jurisdiction proposal | No automatic progression; new jurisdiction and authorization required |

A higher band is not evidence of maturity, certification, or political legitimacy.

### Red Lines and protective pause

A Red Lines alert may flag demonstrable harm, rights violations, service risk, coercion, or authority failure. A technical system may enter a pre-authorized safe state. Legal suspension, rollback, funding action, or remedy follows the named competent route.

### Scaling

Pilot success produces evidence and a proposal. It does not produce authority to scale. Every expanded population, territory, service, institution, or risk profile requires `IAF-43` and `IAF-44`.

# 6. Implementation Health Dashboard

The Dashboard is a navigation and learning instrument. It is not a score of community worth or a control panel for funding and activation.

It may display:

- participation distributions rather than a single engagement percentage;
- accessibility and exclusion;
- resource and maintenance conditions;
- adaptation and response time;
- trust and legitimacy evidence with uncertainty and dissent;
- financial dependence and resilience;
- incidents, harms, corrections, and unresolved duties;
- review and expiry dates;
- authority and activation status.

Illustrative targets such as 60% participation, 90-day adaptation, 50% resource independence, or 75% positive trust are not universal thresholds. A project may adopt a target through a separate record with context, method, reasons, distribution, review, and non-disposition rule.

No dashboard result may automatically:

- activate, pause, terminate, or scale a pilot;
- determine funding eligibility or payment;
- classify a community as ready or legitimate;
- identify fraud or wrongdoing;
- publish protected information;
- renew authority.

# 7. Tool families and flagship guides

The source flagship guides remain examples, subject to their controlling frameworks:

- **Bioregional Compass:** can support a community exploring a BAZ or another bioregional institution, but cannot create BAZ status, territory, Indigenous authority, or public jurisdiction.
- **Great Transition:** can support planning and worker/community dialogue, but industrial closure, security measures, finance, compensation, and legal mandates remain with their controlling frameworks.
- **Regenerative Resource Stewardship:** can integrate Hearthstone, ecological, trade, and Indigenous records, but cannot transfer title, recognize rights, issue permits, or impose trade consequences.

Every guide shall carry a prominent authority-effect statement and exact version pins.

# 8. Funding and resourcing

## 8.1 Capacity seed support

Seed grants may fund meeting space, translation, childcare, accessibility, local facilitators, independent advice, and basic tools. They do not buy consent or political representation.

## 8.2 Proto-GCF and Regenerative Compact

A voluntary compact or proto-fund may support pilots under existing law. It does not create treaty authority, public taxation, BAZ status, or a future claim to compulsory funding.

## 8.3 Funding Onramp

The Funding Onramp separates:

- request;
- eligibility review;
- award;
- agreement;
- payment authorization;
- disbursement;
- audit;
- suspension;
- clawback;
- appeal;
- closure.

Funding may support implementation but cannot activate it. Essential services, worker wages, participant accessibility, and independent advice require continuity safeguards.

## 8.4 No conformity leverage

A funder may require lawful use, financial accountability, safety, and rights safeguards. It may not condition essential or participation support on:

- adopting a particular GGF tool;
- affirming MOS or another philosophy;
- accepting an external cultural classification;
- waiving claims or protected knowledge;
- surrendering local authority;
- suppressing criticism.

# 9. Data, AI, and public learning

## 9.1 Data minimization and community control

Implementation records are collected for declared purposes. “Community ownership” does not erase individual, worker, affected-nation, legal, or protected-source rights. Access, retention, publication, correction, deletion, and withdrawal are separately governed.

## 9.2 AI support

AI may assist translation, accessibility, missing-field detection, pattern comparison, drafting, and non-consequential tool suggestions.

AI may not:

- classify community competence;
- select representatives;
- decide consent;
- pass an Ethics Review;
- verify legal authority;
- activate or scale a pilot;
- determine funding, sanctions, or closure;
- convert stories into risk or reputation scores.

## 9.3 Failure Library and Story Bank

Public learning artifacts must distinguish:

- method lesson;
- identifiable participant story;
- protected operational record;
- whistleblower or evidence material;
- protected Indigenous or cultural knowledge.

Publication requires purpose-specific authority and consent. Withdrawal, correction, de-identification, access restriction, and deletion must remain possible.

---

# 10. Conflict, complaint, and remedy

Every implementation must offer a route proportionate to its effects:

- local feedback and safe reporting;
- facilitator complaint and substitute;
- independent review;
- interim pause or relief;
- correction of assessment, dashboard, and public records;
- compensation or restitution where harm occurred;
- protection against retaliation;
- appeal to the competent domain body;
- reopening after closure when remedy remains unresolved.

The Catalyst Team cannot be the final reviewer of complaints against itself.

---

# 11. Emergency and security boundaries

## 11.1 Emergency

EGP carries asserted signals and adoption claims. CDEE carries cross-domain event identity and handoff. ECRC records the emergency constitutional acts. The Implementation Framework may support those systems but cannot replace them.

## 11.2 Security and coercion

The source Hard Backstop Strategy is outside the scope of `IAF/1.1`. Economic insulation, trade measures, illicit-finance targeting, cyber operations, asset restraint, security pressure, or legitimacy campaigns require their own frameworks, authority, evidence, rights, review, and SCPA handoff where coercive.

Implementation staff shall not collect political intelligence or use context and power maps for pressure campaigns.

# 14. Adoption, normative force, and effect labels

Implementation records use the GMEAIA normative-force states and EGP effect labels.

A tool or method may be:

- `proposed`;
- `advisory`;
- `voluntarily_adopted`;
- `contractually_binding` within a valid contract;
- `internally_binding` within a valid charter;
- `locally_enacted`;
- `nationally_enacted`;
- `treaty_active`;
- `suspended`;
- `expired`;
- `terminated`.

No state may be inferred from publication, popularity, funding, certification, pilot success, or a future date.

An EGP adoption must display one of the EGP/1.1 effect labels. `active_bounded_trial` requires valid IAF-28 through IAF-31 records.

# 17. Implementation sequence

## Phase A — Constitute and request

1. Adopt or reference the framework without granting it public power.
2. Record the principal, scope, actors, conflicts, and source versions.
3. Establish complaint and independent-review routes.

## Phase B — Engage and understand

1. Obtain engagement consent or record lawful outreach.
2. Map affected parties and affected Indigenous authority.
3. Provide accessibility and independent support.
4. Create the Context Assessment Charter.
5. Publish a conditions profile and support recommendation with uncertainty and dissent.

## Phase C — Select and adapt

1. Compare journeys and alternatives.
2. inspect tool authority effects and protected-domain flags.
3. document local adaptations.
4. receive cultural and ethics advice without treating it as approval.
5. record the tool-selection decision.

## Phase D — Authorize and activate a pilot

1. define the pilot and non-action alternative;
2. obtain consent and participation records;
3. verify authority;
4. route protected domains;
5. authorize the trial;
6. register the EGP adoption claim;
7. activate separately;
8. implement with service and rights safeguards.

## Phase E — Review, close, and learn

1. monitor distributions and incidents;
2. pause or roll back when required;
3. conduct midpoint and final reviews;
4. terminate and return authority;
5. complete remedies and data disposition;
6. create bounded learning artifacts;
7. open a new scaling or permanent-adoption process where justified.

# 18. What changed from v1.0

v1.1:

- reclassifies the framework from a Tier 1 “operating system” to a non-constitutional cross-cutting methods layer;
- replaces the universal Readiness Index with a conditions and support profile;
- separates outreach, trust-building, assessment, and intervention;
- removes generic Indigenous-framework representatives from approval and dual-key roles;
- converts the Ethics Gate into advisory review;
- separates tool selection, consent, authority, authorization, adoption, deployment, and activation;
- routes Quick Start emergencies through EGP/1.1, CDEE/0.1, and ECRC/0.1;
- makes pilot tiers complexity bands without authority effects;
- makes metrics and Red Lines review triggers rather than self-executing decisions;
- protects offline parity, participant support, protected data, and story withdrawal;
- separates funding from activation and ideological conformity;
- requires new authority for scaling and permanent adoption;
- retires the Hard Backstop Strategy from the implementation framework;
- adds 50 canonical records, a complete GMEAIA crosswalk, 32 gates, and 36 compound tests.

# 19. Closing doctrine

The framework succeeds when it makes implementation easier **without making authority easier to counterfeit**.

> **Help people understand their context. Offer tools without owning the choice. Support consent without manufacturing it. Document authority without inventing it. Start only after activation. Stop when the mandate ends. Return power, repair harm, and share learning without exposing those who made it possible.**
