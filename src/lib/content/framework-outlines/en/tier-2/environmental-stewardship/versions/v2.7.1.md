## 0. Executive summary

Ecological governance requires excellent sensing without allowing the sensor to become sovereign. This framework provides the distributed scientific, community, and Indigenous-knowledge infrastructure through which the Global Governance Framework ecosystem can observe ecological conditions, specify indicators, assess causation, communicate uncertainty, and recommend review.

It does **not** recognize legal rights, appoint guardians, define binding legal boundaries, transfer title, create stewardship mandates, issue permits or tariffs, direct funds, declare emergencies, impose restrictions, adjudicate disputes, or command enforcement.

> **Observe carefully. Interpret humbly. Recommend explicitly. Never convert measurement into authority.**

The former “Rights Layer” name is retired because it blurred a technical rights-review contribution with lawful rights recognition. v2.7 may support a `CERGTA-11` petition and a `CERGTA-12` recommendation. Only a separately competent body may create `CERGTA-13` legal recognition.

### What this framework provides

- distributed ecological observation and low-tech participation;
- provenance, data sovereignty, and protected-knowledge controls;
- transparent indicators, indices, thresholds, and models;
- causal assessment, uncertainty, dissent, and replication;
- ecological alerts that trigger review rather than consequence;
- bounded evidence handoffs to Planetary Health, rights review, AUBI, trade, biodiversity, water, oceans, soil, and restoration programmes;
- correction propagation, method suspension, and programme closure.

### What this framework cannot do

- declare an ecosystem a legal person;
- grant a guardian standing or a veto;
- make a sacred or inalienability designation;
- determine title, rematriation, tenure, or compensation;
- automatically issue Hearts, Leaves, grants, tariffs, certifications, or clawbacks;
- open a legal investigation, compel entry, seize evidence, or attribute liability;
- activate an emergency, receivership, market restriction, port denial, or enforcement operation;
- classify AI consciousness or govern digital-sentience rights.

# 2. Constitutional role separation

## 2.1 Observation is not consequence

The required chain is:

> **observe → validate → interpret → express uncertainty → recommend review → lawful recipient decides**

No observation, indicator, index, threshold, model, digital twin, dashboard, community score, citizen report, spiritual assessment, or AI output may skip the intermediate steps.

## 2.2 Relationship with Planetary Health

EIOI owns ecological methods and ecological evidence products. The Planetary Health Synthesis Office may receive a versioned ecological module through `EIOI-20`. PHC may synthesize and recommend. PHC may not direct EIOI to alter a method, suppress dissent, produce a predetermined score, or convert an ecological alert into binding action.

## 2.3 Relationship with CERGTA

EIOI primarily implements CERGTA Classes 1–3 and technical parts of Classes 12, 17, and 18. Every higher-class consequence remains with a separately competent authority.

## 2.4 Relationship with MOS

The Moral Operating System may supply advisory ethical questions and vocabulary. It cannot validate evidence, determine ecological identity, establish legal status, define a scientific weighting, appoint a guardian, or create a downstream consequence.

# 3. Guiding principles

1. **Epistemic humility:** uncertainty, disagreement, and model limits are part of the output, not defects to conceal.
2. **Plural evidence without forced commensuration:** scientific, Indigenous, community, historical, and observational evidence may coexist without being collapsed into one scale.
3. **Affected-nation authority:** Traditional Knowledge and sacred knowledge remain governed by the affected nation or lawful knowledge holder.
4. **Purpose limitation:** data collected for restoration, health, education, or community stewardship does not silently become enforcement, immigration, commercial, or intelligence data.
5. **Distribution before average:** aggregated indicators must disclose who, what, and where bears the harm.
6. **Low-tech parity:** paper, radio, oral, community-mapped, and offline routes remain valid where appropriately governed.
7. **Correction propagation:** an error is not corrected until known downstream uses are updated or stopped.
8. **Anti-Goodhart design:** targets and rewards must not convert proxies into the objective or punish actors for external harms.
9. **Non-activation:** pilots test methods, not rights, sanctions, title changes, or emergency powers.
10. **Reversible method governance:** methods can be challenged, suspended, replaced, and archived without erasing historical traceability.

# 4. Institutional architecture

## 4.1 Ecological Intelligence Commons

The **Ecological Intelligence Commons (EIC)** is a distributed technical network rather than a global ecological authority. It maintains open methods, technical registries, calibration practices, peer review, training, and interoperability. It does not command local observation programmes or own contributed data by default.

## 4.2 Local and bioregional observation nodes

Observation nodes may be hosted by Indigenous nations, public agencies, universities, community organizations, BAZ institutions, restoration programmes, or other lawful custodians. Each node retains its own constituting authority, labour duties, safety obligations, and data governance.

## 4.3 Methods and Replication Panel

A rotating, conflict-screened panel reviews indicator and model specifications, replication, calibration, bias, uncertainty, and version transitions. It may approve technical publication or require correction. It cannot approve legal use.

## 4.4 Community and affected-nation review

Communities may validate local interpretation, identify missing variables, contest representation, and restrict knowledge use. Participation is not consent to every downstream use. Global representation quotas cannot substitute for the authority of a specific affected nation.

## 4.5 Public, affected, review, operational, protected, sacred, and embargoed layers

EIOI adopts the seven CERGTA access classes. Public transparency never requires disclosure of endangered-species locations, sacred sites, personal health data, privileged material, protected sources, or Indigenous-restricted knowledge.

# 5. Observation and provenance

Every observation programme SHALL define purpose, variables, scale, sampling, equipment, environmental and worker safety, community roles, foreseeable downstream uses, correction, review, and closure. Citizen science has evidentiary value when its provenance and limitations are recorded; it does not gain or lose validity merely because the observer is or is not a credentialed scientist.

Sampling that requires compulsory entry, seizure, evidence preservation, or regulated inspection is outside EIOI and requires the appropriate CERGTA or SCPA route.

---

# 6. Data sovereignty and protected knowledge

Indigenous nations and other lawful knowledge holders determine whether knowledge may be collected, translated, aggregated, published, modelled, licensed, retained, or transferred. Non-participation must not be coded as missing commitment, poor stewardship, or scientific obstruction.

A public dashboard SHALL use abstraction, delay, range, masking, or omission where exact disclosure would create ecological, cultural, personal, or security harm.

---

# 7. Indicators, indices, thresholds, and models

## 7.1 Specification before use

No indicator or model may be used beyond exploratory work until `EIOI-07`, `EIOI-08`, or `EIOI-09` identifies its purpose, variables, baseline, scale, uncertainty, validation domain, missing-data rule, version, owner, audit, and prohibited inferences.

## 7.2 Thresholds

A threshold may trigger attention, replication, consultation, or referral. It does not automatically create a veto, emergency, sanction, funding change, guardian action, title restriction, permit decision, or legal conclusion.

## 7.3 Composite indices

A composite index must publish its modules and distributional effects. Improvement in one module cannot erase breach of an essential minimum or severe concentrated harm. The composite is a navigation aid, not a unitary truth.

## 7.4 Artificial intelligence and digital twins

AI may assist pattern detection, interpolation, scenario analysis, and anomaly detection. A human accountable owner must review each consequential handoff. Trade secrecy cannot defeat independent validation where public or rights-affecting use is proposed.

# 8. Interpretation, causality, and uncertainty

EIOI separates measured change from causal attribution. Every causal assessment records alternative explanations, confounders, lag, upstream and downstream effects, external drivers, no-fault conditions, and dissent.

A correlation, risk score, anomaly, or model forecast may support further review. It cannot alone establish liability, stewardship failure, ecocide, fraud, eligibility, personhood, dangerousness, or legal causation.

---

# 9. Alerts and review referrals

An ecological alert SHALL state:

- observed condition and source;
- affected entity and provisional boundary;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- plausible harms and affected groups;
- alternative interpretations;
- recommended recipient and review route;
- expiry and update schedule;
- protected information;
- the actions the alert does **not** authorize.

Recipients must expressly accept, reject, or request correction. Silence is not acceptance.

---

# 10. Rights-review boundary

EIOI may support a rights-review petition by packaging ecological identity evidence, boundary evidence, ecological function, threats, restoration needs, Indigenous and community contributions, uncertainty, and dissent. The package must state that legal standing, rights recognition, guardian appointment, rights priority, remedies, and tribunal jurisdiction remain undecided.

The former “Rights Hand-Off Protocol” is retired. It is replaced by `EIOI-14` plus CERGTA-11 through CERGTA-19.

---

# 11. Rewards, certification, and financial firewalls

The former automatic Data-to-Reward Pipeline is retired. EIOI may measure activity or ecological effect and transmit a bounded evidence package. AUBI or another competent authority separately decides eligibility, valuation, payment, correction, clawback, hardship, and appeal.

No person loses material security because an ecological score changes. No community is penalized for upstream pollution, climate shocks, historical damage, missing technology, or refusal to disclose protected knowledge.

---

# 12. Planetary Health module handoff

The ecological module sent to the Planetary Health Synthesis Office includes:

- method and data versions;
- disaggregated observations and indicator results;
- affected territories and nations;
- uncertainty and dissent;
- non-compensation and essential-minimum flags;
- known gaps and excluded knowledge;
- correction status;
- permitted purposes and expiry.

PHG may synthesize but cannot rewrite the ecological source record.

# 13. Interfaces with sectoral frameworks

- **Biodiversity:** EIOI supplies evidence and candidate methods; Biodiversity decides implementation only through its lawful CERGTA routes.
- **Water:** drought, quality, and scarcity indicators may trigger review, not allocation or shutoff.
- **Oceans:** stock and vessel observations may support lawful quota or inspection processes; EIOI does not blacklist or deny port access.
- **Soil:** soil and microbiome metrics inform stewardship and restoration; they do not create land-sale vetoes or tenure consequences.
- **Hearthstone:** ecological performance evidence cannot automatically revoke stewardship or transfer title.
- **Justice and Shield:** evidence handoffs preserve provenance and access restrictions; EIOI does not open investigations or command enforcement.
- **Emergency stack:** ecological alerts may link to CDEE/ECRC; urgency does not create authority.

# 17. Constitutional readiness gates

1. Exact source versions and hashes are pinned.
2. The layer states that it is a technical and advisory system, not a rights, title, guardian, funding, emergency, or enforcement authority.
3. Every observation has purpose, method, provenance, spatial and temporal scope, and correction history.
4. Every Indigenous or sacred contribution records the affected nation or knowledge holder, use permission, non-disclosure, benefit-sharing, and withdrawal conditions.
5. Every indicator and index has a published specification, version, weighting, missing-data rule, uncertainty, and prohibited inference.
6. Composite indicators are accompanied by disaggregated distributions and cannot substitute for the underlying evidence.
7. Model outputs identify calibration domain, known failure modes, accountable human owner, and suspension trigger.
8. Observation, interpretation, causal assessment, and alert are separate records.
9. An alert has confidence, expiry, recommended review, and an explicit no-authority statement.
10. A rights-review handoff uses CERGTA-11/12 and cannot become CERGTA-13 by receipt or silence.
11. The layer cannot appoint, instruct, or remove guardians.
12. The layer cannot establish a legal boundary, title, tenure, trust, covenant, sacred designation, or inalienability status.
13. Reward evidence is separate from reward eligibility, rate, payment, clawback, and appeal.
14. Product or certification evidence is separate from tariffs, blacklists, port denial, or market restriction.
15. Scientific inquiry is distinguished from legal investigation and compulsory inspection.
16. Urgent ecological evidence is routed to the competent emergency or CERGTA authority without activating it.
17. Affected communities receive accessible notice and can challenge data, interpretation, and representation.
18. Traditional Knowledge and protected ecological locations are not placed in open dashboards by default.
19. Every correction propagates to known legal, financial, certification, and public recipients.
