## The diagnosis

The absences aren't random. Grep across all 75 framework documents returns **zero** hits for hot spot, repeat victimisation, desistance, recidivism, focused deterrence, routine activity, procedural justice, clearance rate, criminogenic, or credible messenger. "Retaliation" appears in 37 files and is *always* whistleblower anti-retaliation — never violence retaliation cycles. "Expungement" appears eleven times and is always correction of *wrongful* action, never record relief for lawful convictions.

What that means structurally: **GGF's crime-relevant leverage is bimodal in latency.** It is thorough at the generational timescale (AUBI, Hearthlight, Housing) and thorough at the constitutional-procedural layer (Justice Systems, SCPA, Shield). The **days-to-months band is empty** — exactly the band where measurable crime prevention actually happens, and exactly the latency gap I flagged in ChatGPT's table.

The second structural issue is variety-matching. Every crime-relevant GGF instrument is universalist by design. Crime is one of the most spatially and personally concentrated phenomena in social life. A stack with only uniform instruments cannot express "5% of places generate 50% of harm" — a straightforward Ashby problem, and one your own GaE papers give you the vocabulary for.

## Why I'd argue against a new framework

A "Crime Prevention Framework" would (a) duplicate the root-cause layer already covered by eight documents, and (b) collide with the carefully bounded coercive-authority edges in Kintsugi, Justice Systems, and Shield. It would either have to claim coercive authority — breaking the SCPA architecture — or have none, in which case it's a coordination document, which is Meta-Governance's job. The gaps are patches, not a document.

## Recommended patches

**1. Reintegration and desistance — highest value.** Nothing in the corpus addresses release from custody. Given that SSI removal at 18 raises criminal charges 20% over two decades and income-motivated charges 60%, the post-release moment is the single sharpest observable transition in the whole causal system, and GGF is silent on it. Needs: housing secured before release (Housing §9.4 already has Housing First presumptive status — extend the trigger), record relief for lawful convictions distinct from wrongful-action expungement, and employment access naming justice-involved people as a population in Work in Liberation. **Placement decision:** this belongs in Kintsugi if you frame it as care-and-restoration, or in Justice Systems if you frame it as remedy and record. My read is Kintsugi — Justice Systems is already at 1,700 lines and the substance is voluntary support, not adjudication.

**2. AUBI §5.1 — non-withdrawal constraint.** Layer 1 is unconditional at entry, but there is no rule governing *reduction* or *exit*. The evidence says harm concentrates in removal, not absence. Add a reduction-shock constraint: baseline reductions require notice, phase-out rather than cliff, stated reason, and appeal. This is a small addition that captures the strongest empirical finding in the literature and fits the existing §5.1 safeguards list.

**3. CIS observation section — generalise Kintsugi §21.2.** Kintsugi already excludes reductions in reported harm that may reflect under-reporting from permissible indicators. That rule is correct and siloed. It generalises: *an indicator whose value depends on reporting propensity is not a valid outcome measure for an intervention that changes reporting propensity.* This belongs in CIS observation rules, where every framework using safety, harm, trust, or complaint indicators inherits it. Cheapest patch, widest effect.

**4. Kintsugi — concentration acknowledgment.** A short provision permitting place- and person-differentiated *intensity* of non-coercive provision, with an explicit bar on person-level prediction (which §21.2 already prohibits) and on treating concentration as grounds for coercive escalation. Without this, the framework's universalism is load-bearing in a way the evidence doesn't support.

**5. AUBI §5.1 — payment cadence.** Minor. "Monthly" appears once in the corpus, inside an India worked example. Regular-small-over-infrequent-large should be a stated design parameter, not an implicit one.

I'd skip procedural justice and certainty-over-severity as explicit additions — they're implicit in Justice Systems' due-process architecture and adding them risks the kind of narrative expansion without operational depth you've been guarding against.
