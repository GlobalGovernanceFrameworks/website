## 0. Controlled successor notice

Oceans v2.3 preserves the source framework’s animating vision: the ocean as the Blue Heart of the Planet; Indigenous and coastal stewardship; legal rights for marine ecosystems; precaution toward deep-sea mining, geoengineering, and biotechnology; just transition for fishers; regenerative finance; quieter shipping; marine restoration; and real consequences for destructive practices.

It replaces the v2.2 assumption that an MGCC vote, BAZ designation, data-twin alert, acoustic log, ISA permit, blacklist tier, future date, coalition, or ecological threshold can move directly into quota, tariff, port denial, licence revocation, seizure, emergency deployment, or punishment.

> **Marine urgency does not create marine jurisdiction.**

> **Observation is not attribution. A model is not a quota. A quota is not a licence. A tariff is not a blacklist. A listing is not port denial. Port denial is not seizure. Personhood is not title. Precaution is not permanent prohibition. Emergency need is not command.**

The framework may coordinate, observe, advise, develop model standards, support affected Indigenous nations and coastal communities, facilitate restoration and just transition, administer programmes or funds where separately chartered, and prepare lawful records. It cannot create a global ocean sovereign, grant personhood, authenticate sacredness, issue universal fishing licences or port orders, adjudicate guilt, seize vessels, deny humanitarian assistance, declare emergencies, or command force.

## 2. Vision and constitutional purpose

The ocean is the Blue Heart of the Planet: a living, dynamic system that circulates heat and nutrients, supports food and livelihoods, regulates climate, shelters extraordinary forms of life, carries cultures and histories, and connects territories beyond political borders. Marine governance must therefore operate at several scales without pretending that ecological connection creates one global political jurisdiction.

The constitutional purpose of v2.3 is to connect seven functions while preventing their merger:

1. **ecological and navigational knowledge** from EIOI, affected nations, coastal communities, science, workers, vessels, ports, and lawful monitoring;
2. **marine rights and guardianship** through CERGTA and competent legal authorities;
3. **coastal, high-seas, and transboundary coordination** through voluntary or lawfully adopted standards and agreements;
4. **fisheries, routes, licences, and port services** through competent jurisdiction-specific authorities;
5. **regenerative finance and just transition** through separately authorized financial and labour institutions;
6. **precaution and emergency response** through reviewable legal and CDEE/ECRC routes;
7. **accountability and remedy** through independent investigation, adjudication, restoration, appeal, and—where required—SCPA-governed execution.

## 3. Core principles

### 3.1 The Blue Heart as relationship, not jurisdiction

The Blue Heart expresses ecological interdependence and moral responsibility. It does not create a planetary sovereign or erase Indigenous, coastal, port, flag-state, national, regional, high-seas, and treaty jurisdictions.

### 3.2 Affected-nation authority

The specific affected Indigenous nation or nations determine their representatives, law, marine tenure, relational authority, consent requirements, protected knowledge, sacred sites and routes, and whether to engage. Global Indigenous representation strengthens deliberation but cannot provide FPIC for another people.

### 3.3 Rights of nature through lawful recognition

Marine ecosystems may receive legal rights and standing where competent law provides. MGCC, BAZs, the Blue Heart Cultural Council, and data systems may support petitions and evidence but cannot create personhood by resolution, timeline, dashboard, or threshold.

### 3.4 Observation–consequence separation

AIS, acoustic logs, catch records, satellite imagery, genetic data, digital twins, citizen reports, Indigenous knowledge, and AI classifications may trigger review. They do not themselves create quotas, tariffs, licences, listings, port restrictions, guilt, emergency powers, or financial consequences.

### 3.5 Precaution with bounded authority

Precaution may justify research controls, enhanced monitoring, interim non-prejudice, or a recommendation for a moratorium. It does not erase jurisdiction, participation, reversibility, proportionality, review, expiry, humanitarian duties, or remedy.

### 3.6 Safety of life and crew dignity

Commercial accountability never suspends safety-of-life-at-sea, rescue, urgent medical care, crew food and water, sanitation, wages, repatriation, pollution containment, or safe-harbour obligations. Crew are not collective proxies for owners, operators, charterers, cargo interests, or flag states.

### 3.7 Just transition before restriction

No destructive-practice ban, quota reduction, MPA expansion, route restriction, port measure, or vessel transition is constitutionally complete without affected-worker and community analysis, bargaining, income and debt support, alternatives, sequencing, review, and remedy.

### 3.8 Subsidiarity with transboundary coordination

Marine action should occur at the lowest competent level, while recognizing that migratory species, currents, pollution, fisheries, shipping, and high-seas activities cross boundaries. Coordination does not create sovereignty; every actor retains only lawfully delegated power.

### 3.9 Open methods, protected knowledge

Public standards and non-sensitive methods should be open and accessible. Traditional, sacred, personal, crew, genetic, commercial, security-sensitive, and investigatory information remains purpose-limited and protected.

### 3.10 Every authority ends

Every standard, quota, licence, tariff, listing, port restriction, emergency mission, guardianship, fund, and programme has review, expiry, termination, correction, data-disposition, succession, and remedy.

## 4. Governance architecture

### 4.1 Meta-Governance Coordination Council

The MGCC is retained as a **non-sovereign coordination, deliberation, and model-standard institution**. It does not govern the ocean, own the high seas, replace UNCLOS institutions, or acquire port, flag-state, licensing, inspection, emergency, or enforcement power.

Its possible functions include:

- develop model standards and recommendations;
- coordinate voluntary and lawfully adopted regional programmes;
- publish the Annual State of the Blue Heart Report;
- facilitate peer learning and transboundary dialogue;
- maintain public method and adoption registries;
- support rights petitions and precautionary reviews without deciding them;
- liaise with existing marine bodies and agreements;
- commission transparent programme evaluation.

It may not:

- make a standard binding through Stewardship Assembly approval alone;
- treat State Coordinating Assembly endorsement as sufficient without applicable legal adoption;
- issue quotas, licences, tariffs, port denials, listings, or sanctions;
- declare an emergency or command a response;
- investigate, adjudicate, seize, detain, or use force;
- provide Indigenous consent or sacred-site authority.

#### 4.1.1 Dual-assembly model

The Stewardship Assembly retains Indigenous, scientific, youth, civil-society, worker, fisher, disability, animal-welfare, and affected-region participation. The State Coordinating Assembly manages governmental and treaty interfaces. Their votes determine MGCC recommendations and institutional positions only.

A two-thirds vote, coalition size, or future date does not activate binding power. A recommendation becomes binding only through the legal adoption state recorded in `OCE-28`.

#### 4.1.2 Functional separation

Normative deliberation, scientific review, affected-nation liaison, finance, procurement, complaints, investigation support, adjudication referral, and public communication must be structurally separated. Conflicts and recusals are recorded.

### 4.2 Coastal BAZs and port authorities

A Coastal BAZ is not presumed to possess port, EEZ, fishery, MPA, licensing, inspection, taxation, or enforcement powers. `OCE-11` identifies the host jurisdiction and each exact delegated power.

A BAZ may support monitoring, stewardship, restoration, community participation, low-tech access, and local planning without coercive authority. Port-state, licensing, inspection, and restriction functions require valid law and institutional separation from programme, fund, guardian, and adjudicative roles.

### 4.3 Blue Heart Cultural Council

The Council is an advisory and knowledge-custody body. It may support affected nations, protect restricted knowledge, document cultural routes with permission, and advise on spiritual and cultural dimensions.

It cannot:

- authenticate sacredness for an affected nation;
- provide FPIC;
- publish protected knowledge without authority;
- create a protected area, shipping restriction, veto, title, or legal standing;
- issue a permit or command enforcement.

An “advisory veto” is reclassified as a right to publish a reasoned objection, require reconsideration, and trigger independent review where law provides. It is not an automatic legal prohibition.

### 4.4 Global Marine Data Commons

The Digital Ocean Twin and related systems are technical services under `OCE-13`. They may collect and integrate lawfully available data, publish methods and uncertainty, and generate advisory alerts.

They cannot automatically:

- set or adjust quotas;
- create a migration corridor;
- calculate a binding tariff;
- blacklist a vessel;
- establish tampering, illegal fishing, pollution, or guilt;
- deny port access;
- disclose protected Traditional Knowledge or personal crew data.

Low-tech reports, radio, paper logs, community observations, and non-digital evidence remain valid. A digital outage or refusal of unnecessary tracking cannot eliminate participation or legal protection.

### 4.5 External authorities

Rights recognition, guardianship appointment, port-state control, fishery licensing, taxation, emergency command, adjudication, and coercive execution remain with separately competent institutions. The framework prepares records and interfaces; it does not absorb those powers.

## 5. Constitutional operating chain

The default sequence is:

1. `OCE-02` marine entity, route, port, habitat, or programme intake;
2. `OCE-03` observation and provenance;
3. `OCE-04` data and knowledge controls;
4. `OCE-05` method, threshold, or model specification;
5. `OCE-06` interpretation, alternatives, uncertainty, and dissent;
6. `OCE-07` affected-interest mapping;
7. `OCE-08` affected-nation authority;
8. `OCE-09` participation and support;
9. the appropriate rights, MPA, quota, licence, tariff, listing, port, finance, precaution, emergency, or justice route;
10. a separate competent decision;
11. implementation under its own clock and mandate;
12. review, correction, remedy, termination, and data disposition.

Completion of one stage never implies the next.

## 6. Indigenous and coastal authority

### 6.1 Marine tenure and relational jurisdiction

Affected Indigenous nations may hold legal, customary, spiritual, stewardship, subsistence, navigational, and relational authority that does not fit state property categories. `OCE-08` records these authorities without forcing them into a generic BAZ or MGCC form.

### 6.2 FPIC and non-engagement

Where required, consent follows the affected nation’s own processes and applicable law. Refusal, withdrawal, or non-engagement carries no penalty and cannot justify loss of coastal protection, technical support, grants, standing, services, or remedy.

### 6.3 Traditional Knowledge and sacred routes

The affected nation controls use, attribution, benefit sharing, derivatives, publication, retention, return, and deletion. Cultural navigation knowledge may inform a route only within the permitted purpose. It cannot be silently encoded into an open digital twin or used for surveillance.

### 6.4 Coastal communities, fishers, and internal plurality

Coastal communities and fishing economies are not homogeneous. The framework records small-scale, subsistence, commercial, cooperative, seasonal, migrant, women-led, youth, processing, port, and vessel-worker interests. Participation cannot be satisfied only by inviting a recognized industry association or a single community representative.

## 7. Marine ecological rights and guardianship

### 7.1 Rights petition and recommendation

`OCE-14` and `OCE-15` support a lawful review. They identify the proposed entity, rights, jurisdiction, ecological and cultural evidence, affected interests, boundaries, alternatives, dissent, and requested relief.

### 7.2 Recognition

Only a competent authority creates `CERGTA-13`. MGCC, BAZs, a cultural council, a target, or a timeline cannot grant personhood. `OCE-16` records the external decision and its exact effects.

### 7.3 Rights catalogue and boundary

`OCE-17` and `OCE-18` state the exact rights, conflicts, non-derogable duties, amendment process, and dynamic ecological and legal boundary. Marine rights do not automatically transfer seabed, coastal land, ports, vessels, infrastructure, concessions, licences, fishery shares, or mineral title.

### 7.4 Guardians

`OCE-19` through `OCE-21` separate nomination, appointment, mandate, fiduciary duties, affected-nation instructions, conflicts, complaint, removal, liability, and succession. A guardian represents an ecosystem; it does not own it or become a regulator, port authority, prosecutor, or fleet commander.

### 7.5 Cascading jurisdictional pathway

Local, national, regional, and treaty precedents may inform one another. The v2.2 dates—local by 2030, national by 2038, and high-seas by 2045—remain programme aspirations. They do not self-activate personhood or jurisdiction.

## 8. Marine protected areas, corridors, and restoration

### 8.1 Proposal and claims map

An MPA, no-take zone, slow-steaming corridor, sacred-site protection, or habitat-restoration proposal begins with `OCE-23`. It maps Indigenous and customary rights, food systems, fishers, workers, tourism, shipping, safety, ports, tenure, debt, species, animal welfare, and transboundary effects.

### 8.2 Lawful designation

`OCE-24` records the competent decision. It identifies the exact boundary, allowed and prohibited uses, customary and Indigenous rights, permits, humanitarian and safety exceptions, compensation, review, amendment, expiry, and challenge.

A target such as thirty percent MPA coverage cannot itself designate an area.

### 8.3 Restoration

`OCE-25` and `OCE-26` govern stewardship and restoration. Plans address methods, alternatives, non-target species, individual-animal welfare, workers, fishers, food systems, budget, maintenance, stopping rules, and benefit distribution.

### 8.4 Performance and external shocks

`OCE-27` separates stewardship failure from warming, acidification, storm damage, upstream pollution, war, market shock, inherited degradation, and data error. A poor score does not automatically remove a steward, freeze finance, or alter a legal designation.

## 9. Fisheries, quotas, licences, and dynamic zones

### 9.1 Quota method

`OCE-29` records biological reference points, stock uncertainty, multispecies interactions, climate shifts, bycatch, ecosystem needs, Indigenous and customary rights, small-scale and subsistence uses, food security, livelihood distribution, and model sensitivity.

A model recommendation is advisory. It is not a quota.

### 9.2 Quota and licence decisions

`OCE-30` is the competent legal decision. It identifies the holder, area, species, gear, amount, season, transferability, bycatch conditions, reporting, review, expiry, correction, and appeal.

A stock decline cannot silently convert a valid licence into an offence. Interim restrictions require lawful authority and urgent or ordinary review.

### 9.3 Dynamic no-take zones and migratory corridors

`OCE-31` records a competent time-limited route or zone decision. AI may recommend changes but cannot activate, expand, renew, or enforce them. Navigation safety, rescue, weather diversion, disability and medical needs, Indigenous routes, and alternative routes are considered.

### 9.4 Destructive-practice restrictions

Bottom-trawling and other restrictions remain strong policy goals. Their legal force depends on `OCE-28` adoption and, where relevant, `OCE-24`, `OCE-30`, or `OCE-38`. No ban activates without a corresponding worker and community transition route where livelihoods are materially affected.

## 10. Fishers to Guardians and marine-worker transition

The source **Fishers to Guardians** programme is retained and strengthened through `OCE-42`.

Before a restriction materially reduces livelihood, the competent transition route addresses:

- affected fishers, crew, processors, repair workers, port workers, families, and communities;
- income bridge and AUBI coordination;
- vessel debt, licences, shares, pensions, and insurance;
- collective bargaining and cooperative options;
- retraining in monitoring, restoration, sustainable aquaculture, tourism, vessel recycling, and other locally chosen work;
- food security and local access;
- community ownership and benefit distribution;
- safe refusal and non-retaliation;
- duration, review, correction, and remedy.

Retraining cannot be used to extinguish customary fishing rights or force cultural assimilation into a framework-created occupation.

## 11. Acoustic governance and port fees

### 11.1 Evidence and method

Acoustic logs and route data enter `OCE-03`. The threshold, measurement, verification, noise model, corridor relationship, calibration, and uncertainty enter `OCE-05` and `OCE-06`.

### 11.2 Tariff decision

`OCE-32` separates:

1. the acoustic method;
2. the applicable legal tariff authority;
3. the rate decision;
4. the vessel and liable actor;
5. discount or premium eligibility;
6. invoice and collection;
7. correction and appeal;
8. expiry and review;
9. revenue custody and permitted use.

The source 500 percent noisy-vessel premium remains an illustrative policy option, not a universal rate. A disputed or unpaid invoice is not a blacklist or port-denial order.

### 11.3 Credits

Whale-safe credits and Blue Leaves require separate ecological verification and financial eligibility. A positive score is not a subsidy order; a negative score is not a penalty or evidence of illegal conduct.

## 12. High seas and lawful port-state measures

The v2.2 term **Absolute Port State Control** is retired. v2.3 uses **lawful participating port-state measures**.

Port leverage can be powerful and legitimate, but only where the participating port has authority and preserves international, constitutional, humanitarian, labour, and due-process obligations.

### 12.1 Listing is not restriction

`OCE-36` opens a listing proposal. `OCE-37` records a competent decision, exact listed vessel or legal person, reasons, scope, duration, publication, correction, appeal, and delisting.

No actor is listed automatically because:

- a data system flags tampering;
- an ISA permit exists;
- an acoustic threshold is exceeded;
- a vessel is associated with a flag state or corporate group;
- an allegation is published;
- a prior listing exists in another system.

### 12.2 Actor identity

The vessel, registered owner, beneficial owner, operator, charterer, cargo interest, master, crew, insurer, financier, and flag state are separately identified. Responsibility is not inferred merely from contractual or national association.

### 12.3 Port restriction

`OCE-38` identifies the service restricted—commercial docking, cargo handling, refuelling, repair, provisioning, procurement, or another service—and records jurisdiction, proportionality, alternatives, review, expiry, and restoration.

A port that does not apply an MGCC recommendation does not automatically lose BAZ status or Ethical Trade Zone access. Any such consequence requires its own lawful and contractual process.

### 12.4 Humanitarian and crew exceptions

`OCE-39` protects:

- distress entry and safety of life at sea;
- rescue and urgent medical treatment;
- food, drinking water, sanitation, shelter, and essential medicines;
- crew change, wages, communication, legal assistance, and repatriation;
- pollution containment and environmental emergency action;
- safe harbour where necessary to avoid greater harm;
- persons trafficked, exploited, abandoned, or otherwise vulnerable.

Commercial accountability cannot turn a port into a place of collective punishment or unlawful detention.

### 12.5 Impoundment and seizure

Port denial, inspection, detention, impoundment, cargo hold, seizure, arrest, restraint, and force are separate acts. `OCE-40`, `OCE-47`, CERGTA-48, and SCPA govern any coercive execution.

## 13. Deep-sea mining and permit non-recognition

### 13.1 Precautionary policy

The framework retains a strong precautionary position against commercial deep-sea mining and supports a long-term independent scientific assessment before exploitation.

### 13.2 Non-recognition determination

`OCE-43` allows a competent jurisdiction to record whether and how it recognizes an ISA or other permit within its own legal order. The framework itself cannot universally invalidate an external permit.

A non-recognition decision does not itself:

- prove illegal conduct;
- list a corporation or vessel;
- deny port access;
- seize cargo;
- cancel insurance or finance;
- create criminal liability.

Each consequence requires its own lawful record.

### 13.3 Transition and reliance interests

A lawful process may consider workers, affected states and communities, investment reliance, scientific uncertainty, technology claims, restoration impossibility, alternatives, compensation rules, and transition. “Zero discharge,” “full restoration,” or “community consent” cannot function as an automatic exemption without a competent permit process and affected-nation authority.

## 14. Marine biotechnology, geoengineering, and emerging risk

`OCE-44` provides a staged precautionary review covering:

- technical feasibility and uncertainty;
- ecological and multispecies effects;
- irreversibility and transboundary spread;
- affected-nation and community authority;
- animal welfare;
- cultural and sacred relationships;
- worker and public-health effects;
- alternatives and non-deployment options;
- research scale, containment, stopping conditions, liability, monitoring, and restoration;
- dissent and missing knowledge.

MGCC and the Blue Heart Cultural Council may recommend conditions or a moratorium. Binding prohibitions, permits, research controls, inspections, and enforcement require competent law.

A five-year review does not automatically renew a coercive restriction unless the governing law expressly and constitutionally provides that effect. Silence never expands authority.

## 15. Regenerative blue economy and finance

### 15.1 Ocean Impact Bonds

`OCE-41` separates ecological objectives, public authority, investor terms, risk allocation, community and affected-nation authority, procurement, verifier independence, default, audit, exit, and remedy.

A marine ecosystem’s rights, sacred status, future restoration, or community tenure cannot be collateral.

### 15.2 Grants, credits, Hearts, and Leaves

Ecological contribution is verified separately from eligibility, amount, payment, withholding, overpayment, fraud review, and appeal. Reporting suspected illegal fishing may qualify for separately authorized support, but payment does not make the allegation credible or establish guilt.

### 15.3 Marine plastic accountability

Ports and coastal authorities may adopt lawful vessel-waste, fishing-gear, and producer-responsibility rules. Observation, inspection, fee, levy, restriction, and adjudication remain distinct. Revenue use requires transparent custody, community participation, audit, and correction.

### 15.4 Polluter pays

Restoration bonds, levies, reparations, and compensation require a competent financial or adjudicative decision. A pollution model or public allegation cannot create debt by itself.

## 16. Monitoring, inspection, investigation, and evidence

### 16.1 Monitoring

Routine ecological and navigational monitoring follows `OCE-03` through `OCE-06`. Open marine data must not expose protected knowledge, personal crew data, vulnerable routes, or active investigatory material.

### 16.2 Port inspection

`OCE-33` records the exact inspection authority, vessel, purpose, scope, safety, documents, samples, crew rights, report, and closure. Routine inspection cannot silently become an ecological, criminal, immigration, intelligence, or labour investigation.

### 16.3 Investigation

`OCE-34` independently opens an investigation. It identifies legal basis, allegation, parties, methods, protected sources, exculpatory duties, clocks, and closure.

### 16.4 Evidence and attribution

`OCE-35` distinguishes:

- observation from legal evidence;
- vessel identity from corporate identity;
- correlation from causation;
- model output from admissible attribution;
- owner, operator, charterer, cargo, master, crew, and flag-state responsibility;
- missing data from adverse inference;
- corrected data from immutable historical error.

## 17. Urgent protection and marine emergencies

### 17.1 Urgent ecological protection

Where imminent irreversible harm is plausibly established, `OCE-45` may request CERGTA-42. The authorization states the exact act, authority, entity, alternatives, affected people and workers, humanitarian duties, duration, review, expiry, restoration, and remedy.

A bleaching percentage, spill alert, or model threshold indicates urgency. It does not create jurisdiction.

### 17.2 Marine Emergency Assistance Missions

`OCE-46` defines optional civilian missions for:

- oil and chemical spill containment support;
- wildlife rescue;
- coral and habitat stabilization;
- navigation and public-safety information;
- pollution monitoring and sampling under authority;
- restoration planning;
- logistics and community assistance.

Missions operate only after acceptance by a competent CDEE/ECRC command. They cannot independently board, search, seize, detain, use force, gather intelligence, impose port measures, or release funds.

### 17.3 Fishing conflict

MGCC or a Peace interface may facilitate dialogue. Maritime safety, law enforcement, navigation control, boarding, restraint, custody, or force remain with competent authorities under SCPA where applicable.

### 17.4 Emergency finance

No automatic `$5 million–$20 million` release occurs. Emergency finance requires an identified authority, amount, purpose, custodian, procurement route, audit, expiry, correction, and restitution.

## 18. Adjudication, restoration, and enforcement

### 18.1 Independent adjudication

`OCE-47` transfers a bounded case to a competent justice institution. The recipient independently decides jurisdiction, applicable law, standing, evidence, interim relief, judgment, restoration, compensation, penalty, appeal, and enforcement.

### 18.2 Restoration and compensation

`OCE-48` records ecological restoration, polluter-pays obligations, community and worker compensation, verification, requalification, correction propagation, and completion. Restoration cannot be reduced to a financial payment where ecological repair remains possible and legally required.

### 18.3 Execution

A listing, port decision, administrative order, or judgment does not execute itself. Search, seizure, detention, impoundment, restraint, custody, force, cyber action, border action, and asset freezing require `OCE-40`, CERGTA-48, and SCPA.

## 19. Adoption, implementation, and scaling

### 19.1 Adoption states

Every mechanism is labelled:

- `proposed`;
- `advisory`;
- `voluntarily_adopted`;
- `contractually_binding`;
- `locally_enacted`;
- `nationally_enacted`;
- `treaty_active`;
- `suspended`;
- `expired`;
- `terminated`.

### 19.2 Phase 1: Secure the Heart

Early work may include voluntary monitoring, affected-nation partnerships, restoration, fisher transition design, model standards, rights petitions, low-tech participation, and locally authorized pilots. No port, quota, tariff, inspection, or enforcement power arises from programme launch.

### 19.3 Phase 2: Scale and transform

MPAs, acoustic programmes, transition finance, fishing reforms, port measures, and precautionary rules scale only through demonstrated legitimacy, ecological benefit, affected-nation authority, workforce protection, institutional capacity, correction, and remedy.

### 19.4 Phase 3: Full integration

High-seas cooperation, rights recognition, decarbonized shipping, zero-waste fisheries, and cultural-knowledge integration remain goals. “Full integration” does not mean one central authority; it means interoperable lawful institutions with visible boundaries.

### 19.5 Non-activation

The following never activate power by themselves:

- a date or implementation phase;
- thirty percent MPA coverage;
- ten personhood cases;
- one hundred percent seabed-protection target;
- a coalition of states or BAZs;
- pilot success;
- an MGCC supermajority;
- an AI threshold;
- an ISA permit or non-recognition declaration.

## 20. What success looks like under v2.3

### 20.1 A reef gains standing

Community and affected-nation petitioners submit `OCE-14`; evidence and dissent enter `OCE-15`; the competent jurisdiction recognizes exact rights; boundary and guardian are separately constituted. Shipping and coastal title do not silently transfer.

### 20.2 A mining vessel seeks port service

The port determines its own legal position on the permit through `OCE-43`. A proposed listing receives notice and review. Any commercial restriction is individualized and time limited. Distress, medical care, crew welfare, repatriation, and pollution-control duties remain protected.

### 20.3 A fisher faces a quota reduction

The stock method and uncertainty are visible. The allocation decision considers Indigenous and customary rights, small-scale access, food security, bycatch, distribution, and appeal. Income, debt, retraining, and cooperative options begin before material livelihood loss.

### 20.4 A quiet ship receives an incentive

Acoustic evidence is verified under a published method. A competent authority independently determines the tariff discount or financial credit. The data system does not pay or punish by itself.

### 20.5 An oil spill triggers rapid action

An alert is validated, an emergency event is opened under CDEE/ECRC, competent command accepts a civilian assistance mission, finance is separately authorized, and evidence for later liability remains protected from operational contamination.

### 20.6 A sacred marine route remains protected

The affected nation controls the knowledge and determines what may be disclosed. The Cultural Council supports the requested legal and technical route but does not reveal the route or claim authority to authenticate it.

## 24. Constitutional readiness gates

| Gate | Title | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| G01 | Exact marine authority | Every consequential act identifies the constituting instrument, exact port/coastal jurisdiction, delegated power, adoption state, expiry, and remedy. |
| G02 | No global ocean sovereign | MGCC, BAZ networks, cultural councils, and data trusts cannot become a universal ocean government by framework declaration. |
| G03 | Affected-nation authority | Only the specific affected Indigenous nation can provide consent, define representatives, govern protected knowledge, and determine applicable Indigenous law. |
| G04 | Observation–consequence separation | Sensor, AIS, acoustic, catch, satellite, AI, citizen, and Indigenous evidence may trigger review but not rights, quotas, tariffs, listings, or sanctions. |
| G05 | Model transparency and human review | Quota, tariff, route, and risk models disclose methods, uncertainty, distributional effects, corrections, and human decision responsibility. |
| G06 | Rights recognition external | MGCC, BAZs, cultural councils, and data systems may support petitions but cannot grant personhood or standing. |
| G07 | Rights–title separation | Marine personhood does not transfer coast, seabed, port, infrastructure, concession, vessel, or resource title. |
| G08 | Guardianship bounded | Guardians represent lawfully recognized entities but do not own them or acquire regulatory, licensing, inspection, or enforcement power. |
| G09 | Sacredness not proxy-recognized | Global councils may support confidentiality and legal protection but cannot authenticate sacredness or waive affected-nation authority. |
| G10 | MPA due process | MPAs, no-take zones, and corridors require lawful boundaries, customary and Indigenous rights, food and livelihood analysis, notice, review, expiry, and remedy. |
| G11 | Quota method–decision separation | Scientific quota methods remain distinct from allocation, licence, catch-share, transfer, and enforcement decisions. |
| G12 | AI cannot adjust law | AI may recommend dynamic zones or quotas but cannot activate, expand, renew, or enforce them. |
| G13 | Fisher just transition first | Material income, debt, retraining, community ownership, and food-security pathways precede restrictive measures affecting livelihoods. |
| G14 | Tariff–listing separation | An acoustic log, tariff invoice, or disputed premium cannot automatically create a blacklist entry or port denial. |
| G15 | No automatic blacklisting | Tampering alerts, ISA permits, model scores, and allegations open review; listing requires notice, reasons, independent decision, duration, appeal, and delisting. |
| G16 | Actor identity resolution | Vessel, registered owner, beneficial owner, operator, charterer, cargo interest, master, crew, and flag state are not presumed to be one legal actor. |
| G17 | Port restriction proportionality | Port measures are individualized, lawful, proportionate, time limited, reviewable, and technically separable. |
| G18 | Humanitarian port exceptions | Distress, rescue, medical care, crew welfare and repatriation, food, water, sanitation, pollution containment, and safe harbour cannot be denied. |
| G19 | Inspection–investigation separation | Routine port inspection, ecological investigation, evidence custody, adjudication, and execution remain distinct. |
| G20 | Crew non-punishment | Crew and fishers are not collectively punished for owner, operator, flag-state, or cargo conduct without individualized evidence and lawful process. |
| G21 | ISA position non-self-executing | A non-recognition policy does not itself list an actor, deny port access, seize cargo, or establish guilt. |
| G22 | Moratorium adoption clarity | Deep-sea, biotech, or geoengineering moratoria distinguish scientific recommendation from enacted legal prohibition and research authorization. |
| G23 | No default-renewed coercion | Review cycles cannot renew bans, restrictions, permits, or emergency powers by silence where law requires affirmative action. |
| G24 | Finance bounded | Impact bonds, credits, grants, tariffs, and subsidies cannot collateralize ecosystem rights, sacred relations, crew welfare, or future restoration duties. |
| G25 | Reward–allegation separation | Hearts, Leaves, whistleblower support, and reporting rewards do not establish credibility, guilt, licence breach, or payment eligibility automatically. |
| G26 | Emergency command external | Oil-spill, bleaching, conflict, and wildlife response operate under CDEE/ECRC; MGCC and data systems do not command response. |
| G27 | No automatic emergency funds | Alerts and thresholds do not release funds, deploy teams, or authorize procurement without a competent financial and emergency decision. |
| G28 | Justice independence | Marine bodies may refer cases, but independent justice institutions decide jurisdiction, law, evidence, guilt, remedy, and appeal. |
| G29 | SCPA execution boundary | Boarding beyond inspection authority, search, seizure, impoundment, restraint, custody, force, cyber action, or asset freezing requires separate SCPA authority. |
| G30 | Adoption-state clarity | Advisory standards, voluntary commitments, contracts, local enactment, national enactment, treaty activation, suspension, expiry, and termination are explicit. |
| G31 | Pilot and target non-activation | Future dates, coalition size, MPA percentages, personhood counts, and pilot success do not create authority. |
| G32 | Closure and unresolved duties | Authority ends while restoration, crew welfare, claims, correction, compensation, and protected-data duties remain assigned without residual power. |

## 26. Versioning, adoption, and compatibility

A jurisdiction adopts v2.3 only through a controlled instrument identifying the exact version and hash, local authority, powers activated, powers withheld, implementing bodies, transition from v2.2, open cases, current listings and permits, data migration, review date, and withdrawal path.

A higher version number or similar title is not automatically compatible. Material changes to rights, Indigenous authority, quotas, licences, port measures, finance, emergency response, investigation, adjudication, or enforcement require a new conformance review.

## 27. Transition from v2.2

The following v2.2 mechanisms are retired or reclassified:

- MGCC standards endorsed by a two-thirds state vote → binding only where separately enacted;
- Coastal BAZ powers assumed by framework → exact host-law delegation through `OCE-11`;
- Blue Heart Cultural Council sacred-site protection and advisory veto → affected-nation-controlled advice, confidentiality, reconsideration, and review;
- Digital Ocean Twin enforcement verification → technical evidence only;
- AI-adjusted quotas and no-take zones → advisory model followed by competent decisions;
- automatic blacklisting for acoustic-log tampering → investigation and due-process listing route;
- 500 percent noisy-vessel premium → illustrative tariff option requiring lawful adoption;
- **Absolute Port State Control** → lawful participating port-state measures with actor identity, due process, proportionality, expiry, and humanitarian exceptions;
- ISA permit → automatic Tier 1 blacklist and port denial → separate legal-position, listing, and port decisions;
- indefinite full blacklist and two-year removal rule → individualized duration, review, correction, appeal, and delisting;
- loss of BAZ certification or Ethical Trade Zone access for port non-enforcement → separate lawful or contractual process;
- automatic 48-hour crisis deployment → CDEE/ECRC mission acceptance;
- automatic emergency-fund release → separate financial authorization;
- advisory moratoria treated as universal bans → explicit legal adoption state;
- five-year default renewal → governing-law renewal or expiry process;
- legal-personhood timeline → non-activating programme pathway;
- MPA, seabed, sacred-site, noise, and personhood targets → evaluation metrics only.

## 28. Closing doctrine

> **The ocean needs rights, stewardship, precaution, restoration, and enforceable protection. Those aims are weakened when a sensor becomes a judge, a tariff becomes a blacklist, a blacklist becomes a closed port, or a closed port becomes collective punishment.**

> **Oceans v2.3 makes marine action stronger by making every authority visible: who observed, who interpreted, who adopted the rule, who set the quota, who listed the actor, who restricted the service, who protected the crew, who adjudicated, who executed, until when, and how the decision can be challenged, corrected, terminated, and repaired.**
