## 0. Controlled successor notice

Water v5.2 preserves the source framework's strongest commitments: water as a commons and human right; watershed-scale democracy; universal, affordable water and sanitation; Indigenous water authority and protected knowledge; community control and anti-privatization; ecological flow and aquifer regeneration; transboundary cooperation; public finance; and rapid emergency WASH.

It replaces the assumption that forming a Water Assembly can simultaneously create legal personhood, guardianship, operational authority, allocation power, tariff power, utility control, fund custody, emergency command, and adjudicatory jurisdiction.

> **Water is a commons. A commons still needs a constitution.**

> **Hydrological fact is not legal boundary. Water-body rights are not title. Guardianship is not utility ownership. Democratic planning is not adjudication. Scarcity is not jurisdiction. A tariff is not a shutoff order. Emergency need is not permanent command.**

This framework may provide model charters, methods, technical standards, finance pathways, participatory planning, service-design guidance, lawful record structures, training, and coordination. It does not itself recognize a water body as a legal person, appoint a guardian, transfer infrastructure, impose a tariff, disconnect a user, enter premises, open an investigation, declare an emergency, adjudicate a dispute, seize an asset, or use force.

## 1. Purpose and constitutional posture

### 1.1 Purpose

The framework enables communities and lawful public institutions to govern water according to hydrological reality while preserving human rights, affected-nation authority, democratic legitimacy, service continuity, due process, and ecological integrity.

It supplies a common architecture for:

- watershed and aquifer mapping;
- water-quality and quantity observation;
- democratic Water Assemblies;
- universal WASH and affordability;
- water-body rights review and guardianship;
- infrastructure and utility accountability;
- scarcity allocation and ecological reserves;
- abstraction, discharge, industrial, agricultural, and commercial permits;
- the Compute-Water Tariff where lawfully enacted;
- anti-privatization and community-equity safeguards;
- public and commons finance;
- inspection, investigation, evidence, remediation, and adjudication;
- transboundary basin coordination;
- emergency WASH and recovery;
- correction, appeal, compensation, termination, and unresolved-duty custody.

### 1.2 Non-authority clause

No actor may cite this framework as the legal source of a consequential act. Every such act must identify a current constituting instrument, competent authority, jurisdiction, exact power, activation state, limits, review, expiry, correction, and remedy.

Publication, coalition membership, a framework vote, a target year, a model threshold, an Assembly charter, a dashboard alert, a funding condition, or a CERGTA record cannot create power by itself.

## 2. Constitutional doctrines

### 2.1 Water as commons and right

Water and sanitation are life-support systems and public goods. Governance shall protect universal access, ecological regeneration, public accountability, and community participation. Commons status does not erase lawful human, Indigenous, constitutional, labour, property, public-health, disability, privacy, or procedural rights.

### 2.2 Essential minimum before allocation

Every jurisdiction adopting this framework shall define an unconditional essential minimum covering at least drinking, cooking, hygiene, sanitation, healthcare, disability support, emergency shelter, fire and public safety, essential food production, and the ecological flow or recharge necessary to prevent irreversible system failure.

No tariff, debt, contract, permit dispute, documentation gap, migration status, biometric or data refusal, political disagreement, or commercial enforcement measure may interrupt that minimum.

### 2.3 Watershed subsidiarity without jurisdictional fiction

Hydrological units should guide planning and coordination because water crosses administrative boundaries. They do not automatically replace constitutional, treaty, Indigenous, municipal, national, or transboundary legal jurisdictions.

Authority should be exercised at the lowest level capable of protecting the watershed and affected people, but only through lawfully delegated functions. Upward coordination shall solve cross-boundary problems without converting a technical or coordinating body into a sovereign.

### 2.4 Observation–consequence firewall

Community observations, professional sampling, Traditional Knowledge, meters, satellite data, wastewater signals, digital twins, and water-stress models may inform review. They do not automatically create a legal boundary, scarcity declaration, tariff, permit decision, personhood, guardianship, investigation, liability, restriction, or emergency.

### 2.5 Rights, guardianship, service governance, and regulation are separate

A water body may receive legal rights only through `CERGTA-11` through `CERGTA-15`. A guardian may be appointed only through `CERGTA-16` through `CERGTA-19`.

A guardian represents the water body and owes fiduciary duties. The guardian does not own the water body, infrastructure, land, utility, licences, funds, or regulatory jurisdiction. A Water Assembly may serve as or nominate an institutional guardian only where competent law expressly authorizes it and creates independent fiduciary, conflict, review, removal, and succession arrangements.

### 2.6 Affected Indigenous nations speak for themselves

The specific affected nation or nations determine their representatives, applicable Indigenous law, water relationships, consent process, non-engagement, sacred knowledge, and disclosure limits.

The Earth Council, a global Indigenous quota, another nation, an NGO, a Water Assembly, a Basin Council, or the GWSC may support, advise, resource, or audit process integrity. None may provide FPIC, waive a claim, disclose restricted knowledge, establish territorial boundaries, or designate sacredness for the affected nation.

### 2.7 Scarcity without deprivation

Scarcity may require conservation and temporary allocation changes. The allocation shall be evidence-informed, legally adopted, distributionally assessed, least harmful, time-bounded, reviewable, and reversible. Human survival and sanitation minimums remain unconditional; ecological reserves and Indigenous rights remain explicit; hardship cannot be hidden in aggregate efficiency.

### 2.8 Commercial enforcement is not essential-service enforcement

Industrial and commercial users may be subject to lawful tariffs, permits, conditions, and proportionate curtailment. A billing default does not itself authorize water shutoff. Any curtailment must be separately authorized, technically scoped, independently reviewable, and designed not to endanger households, workers, hospitals, sanitation, fire systems, community supply, or ecological survival.

### 2.9 Emergency urgency without general command

A Water Assembly may detect and report an emergency and may exercise only emergency functions already granted by law. CDEE/ECRC supplies common event identity, command, logistics, clocks, finance, information controls, termination, and remedy where adopted.

A water emergency does not create title, guardianship, general police power, compulsory data sharing, permanent rationing, asset transfer, or indefinite utility administration.

## 3. Institutional architecture

### 3.1 Water Assemblies

A Water Assembly is a democratically constituted watershed body. Its charter shall identify the exact functions lawfully assigned to it. Possible functions include participatory planning, infrastructure prioritization, budget recommendation, public monitoring, service oversight, allocation proposals, partnership review, and transboundary representation.

No Assembly receives all functions automatically.

The charter shall separate or independently control:

- democratic planning and public participation;
- utility operation and infrastructure custody;
- guardian fiduciary representation;
- permit, tariff, and allocation decisions;
- inspection and investigation;
- trust-fund custody and procurement;
- adjudication and appeal;
- emergency command.

Where local capacity requires one institution to host several administrative units, separate mandates, staff roles, records, conflicts, review authorities, budgets, access controls, and appeal routes are mandatory.

#### Model composition baseline

A jurisdiction should adapt representation to local law and demography while ensuring:

- a directly elected resident majority;
- gender parity or an equivalently strong anti-exclusion rule;
- affected Indigenous nations participating through their own chosen institutions rather than generic reserved seats;
- meaningful youth participation;
- disability representation and accessibility;
- representation of informal settlements, rural users, workers, tenants, essential services, and downstream communities;
- conflict-of-interest rules for utilities, large extractors, contractors, and landholders.

Representation does not substitute for affected-nation consent or individual rights.

### 3.2 Global Water and Sanitation Council

The GWSC is a technical, financing-support, and coordination institution. Its functions may include:

- open-source mapping and WASH standards;
- capacity support and low-tech alternatives;
- grant administration where separately authorized;
- method comparison and technical assistance;
- regional emergency stockpile support under competent emergency command;
- transboundary facilitation and diplomacy;
- publication of non-binding global assessments.

GWSC standards are advisory until adopted by competent law or incorporated into a valid contract, permit, grant, or service mandate. The GWSC cannot recognize water-body rights, appoint guardians, impose tariffs, ration water, issue permits, command utilities, declare emergencies, adjudicate disputes, or compel data disclosure by implication.

### 3.3 Basin Councils

A Basin Council coordinates jurisdictions, affected nations, Water Assemblies, utilities, and other lawful authorities sharing a watershed or aquifer. Its constituting agreement shall specify:

- participating parties and legal source;
- delegated functions;
- decision method and non-participation rules;
- data and protected-knowledge controls;
- upstream and downstream duties;
- allocation and drought coordination;
- dispute resolution;
- finance and audit;
- amendment, withdrawal, expiry, and succession.

A Basin Council is not a territorial government merely because water crosses borders.

### 3.4 Service operators and utilities

Service operation shall be assigned through `WAT-17`, whether the operator is public, community, cooperative, Indigenous, private, or hybrid. Operators owe quality, affordability, maintenance, reporting, worker, emergency, and continuity duties. They cannot convert operational custody into title, regulatory, guardian, or adjudicatory power.

### 3.5 Guardians and adjudicators

Guardianship and adjudication are external constitutional functions unless separately and lawfully constituted. A competent hydrological chamber may exist within a court or tribunal system, but this framework does not create one. The adjudicator must be independent of the Assembly, guardian, operator, GWSC, Basin Council, fund, and parties to the dispute.

## 4. The constitutional starter path

### Step 1 — Map without deciding

A watershed initiative begins with `WAT-02` through `WAT-06`:

1. map waters, infrastructure, users, ecological dependencies, and transboundary connections;
2. document observation provenance and uncertainty;
3. identify protected data and Traditional Knowledge;
4. publish the water-stress and quality methods;
5. map all affected persons, communities, nations, workers, users, and ecosystems.

The map is a planning and evidentiary object, not a legal boundary, title registry, rights decision, permit, or scarcity order.

### Step 2 — Constitute the Assembly

The jurisdiction adopts `WAT-07` through a lawful public process. The charter records exact functions and exclusions. It creates participation and accountability, not automatic guardianship or universal operational authority.

### Step 3 — Establish universal WASH duties

Before new tariffs, partnerships, or scarcity rules, the jurisdiction adopts `WAT-18`, `WAT-22`, and the service-operator mandate. It establishes affordability support, non-disconnection, emergency supply, repair duties, complaint routes, and public reporting.

### Step 4 — Establish lawful finance

Funding proceeds through `WAT-16`, `WAT-29`, `WAT-30`, and `WAT-31`. A Community Water Trust Fund must have a legally identified custodian, segregated accounts, public procurement, beneficial-ownership disclosure, audit, restitution, and dissolution rules.

A Water Assembly application does not create a grant entitlement. A fund condition cannot override essential access, Indigenous rights, or due process.

### Step 5 — Adopt allocation and permit law

Scarcity, abstraction, discharge, industrial use, agricultural use, domestic connections, and ecological reserves are separately governed through `WAT-19` through `WAT-23`. The jurisdiction decides which body exercises each function and establishes independent review.

### Step 6 — Consider the Compute-Water Tariff

The Compute-Water Tariff is optional and jurisdiction-specific. It may be adopted where large-scale computing imposes material hydrological costs. `WAT-24` shall specify:

- the legal source and taxable or chargeable event;
- facility and beneficial owner;
- metered or estimated use;
- the current water-stress method and uncertainty;
- rate formula and impact assessment;
- closed-loop or zero-extraction alternatives;
- transition period and notice;
- treatment of shared infrastructure and worker/public safety;
- review, correction, appeal, expiry, and revenue custody.

The v5.1 multipliers of 5×, 10×, and 20× may be used as policy options or modelling scenarios. They are not self-executing rates. The adopted jurisdiction must justify the actual rate and review it periodically.

A default revenue-policy model may recommend at least half for household affordability or community benefit, substantial investment in infrastructure, and the remainder in watershed restoration. Actual distributions require separate financial authority and annual needs review; tariff collection does not automatically issue AUBI payments.

### Step 7 — Resolve disputes lawfully

Mediation may be offered, but urgent relief cannot be withheld merely because mediation is incomplete. Adjudication proceeds through `WAT-36`, with standing, notice, evidence, reasons, remedies, interim relief, appeal, correction, and external execution.

## 5. Universal WASH and essential-resource constitution

### 5.1 Essential minimum

Each adopting jurisdiction shall publish an essential-minimum schedule. It should include quantity, quality, reliability, accessibility, distance or delivery standards, sanitation, menstrual health, disability accommodation, affordability, emergency supply, repair response, and culturally appropriate service.

The schedule shall distinguish:

- the unconditional minimum;
- ordinary household service above the minimum;
- essential institutional service;
- livelihood and food-production needs;
- ecological reserve;
- discretionary and commercial use.

### 5.2 Affordability

A jurisdiction may use an affordability ceiling, lifeline tariff, income-based support, universal basic service, debt forgiveness, payment plans, cross-subsidy, or public finance. A model threshold such as three percent of low-income household income may inform policy but must be tested against actual housing, energy, disability, family-size, and local cost burdens.

### 5.3 Non-disconnection

Essential water and sanitation shall not be disconnected for inability to pay. Where lawful service restrictions exist for deliberate damage, dangerous misuse, or non-essential commercial consumption, the authority must preserve the unconditional minimum, provide notice and challenge, and prevent collective punishment of tenants, workers, families, or neighbouring users.

### 5.4 Informal settlements and documentation

Lack of formal title, address, identity document, citizenship, migration status, or utility account shall not bar essential WASH. The service authority shall provide safe community, shared, mobile, or temporary solutions while regularization proceeds.

### 5.5 Private and hybrid participation

Private or hybrid participation is permissible only under enforceable public-interest conditions. A model community-equity requirement of twenty percent may be adopted, but no universal percentage is created by this framework. The governing instrument shall address ownership, revenue share, affordability, quality, maintenance, transparency, beneficial ownership, procurement, worker rights, service data, exit, asset condition, and continuity.

Contract termination does not itself transfer assets. Transfer requires `WAT-28` and applicable CERGTA, Hearthstone, property, insolvency, labour, and compensation law.

## 6. Allocation, permits, tariffs, and commercial restrictions

### 6.1 Allocation hierarchy

The default normative priority is:

1. unconditional human survival and sanitation;
2. essential healthcare, disability, shelter, public safety, and emergency food needs;
3. legally protected Indigenous and treaty rights;
4. ecological flow, quality, and aquifer-regeneration minimums;
5. livelihood and food-system uses;
6. other social and economic uses according to adopted law.

The order does not erase context. Some ecological minimums are prerequisites for all later human supply; some Indigenous rights are not reducible to a numerical tier; and food, energy, or sanitation systems may be mutually dependent. `WAT-19` must document trade-offs and non-compensable limits.

### 6.2 Scarcity and drought

A water-stress category is a scientific assessment, not a rationing order. `WAT-20` must identify legal activation, duration, geographic scope, protected uses, conservation duties, exemptions, assistance, communications, enforcement separation, review, and automatic expiry.

### 6.3 Permits and quotas

Each abstraction, discharge, industrial, agricultural, infrastructure, or other regulated use must identify its own source, conditions, volume, location, ecological limits, monitoring, review, expiry, and remedy. One permit cannot silently authorize another act.

### 6.4 Compute-Water Tariff

The tariff captures hydrological externalities and funds water security only where lawfully adopted. It shall not be used to discriminate among technologies or firms without a water-related basis. Comparable water users should be governed through a coherent industrial-water policy, with additional compute-specific rules justified by cooling patterns, location, scale, or infrastructure effects.

Alternative compliance must be technically verified. “Closed-loop” claims shall account for initial fill, leakage, blowdown, embodied water, contamination, energy-water trade-offs, and emergency operation.

### 6.5 Commercial restriction

`WAT-26` is required before reducing or stopping a commercial supply for non-payment, permit breach, scarcity, or unsafe operation. The decision must be proportionate, technically specific, reviewable, expiring, and accompanied by worker, public-safety, environmental, and essential-service protections.

## 7. Water-body rights and guardianship

### 7.1 Rights review

A community, affected nation, public institution, or other lawful claimant may submit `WAT-10`. The review shall distinguish ecological evidence, moral or cultural reasons, legal standing, boundary, proposed rights, existing rights, conflicts, representation, and remedies.

### 7.2 Recognition

Only a competent authority may issue `WAT-11`. The decision shall define the legal water body, boundary, rights, standing, priority, conflict rules, non-derogation, duration or non-expiry basis, review, and relation to title, permits, and Indigenous law.

Rights recognition does not itself transfer infrastructure, land, water rights, concessions, or utility assets.

### 7.3 Guardianship

A guardian must be nominated, appointed, instructed, reviewed, removable, and replaceable through `WAT-12` through `WAT-14`. Institutional guardianship may be shared, but shared guardianship must define each guardian's authority, instructions, conflicts, deadlock process, standing, budget, and accountability.

Good-faith immunity cannot become blanket immunity. It should protect reasonable fiduciary action while preserving liability for corruption, bad faith, gross negligence, knowing misuse of protected knowledge, discrimination, or acting outside mandate.

## 8. Inspection, investigation, evidence, and remedy

### 8.1 Inspection

Routine monitoring does not authorize entry. `WAT-32` defines who may inspect which premises, infrastructure, records, or meters, for what purpose, with what notice, safety, privacy, sampling, report, and challenge.

### 8.2 Investigation

An anomaly, complaint, or sample may trigger assessment. It does not establish theft, pollution, fraud, diversion, or liability. `WAT-33` opens a distinct investigation and protects exculpatory evidence, sources, subjects, workers, whistleblowers, and closure rights.

### 8.3 Evidence and attribution

`WAT-34` records scientific reliability and legal admissibility separately. Modelled attribution, upstream inference, microbial or chemical signatures, meter anomalies, satellite images, citizen observations, and Traditional Knowledge each retain their provenance, uncertainty, access restrictions, and contestability.

### 8.4 Adjudication

A Hydrological Chamber is a descriptive specialization, not a framework-created universal court. Where lawfully constituted, `WAT-36` requires jurisdiction, independence, standing, notice, counsel, evidence, reasons, remedies, interim relief, appeal, and closure.

Remedies may include cessation, repair, restoration, compensation, permit modification, contract remedy, or lawful asset action. Each remedy must have an action-specific legal basis. Physical execution, seizure, restraint, custody, cyber action, or financial coercion requires `CERGTA-48` and SCPA.

## 9. Transboundary waters

Shared waters require polycentric coordination rather than automatic centralization. `WAT-39` shall record:

- participating jurisdictions, nations, affected Indigenous authorities, and communities;
- treaties, domestic law, customary law, and delegated mandates;
- common facts and unresolved scientific disagreement;
- upstream and downstream duties;
- ecological reserves and essential minimums;
- allocation and drought proposals;
- infrastructure and pollution dependencies;
- data and protected-knowledge rules;
- finance and burden sharing;
- dispute, interim relief, withdrawal, expiry, and succession.

No majority of Water Assemblies may extinguish a non-consenting nation's rights or create authority over a non-party. Coordination shall preserve emergency communication and essential continuity even during legal disagreement.

## 10. Crisis and emergency WASH

### 10.1 Event identity and command

Every significant flood, drought, contamination event, infrastructure failure, conflict-zone WASH emergency, or public-health emergency should receive a CDEE event identity where the emergency stack is adopted. `WAT-38` links the water route to emergency authority without merging the two.

The competent emergency authority—not a framework label—defines command, logistics, procurement, privileges, information access, duration, review, and termination.

### 10.2 Operational priorities

Emergency WASH shall prioritize:

- safe drinking water and sanitation;
- healthcare, disability, shelter, and hygiene;
- rapid contamination assessment;
- worker and volunteer safety;
- accessible public communication;
- continuity for informal settlements and displaced people;
- ecological containment where necessary to preserve future supply;
- family and community participation;
- transition to climate-resilient recovery.

### 10.3 Funding

A standing emergency reserve is encouraged. Rapid disbursement may use expedited review, but must still identify authority, amount, purpose, custodian, procurement, audit, modification, termination, restitution, and remedy. A Water Assembly alert may support an application but does not itself release funds unless the fund law says so.

### 10.4 Conflict zones and humanitarian access

WASH workers and civilian infrastructure shall receive all protection available under applicable humanitarian and domestic law. This framework cannot create protected legal status merely by designation.

Neutral corridors, escorts, checkpoints, armed protection, searches, detention, or force require competent Peace, humanitarian, security, and SCPA routes. Refusal of intelligence or political cooperation shall not condition essential WASH.

### 10.5 Wastewater and public-health surveillance

Wastewater surveillance may support public-health assessment where lawfully authorized. It shall use data minimization, aggregation, purpose limitation, independent oversight, retention limits, public notice, correction, and prohibition on silent transfer to immigration, intelligence, criminal, employment, insurance, or behavioural systems.

### 10.6 Termination and recovery

Emergency water restrictions, credentials, procurement exceptions, temporary access, data permissions, and command roles expire independently. Recovery uses `WAT-35`, `WAT-43`, and `WAT-44` to rebuild safely, compensate wrongful harm, return authority and assets, dispose of data, and preserve unfinished restoration without residual emergency power.

## 11. Finance and anti-privatization

### 11.1 Global Commons Fund

The GCF may support universal WASH, infrastructure, maintenance, emergency readiness, technical assistance, and restoration where its own constituting law authorizes. Eligibility should prioritize need and capacity support rather than reward only already capable jurisdictions.

Digital reporting cannot be the sole access route. Paper, radio, assisted, and community-verification pathways are required.

### 11.2 Community Water Trust Funds

`WAT-16` shall separate public money from operator accounts, political campaign finance, private investment, AUBI administration, emergency reserve, restoration escrow, and compensation funds. All transfers require authority and audit.

### 11.3 Revenue allocation

The source framework's 50/30/20 tariff allocation may serve as a planning default, not an immutable constitutional formula. Each jurisdiction shall publish needs, beneficiaries, distributional effects, maintenance liabilities, ecological duties, public deliberation, and annual revision.

### 11.4 Anti-privatization safeguards

Every partnership shall preserve:

- universal essential access;
- affordability;
- public control over standards and accountability;
- transparent beneficial ownership and finance;
- maintenance and resilience;
- worker and community rights;
- audit and data access;
- lawful exit and service continuity;
- prohibition on scarcity profiteering;
- remedy and compensation.

## 12. Data, technology, and public communication

### 12.1 Data minimization

Water data can reveal household presence, health, religion, work patterns, industrial secrets, sacred sites, and Indigenous knowledge. Collection and sharing shall be limited to the lawful purpose and minimum necessary detail.

### 12.2 Automated systems

AI, digital twins, smart meters, anomaly detection, satellite systems, and blockchain may support observation, modelling, billing, or record integrity. They cannot constitute legal boundaries, set rates, issue permits, classify criminality, recognize rights, appoint guardians, ration supply, or order shutoff without a competent human decision and all applicable records.

### 12.3 Public dashboards

Public reporting should show methods, uncertainty, general conditions, finance, service performance, decisions, expiry, review, and correction. It shall not expose household behaviour, protected ecological locations, sacred sites, Traditional Knowledge, security-sensitive infrastructure, whistleblowers, or evidence beyond lawful access.

### 12.4 Registry

The registry in `WAT-40` is non-constitutive. It records legal facts and disputed status; it does not create personhood, guardianship, title, permits, tariffs, restrictions, or emergency authority. `WAT-41` ensures errors can be corrected even if the technical system is described as immutable.

## 16. Readiness gates

| Gate | Title | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| G01 | Legal source and activation | Every Assembly, Council, operator, guardian, fund, inspector, emergency actor, and adjudicator identifies exact current authority and jurisdiction. |
| G02 | Watershed map non-constitutive | Hydrological mapping and monitoring do not themselves establish legal boundaries, title, personhood, guardianship, permits, or allocation. |
| G03 | Observation–consequence firewall | Samples, meters, dashboards, citizen reports, Traditional Knowledge, and models may trigger review but not automatic legal or financial consequence. |
| G04 | EIOI method inheritance | Water indicators and stress models inherit EIOI provenance, uncertainty, dissent, versioning, correction, and permitted-use controls. |
| G05 | Assembly constitution | The Water Assembly has an enacted charter, democratic legitimacy, accessibility, recall, conflicts, and an exact list of functions. |
| G06 | Functional separation | Planning, service operation, guardianship, regulatory decision, fund custody, inspection, adjudication, and emergency command are separated or independently controlled. |
| G07 | Affected-nation authority | The specific affected Indigenous nation controls its own representatives, law, consent, non-engagement, and protected knowledge. |
| G08 | No Earth Council proxy | A global body cannot provide FPIC, sacred designation, territorial consent, or waiver for an affected nation. |
| G09 | Rights recognition route | Water-body personhood or rights require CERGTA-11–15 and competent law; Assembly chartering is not recognition. |
| G10 | Guardian appointment route | Guardianship requires CERGTA-16–19; it is not automatic upon Assembly formation and does not confer ownership or regulatory power. |
| G11 | Essential minimum | Household, sanitation, healthcare, disability, shelter, emergency food, worker safety, and ecological survival minimums are explicit and non-derogable. |
| G12 | Affordability and non-disconnection | Inability to pay, documentation gaps, migration status, data refusal, or dispute cannot remove essential water and sanitation. |
| G13 | Allocation legality | Scarcity allocations identify source, method, protected uses, burdens, alternatives, duration, review, and restoration of ordinary rules. |
| G14 | Ecological reserve | Allocation protects legally adopted ecological flow and recharge requirements without treating a model threshold as self-executing law. |
| G15 | Permit separation | Domestic service, essential service, agriculture, industry, abstraction, discharge, and infrastructure permissions remain legally distinct. |
| G16 | Tariff authority | The Compute-Water Tariff requires lawful enactment, a transparent formula, evidence, notice, alternatives, review, and appeal. |
| G17 | No automatic multiplier | Water-stress categories inform a rate decision but do not automatically impose 5×, 10×, or 20× rates. |

## 18. Implementation pathway

### Phase 0 — Constitutional preparation

- map the watershed without treating the map as law;
- identify existing authorities, utilities, funds, courts, permits, affected nations, users, workers, and service dependencies;
- adopt the essential-minimum and non-disconnection rules;
- draft the Water Assembly charter and functional-separation map;
- publish data, method, access, correction, and low-tech protocols;
- freeze all automatic personhood, guardianship, tariff, shutoff, asset-transfer, and emergency assumptions.

### Phase 1 — Service and democratic foundation

- constitute the Water Assembly;
- adopt operator mandates and public reporting;
- establish the Community Water Trust Fund;
- provide universal or progressive WASH coverage;
- establish complaint, affordability, and emergency-supply routes;
- negotiate affected-nation and transboundary interfaces.

### Phase 2 — Allocation and regulated use

- adopt scarcity, ecological-reserve, permit, inspection, and adjudication law;
- pilot industrial and compute-water policy with distributional and technical testing;
- establish independent review and correction;
- test partnership exit and operator succession without service interruption.

### Phase 3 — Rights, guardianship, and basin maturity

- consider water-body rights through the CERGTA sequence;
- appoint guardians only where lawfully recognized and necessary;
- strengthen Basin Council agreements;
- integrate climate resilience, restoration, and emergency WASH;
- conduct compound tabletop and live exercises.

### Phase 4 — Review and controlled scaling

Scaling depends on demonstrated universal access, affordability, ecological improvement, democratic legitimacy, affected-nation authority, legal interoperability, correction, remedy, and clean termination—not merely the number of Assemblies, tariff revenue, dashboard scores, or target dates.

## 19. Plain-language operating rules

1. Mapping the watershed does not give anyone power over it.  
2. Forming a Water Assembly does not automatically make it the guardian, utility owner, regulator, emergency command, or court.  
3. Every person receives essential water and sanitation even when they cannot pay.  
4. A drought score starts a lawful review; it does not ration water by itself.  
5. A data centre tariff must be enacted, explained, appealable, and separated from essential supply.  
6. Non-payment is not a shutoff order.  
7. Water-body rights do not transfer land or infrastructure.  
8. Guardians speak for a water body but do not own it or govern everyone around it.  
9. Affected Indigenous nations decide their own consent, representatives, law, and protected knowledge.  
10. Emergency action ends when its authority ends; unfinished repair continues without unfinished emergency power.

## 20. Conclusion

Water governance must be strong enough to guarantee life, restrain extraction, maintain infrastructure, allocate scarcity, restore watersheds, and coordinate across borders. It must also be precise enough to prevent the institutions protecting water from becoming unreviewable owners, regulators, funders, guardians, courts, and emergency commands at once.

> **Water is governed best when hydrology guides scale, communities shape priorities, affected nations retain authority, essential access is unconditional, science remains corrigible, and every consequential power has a lawful beginning, bounded route, independent challenge, and definite end.**
