## 1. The Starter Kit: How to Begin

*This is the core of the framework. If you are a BAZ leader, a community organizer, or a local official, start here. The rest of the document provides the legal and financial architecture that supports these steps.*

---

### Step 1: Map Your Watershed

**What You Need to Know**

Before you can govern water, you must know where it comes from, where it goes, and who uses it. A watershed is the land that drains into a common body of water. It is the natural unit for water governance.

**What to Do**

| Action | Description | Timeline |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Identify your watershed boundaries** | Use existing maps (USGS, national hydrological services) or community mapping. Follow the water. | 1-2 months |
| **Map water sources** | Rivers, streams, lakes, springs, groundwater aquifers, rainwater catchment. | 2-3 months |
| **Map water users** | Households, agriculture, industry, data centers, ecosystems. Estimate volumes. | 2-3 months |
| **Assess water health** | Water quality testing (where possible). Ecosystem health. Sacred sites. | 3-6 months |
| **Document Indigenous knowledge** | Partner with Indigenous knowledge holders. Follow FPIC protocols. Respect sacred knowledge that cannot be shared. | Ongoing |
| **Identify transboundary connections** | Does your watershed cross BAZ, national, or regional borders? Identify upstream/downstream dependencies and neighboring water authorities. | 1-2 months |

**Resources**

- The **Global Water & Sanitation Council** provides watershed mapping tools and technical assistance.
- The **Open Source WASH Tech Library** contains low-cost water testing protocols.
- **Indigenous knowledge holders** in your BAZ are essential partners.

**Deliverable:** A watershed map with documented sources, users, health status, and transboundary connections.

---

### Step 2: Form Your Water Assembly

**What You Need to Know**

Water governance must be democratic, inclusive, and accountable. The Water Assembly is the decision-making body for your watershed. It holds operational authority over water infrastructure, allocation, and protection.

**Composition Requirements**

| Category | Minimum Representation | Selection Method |
|----------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Watershed residents** | 50% | Direct election by water users |
| **Women** | 50% of elected seats | Ensured through nomination requirements |
| **Indigenous governing bodies** | Co-governance in their territories | FPIC process; may hold veto |
| **Youth (under 25)** | 20% | Elected by youth water councils |
| **Disability advocates** | Included | Accessibility standards enforced |

**What to Do**

| Action | Description | Timeline |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Convene founding meeting** | Open invitation to all water users in the watershed. Public notice. | 1 month |
| **Adopt governing charter** | Simple rules for decision-making (consensus where possible, majority vote when needed). Must include: conflict resolution process, transparency requirements, recall provisions, and anti-privatization safeguards. | 2 months |
| **Elect Water Assembly** | First election. Use existing community structures where trusted. | 2 months |
| **Establish Guardianship** | The Assembly is designated as legal Guardian of the watershed (see Appendix C). | Concurrent |
| **Open a Community Trust Fund** | Bank account or equivalent for water revenues. Must be publicly audited. | 2 months |

**What the Assembly Does**

- Approves water infrastructure projects
- Sets water allocation priorities (human needs first, then ecosystems, then economic use)
- Establishes water tariffs (with affordability protections)
- Manages the Compute-Water Tariff (Step 4)
- Approves or rejects private sector partnerships (with anti-privatization safeguards — see Section 3, Mechanism 3)
- Holds the legal guardianship of the watershed
- Reports annually to the Global Water & Sanitation Council
- Participates in Basin Council if watershed is transboundary (see Section 2.4)

**Deliverable:** A chartered Water Assembly with elected members, operating budget, and Community Trust Fund.

### Step 3: Draw Down from the Global Commons Fund

**What You Need to Know**

The Global Commons Fund provides base funding for water infrastructure, operations, and maintenance. It is available to all BAZs with a recognized Water Assembly.

**Eligibility Requirements**

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Chartered Water Assembly** | Step 2 completed |
| **Watershed map** | Step 1 completed |
| **FPIC compliance** | Indigenous consent documented |
| **Commitment to standards** | Agreement to follow Global Water & Sanitation Council technical standards |

**Funding Available**

| Purpose | Amount | Terms |
|---------|--------|-------|
| **Initial infrastructure** | Up to $5M per BAZ, based on population and need | Grant |
| **Operations & maintenance** | Annual allocation based on population and infrastructure | Grant |
| **Emergency response** | Rapid disbursement for floods, droughts, contamination events (see Section 4) | Grant with expedited approval |
| **Technical assistance** | Training, mapping, planning support | In-kind support |

**How to Apply**

1. **Submit application** to Global Water & Sanitation Council through the Planetary Dashboard
2. **Include documentation**: Watershed map, Assembly charter, FPIC documentation, infrastructure plan
3. **Review period**: 3 months
4. **Disbursement**: Funds released to Community Trust Fund upon approval

**Accountability**

- Annual financial audit published publicly
- Infrastructure progress reports
- Water quality monitoring data shared with GWSC

**Deliverable:** Funding in the Community Trust Fund; infrastructure projects initiated.

### Step 4: Secure Your Compute-Water Tariff

**What You Need to Know**

Data centers consume massive amounts of water for cooling. In water-stressed regions, this competes directly with human and ecosystem needs. The Compute-Water Tariff ensures that data centers operating in your watershed contribute to water security.

**The Mechanism**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Who pays** | Any data center, AI training facility, or large-scale computing operation using water for cooling |
| **Tariff rate** | Tiered based on water stress: 5× standard industrial rate (moderate stress), 10× (high stress), 20× (extreme stress) |
| **Alternative compliance** | Data centers may build closed-loop, zero-extraction water recycling facilities instead of paying the tariff |

**Water Stress Categories**

| Category | Definition | Tariff Multiplier |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Low stress** | Water availability exceeds demand | 1× standard rate |
| **Moderate stress** | Demand approaching supply | 5× standard rate |
| **High stress** | Demand exceeding supply; seasonal shortages | 10× standard rate |
| **Extreme stress** | Chronic shortages; aquifer depletion | 20× standard rate |

**What to Do**

| Action | Description | Timeline |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Identify data centers** | Map existing and planned facilities in your watershed | 1 month |
| **Assess water stress** | Use GWSC water stress index or local data | 1 month |
| **Set tariff rate** | Water Assembly approves rate based on stress category | 1 month |
| **Notify operators** | Formal notice of tariff requirement; 12-month compliance window | 1 month |
| **Collect and distribute** | Tariff revenues deposited in Community Trust Fund | Ongoing |

**Revenue Use**

Tariff revenues must be used for:
- **AUBI top-ups** (at least 50% of revenue) for households in the watershed
- **Community water infrastructure** (at least 30% of revenue)
- **Watershed restoration** (at least 20% of revenue)

**Enforcement**

- Water Assembly monitors usage
- Non-payment results in water shutoff
- Hydrological Chamber adjudicates disputes (see Step 5)

**Deliverable:** Data centers in your watershed are paying the tariff or using closed-loop cooling; revenues flowing to the Community Trust Fund.

### Step 5: Resolve Disputes Through the Hydrological Chamber

**What You Need to Know**

When conflicts arise — pollution, water theft, tariff disputes, rights violations — the Hydrological Chamber of the Digital Justice Tribunal provides binding resolution.

**The Legal Foundation: Guardianship**

Your Water Assembly is the legal Guardian of your watershed. This means:

- The watershed itself has legal personhood (rights, standing, capacity to sue)
- The Water Assembly acts on its behalf
- Cases are brought in the name of the water body itself, not just for community damages

**Who Can Bring a Case**

| Party | Standing |
|-------|----------|
| **Water Assembly** | Full standing as Guardian |
| **Indigenous governing body** | Full standing for waters within their territory |
| **Community member** | May petition Water Assembly to bring case; if refused, may appeal directly to Chamber |
| **Global Water & Sanitation Council** | Standing for violations of global standards |
| **Basin Council** | Standing for transboundary violations (see Section 2.4) |

**Types of Cases**

| Case Type | Description | Remedy |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Pollution** | Contamination of water body | Cessation order; restoration; damages to Watershed Restoration Fund |
| **Water theft** | Unauthorized extraction | Cessation; fines; restitution |
| **Tariff violation** | Data center non-payment | Shutoff; fines; back payments |
| **Rights violation** | Denial of access to safe water | Injunction; damages; infrastructure requirement |
| **FPIC violation** | Use of Indigenous knowledge or territory without consent | Cessation; data destruction; damages |
| **Privatization violation** | Breach of Community Equity Requirement | Cessation; asset transfer to community; contract dissolution |
| **Transboundary harm** | Upstream action damaging downstream watershed | Cessation; restoration; compensation to downstream Assembly |

**How to Bring a Case**

1. **Document the violation** — water quality tests, usage records, photographs, witness statements
2. **File with Hydrological Chamber** — through the Digital Justice Tribunal portal (low-tech: paper filing with support from GWSC)
3. **Chamber reviews** — standing confirmed; mediation offered; if unresolved, hearing scheduled
4. **Hearing** — evidence presented; Guardians and respondents represented
5. **Decision** — binding; appeal only on procedural grounds

**Deliverable:** A clear pathway for accountability when water rights are violated.

## 2. Governance: The Subsidiarity Hierarchy

*To resolve the confusion of overlapping bodies, this framework establishes a strict hierarchy.*

---

### Earth Council: The Moral Veto

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Authority** | Supreme moral authority over planetary boundaries and sacred sites |
| **Role** | Intervenes only when: (a) a planetary boundary is crossed, (b) a sacred watershed is threatened with irreversible harm, or (c) Indigenous rights are systematically violated |
| **Mechanism** | Binding veto over any water governance decision. Veto may be overridden only by supermajority (90%) of BAZ Water Assemblies in the affected region. |
| **Composition** | Indigenous knowledge holders, Earth stewards, Rights of Nature advocates |

### Global Water & Sanitation Council (GWSC): Standards & Finance

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Authority** | Sets technical standards; manages Global Commons Fund allocation |
| **Role** | Does not operate infrastructure. Does not override local decisions that meet standards. |
| **Standards** | Water quality (WHO guidelines), sanitation safety, infrastructure resilience, data reporting |
| **Finance** | Allocates Global Commons Fund to BAZs meeting standards. Provides technical assistance. |
| **Additional Role** | Convenes and supports Basin Councils for transboundary watersheds (see Section 2.4). Coordinates emergency WASH response across BAZs (see Section 4). |
| **Composition** | 50% BAZ Water Assembly representatives, 30% technical experts, 20% Indigenous representatives |

### BAZ Water Assemblies: Operational Authority

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Authority** | Full operational authority over water infrastructure, allocation, and protection within their watershed |
| **Role** | Builds and maintains infrastructure. Sets tariffs. Manages Compute-Water Tariff. Exercises guardianship. |
| **Accountability** | Annual reporting to GWSC. Public audits. Subject to Hydrological Chamber rulings. |
| **Composition** | As defined in Step 2 of the Starter Kit |

### Transboundary Watersheds: Shared Basin Governance

**The Problem:** Many of the world's most critical watersheds cross BAZ, national, and regional borders. The Nile, Mekong, Indus, Amazon, Congo, Danube, and dozens of others involve multiple jurisdictions with competing interests. The subsidiarity principle — authority flows from the watershed up — does not resolve the case where the watershed itself spans multiple governance structures.

**The Mechanism: Basin Councils**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Formation** | When a watershed crosses two or more BAZ or national boundaries, the affected Water Assemblies jointly form a Basin Council |
| **Composition** | Equal representation from each Water Assembly in the basin, plus Indigenous governing bodies with territory in the basin, plus a GWSC technical advisor (non-voting) |
| **Authority** | Binding authority over: water allocation between jurisdictions, upstream-downstream impact disputes, shared infrastructure projects, and basin-wide pollution standards |
| **Decision-Making** | Consensus required for water allocation decisions; supermajority (75%) for shared infrastructure; any Water Assembly may invoke the Hydrological Chamber for disputes |
| **Limits** | Basin Councils do not override individual Water Assemblies on purely local matters. They govern only the shared resource and cross-boundary effects. |

**Allocation Principles**

When water must be shared between upstream and downstream users, allocation follows this priority order:

1. Human needs (drinking water, sanitation, hygiene) — all jurisdictions equally
2. Ecosystem minimum flows — scientifically determined, co-designed with Indigenous knowledge holders
3. Smallholder agriculture — prioritized over industrial agriculture
4. Industrial and commercial use — allocated proportionally with preference to less water-stressed jurisdictions

**Dispute Resolution**

Transboundary disputes that cannot be resolved within the Basin Council are referred to the Hydrological Chamber with expedited review (30 days for mediation, 60 days for hearing). The GWSC may intervene as amicus if the dispute affects global water security.

**Existing Treaty Integration**

Basin Councils operate alongside existing international water agreements (UN Watercourses Convention, bilateral river treaties). Where existing agreements are consistent with this framework, they are respected. Where they conflict, the framework's human-rights-first allocation principle takes precedence for participating BAZs, but does not override non-participating sovereign states.

### What Was Eliminated

| Body | Reason |
|------|--------|
| **Planetary Health Council oversight** | Redundant; Earth Council holds veto |
| **Interspecies Councils as governance bodies** | Moved to advisory role |
| **Multiple overlapping advisory bodies** | Consolidated under BAZ Water Assemblies |
| **Global Equity Ombudsperson** | Functions absorbed by Hydrological Chamber |

**Principle:** Authority flows from the watershed up. Global bodies set standards and provide funding. Local bodies hold all operational power. Basin Councils coordinate where water itself crosses boundaries.

## 3. The Economic Model: Three Core Mechanisms

*This framework relies on three core funding mechanisms. All other financing options (green bonds, microcredit, carbon credits) are available as supplements but are not required for implementation.*

---

### Mechanism 1: Global Commons Fund

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Base funding for water infrastructure, operations, and maintenance |
| **Eligibility** | Any BAZ with chartered Water Assembly, watershed map, and FPIC compliance |
| **Allocation** | Needs-based formula: population, water stress, existing infrastructure, capacity |
| **Annual Target** | $10B for Africa, $8B for Asia, $5B for Latin America, $2B for SIDS by 2030. These are aspirational targets; actual mobilization will depend on contributor commitments and RTZ growth. |
| **Accountability** | Annual public audit; progress reporting; water quality monitoring |

### Mechanism 2: Compute-Water Tariff

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Capture value from digital economy to fund water security and community well-being |
| **Who Pays** | Data centers, AI training facilities, large-scale computing operations using water for cooling |
| **Rate** | Tiered by water stress: 5× (moderate), 10× (high), 20× (extreme) standard industrial rate |
| **Alternative** | Closed-loop, zero-extraction water recycling facilities exempt from tariff |
| **Revenue Use** | At least 50% to AUBI top-ups for watershed households; 30% to community water infrastructure; 20% to watershed restoration |
| **Enforcement** | Water Assembly monitors; non-payment results in water shutoff; Hydrological Chamber adjudicates |

### Mechanism 3: Community Equity Requirement & Anti-Privatization Safeguards

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Ensure that private or hybrid water projects benefit communities, not just investors. Prevent predatory privatization of water infrastructure. |
| **Requirement** | Any private or hybrid WASH project must have ≥20% community ownership or revenue share |
| **Vehicle** | Revenue held in Community Trust Fund managed by Water Assembly |
| **Scope** | Applies to: desalination plants, bottled water operations, wastewater treatment for private use, any extraction for commercial purposes |
| **Enforcement** | Hydrological Chamber may order cessation or redistribution for non-compliance |

**Anti-Privatization Safeguards**

These safeguards apply to any private sector involvement in water infrastructure within a BAZ:

| Safeguard | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Human Rights First** | No private partnership may reduce access to safe water for any household in the watershed. If access declines following a private partnership, the Water Assembly may invoke emergency review. |
| **Exit Clauses** | Every private partnership contract must include exit clauses that allow the Water Assembly to dissolve the partnership and transfer assets to community stewardship if: (a) the partner violates water quality standards, (b) the partner raises prices beyond affordability thresholds set by the Assembly, (c) community ownership requirements are not met, or (d) the partner is found by the Hydrological Chamber to have violated the framework. |
| **Asset Transfer on Exit** | When an exit clause is triggered, water infrastructure built or operated by the private partner transfers to the Community Trust Fund at depreciated book value, not market value. The partner may not remove or disable infrastructure during the transition period. |
| **Affordability Lock** | Water tariffs for household use may not exceed 3% of the lowest income quintile's monthly income in the watershed. This threshold may be adjusted by the Water Assembly but never upward without supermajority (75%) approval. |
| **No Profit on Scarcity** | Private partners may not increase prices during water shortages or emergencies. Emergency pricing is frozen at pre-crisis levels; any additional costs borne by the partner or covered by the Global Commons Fund emergency allocation. |
| **Transparency** | Private partners must publish annual financial reports on water operations, including profit margins, investment, and water quality data, accessible through the public dashboard. |

## 4. Crisis & Emergency WASH

*Water crises do not wait for governance structures to mature. This section provides the operational framework for emergency response.*

### Principles

Water emergencies follow a single allocation rule: **human survival needs are met first, unconditionally.** No tariff, contract, or commercial obligation takes precedence over emergency drinking water and sanitation.

### Crisis Types and Response

| Crisis | Detection | Immediate Response (0-72 hrs) | Medium-Term (weeks-months) |
|--------|-----------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Flood / Contamination Event** | Water Assembly monitoring; GWSC early warning systems; community reporting | Deploy mobile water treatment and distribution units; activate emergency Global Commons Fund disbursement; coordinate with Red Cross/UNHCR if cross-border | Assess infrastructure damage; temporary systems operational; rebuild planning begins with climate-adaptation requirements |
| **Drought / Scarcity Emergency** | GWSC Water Stress Index alerts; Basin Council monitoring for transboundary impacts | Activate tiered rationing (human needs → ecosystems → agriculture → industry); emergency water transport if needed; suspend all commercial extraction | Groundwater recharge acceleration; emergency infrastructure (desalination, rainwater) deployed; Compute-Water Tariff revenues redirected to emergency fund |
| **Conflict Zone WASH** | GWSC conflict monitoring; coordination with Peace & Conflict Resolution Framework | Deploy mobile WASH units with protected status; coordinate with humanitarian agencies; WASH workers designated as protected non-combatants | Establish parallel supply systems independent of contested infrastructure; fund through emergency Global Commons Fund allocation |
| **Pandemic / Health Emergency** | BAZ health monitoring; coordination with Planetary Health Framework | Scale hygiene infrastructure rapidly; deploy community handwashing stations; emergency sanitation for overflow healthcare facilities | Integrate permanent hygiene infrastructure; wastewater surveillance for pathogen monitoring |

### Emergency Funding

The Global Commons Fund maintains a standing emergency reserve equal to 10% of annual disbursements. Emergency funding is:
- Disbursed within 48 hours of Water Assembly declaration of emergency
- Does not require full application process — Assembly declaration and GWSC confirmation sufficient
- Replenished from annual contributions, not deducted from BAZ's regular allocation

### Coordination

- **Within a BAZ:** Water Assembly leads response, coordinating with local health and emergency services
- **Across BAZs:** GWSC coordinates cross-BAZ response, allocating shared resources and preventing competition between affected watersheds
- **Across nations:** Basin Councils coordinate transboundary emergency response; GWSC provides diplomatic support
- **With external agencies:** GWSC maintains standing coordination agreements with UNHCR, Red Cross/Red Crescent, and WHO for emergency WASH deployment

*Full crisis protocols including equipment specifications, supply chain pre-positioning, and coordination templates are in Appendix E.*

## 5. Implementation Pathway

| Phase | Timeframe | Key Activities |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| **Phase 0: Assembly** | 0-12 months | BAZ forms Water Assembly. Maps watershed. Conducts needs assessment. Establishes guardianship. Identifies transboundary connections. |
| **Phase 1: Foundation** | 1-3 years | BAZ draws down Global Commons Fund. Builds priority infrastructure. Enacts Compute-Water Tariff. Joins Basin Council if transboundary. |
| **Phase 2: Scaling** | 3-7 years | Universal access achieved. Watershed restoration underway. Tariff revenues flowing to AUBI. |
| **Phase 3: Stewardship** | 7+ years | Self-sustaining systems. Intergenerational monitoring. Watershed health improving. |

### Tiered Adoption

| Tier | Components | Suitable For |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Tier 1: Foundational** | Starter Kit Steps 1-3 | Low-capacity or fragile regions |
| **Tier 2: Intermediate** | Add Compute-Water Tariff | Regions with data center presence |
| **Tier 3: Full** | All five Starter Kit steps + transboundary governance | All BAZs |

### Appendix C: Legal Framework: Hydrological Chamber & Guardianship

**Legal Personhood**

Under the Treaty for Our Only Home, major rivers, lakes, aquifers, and watersheds are recognized as legal persons with:
- The right to exist, flow, and regenerate
- The right to be free from pollution
- The right to legal representation and standing

**Guardianship**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Primary Guardian** | BAZ Water Assembly for the watershed |
| **Co-Guardian** | Indigenous governing bodies for waters within their territories |
| **Basin Guardian** | Basin Council for transboundary water bodies (shared guardianship between member Assemblies) |
| **Appointment** | Automatic upon Assembly chartering; no additional legal action required |
| **Duties** | Monitor water health; bring cases for violations; manage restoration funds; report annually |
| **Liability** | Guardians are immune from personal liability for good-faith actions |

**Hydrological Chamber Procedures**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Filing** | Any Guardian may file; community members may petition Guardian; if refused, appeal directly |
| **Mediation** | Mandatory 60-day mediation period before hearing (30 days for transboundary emergencies) |
| **Hearing** | Open to public; evidence presented; Guardian and respondent represented |
| **Standing** | Water body itself has standing; Guardian acts as representative |
| **Remedies** | Cessation orders; restoration requirements; fines; damages to Watershed Restoration Fund; contract dissolution for privatization violations; asset transfer orders |
| **Appeals** | Limited to procedural error; no re-litigation of facts |

### Appendix E: Crisis & Emergency WASH Protocols (Detail)

*This appendix provides operational detail supporting Section 4.*

**Pre-Positioned Emergency Supplies**

The GWSC maintains regional emergency stockpiles in coordination with UNHCR and Red Cross/Red Crescent:
- Mobile water treatment units (capacity: 10,000 liters/day per unit)
- Emergency sanitation kits (portable latrines, hygiene supplies)
- Water testing kits for rapid contamination assessment
- Emergency communication equipment for coordination

**Emergency Declaration Process**

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Water Assembly declares water emergency | Immediate |
| 2 | GWSC confirms and activates emergency funding | Within 48 hours |
| 3 | Regional stockpile deployment authorized | Within 48 hours |
| 4 | Basin Council notified if transboundary | Within 24 hours |
| 5 | Coordination with humanitarian agencies activated | Within 72 hours |

**Conflict Zone Protections**

- WASH workers in conflict zones are designated as protected non-combatants under the Treaty for Our Only Home
- Water infrastructure is designated as protected civilian infrastructure; deliberate destruction constitutes a violation adjudicable by the Hydrological Chamber
- Emergency WASH delivery may operate through neutral corridors negotiated by the GWSC with support from the Peace & Conflict Resolution Framework

**Post-Crisis Recovery**

- Infrastructure rebuilt to climate-adapted standards (not just restored to pre-crisis condition)
- Emergency debt moratoria: BAZs in crisis may pause Global Commons Fund reporting requirements for up to 12 months
- Community-led recovery planning: Water Assembly leads reconstruction priorities, with GWSC technical support
