# The Sundown Protocol v2.2

## A Just Transition Framework for Retiring Harmful Industries and Rebirthing Regenerative Futures

**Document Type:** Tier 2 Foundational Application Layer — Core Governance Document
**Status:** Governance Architecture — Representation-Authority Conformance Draft
**Version:** 2.2
**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Representation interface:** Integrated Meta-Governance Framework v1.6.1

### v2.2 revision

This revision adds displacement accounting to the pre-ratification leverage strategy (§4.6) and an institutional lifecycle rule to the architecture (§3.5). Neither changes the transition timeline, the sector list, or any body's authority.

§4.6 records what the leverage instruments conserve. Procurement embargo, supply chain exclusion, divestment, and asset freeze all operate under partial jurisdictional coverage, and under partial coverage an excluded industry concentrates in the most permissive destination it can reach rather than distributing evenly. The Standards Board now publishes a displacement ledger before each escalation level and pauses supply-side escalation where relocated volume exceeds reduced volume. The section claims only that exclusion relocates until substitution arrives — it does not claim the strategy creates harm that would not otherwise exist.

§3.5 separates the bodies built to complete from the bodies maintaining permanent obligations, and guards each against its own characteristic failure: finite bodies deferring their completion criterion, standing bodies defunded because nothing has gone wrong. Standing bodies transfer their obligations on capacity loss rather than lapsing.

### v2.1.1 bounded revision

This revision applies Meta-Governance v1.6.1 §3.7 to institutional composition and selection only. Sundown may identify the representation functions needed for a just transition, but a percentage, seat, selector, voting rule, veto, term, or vacancy rule becomes binding only through the valid constituting instrument of the body concerned. Indigenous participation and authority arise through affected peoples' own processes. No electoral-administration machinery is created.

## 1. Executive Summary

The Sundown Protocol provides a just, compassionate, and enforceable pathway for retiring industries that are incompatible with planetary health — fossil fuels, extractive mining, industrial agriculture, and others identified by the Planetary Health Council.

### The Challenge

Legacy industries represent trillions in infrastructure and employ millions. They cannot be left to chaotic market-driven decline, which would devastate communities and strand workers. Yet voluntary transition is insufficient to meet planetary boundary timelines. We need a managed, just, and binding process.

### The Opportunity

This protocol is not anti-industry. It is pro-future. It offers a roadmap for vital sectors and their workforces to "graduate" into the regenerative economy — protecting workers, healing ecosystems, and positioning first-movers as global leaders in the new economy.

## 3. Institutional Architecture

The Sundown Protocol uses three bodies with clear roles. This replaces the complex web of councils, offices, and teams in previous versions.

### 3.1 Three Bodies, Clear Hierarchy

| Body | Role | Composition |
|------|------|-------------|
| **Planetary Health Council (existing)** | Sets the trigger only to the extent its own constituting instrument grants that authority | Existing GGF body; Sundown does not alter its composition |
| **Global Transition Standards Board** | Sets transition standards and templates to the extent validly constituted | **Representation requirements:** strong worker/union voice; affected Indigenous authority where relevant; technical, ecological, community, disability, and implementation expertise. The former 50/25/25 formula is illustrative historical design material, not a self-executing quota. |
| **BAZ Transition Authorities** | Executes locally within the authority granted by the participating BAZ/jurisdiction | A participating BAZ or competent local authority constitutes its own body. Worker, affected-community, and relevant Indigenous participation are requirements to be translated into lawful local composition rather than imposed by Sundown. |

**Composition and selection rule:** The Global Transition Standards Board and any BAZ Transition Authority shall identify constituency/affectedness, expertise, delegated institutional authority, and Indigenous authority separately. Their own valid charters determine selection, terms, vacancies, recall/removal, voting thresholds, and challenges. Selection does not expand jurisdiction, and representation is not FPIC or consent.

### 3.5 Completion and Succession

Sundown's bodies are not all the same kind of institution, and the difference determines how each should end. A body created for a bounded task should be built to complete; a body maintaining a permanent obligation should not carry a sunset clause it will only be tempted to evade. Confusing the two is a design error in both directions.

| Body | Type | Terminal condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Global Transition Standards Board** | Finite | Standards and templates published and adopted for every listed sector, with no sector in active transition. Dissolves; template library transfers to the Institutional Regeneration Framework. |
| **BAZ Transition Authority** | Finite | All target facilities within the BAZ have completed remediation sign-off. Dissolves; residual obligations transfer to standing BAZ governance. |
| **Just Transition Fund** | Finite with tail | Bond obligations discharged and the supported worker cohort has completed its entitlement period. Residual remediation liabilities transfer to the BAZ before dissolution, never lapse with it. |
| **Indigenous Monitoring Teams** | Standing | None. FPIC compliance and sacred-site integrity are permanent couplings that do not end with the transition. |
| **Transition Heritage Council** | Standing | None. |
| **Long-term ecological monitoring (§10.4)** | Standing | None. Decadal audit intervals continue past the dissolution of the bodies that created the sites. |

**Manufactured-demand guard (finite bodies).** A finite body may not expand its sector list, extend its timelines, or redefine its remit in a way whose effect is to defer its own completion criterion. Any extension requires ratification by the Planetary Health Council with published justification addressing this specific risk.

**Premature-sunset guard (standing bodies).** The success of a monitoring body shows up as the absence of violations. Quiet years are therefore not evidence that its coupling has ended — they are the expected signature of it working. Defunding, merging, or dissolving a standing body requires an affirmative demonstration that the underlying obligation no longer exists, not an observation that nothing has gone wrong. Budget reductions to standing bodies may not be justified by favourable readings on any indicator those bodies themselves produce.

**Succession on capacity loss.** Where a standing body can no longer maintain its obligation — loss of independent funding, erosion of the Indigenous governing bodies that appoint it, loss of technical capacity — its final function is to transfer the obligation to a successor that can, before its own decay drops the obligation entirely. Transfer, not lapse, is the terminal act for a standing body.

## 4. Pre-Ratification Strategy: Building Leverage

The Sundown Protocol does not assume the Treaty for Our Only Home is fully ratified or that the Digital Justice Tribunal has binding authority. This section provides mechanisms for building leverage before those institutions exist.

### 4.1 Pathfinder Compact

Voluntary coalition of BAZs, cities, and nations committed to the transition.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Commitment** | Signatories commit to sunset target industries by agreed timelines |
| **Benefits** | Preferential access to Global Commons Fund support; technical assistance; leadership recognition |
| **Coordination** | Pathfinder members coordinate procurement, investment, and trade policies |

### 4.2 Procurement Embargo

The primary enforcement mechanism before global treaties exist.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Commitment** | Pathfinder members legally bind themselves to refuse all public contracts, subsidies, and market access to corporations in target industries that refuse to enter the sunset process |
| **Scope** | Includes municipal, regional, and national procurement; infrastructure projects; public services |
| **Implementation** | Model legislation provided; coordinated through Pathfinder network |
| **Consequence** | Non-compliant corporations lose access to markets representing trillions in annual procurement |

### 4.3 Supply Chain Exclusion

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Requirement** | Pathfinder members require all suppliers to certify they are not sourcing from non-compliant corporations |
| **Verification** | Digital Product Passports (Gaian Trade Framework) track supply chain compliance |
| **Escalation** | Non-compliant suppliers are excluded from Pathfinder supply chains |

### 4.4 Divestment Coordination

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Commitment** | Pathfinder pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, and public investment vehicles commit to divesting from non-compliant corporations |
| **Coordination** | Shared divestment timelines; coordinated public announcements |
| **Target** | Create capital markets penalty for non-compliance |

### 4.5 Escalation Ladder

| Level | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **1. Public Naming** | Non-compliant corporations listed on Public Transition Dashboard |
| **2. Contract Exclusion** | Pathfinder members exclude from public contracts |
| **3. Supply Chain Exclusion** | Pathfinder supply chains reject non-compliant sources |
| **4. Investment Freeze** | Pathfinder public funds divest; new investment prohibited |
| **5. Asset Freeze** | Assets in Pathfinder jurisdictions frozen pending compliance |

### 4.6 Displacement Accounting

Every instrument in §4.1–4.5 has partial jurisdictional coverage. Exclusion from Pathfinder markets does not by itself reduce global production of a target commodity — it changes where that production sits and who owns it. The Protocol's leverage strategy is sound, but it is a *relocation* instrument until demand-side substitution catches up, and it must be accounted for as one.

**The planning benchmark.** An industry facing partial-coverage exclusion does not distribute itself evenly across the remaining options. It concentrates in the most permissive jurisdiction it can reach and discover. Escalation should therefore be planned against the tail of the reachable regulatory environment, not its average — the relevant question at each level is not "how much of the world now excludes this industry" but "what is the single weakest reachable destination, and how much capacity can it absorb."

**Displacement ledger.** Before each escalation level is triggered, the Global Transition Standards Board publishes a ledger naming, for each instrument in play: the quantity that is conserved absent demand-side change, the mechanism by which it relocates, and the boundary beyond which that conservation fails.

| Instrument | Quantity conserved absent demand-side change | Relocation mechanism | Escape boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Procurement embargo** | Output volume of the target commodity | Sale into non-Pathfinder public and private markets | Pathfinder demand share large enough that excluded output cannot clear at a viable price |
| **Supply chain exclusion** | Output volume, one tier removed | Re-intermediation through uncertified suppliers; relabelling at the border | Digital Product Passport coverage extending beyond Pathfinder supply chains |
| **Divestment** | Ownership of the asset — not its emissions | Transfer of shares to buyers with weaker disclosure and no transition obligation | Divestment paired with production covenants or retirement conditions attached at sale |
| **Asset freeze** | Productive capacity | Reconstitution of equivalent capacity in non-Pathfinder jurisdictions | Capacity is location-bound — a specific ore body, field, or watershed that cannot be rebuilt elsewhere |

Divestment is the clearest case and worth stating plainly: a divested share is bought by someone. Unless retirement or covenant conditions attach at sale, the transaction conserves the emissions exactly and changes only the holder — and typically moves the asset toward a holder with less disclosure obligation than the seller.

**Escalation pause.** Where the ledger shows relocated volume exceeding reduced volume across two consecutive reporting periods, further supply-side escalation pauses and the Standards Board must report to the Planetary Health Council on demand-side substitution as the binding constraint. Escalating a supply-side instrument that is measurably relocating rather than reducing spends Pathfinder political capital to move a problem across a border.

**What this section does not claim.** It does not assert that the leverage strategy creates harm that would not otherwise exist. The conserved quantities named above are conserved only within the stated domain and only absent demand-side change; the whole point of §5–§6 is to move that boundary. The claim is narrower and checkable: where coverage is partial and substitution has not yet arrived, exclusion relocates, and a framework that does not measure the relocation cannot tell the difference between winning and exporting.

## 5. Implementation Pathway

### 5.1 Phased Timeline with Transition Readiness Indicators

Each phase advances when specific, measurable Transition Readiness Indicators (TRIs) are met — not on fixed calendar dates alone. TRIs ensure that no phase advances before the ground conditions support it, and no phase lingers when conditions are ready.

| Phase | Duration | Key Activities | Decision Point | Transition Readiness Indicators |
|-------|----------|----------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| **Phase 0: Pathfinder** | Ongoing | Voluntary coalition building; procurement embargos; pilot transitions | Pathfinder Compact signed | 15+ Pathfinder signatories; model legislation adopted in 3+ jurisdictions; first procurement embargos operational |
| **Phase 1: Designation** | Year 1 | Planetary Health Council triggers sunset; bond posted; timeline set | Sunset mandate issued | BHI threshold formally breached; bond calculation methodology published; target corporations notified |
| **Phase 2: Roadmap** | Years 1-2 | BAZ Transition Authorities co-create plan with unions, communities, Indigenous governments | Roadmap ratified locally | 80% of affected BAZs have ratified roadmaps; AUBI enrollment mechanisms operational; Capacity Engine training slots allocated |
| **Phase 3: Transition** | Years 3-15+ | Managed decommissioning; worker retraining; remediation; asset transfer | BHI improvement verified | 50% of workforce retrained and re-employed; 30% of remediation targets met; no unresolved FPIC violations |
| **Phase 4: Stewardship** | Ongoing | Remediated land under BAZ stewardship; long-term monitoring | Transfer completed | BHI targets met for ecosystem recovery; 90% of workers re-employed; asset transfer legally executed |

### 5.2 Adaptive Mechanisms

| Factor | Adjustment | Trigger |
|--------|------------|---------|
| **Technology Breakthrough** | Timeline accelerates | Planetary Health Council certifies a technology that materially reduces transition cost or duration |
| **Worker Transition Needs** | Timeline extends | Worker Satisfaction Trajectory falls below 60%; re-employment rate below 50% at midpoint |
| **Climate Urgency** | Timeline accelerates | BHI threshold breach reaches critical severity; Planetary Health Council invokes Climate Emergency Clause |
| **Crisis** | Temporary pause or acceleration | Crisis Command Protocol activated; 90-day limit on emergency adjustments |

### 5.3 Phase Details

**Phase 0: Pathfinder (Ongoing)**
- Voluntary coalition building
- Procurement embargos established
- Pilot transitions in willing jurisdictions
- Pathfinder Compact signed

**Phase 1: Designation (Year 1)**
- Planetary Health Council issues binding sunset trigger based on BHI thresholds
- Target corporations notified
- Pre-Emptive Ecological Bond posted (see Section 9.2)
- Sunset timeline set (varies by sector — see Section 6)

**Phase 2: Roadmap (Years 1-2)**
- BAZ Transition Authority convenes unions, communities, Indigenous governments
- Binding Transition Roadmap developed using sector-specific templates (see Section 6), including:
  - Decommissioning schedule
  - Worker retraining plan (tethered to Capacity Engine)
  - Community investment priorities
  - Remediation plan
  - Asset transfer plan
- Roadmap ratified locally; Global Transition Standards Board certifies compliance

**Phase 3: Transition (Years 3-15+)**
- Managed decommissioning proceeds per roadmap
- Workers retrained and placed in Community Work Teams
- Just Transition Fund disburses support
- Indigenous Monitoring Teams oversee remediation
- Remediated land/assets prepared for transfer

**Phase 4: Stewardship (Ongoing)**
- Remediated land/assets transferred to BAZ Stewardship Trusts
- Long-term monitoring (10-year intervals) ensures ecological recovery
- Long-term land-use plans require a credible youth/intergenerational participation pathway. A Youth Future Council has review or veto power only where the competent land-use authority's own charter or law grants it.

## 6. Sector-Specific Transition Guidance

Shutting down a coal mine is operationally different from transitioning industrial agriculture. The Global Transition Standards Board develops sector-specific templates that BAZ Transition Authorities adapt to local conditions. The following provides guidance for the primary target sectors.

### 6.1 Fossil Fuels (Oil, Gas, Coal)

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|---------|
| **Typical Timeline** | 10-15 years from designation to full decommissioning |
| **Key Workforce Pathways** | Oil/gas rig workers → offshore wind installation/maintenance; coal miners → geothermal drilling, mine remediation; refinery workers → hydrogen/biofuel processing |
| **Remediation Priorities** | Well capping and orphaned well cleanup; mine site ecological restoration; soil and groundwater decontamination |
| **Bond Calculation Focus** | Orphaned well liability; tailings dam remediation; groundwater treatment duration |
| **Critical Dependencies** | Energy replacement must be operational before decommissioning completes; coordinate with Hearthstone Protocol for community energy replacement |
| **Relevant Precedents** | Germany's coal phase-out; North Sea oil decommissioning; Appalachian transition (as cautionary case) |

### 6.2 Industrial Agriculture

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|---------|
| **Typical Timeline** | 8-12 years from designation to full transition |
| **Key Workforce Pathways** | Monoculture farmworkers → regenerative agriculture, agroforestry; agrochemical workers → biological soil amendment production; logistics workers → local food distribution cooperatives |
| **Remediation Priorities** | Soil health restoration (decompaction, organic matter rebuilding); watershed rehabilitation from fertilizer/pesticide runoff; pollinator habitat restoration |
| **Bond Calculation Focus** | Soil restoration cost per hectare; downstream water treatment; biodiversity recovery timeline |
| **Critical Dependencies** | Food supply continuity — transition cannot create food shortages; phased crop-by-crop approach; link to Regenerative Food Systems Framework |
| **Relevant Precedents** | Cuba's forced organic transition (1990s); Sikkim's organic state transition (India); EU Common Agricultural Policy reform |

### 6.3 Extractive Mining (Non-Fuel)

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|---------|
| **Typical Timeline** | 10-20 years depending on mineral criticality |
| **Key Workforce Pathways** | Miners → urban mining/recycling operations; processing workers → circular economy material recovery; geologists → ecological survey and remediation |
| **Remediation Priorities** | Tailings dam stabilization and remediation; acid mine drainage treatment; landscape reconstruction |
| **Bond Calculation Focus** | Perpetual water treatment costs; tailings storage facility monitoring (some require centuries); community health monitoring |
| **Critical Dependencies** | Minerals critical to the energy transition (lithium, cobalt, rare earths) require extended timelines and mandatory investment in circular alternatives before sunset can complete |
| **Special Provision** | Industries deemed harmful but temporarily essential receive extended timelines under a "Critical Transition Industry" designation from the Planetary Health Council, with mandatory annual review and binding investment in substitution research |

### 6.4 Petrochemicals & Plastics

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|---------|
| **Typical Timeline** | 12-18 years given supply chain complexity |
| **Key Workforce Pathways** | Chemical plant operators → bio-based materials production; plastics workers → circular economy sorting/reprocessing; R&D staff → green chemistry |
| **Remediation Priorities** | Chemical contamination cleanup; plastic waste legacy management; air and water quality restoration near facilities |
| **Bond Calculation Focus** | Contaminated site cleanup; long-term health monitoring for surrounding communities; legacy waste processing |
| **Critical Dependencies** | Medical and safety-critical plastics require substitution before sunset; coordinate with Technology Governance Framework for alternative material certification |

### 6.5 Template Development

The Global Transition Standards Board develops sector-specific templates that include: standardized bond calculation methodologies, workforce skills-mapping tools, remediation benchmarks tied to BHI thresholds, and model roadmap structures. BAZ Transition Authorities adapt these templates to local conditions — the templates provide a starting point, not a mandate.

## 7. Differentiated Pathways: Petro-States and the Global South

The Sundown Protocol cannot apply uniformly to all countries. A nation where fossil fuel revenues constitute 80% of GDP faces a fundamentally different transition challenge than a diversified industrial economy phasing out one sector. This section provides differentiated pathways for the hardest cases.

### 7.1 Petro-State Transitions

| Element | Approach |
|---------|---------|
| **The Problem** | For nations like Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, and Venezuela, fossil fuels are not one sector among many — they are the economic foundation. A standard sunset timeline would collapse the state. |
| **Extended Timelines** | Petro-states receive extended Phase 3 timelines (15-25 years), calibrated to the pace at which alternative revenue streams can replace hydrocarbon income |
| **Diversification Support** | Pathfinder members and the Global Commons Fund provide priority technical assistance for economic diversification: renewable energy export capacity, sovereign wealth fund restructuring, and industrial policy development |
| **Revenue Replacement Sequencing** | Sunset milestones are tied to diversification milestones — decommissioning does not outpace revenue replacement. Specific TRIs: non-hydrocarbon GDP share, renewable energy capacity, employment diversification index |
| **Sovereign Wealth Restructuring** | The Global Transition Standards Board provides advisory support for restructuring sovereign wealth funds away from hydrocarbon dependence, including portfolio transition pathways and renewable energy investment strategies |
| **Geopolitical Playbook** | Pathfinder members offer trade access, technology transfer, and investment as positive incentives for voluntary petro-state participation, coordinated through the Gaian Trade Framework |

### 7.2 Global South Differentiated Pathways

| Element | Approach |
|---------|---------|
| **The Problem** | Many Global South nations have extractive industries imposed during colonial periods and lack the institutional capacity, capital, or alternative infrastructure for rapid transition |
| **Enhanced Support** | Priority access to Global Commons Fund grants (not loans); technical assistance from RTZ mentorship programs; debt relief linked to transition compliance via the Financial Systems Framework |
| **Capacity Building** | Pre-transition institutional development support — helping establish BAZ governance structures, train local transition authorities, and build monitoring capacity before sunset mandates are issued |
| **Colonial Legacy Recognition** | Reparations Levy calculations account for colonial extraction history; Global North nations with historical extraction operations in the Global South bear proportionally higher levy burden |
| **Food Sovereignty Safeguard** | For industrial agriculture transitions in countries with food security vulnerabilities, transition timelines are extended and food self-sufficiency milestones are mandatory prerequisites before monoculture phase-out advances |

### 7.3 What This Does Not Mean

Differentiated pathways are not exemptions. Every nation and corporation is subject to the Sundown Protocol. Extended timelines and enhanced support are provided precisely to make compliance achievable — not to create permanent exceptions. The Planetary Health Council reviews all differentiated pathways at 5-year intervals and adjusts based on progress.

## 8. Worker & Community Protection

Worker protections are tethered to existing GGF frameworks rather than inventing new mechanisms.

### 8.1 Income Protection

| Element | Mechanism |
|---------|-----------|
| **Income Replacement** | Workers displaced by sunset mandates are automatically enrolled in AUBI at the highest tier |
| **Duration** | Full income replacement for 3 years, tapering to 70% until re-employment |
| **No Gap** | Enrollment begins immediately upon displacement; no waiting period |

### 8.2 Retraining & New Employment

| Element | Mechanism |
|---------|-----------|
| **Retraining** | Workers access the Capacity Engine for retraining; certifications recognized across BAZs |
| **Pathways** | Priority training in remediation, renewable energy, regenerative agriculture, and community care. Sector-specific pathways outlined in Section 6. |
| **Employment** | Retrained workers placed in Community Work Teams (Work in Liberation Framework) for remediation work |

### 8.3 Healthcare & Housing

| Element | Mechanism |
|---------|-----------|
| **Toxic Exposure** | Lifetime healthcare coverage for pollution-linked illnesses funded by Just Transition Fund |
| **Relocation** | Subsidies for workers who must relocate; priority access to Community Land Trust housing (Hearthstone Protocol) |

### 8.4 Community Reparations

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Health Legacy Monitoring** | Independent monitoring of pollution-affected communities; ongoing healthcare funding |
| **Infrastructure Investment** | Priority funding for community infrastructure in affected areas (schools, clinics, transit) |

### 8.5 The Transition Workforce Bill of Rights

Every worker affected by a sunset mandate is guaranteed:

1. **Income Security:** AUBI income replacement from day one
2. **Retraining:** Free access to Capacity Engine retraining programs
3. **New Employment:** Placement in Community Work Teams
4. **Healthcare:** Lifetime coverage for toxic exposure
5. **Housing:** Relocation support and housing access
6. **Dignity:** Ceremonial recognition of service
7. **Voice:** Representation on BAZ Transition Authority

## 9. Financial Mechanisms

### 9.1 Just Transition Fund

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Manages bonds, disburses funds for remediation and worker support |
| **Oversight** | Co-chaired by Global Transition Standards Board and Indigenous Monitoring Teams |
| **Transparency** | Real-time dashboard on public transition platform; audited annually |

### 9.2 Pre-Emptive Ecological Bond

This closes the bankruptcy loophole that has allowed extractive industries to abandon cleanup costs.

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Trigger** | When an industry is designated for sunset under Phase 1, all corporations operating in that sector must post a financial bond within 12 months |
| **Bond Size** | 100% of estimated ecological cleanup cost, calculated by independent assessors (BAZ environmental councils + Indigenous monitoring teams) using sector-specific methodologies (see Section 6) |
| **Bond Structure** | Held in escrow by Just Transition Fund. Cannot be used as collateral. Cannot be insured against. Executive bonuses frozen until bond is posted. |
| **Consequence of Non-Compliance** | If bond not posted, the corporation's physical assets (mines, wells, facilities) are immediately placed under BAZ trusteeship. Intellectual property licenses for transition-relevant technologies are made openly available under compulsory license. |

**Why This Matters:** Without this mechanism, corporations extract value until the last moment, declare bankruptcy, and leave cleanup to taxpayers. The bond ensures they cannot escape accountability.

### 9.3 Funding Sources

| Source | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Pre-Emptive Ecological Bonds** | Primary funding for remediation |
| **Reparations Levy** | Additional levy on historical emissions and extraction |
| **Transition Bonds** | Issued by Pathfinder members, backed by Global Commons Fund guarantees |
| **Global Commons Fund** | Base funding for worker support and capacity building |

### 9.4 Reparations Levy

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Base** | Calculated on historical emissions and extraction (1990-present). For Global South operations with colonial extraction history, the levy base extends to the colonial period and is borne proportionally by successor corporations and former colonial states. |
| **Rate** | Set by Global Transition Standards Board, calibrated to avoid economic shock |
| **Allocation** | 60% to Just Transition Fund, 20% to worker support, 20% to Global South capacity building |

| **Youth / Intergenerational Oversight** | Plans with 50+ year impacts require meaningful youth/intergenerational review. Any veto or approval right exists only if enacted by the competent BAZ, land-use, Indigenous, or other constituting authority. |

### 10.4 Long-Term Monitoring

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Audit Intervals** | Decadal audits (every 10 years) of transitioned regions |
| **Metrics** | BHI recovery, economic diversification, community well-being |
| **Transparency** | Results published on Public Transition Dashboard |

## 11. Cultural & Ceremonial Transition

### Appendix D: The Transition Workforce Bill of Rights

*Every worker affected by a sunset mandate is guaranteed:*

| Right | Guarantee |
|-------|-----------|
| **1. Income Security** | AUBI income replacement from day one; full wages for 3 years, tapering to 70% until re-employment |
| **2. Retraining** | Free access to Capacity Engine retraining programs; credentials recognized across BAZs |
| **3. New Employment** | Placement in Community Work Teams; 90% re-employment target within 24 months |
| **4. Healthcare** | Lifetime coverage for toxic exposure illnesses; funded by Just Transition Fund |
| **5. Housing** | Relocation subsidies; priority access to Community Land Trust housing |
| **6. Dignity** | Ceremonial recognition of service; heritage preservation; Transition Heritage Council representation |
| **7. Voice** | Meaningful worker representation in transition governance. A binding vote, reserved seat, or other decision right exists where the competent BAZ Transition Authority charter or applicable labour/public law enacts it. |

*This Bill of Rights is enforceable by the Digital Justice Tribunal. Violations subject to sanctions.*
