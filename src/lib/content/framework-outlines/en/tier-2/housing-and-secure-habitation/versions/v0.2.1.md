# Housing & Secure Habitation Framework v0.2.1

## Constitutional architecture for habitation interests, housing decisions, continuity, and cross-domain handoffs

**Tier:** 2 — Foundational Application Layer (provisional)  
**Status:** Consolidation-regression patch — schema-readiness review pending  
**Version:** 0.2.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Responsible:** Global Governance Frameworks initiative  
**Constitutional interface:** Constitutional Interface Specification `CIS/0.2.3`  
**Lifecycle interface:** Governance Method, Experiment, Adoption, and Implementation Authority Interface Specification `GMEAIA/0.2.1` — current compatible interface; canonical source verification required before controlled schema release  
**Authority effect:** None until lawfully adopted by a competent jurisdiction, Indigenous authority, municipal or regional authority, public-housing body, cooperative, trust, contract, or other valid constituting instrument  
**Primary role:** Governance architecture for the relationship between people and the places or accommodation duties through which they are housed, independent of who holds title and without absorbing land, finance, care, justice, migration, or infrastructure authority

> **Standfirst:** Shared constitutional rules for secure habitation: who or what holds a housing claim, what place or accommodation duty it concerns, which independently governed bases support or condition it, who may make consequential housing decisions, and how continuity survives eviction, insolvency, care or employment changes, displacement, redevelopment, disaster, climate degradation, and other cross-domain transitions. Title is not habitation. Dependency is not priority. A person may have more than one legitimate home. No metric, registry entry, external-status decision, or missing owner creates housing authority by itself.

> **Consolidation note:** v0.2 is a reduction release, not another housing-policy expansion. The v0.1 series used adversarial review to discover hidden objects and failure routes. v0.2 keeps the cases those reviews earned while reducing the architecture to four canonical object classes, nine Housing functional profiles, six shared semantic blocks, and a bounded set of conformance rules. Topic-specific records from v0.1 are retained only where they perform distinct legal work.

# 1. Purpose, constitutional object, scope, and non-authority

## 1.1 Purpose

The Framework exists to make habitation governable as a distinct constitutional and administrative object.

Its core question is:

> **What rights, duties, records, process, and continuity consequences arise because a person or collective actually inhabits, claims, or must be accommodated in relation to a place?**

Land title, zoning, disaster command, migration status, care eligibility, employment, taxation, banking, and infrastructure can all affect that question without answering it.

## 1.2 Primary constitutional object

The public constitutional object is the **secure-habitation interest**.

For the machine-readable model, the canonical object is **Habitation Interest**:

> A legally or socially cognizable habitation claim, permission, protected relation, or accommodation entitlement concerning a person or collective.

An interest may be based on tenancy, ownership occupation, customary or Indigenous law, cooperative arrangements, family or court orders, public/social housing, informal/factual residence where law attaches protection, employment, care, education, migration/reception, community arrangements, or other competent sources.

Recording an interest does not create title, permanent possession, compensation, subsidy, transferability, priority, or immunity from lawful change.

## 1.3 Why Housing and Secure Habitation remain together

**Secure habitation** is the constitutional relation.

**Housing** is the wider material and institutional system through which dwellings, affordability, allocation, repair, supply, finance, and continuity are produced.

They remain in one framework because:

- housing policy without resident continuity can produce units while displacing people;
- habitation protection without material housing systems can become a promise without dwellings, infrastructure, or affordability.

The seam is explicit. Housing may state resident-facing conditions and consequences. Land-use, construction, fiscal, employment, infrastructure, care, justice, migration, credit, and other authorities retain their own instruments.

A later split remains possible if the authority chains diverge substantially.

## 1.4 Matters outside direct scope

This Framework does not itself determine:

- title, conveyance, rematriation, property succession, or trust constitution;
- zoning, density, growth boundaries, or general municipal land-use planning;
- technical building/fire/engineering codes;
- tax liability, tax rates, or fiscal collection powers;
- mortgage underwriting, creditor priority, bank solvency, or monetary policy;
- migration/asylum/citizenship status;
- family custody, guardianship, marriage, divorce, or succession;
- care diagnosis, treatment, restraint, or custody;
- criminal guilt or punishment;
- Indigenous title or internal Indigenous law;
- tourism licensing or platform regulation;
- compulsory acquisition without separate authority;
- a universal rent formula, tenure model, social-housing quota, or construction target;
- a universal definition of **household**.

Where another domain requires a household classification, Housing references the applicable law or competent authority for that purpose.

## 1.5 No residual Housing jurisdiction

Need is not authority.

A housing crisis, homelessness, unaffordable rent, an unsafe building, a failed provider, climate risk, a conflicting claim, or absence of an obvious owner does not authorize Housing to invent:

- eviction power;
- title power;
- allocation power;
- funding power;
- tax power;
- expropriation;
- care/custody power;
- family-law power;
- immigration power;
- technical-code power.

Every consequential act must identify the competent source of authority.

## 1.6 Tier and standing

Housing remains provisionally **Tier 2 — Foundational Application Layer**.

Cross-framework standing comes from:

- adoption by competent law or institution;
- shared CIS/GMEAIA interfaces;
- explicit reciprocal framework interfaces;
- lawful contracts/charters;
- recognized Indigenous/customary authority where applicable.

Tier does not create jurisdiction.

# 2. Canonical model

v0.2 has four canonical object classes. Other familiar housing terms are roles, attributes, predicates, or prose.

## 2.1 Party

A **Party** is a person, collective, organization, authority, provider, owner, adjudicator, or other actor that holds, administers, affects, challenges, or bears duties concerning habitation.

A Party may carry:

- a purpose-appropriate identifier, including a provisional/local identifier;
- roles;
- mandates;
- representation information;
- authority source where the Party acts institutionally.

Roles may include:

- interest holder;
- owner;
- landlord;
- provider;
- operator;
- custodian/trustee;
- allocator;
- standards authority;
- continuity service;
- reviewer/adjudicator;
- employer;
- care/service authority;
- other basis authority.

**Declarative rule:** recording a role or mandate evidences it for Housing administration. It does not constitute, validate, confer, or extend the underlying role.

### 2.1.1 Presence is relational

Presence is not a global Party property.

A Party may be present differently in different places:

- none;
- transient;
- recurring/seasonal;
- ordinary/durable;
- temporarily displaced while retaining an interest;
- another jurisdiction-defined state.

Where procedural protection depends on factual residence, the jurisdiction must publish the relevant threshold.

## 2.2 Habitation Unit

A **Habitation Unit** is the purpose-appropriate physical or locally recognized place to which a unit-scoped habitation interest attaches.

It may be:

- a dwelling;
- apartment;
- room;
- house;
- mobile dwelling;
- seasonal or pastoralist site;
- informal structure;
- collective residence unit;
- another recognizable living place.

A Unit identifier is not proof of title, priority, legality, exclusivity, tax residence, household composition, or continued habitability.

### 2.2.1 Unit lifecycle

A unit may undergo:

- subdivision;
- merger/amalgamation;
- demolition;
- reconstruction;
- renumbering/re-addressing;
- re-registration;
- movement;
- informal/community reconfiguration;
- change of use.

Physical continuity and legal identity are not assumed to be identical.

A rebuilt structure on the same footprint may or may not count as the same unit for tenancy, return, restitution, allocation, or compensation. The competent external law/adjudicator decides.

Housing preserves predecessor/successor linkage without deciding substantive priority.

## 2.3 Habitation Interest

A **Habitation Interest** is the canonical representation of the secure-habitation object.

It should be able to state at minimum:

- holder Party reference(s);
- recognition status;
- scope;
- factual presence where relevant;
- relationship to a formal instrument where relevant;
- applicable habitation standard;
- exclusivity where relevant;
- Habitation Basis references;
- basis-sufficiency rule or legal-rule reference;
- Unit/place reference where scope requires one;
- challenge/continuity links.

### 2.3.1 Recognition status

A Housing record is declarative rather than constitutive.

Useful states may include:

- asserted;
- disputed;
- recognized;
- determined-not-to-exist;
- terminated;
- transitioned/reconstituted under a lawful event.

Exact lifecycle/state names should conform to the shared schema rather than be duplicated locally.

Changing recognition state should not require closing one interest record and opening an unrelated one.

### 2.3.2 Scope

An interest may be:

- **unit-scoped** — concerning a particular Habitation Unit;
- **area-scoped** — concerning a locality/territory without one fixed unit, where applicable;
- **obligor-only** — a duty to provide suitable accommodation somewhere, without guaranteeing a specific place.

This replaces the v0.1 distinction between separate place-specific and non-place-specific object types.

Whenever `scope = obligor-only`, the Interest must carry an explicit **obligor relation state**:

- **identified** — a competent Party/source of duty is named;
- **disputed** — two or more candidate duty-holders or authorities are contested;
- **unallocated** — the asserted duty is representable but no competent duty-holder has yet been established.

An `obligor-only` Interest may therefore lack a **resolved** obligor, but it may not omit the obligor state. `disputed` and `unallocated` states remain open and route through K7/GMEAIA as appropriate; Housing does not invent the missing duty-holder.

A statutory duty to accommodate a discharged care resident may be `obligor-only` even after a lawful licence to a specific room ends.

### 2.3.3 Formal-instrument relation

Where useful, Housing may record whether the interest holder is:

- named on a formal instrument;
- not named on the relevant instrument;
- in a context where no such instrument exists.

This replaces the canonical label "secondary household resident."

The absence of a name on a lease or allocation document does not itself establish that no habitation interest exists.

### 2.3.4 More than one legitimate home

No uniqueness constraint shall require one Habitation Interest per Party.

A child may have two parental homes. A pastoralist may have several seasonal sites. A student may have a term-time residence and family residence. A rotational worker may have worksite and family habitation.

An external tax, voting, school, migration, or benefits system may select one "primary residence" for its own purpose. That classification does not silently redefine Housing's interest set.

## 2.4 Habitation Basis

A **Habitation Basis** is an independently constituted relationship relevant to the existence, scope, conditions, or transition of a Habitation Interest.

Sources may include:

- tenancy/contract;
- ownership occupation;
- allocation;
- customary or Indigenous law;
- family/court order;
- membership;
- employment;
- care/support eligibility;
- education;
- migration/reception status;
- community rule;
- another competent source.

Each basis should record:

- source/constituting instrument or evidence;
- authority/domain;
- effective period;
- effect on the interest;
- decision force where an external decision affects habitation;
- source-domain challenge/correction route where relevant;
- transition history where relevant.

### 2.4.1 Per-basis effects

A basis may carry one or more effects such as:

- constitutive;
- conditional;
- limiting;
- potentially terminating;
- procedural.

These are effects of the individual basis.

### 2.4.2 Basis-set sufficiency

Whether habitation survives the loss of a basis is a property of the **set of bases and applicable law**, not necessarily of one edge.

The interest should therefore support:

- conjunctive sufficiency;
- disjunctive sufficiency;
- mixed/structured sufficiency;
- or a reference to the competent external legal rule where formal encoding would imply false certainty.

> **Dependency is representational. Priority is adjudicatory.**

No basis is presumed superior merely because it comes from title, property law, state law, or appears first in a record.

### 2.4.3 External decision force

A basis may be affected by an actor or process that is not an owner/provider.

Rather than create a separate "gatekeeper" object, Housing records the external authority and how its decision operates:

- advisory/nominating;
- eligibility-determinative;
- conditional;
- binding veto where law provides;
- review-triggering;
- another jurisdiction-defined force.

A non-housing decision may lawfully change a housing condition. It does not thereby become a Housing decision.

## 2.5 Derived and computed relations

The following are computed/interpretive, not canonical object classes.

### Occupant
A Party has actual presence at a Unit.

### Resident
A Party's presence satisfies the jurisdiction's published factual/ordinary-residence threshold.

### Claimant
A Party holds an asserted or disputed Habitation Interest.

### Derived interest
An interest is **derived** where its existence or continuation materially depends on an independently constituted upstream relationship or basis distinct from the direct constituting relation recorded for that interest.

`Derived` is a diagnostic predicate over the basis graph. It does not presume that title, ownership, or a landlord/provider relationship is the natural root.

### Competing claims
Two or more interests concerning an indivisible Unit require mutually incompatible exclusive outcomes.

Competing status triggers an adjudication referral; it does not decide priority.

### Concurrent interests
One Party holds more than one legitimate Habitation Interest simultaneously.

### Shelter, habitation, home, and housing security
These remain useful human-facing terms:

- **shelter** — emergency physical protection under an emergency/transitional standard;
- **habitation** — factual occupation of a place as living space under the applicable conditions;
- **home** — plain-language term for a meaningful place of residence; not a schema object;
- **housing security** — outcome describing protection against arbitrary, opaque, or materially impossible-to-challenge loss.

# 3. Constitutional rules

## 3.1 Title is not habitation

Title and habitation are related but distinct objects.

A title transition does not silently extinguish a Habitation Interest. A Habitation Interest does not silently create title.

## 3.2 Observation is not authority

A housing metric, affordability threshold, screening score, arrears alert, hazard model, occupancy estimate, benefit status, employment change, care-status decision, migration status, or other observation may trigger review where lawful.

It does not itself:

- deny entry;
- allocate housing;
- set individualized rent;
- establish fraud;
- cancel a waiting-list position;
- terminate an interest;
- authorize eviction;
- order relocation;
- remove essential services.

Consequential action requires a separately valid rule and authority.

## 3.3 No administrative disappearance

A person or claim shall not disappear merely because:

- there is no formal title;
- no written lease exists;
- the person's name is absent from the relevant formal instrument;
- the superior lease or provider ends;
- one basis terminates;
- an owner becomes insolvent;
- a unit is renumbered or rebuilt;
- a claimant lacks national identity documents;
- another domain changes status.

The system must either preserve the interest/basis/unit linkage, determine through competent process that it no longer exists, or record the unresolved gap.

## 3.4 One basis ending does not erase another

Termination, invalidation, expiry, withdrawal, or loss of one Habitation Basis does not extinguish another independently constituted basis unless the competent law/adjudicator establishes that consequence.

Examples:

- cooperative expulsion does not silently decide an independently protected tenancy;
- job loss does not silently erase a separately constituted tenancy;
- divorce does not silently erase an independent customary residence claim;
- loss of a care-room licence does not silently erase a public duty to accommodate elsewhere.

## 3.5 No single-primary-home ontology

Housing does not assume one person has exactly one legitimate home.

Absence from one interest while occupying another does not by itself establish abandonment.

A single-address classification from another domain applies only for that domain's lawful purpose unless applicable law separately changes the Housing interest.

## 3.6 Unit transformation preserves linkage

Subdivision, merger, demolition, reconstruction, renumbering, re-registration, movement, or change of use shall not erase attached housing claims merely because the Unit identifier changed.

Registry change is not adjudication.

## 3.7 Grounds do not execute themselves

Rent arrears, expiry, nuisance, redevelopment, owner need, employment loss, membership loss, care change, overcrowding, hazard, illegal activity, abandonment allegation, or another ground may open a lawful process.

The ground itself does not authorize physical exclusion, lockout, service withdrawal, seizure of belongings, harassment, or eviction unless applicable law separately authorizes the exact act.

## 3.8 No disguised or constructive eviction

Essential services, repairs, access, security, building condition, or administrative delay shall not be deliberately manipulated as a substitute for a lawful termination route.

A safety authority may still order urgent closure. That protective act does not decide who caused the unsafe condition or erase separately applicable repair, relocation, compensation, or continuity duties.

## 3.9 Functional separation

Where practical, the actor that owns/provides housing should not be the sole actor that:

- sets binding habitability standards;
- investigates its own serious violations;
- authorizes compulsory loss;
- determines appeals from its own allocation decisions;
- controls the only resident-support route.

Where functions are combined, the constituting law must identify conflict controls and independent review.

Where CIS already supplies a stronger separation rule, CIS controls.

## 3.10 Mixed mandates remain visible

An institution providing accommodation plus care, treatment, disability support, education, employment, custody, supervision, or another dependency-intensive service must declare those mandates separately.

A Housing record cannot manufacture the non-housing power.

A non-housing power cannot silently become an eviction or housing sanction unless law creates that consequence through a Housing decision.

## 3.11 Legal pluralism without silent hierarchy

Housing may operate under overlapping:

- municipal;
- national;
- customary;
- Indigenous;
- cooperative;
- community;
- contractual;
- religious;
- informal;
- other lawful authorities.

The Framework does not resolve conflicts by silently privileging state form, title, numerical majority, or whichever rule appears most protective.

Framework/domain conflicts use CIS allocation rules. Competing resident claims require their own competent adjudicator.

## 3.12 Indigenous authority is non-substitutable

Housing does not translate Indigenous title, residence relations, consent, territorial law, or sacred/community governance into ordinary tenancy.

Affected Indigenous nations/communities determine their own internal law and representation.

Housing may govern interfaces and resident consequences only where a competent relationship lawfully exists.

## 3.13 Non-discrimination and practical accessibility

Housing processes shall conform to applicable rights and equality law.

Notice, application, hearing, allocation, inspection, challenge, and remedy should provide:

- language access;
- disability access;
- low-bandwidth/offline routes;
- representation/support where applicable;
- reasonable assistance.

## 3.14 No forced digital dependence

Essential housing protection, homelessness assistance, challenge, or continuity shall not depend solely on:

- smartphone;
- biometric credential;
- digital wallet;
- high-bandwidth connection.

## 3.15 Representation does not create rights or powers

Recording:

- a Party role;
- a Habitation Interest;
- a Habitation Basis;
- a Unit link;
- a transition;
- a handoff;

does not itself create the substantive right, role, title, mandate, priority, or jurisdiction represented.

# 4. Minimum factual process and minimum habitation conditions

## 4.1 Factual residence without formal title or instrument

A jurisdiction shall not make absence of title, written lease, national identity number, citizenship document, or civil-registration credential a complete procedural void.

Where a person satisfies the published factual-residence threshold, the applicable process should identify what minimum protections attach before removal or exclusion.

Possible minimums include:

- notice where safe and legally required;
- opportunity to contest facts;
- protection against unlawful self-help exclusion;
- safe handling of belongings;
- access to medicines/assistive devices;
- emergency/continuity referral where applicable.

This floor does not automatically create:

- title;
- tenancy;
- inheritance;
- compensation;
- permanent possession;
- priority over another claimant.

## 4.2 Identity-light standing

Where ordinary civil identity is unavailable, disputed, unsafe to disclose, or unnecessary for the housing purpose, a Housing process may use a **purpose-limited provisional/local Party identifier**.

Evidence may include, as lawful and appropriate:

- testimony;
- witness/community attestation;
- service/provider records;
- correspondence;
- photographs;
- utility/service evidence;
- locally recognizable place reference;
- other evidence accepted by the jurisdiction.

The identifier shall not become:

- citizenship;
- migration status;
- tax residence;
- voter registration;
- general civil identity;
- generalized reputation score.

## 4.3 Published factual-residence threshold

If a resident-level protection depends on ordinary/durable factual presence, the jurisdiction must publish or reference:

- relevant duration/pattern;
- accepted evidence;
- treatment of seasonal/shared-custody/mobile residence;
- temporary absence;
- displacement;
- hospitalization/detention;
- disaster/conflict absence;
- correction route.

An unpublished internal intuition about who "really lives" somewhere is non-conformant.

## 4.4 Applicable habitation standard

Every factual accommodation state relevant to Housing must identify an applicable condition standard.

At minimum:

- **ordinary habitation floor**; or
- **emergency/transitional floor**.

External status shall not produce:

`applicable_standard = none`.

This is especially important for migrant/reception accommodation, informal habitation, shelters, temporary relocation, and post-disaster settings.

## 4.5 Ordinary habitation outcome floor

The adopting jurisdiction should define resident-facing outcomes appropriate to climate, culture, building form, infrastructure, and disability/access needs, including as applicable:

- structural safety sufficient for habitation;
- fire safety sufficient for habitation;
- potable water and sanitation;
- weather/climate protection;
- ventilation/indoor environmental quality;
- necessary energy for survival and ordinary residence;
- privacy and personal security;
- accessibility/reasonable accommodation;
- safe occupancy conditions;
- protection from severe contamination/infestation/hazardous materials;
- essential-service access where locally necessary.

Housing specifies outcomes.

The competent building, fire, engineering, health, sanitation, energy, or infrastructure authority owns technical codes, permits, engineering methods, and enforcement tools.

## 4.6 Emergency/transitional floor and anti-ratchet rule

Emergency or transitional accommodation may lawfully operate under a different standard for a bounded period.

The durable route must identify how and when the ordinary floor is restored.

Forced relocation, provider failure, managed retreat, disaster, or redevelopment shall not normalize permanent sub-minimum habitation merely because the original place became unavailable.

## 4.7 High-dependency consequences

Housing decisions should identify foreseeable disproportionate effects on:

- children;
- elders;
- disabled people;
- people with illness or medical needs;
- care-dependent people;
- people at risk of domestic violence/coercive control;
- others whose survival, schooling, care, mobility, communication, or safety strongly depends on habitation continuity.

This is not a universal veto.

The relevant family, disability, health, care, justice, or protection authority retains its mandate.

# 5. Authority and institutional roles

The Framework creates no mandatory global housing ministry or tribunal.

An adopting jurisdiction should map the following functions to lawful existing or newly constituted Parties.

## 5.1 Policy/needs coordination

May observe:

- housing need;
- affordability;
- accessibility;
- homelessness;
- distribution;
- supply mismatch;
- hazard exposure.

Observation does not create zoning, tax, construction, credit, or allocation power.

## 5.2 Housing provider / landlord / operator

May supply, lease, manage, or allocate housing under applicable law.

Its Housing authority must be distinguished from:

- title;
- care;
- employment;
- education;
- custody;
- migration;
- other mandates.

## 5.3 Habitation-condition authority

May inspect or determine conditions only under separate lawful authority.

Housing's §4 outcome floor does not itself create inspection or entry power.

## 5.4 Allocation authority

Where public/social/community/cooperative housing is administratively allocated, the authority should publish or reference:

- eligibility;
- priority/queue rules;
- required evidence;
- accessibility;
- reasons;
- correction;
- challenge.

## 5.5 Homelessness and continuity service

May coordinate:

- prevention;
- emergency accommodation;
- stabilization;
- service access;
- durable-housing route.

It need not manufacture punitive status before assistance is possible.

## 5.6 Independent review/adjudication

Serious eviction, allocation, discrimination, habitability, rent/deposit, provider, and competing-claim disputes should have an independent route appropriate to the jurisdiction.

Housing does not require one universal court model.

## 5.7 Resident representation and advocacy

Jurisdictions may recognize:

- tenant unions;
- resident councils;
- legal aid;
- disability advocates;
- community organizations;
- ombuds routes;
- other representation.

Where representation is legally protected, retaliatory termination or service withdrawal should not be used to defeat it.

## 5.8 Fallback continuity custodian

An adopting jurisdiction may constitute a lawful body/function to hold unresolved Housing duties when the ordinary responsible actor disappears, refuses the handoff, or loses mandate.

The custodian may:

- preserve the unresolved duty;
- preserve Party contact/evidence;
- attempt referral;
- use only emergency/interim powers it already lawfully possesses;
- keep the case in the GMEAIA lifecycle until valid closure.

The custodian gains no new:

- title;
- allocation;
- eviction;
- tax;
- funding;
- expropriation;
- permanent-housing;
- family-law;
- care;
- enforcement;

power merely because it holds the duty.

# 6. Habitation-interest and basis lifecycle

## 6.1 Constituting and evidentiary sources

A Habitation Interest may be evidenced or constituted through:

- written instrument;
- oral agreement;
- statute/regulation;
- court/tribunal order;
- customary or Indigenous law;
- cooperative/community rule;
- factual residence to which law attaches procedural protection;
- public allocation;
- another competent source.

Housing records the source and recognition state without pretending all forms are legally identical.

## 6.2 Multiple bases

One interest may depend on several bases.

Example:

- direct tenancy with a housing association;
- cooperative membership controlling nomination;
- family/court order affecting occupancy.

Housing records all relevant bases rather than choosing one "true" superior relationship.

## 6.3 Basis change

When a basis changes:

1. record the source-domain decision/event;
2. identify its lawful force;
3. determine whether the Housing effect is automatic under applicable law or requires a separate K5 Housing decision;
4. preserve other bases;
5. preserve challenge/correction pointers;
6. route resulting continuity duty where applicable.

A membership, care, employment, education, migration, or other decision is not transformed into a Housing decision merely because it affects housing.

## 6.4 Status transition and reconstitution

Lawful status changes should preserve causal continuity.

Examples:

- not-named resident → statutory successor;
- subtenant → direct tenant;
- temporary allocation → durable tenancy;
- tied housing → independent tenancy;
- accommodation duty → allocated unit.

Use the shared transition block rather than close-and-reopen.

The transition record does not decide whether the transition is legally available.

## 6.5 Unit transition

A Unit may change independently of the interest.

The system should preserve:

- predecessor Unit;
- successor Unit(s);
- transformation event;
- authority/source;
- uncertainty;
- attached interests;
- unresolved questions.

Housing may not infer tenancy/return/restitution survival from linkage alone.

## 6.6 External registry modes

### External-reference mode

Where a competent cadastral/building/municipal/place registry exists, Housing references it.

### Housing-purpose-linkage mode

Where no suitable registry exists, Housing may preserve the minimum local place linkage needed for continuity.

That linkage must be explicitly non-authoritative for:

- title;
- land boundary;
- taxation;
- general identity;
- cadastral priority;
- non-Housing purposes.

## 6.7 Collective holding and representation

A Habitation Interest may be held by a collective Party.

The constituting authority should identify how that collective:

- receives notice;
- provides consent/dissent;
- challenges a decision;
- appoints representatives;
- protects internal non-engagement or restricted-knowledge rules where applicable.

Housing does not impose an individual-tenancy model on collective interests.

# 7. Consequential decisions, termination, and adjudication

## 7.1 Consequential Housing decisions

A consequential Housing decision includes an act that materially changes:

- initial access;
- allocation;
- rent/fee/deposit;
- repair/closure duty;
- use/status;
- termination;
- compulsory loss;
- another legally significant habitation condition.

Each decision should state:

- class;
- general or individual scope;
- authority/source;
- affected Party/Interest/Unit;
- reasons;
- evidence;
- notice/process block;
- effective time;
- challenge route;
- continuity consequence.

## 7.2 General rule versus individual decision

Housing must distinguish:

- a general law/rule/formula/policy instrument;
- the individual decision applying it.

An individual denial or eviction cannot hide the general criterion it relies on.

A case record shall not become a stealth rule-making instrument.

## 7.3 Entry and screening

A housing-entry process may use lawful eligibility/screening criteria.

It must identify:

- purpose;
- authority;
- relevant facts;
- data provenance;
- prohibited/protected inferences;
- automated or third-party role;
- reasons for denial;
- accommodation/alternative-evidence route;
- correction/challenge.

A screening score or data-broker profile is not self-authenticating evidence.

The Framework does not create a universal duty for every private owner to accept every applicant.

## 7.4 Allocation

Where scarce public/social/community housing is allocated administratively, the system should identify:

- eligibility;
- priority;
- queue/wait status where applicable;
- accessibility;
- reason;
- review.

The Framework does not prescribe one universal allocation formula.

## 7.5 Rent, fees, and deposits

The Framework does not constitutionalize one rent-control system.

Jurisdictions may adopt:

- market rent with safeguards;
- stabilization;
- caps/reference rents;
- social/cost-linked rents;
- income-linked support;
- deposit limits/guarantees;
- other lawful models.

Whatever model is adopted should expose:

- objective;
- legal source;
- general formula/rule;
- individual application;
- review;
- revision path.

## 7.6 Termination notice and compulsory loss remain separate stages

A termination/opening act and a compulsory-loss authorization may use the same K5 profile but are distinct instances.

A compulsory-loss authorization should reference:

- prior termination/opening act; or
- the lawful emergency/exception that makes one unnecessary.

Where functional separation applies, the authorization owner remains independently visible.

## 7.7 Eviction / compulsory loss

A lawful compulsory-loss route should identify:

- affected interest;
- legal ground;
- competent authorizer;
- evidence/contested facts;
- notice/accessibility;
- response period;
- representation;
- vulnerability/continuity effects;
- interim protection;
- adjudication/review;
- execution authority;
- belongings/essential-support continuity;
- closure/remedy.

## 7.8 Abandonment

Absence does not automatically establish abandonment.

The jurisdiction should account for:

- hospitalization;
- detention;
- displacement;
- disaster;
- conflict;
- care duties;
- seasonal mobility;
- shared custody;
- rotational work;
- other concurrent legitimate habitation.

## 7.9 Intra-resident / non-named exclusion

A person not named on a relevant formal instrument may still hold:

- a distinct Habitation Interest;
- factual-residence process protection;
- one or more independent bases.

Housing need not define a universal household to protect the person from administrative disappearance.

Where domestic violence, coercive control, child protection, abuse, or immediate danger exists, competent family/justice/protection authorities retain their powers.

Housing tracks:

- housing consequence;
- safe belongings access;
- medicines/devices;
- alternative accommodation/continuity;
- lawful exclusion source.

It does not force continued co-residence.

## 7.10 Competing claims

When multiple interests over one indivisible Unit require incompatible exclusive outcomes:

- preserve each claim/provenance;
- preserve current occupation;
- record safety constraints;
- apply interim protection where separately authorized;
- record the adjudication-owner state;
- name the competent adjudicator where one is identified;
- route downstream continuity.

The adjudication-owner state must remain explicit:

- **identified** — a competent adjudicator is named;
- **disputed** — candidate adjudicators/authorities exist but competence is contested;
- **unallocated** — no competent adjudicator has been established.

A K8 referral must still exist when the adjudicator is disputed or unallocated. Those states route the unresolved authority problem through K7/CIS/GMEAIA as appropriate rather than making the competing claims disappear.

Housing does not rank claimants.

A losing place claim does not automatically create a universal publicly funded replacement right; the source of any replacement duty must be separately identified.

## 7.11 Community/customary termination

Where a competent community/customary/Indigenous/religious/cooperative authority lawfully terminates a residence relation:

- Housing does not silently overturn that decision;
- downstream belongings, shelter, relocation, compensation, or other duties remain separately visible where law creates them;
- absence of such a duty-owner is a gap, not a Housing veto over the community decision.

# 8. Habitability, repair, and progressive degradation

## 8.1 Condition observation

Condition assessment should distinguish:

- observed facts;
- source;
- uncertainty;
- inspection authority;
- resident evidence;
- applicable standard;
- required external technical determination.

Housing may import an existing building, health, fire, sanitation, energy, or infrastructure record.

## 8.2 Condition is not remedy

A failed condition does not automatically determine:

- eviction;
- closure;
- repair method;
- compensation;
- rent adjustment;
- relocation.

The competent authority determines the consequence under applicable law.

## 8.3 Repair and temporary habitation

Where repair is required, continuity planning may identify:

- responsible Party;
- deadline;
- temporary accommodation;
- accessibility;
- essential services;
- return;
- verification;
- unresolved liability.

## 8.4 Condemnation does not erase responsibility

A genuine urgent closure protects residents from an unsafe building.

It does not by itself erase:

- owner/provider repair duty;
- relocation duty;
- compensation;
- deposits;
- successor-provider obligations;
- claims arising from deliberate neglect.

## 8.5 Constructive eviction through deterioration or service withdrawal

A landlord/provider should not evade ordinary termination safeguards by:

- deliberate maintenance stripping;
- intentional preventable deterioration;
- unlawful water/sanitation/energy withdrawal;
- engineered access failure;
- harassment;
- another tactic designed to make continued residence impossible.

A service disconnection lawful under infrastructure rules is not automatically constructive eviction.

Where purpose is contested, the competent Housing review/adjudication route determines whether the service action was used as a housing-loss mechanism.

## 8.6 Slow-onset degradation

Heat, repeated flood, coastal erosion, subsidence, water scarcity, contamination, wildfire exposure, permafrost thaw, infrastructure decay, or another progressive condition may degrade habitation before a declared emergency.

A credible-pathway finding may trigger:

- monitoring;
- repair/adaptation planning;
- infrastructure handoff;
- participation;
- funding analysis;
- proactive replacement planning.

It does not itself order relocation.

Where the adopted trigger is met, the K4 condition profile must open/reference K6 planning.

# 9. Continuity, homelessness, replacement, and unresolved duties

## 9.1 Continuity is a protected interest, not permanence

Secure habitation does not mean every occupancy must continue forever.

It means lawful change should preserve:

- authority;
- reasons;
- process;
- challenge;
- affected people;
- downstream housing consequences;
- remedy/closure.

## 9.2 Homelessness states

Housing systems should distinguish, as locally relevant:

- unsheltered homelessness;
- emergency shelter;
- temporary/transitional accommodation;
- hidden/insecure homelessness;
- imminent loss;
- institutional discharge without habitation;
- repeated cycling;
- durable habitation.

These are conditions/service states, not moral statuses.

## 9.3 Prevention

Possible prevention mechanisms include:

- arrears mediation/support;
- legal assistance;
- provider negotiation;
- domestic-violence relocation;
- institutional discharge planning;
- employment-transition continuity;
- mortgage-distress referral;
- temporary relocation during repair.

The Framework does not require one universal service model.

## 9.4 Housing First and other models

Housing First, supported housing, transitional models, cooperative/community placement, shelter-led systems, and other approaches may be tested/adopted.

A programme should state:

- population;
- conditions;
- evidence;
- resident rights;
- exit route;
- failure mode;
- durable outcome.

No service model becomes constitutional solely by appearing in this Framework.

## 9.5 When return to the place becomes impossible

Where competent ecological, safety, disaster, public-health, Indigenous, or other lawful authority determines that long-duration/permanent return is impossible:

- the place-scoped interest may end, convert, or coexist with an `obligor-only`/area-scoped continuity entitlement where law provides;
- Housing records the transition;
- the source law determines the duty.

Possible lawful continuity components may include:

- replacement accommodation;
- affordability/accessibility continuity;
- relocation support;
- compensation;
- return priority if conditions change;
- preservation of community/school/care ties where practicable.

The Framework does not itself create all of these duties.

## 9.6 Insolvency, receivership, and creditor sale

Owner/provider insolvency does not prove that resident interests never existed.

The external insolvency/property process should make visible:

- creditor/title claims;
- resident interests;
- maintenance/utilities;
- deposits/resident funds;
- vacancy demand;
- successor obligations;
- lawful termination;
- relocation/remedy where applicable.

Housing does not reorder creditor priority or create an insolvency stay.

General insolvency and secured-credit law remain external-law dependencies unless a later compatible GGF interface explicitly owns them.

## 9.7 Housing finance distress

Foreclosure/repo/home-loss processes concerning owner-occupied habitation should identify, where local law provides:

- valid claim/security;
- notice/correction;
- hardship/restructuring;
- adjudication;
- dependants/vulnerability;
- execution;
- residual debt/surplus;
- continuity referral.

Financial Systems may provide settlement/financial infrastructure. It does not replace national mortgage, banking, secured-credit, or insolvency law.

## 9.8 Cross-domain Housing duty custody

When another framework/domain action foreseeably causes a Housing consequence, the initiating process should identify:

- causal action;
- affected Party/Interest;
- candidate receiving function;
- claimed source of duty;
- urgency;
- expected harm during delay;
- continuity need;
- unresolved Housing duty.

The handoff then uses the compatible GMEAIA lifecycle.

Housing does not redefine:

- accepted;
- refused;
- deferred;
- referred;
- timed-out;
- misrouted;
- capacity-limited;
- constitutional-gap;
- correction;
- closure;

states locally.

## 9.9 Capacity limitation is not constitutional gap

A competent authority that lacks staffing, units, money, or throughput presents a capacity/performance problem.

That is not the same as:

- absence of lawful authority;
- absence of sufficient mandate/jurisdiction.

The GMEAIA-compatible state must preserve that distinction.

## 9.10 Fallback continuity custody

Where an ordinary recipient disappears, refuses, or cannot accept a duty, a lawfully constituted fallback custodian may keep the unresolved duty visible using existing powers only.

If no competent authority exists, the result is a constitutional/interface gap under the shared grammar.

No gap creates Housing sovereignty.

# 10. Housing-systems interfaces

This section identifies **resident-facing conditions and handoffs**, not a universal Housing economic programme.

## 10.1 Affordability as a distributed outcome

Housing affordability is deliberately a **monitored distributed outcome** rather than one sovereign function.

Housing may observe:

- housing-cost burden;
- residual income;
- arrears;
- overcrowding;
- displacement;
- homelessness;
- waiting time;
- vacancy/underuse;
- dwelling/land price;
- mortgage distress;
- energy/utility burden;
- accessibility-adjusted availability;
- geographic mismatch.

No metric automatically creates policy.

Possible instrument owners include:

- AUBI/social protection;
- Work in Liberation/labour systems;
- Adaptive Tax;
- competent rent authorities;
- Bioregional Polis;
- infrastructure;
- external credit/finance law.

Housing owns resident-facing diagnosis and continuity consequences, not all instruments.

## 10.2 Public, social, cooperative, and community housing

Where such housing exists, governance should identify:

- owner/custodian;
- provider/operator;
- public/community purpose;
- funding;
- allocation;
- rent/contribution method;
- accessibility;
- resident governance;
- maintenance reserves;
- procurement/conflicts;
- audit;
- sale/conversion rules;
- provider failure;
- challenge.

The Framework does not mandate one universal public-housing model.

## 10.3 Housing supply / land-use seam

Housing may state:

- need;
- accessibility;
- geographic mismatch;
- displacement effects;
- distributional consequences;
- underuse/vacancy observation.

Bioregional Polis and competent local land-use authorities retain:

- zoning;
- density;
- permits;
- growth boundaries;
- municipal development instruments.

Hearthstone retains title/stewardship transition where applicable.

Housing need does not create zoning power.

## 10.4 Construction / infrastructure seam

Housing states habitation outcomes.

Competent infrastructure/building/materials authorities retain technical delivery.

Conduit Protocol / relevant infrastructure authorities may own shared water/energy/data infrastructure operation where adopted.

Housing does not command construction merely because need is high.

## 10.5 Tourism and short-term conversion

Regenerative Journeys and competent tourism/platform/land-use authorities own tourism policy/licensing within their mandates.

Housing owns resident consequences when an ordinary habitation Unit changes use.

A conversion process affecting residents should make visible:

- current Unit/use;
- resident interests;
- external authorization;
- whether separate Housing termination is required;
- notice/challenge;
- relocation/continuity;
- data-purpose boundary.

Tourism pressure metrics do not execute eviction.

## 10.6 Tax interface

Adaptive Tax owns tax liability and collection.

Housing may receive a continuity handoff where tax enforcement threatens habitation.

A Housing vulnerability finding does not itself alter tax liability.

Where another domain needs one "primary residence" for taxation, that classification is tax-specific and does not erase concurrent Housing interests.

## 10.7 Employment / tied housing

Work in Liberation and relevant labour law own employment relations.

Housing records how employment operates as a basis for habitation.

Employment termination may:

- automatically change habitation where law clearly provides;
- trigger a separate Housing decision;
- leave another independent Housing basis intact.

The applicable law must state which.

## 10.8 Care / disability / health

Global Health, Disability Justice, Justice Systems, local care law, and other competent authorities own their substantive non-housing mandates.

Housing owns:

- accommodation relation;
- belongings;
- housing notice/process;
- continuity;
- place-specific versus obligor-only entitlement;
- Housing consequences of discharge/eligibility change.

There is not yet evidence of a complete GGF residential-care architecture. Repeated gaps should trigger a separate orphan audit rather than Housing expansion.

## 10.9 Migration / reception

Migration owns:

- status;
- reception;
- movement;
- migration-specific entitlements.

Housing owns factual accommodation conditions and any lawfully applicable habitation relation.

Migration status shall not produce an unowned condition state: factual accommodation still receives an emergency/transitional or ordinary habitation standard under §4.

This does not convert reception accommodation into ordinary tenancy.

## 10.10 Disaster, climate, and managed retreat

DRR/Living Shield owns emergency/hazard authority.

Climate & Energy and relevant ecological/infrastructure authorities own their substantive domains.

Housing owns:

- pre-existing interest visibility;
- post-emergency transition to ordinary/durable habitation;
- replacement-habitation consequences;
- progressive condition planning under K4/K6.

The adopting authority map should specify the trigger for transition from emergency shelter governance into ordinary Housing continuity.

## 10.11 Water, sanitation, energy, and shared infrastructure

Water & Sanitation and relevant Conduit/energy/infrastructure authorities own technical systems.

Housing owns resident-facing habitability and continuity consequences.

A lawful infrastructure decision does not become a Housing decision; an infrastructure system cannot use its power as a hidden eviction mechanism without triggering Housing review.

## 10.12 Justice, family, succession, and competing claims

Justice Systems may supply justice modalities/remedy interfaces where adopted.

Ordinary family, succession, and court jurisdiction may remain external-law dependencies.

Housing does not decide:

- custody;
- divorce;
- inheritance;
- domestic-violence responsibility;
- criminal guilt;
- substantive customary priority.

It preserves the Housing interest and routes the dispute to a competent owner.

## 11.1 CIS inheritance

Personal/claimant Housing data ordinarily fall within the applicable CIS personal/claimant-controlled class.

Community/Indigenous restricted information may require stronger restrictions.

Housing inherits:

- notice;
- access;
- correction;
- explanation;
- appeal;
- retention;
- purpose limitation;
- protected non-recording where applicable.

It does not create a parallel data constitution.

A Housing record created for housing administration does not itself authorize reuse for:

- immigration enforcement;
- tax assessment;
- criminal investigation;
- intelligence;
- political policing;
- generalized credit/reputation scoring;
- unrelated eligibility.

A separately lawful secondary use must identify its own authority and satisfy the applicable CIS test.

## 11.3 Recognition-record firewall

Civic, care, cultural, ecological, relational, or recognition records are not automatically:

- tenancy-reliability records;
- fraud records;
- allocation-priority records;
- credit records.

Automation may support:

- application processing;
- maintenance triage;
- modelling;
- allocation support;
- anomaly review;
- vacancy analysis;
- inspection scheduling;
- service coordination.

It may not by itself produce a consequential denial/loss unless a separately constituted rule explicitly authorizes the bounded automation and supplies:

- purpose-specific evidence;
- correction;
- review;
- human/institutional responsibility.

## 11.5 No assistance-by-surveillance default

Basic homelessness/continuity support should not require surrender of unrelated:

- health;
- migration;
- policing;
- employment;
- family;
- biometric;
- behavioural;

data unless separately lawful and necessary.

## 11.6 Civil-registration dependency

Housing's provisional identifier solves only an in-domain administrative problem.

It does not solve the wider GGF absence of a general civil-registration/legal-identity owner.

That dependency should be visible in the corpus gap register and investigated separately.

# 12. Phase 0 and low-capacity profile

Phase 0 remains the practical entry point.

## 12.1 Phase 0 Light

A municipality, community, cooperative, BAZ, or other competent actor may begin with six questions:

1. What habitation relations actually exist locally?
2. Who may lawfully allocate, terminate, evict, condemn, relocate, or order repair?
3. Where do people lose habitation through coordination failure?
4. What emergency and durable continuity routes already exist?
5. Which external processes currently cause Housing consequences without an owner/handoff?
6. How can a person challenge imminent or erroneous Housing loss?

Phase 0 creates no new power.

Map at least where relevant:

- formal tenancies;

- non-named residents;
- multi-basis arrangements;
- competing claims;
- multiple legitimate homes;
- tourism conversion;
- progressive hazard;
- Unit transformations.

## 12.3 Map authority

Identify owners of:

- housing access/allocation;
- rent/fees;
- termination;
- compulsory loss;
- condition inspection;
- repair/closure;
- homelessness services;
- land-use;
- title;
- tax;
- finance/insolvency;
- care;
- family/justice;
- migration;
- infrastructure;
- disaster/emergency;
- review/adjudication;
- fallback continuity custody.

## 12.4 Map continuity failures

Examples:

- payment/benefit interruption;
- employment loss;
- provider insolvency;

- domestic exclusion;
- care discharge;
- redevelopment;

## 12.5 Minimum viable semantic profile

A low-capacity implementation needs only the functions necessary to keep authority, interest, decision, continuity, handoff, and remedy reconstructable.

- K5 Consequential Decision;

Trigger-dependent profiles:

- K3 when independent/multiple bases materially matter;
- K4 when condition assessment is consequential;

Paper, oral/community evidence with lawful recording, existing municipal systems, or linked legacy systems may conform. A new database is not required.

## 12.6 Pilot substantive reforms separately

Rent regulation, Housing First, public development, vacancy measures, cooperative acquisition, legal support, accessible housing, mortgage mediation, or other reforms should be piloted under their own lawful authority and failure criteria.

The constitutional framework does not declare every pilot successful in advance.

# 13. Canonical Housing functional profiles and shared blocks

These are **semantic profiles**, not mandatory database tables.

## 13.0 Record shape is orthogonal to function

A K-profile states **what legal/administrative function is represented**. Record shape states **what kind of persisted or referenced thing carries that function**. These are independent axes.

At minimum, implementations should distinguish:

- **standing/profile state** — durable description of a Party, Interest, Basis, Unit-related authority configuration, or other continuing relation;
- **case/lifecycle state** — an ongoing matter that may accumulate decisions, plans, handoffs, challenges, correction, and closure;
- **event/decision** — a time-bounded consequential act, observation, or transition.

Typical dominant shapes are:

| Profile | Dominant record shape |
|---|---|
| K1 | standing/profile |
| K2 | standing relationship / case object |
| K3 | standing relation/edge with lifecycle |
| K4 | observation/snapshot, optionally longitudinal |
| K5 | event/decision |
| K6 | case/plan |
| K7 | case/handoff lifecycle |
| K8 | case/referral |
| K9 | case/remedy |

The mapping is not a database prescription. A jurisdiction may store these functions differently if reconstruction is preserved.

B1–B6 are **embedded/shared semantic blocks**. They do not become independent cases merely because they contain fields.

This distinction prevents the v0.1 failure mode in which standing registers, ongoing cases, and one-time events were treated as coordinate topic-specific record types.

## 13.1 K1 — Authority & Mandate Profile

Records:

- Party;
- constituting authority/instrument;
- jurisdiction/scope;
- Housing powers;
- non-housing mandates over residents;
- conflicts;
- review;
- expiry/transition.

Absorbs legacy `HAB-01`, `HAB-14`, `HAB-22`, and authority-expiry semantics from `HAB-18`.

## 13.2 K2 — Habitation Interest Profile

Records:

- holder Party;
- recognition state;
- scope;
- Unit/area reference where applicable;
- obligor relation state and obligor Party/source where `scope = obligor-only`;
- presence/instrument relation where needed;
- standard;
- basis references;
- challenge/continuity pointers.

Absorbs legacy `HAB-02`, `HAB-03`, and standing portions of `HAB-21`.

K2 is declarative.

## 13.3 K3 — Basis, Dependency & External-Decision Profile

Records:

- basis source;
- authority/domain;
- effect set;
- decision force;
- effective period;
- sufficiency relation/reference;
- source-domain challenge;
- transition.

Absorbs legacy `HAB-25` and person-specific mixed-mandate/gatekeeper semantics.

## 13.4 K4 — Habitation Condition Profile

Records/imports:

- condition evidence;
- standard;
- uncertainty;
- trajectory;
- inspection source;
- resident evidence;
- external technical authority;
- K6 trigger where degrading.

Absorbs legacy `HAB-05` and observation/trajectory portions of `HAB-24`.

## 13.5 K5 — Consequential Housing Decision Profile

Classes may include:

- access/screening;
- allocation;
- rent/fee/deposit;
- repair/condemnation decision;
- termination notice;
- compulsory-loss authorization;
- use/status conversion;
- collective/customary housing consequence.

Absorbs decision functions from legacy `HAB-06`, `07`, `09`, `10`, `13`, `19`, `23`, `27`.

## 13.6 K6 — Continuity & Obligation Plan

Records what is owed, by whom, by when, to preserve, repair, stabilize, replace, or transition habitation.

Absorbs plan functions from legacy `HAB-06`, `11`, `12`, `15`, `20`, `23`, `24`, `27`.

# 14. Framework and external-law interfaces

## 14.1 Interface rule

Every interface must state:

- Housing function;
- external function;
- owner;
- source status;
- what Housing may require/observe;
- what Housing may not decide;
- whether the interface is reciprocal or pending.

## 14.2 CIS

**Housing:** imports constitutional authority, rights, data-purpose and conflict-allocation rules.

**Boundary:** Housing does not redefine them locally.

## 14.3 GMEAIA

**Housing:** supplies domain consequence/handoff payload.

**GMEAIA:** owns controlled response states, causal responsibility, acknowledgment/action clocks where applicable, capacity limitation, constitutional gaps, correction, unresolved duties, closure.

**Release blocker:** verify canonical `GMEAIA/0.2.1` file/digest before controlled schema release.

## 14.4 Hearthstone

**Hearthstone:** title/stewardship/rematriation/property transition.

**Housing:** resident continuity, termination/relocation consequences, belongings, accessibility, unresolved habitation duties.

Neither substitutes for the other.

## 14.5 Bioregional Polis

**Polis/local land-use authority:** zoning, municipal spatial policy, land-use instruments, CLT/place governance within mandate.

**Housing:** need, resident continuity, allocation/habitability consequences.

Where jointly implicated functions conflict, use CIS allocation rules rather than informal co-ownership.

## 14.6 DRR / Living Shield

**DRR:** emergency shelter, evacuation, disaster command, recovery coordination.

**Housing:** transition to ordinary/durable habitation and preservation of pre-existing interests.

The adopting authority map should specify the emergency-to-Housing handoff trigger.

## 14.7 Migration

**Migration:** status/reception/mobility.

**Housing:** factual accommodation conditions and Housing relations.

Migration status does not turn off the applicable habitation condition floor.

## 14.8 Adaptive Tax

**Adaptive Tax:** fiscal liability and collection.

**Housing:** receives/records continuity consequences where tax action threatens habitation.

Housing does not alter liability by itself.

## 14.9 AUBI / Work in Liberation

**AUBI:** material-security entitlement architecture.

**Work in Liberation:** employment/contribution governance.

**Housing:** affordability/continuity consequences, tied-housing relations.

Existence is verified-current; reciprocal Housing interface should be migrated/pinned.

## 14.10 Shared Capital

**Shared Capital:** pooled capital, funds, beneficiary claims.

**Housing:** resident relations where those institutions own/finance dwellings.

## 14.11 Financial Systems and external finance law

**Financial Systems:** GGF financial rails/settlement infrastructure within its scope.

**External law:** general mortgage, secured-credit, banking, foreclosure, insolvency, receivership.

**Housing:** home-loss visibility and continuity.

No hypothetical future Monetary/Credit framework is treated as a current owner.

## 14.12 Disability Justice / Global Health / Justice Systems / care law

These frameworks may supply rights, health, disability, justice/remedy interfaces.

Ordinary residential-care law may remain external and may reveal an orphan in some contexts.

Housing owns habitation consequences only.

## 14.13 Water, Sanitation, Climate & Energy, Conduit

Technical services and infrastructure remain external.

Housing imports the resident-facing condition/continuity effect.

Energy is not treated as ownerless in the current GGF.

## 14.14 Kintsugi / Peace / Hearthlight

Housing may receive continuity handoffs for:

- survivor safety;
- reintegration;
- reconstruction/return;
- child/family stabilization.

Refusal of mediation, reconciliation, testimony, or unrelated programme participation cannot become a Housing exclusion rule unless an independent Housing law permits the consequence.

## 14.15 Regenerative Journeys

Regenerative Journeys owns tourism/visitor-economy policy within its mandate.

Housing owns resident-facing consequences of dwelling-use conversion.

The interface remains reciprocal-interface-pending until the receiving clauses are verified/migrated.

## 14.16 Civil registration / legal identity

Status: **unowned-or-audit-required**.

Housing retains the provisional identifier workaround.

A separate canopy/orphan audit should determine whether a civil-registration/legal-identity architecture is warranted.

## 14.17 Cross-framework handoffs are not live merely because Housing names them

A Housing-side handoff declaration is an **interface offer**.

It becomes reciprocal only when:

- the other framework contains a compatible receiving/sending clause; or
- a controlling shared specification supplies the interface.

v0.2 shall not describe pending migration as completed interoperability.

# 15. Conformance, falsification, and schema readiness

## 15.1 Core conformance constraints

### C1 — GMEAIA-governed duty closure
An unaccepted or unperformed Housing duty cannot be treated as discharged merely because a handoff was sent. Capacity limitation remains distinct from constitutional gap.

### C2 — Decision-stage separation
Compulsory-loss authorization references the applicable prior opening/termination act or a lawful exception. Distinct authorities remain visible where required.

### C3 — Notice is validity-bearing where required
A missing/defective notice/process block is independently challengeable.

### C4 — Other mandates are declared
Institutional Housing actors disclose relevant non-housing mandates over residents; `none` is explicit.

### C5 — Degrading condition opens planning
A credible-pathway K4 condition triggers K6 planning without automatically ordering relocation.

### C6 — Transition referential integrity
Material changes preserve predecessor/successor causality through B3 or expressly state that adjacent records are unrelated.

### C7 — Continuity without a Unit
K6 may be opened for homelessness, discharge, reception, or another accommodation duty without a current Unit.

Where a Habitation Interest has `scope = obligor-only`, its obligor relation state is mandatory: `identified`, `disputed`, or `unallocated`. A disputed or unallocated obligor remains visible through K7/GMEAIA rather than being omitted.

### C8 — General versus individual
An individual decision references the general rule/instrument it applies where one exists.

### C9 — Applicable standard cannot be null
Factual accommodation carries an ordinary or emergency/transitional standard; external status cannot make conditions ownerless.

### C10 — Competing claims trigger adjudication routing
Mutually incompatible exclusive interests require K8 or a reference to an equivalent competent adjudication route.

K8 must be representable when the adjudication owner is `identified`, `disputed`, or `unallocated`. Missing adjudication ownership is a recorded failure/gap state, not a reason to omit the referral.

### C11 — Residence threshold is published
Factual-residence standing cannot depend on an unpublished internal threshold.

### C12 — Party roles are declarative
Role representation does not create mandate.

## 15.2 Reduced readiness gates

A jurisdiction/profile is not ready if it cannot answer:

1. Which Party/authority owns each consequential Housing class?
2. How are factual/non-formal residents prevented from disappearing?
3. What condition standard applies to every factual accommodation state?
4. How are multiple independent bases represented?
5. How is loss of one basis prevented from silently erasing another?
6. How are Unit transformations linked without creating title?
7. Can a person hold multiple legitimate Housing interests?
8. Who authorizes compulsory loss, and is that stage separate from notice/opening where required?
9. What independent challenge/remedy route exists?
10. How are mixed care/employment/migration/etc. mandates separated from Housing powers?
11. How are competing claims routed when the adjudication owner is identified, disputed, or unallocated?
12. How are homelessness/replacement obligations represented without a current Unit, and is the obligor relation explicitly identified, disputed, or unallocated?
13. How are unaccepted duties preserved through GMEAIA?
14. How are capacity limitation and constitutional gap kept distinct?
15. Which interfaces are frozen, verified-current, external-law, pending, or genuinely unowned?
16. Can the low-capacity profile operate without a new digital registry?

## 15.3 Falsification gates

The consolidated architecture should be substantially revised if it:

1. causes an interest to disappear because it lacks title/formal instrument;
2. makes one basis the presumed root or legal priority;
3. lets loss of one basis erase another without authority;
4. cannot represent a Unit split/merge/rebuild without losing claims;
5. assumes one legitimate home per Party;
6. turns a tax/migration/benefit primary-address classification into Housing truth by default;
7. cannot represent `obligor-only` accommodation duties, including a disputed or genuinely unallocated obligor;
8. cannot keep a competing-claim referral open when no adjudicator has yet been identified;
9. lets an external care/employment/membership decision execute eviction where a separate Housing act is required;
10. allows indicator/model output to execute a consequential decision;
11. loses the separation between termination notice and compulsory-loss authorization;
12. lets self-help/service withdrawal replace lawful termination;
13. lets a condition finding determine remedy without authority;
14. lets slow degradation remain observable but inert until disaster;
15. lets durable replacement remain permanently below the adopted ordinary floor without lawful justification;
16. lets an unaccepted cross-domain duty disappear;
17. treats capacity failure as constitutional gap;
18. makes a Housing-only provisional identity into a general civil identity;
19. makes Housing's local Unit linkage into a title/cadastre;
20. makes Housing's affordability diagnosis into tax/zoning/credit jurisdiction;
21. grows topic-specific canonical record types again without demonstrating a new legal function.
## 15.4 Schema-readiness blockers

A controlled v0.2.1 schema release remains blocked until:

The consolidation-regression patch has already resolved three prior schema-design omissions: K8 missing-adjudicator representation, `obligor-only` obligor-state representation, and the record-shape axis.

Remaining blockers are:

1. the canonical `GMEAIA/0.2.1` source is locally verified and its compatibility claims checked;
2. final machine field names for Party, Unit, Interest, Basis, scope, recognition, presence, basis effects, sufficiency, exclusivity, and standard are frozen;
3. K1–K9 and B1–B6 receive stable schema identifiers/names;
4. retained interface sources are pinned or explicitly marked by status;
5. every cross-framework interface is classified reciprocal/pending/external-law/gap;
6. the schema cluster/entity location for Housing is decided;
7. regression reconstruction succeeds for the earned adversarial cases.

## 15.5 Regression reconstruction set

Before schema wiring, reconstruct at least:

- applicant screening denial;
- factual resident without civil ID;
- non-named resident exclusion after bereavement/separation;
- multi-basis cooperative tenancy;
- tied housing after job loss;

- Unit subdivision;
- Unit merger;

If a reduced representation fails, repair the reduced architecture first. Do not automatically restore the old topic-specific `HAB-*` type.

# Conclusion

The v0.1 series discovered the framework by repeatedly trying to break it.

The result was useful but too literal: each new failure mode tended to become a new concept or `HAB-*` record.

v0.2.1 keeps the discoveries and discards that inflation.

The consolidated constitutional structure is:

> **Parties hold or affect Habitation Interests concerning Units, areas, or accommodation duties. Interests may rest on several independently governed Habitation Bases. Units and interests have distinct lifecycles. Consequential Housing decisions require lawful authority, reasons, process, challenge, and continuity. Cross-domain Housing duties use the shared lifecycle rather than creating residual Housing jurisdiction.**

That structure preserves the v0.1 insights:

- title is not habitation;
- shelter is not yet durable habitation;
- observation is not authority;
- a person need not hold title or a named lease to remain visible;
- one basis ending does not erase another;
- dependency does not decide priority;
- external decisions may have Housing consequences without becoming Housing decisions;
- loss of one place is distinct from a duty to accommodate somewhere;
- a Unit can transform without erasing claims;
- one Party may have multiple legitimate homes;
- a missing owner is a gap, not permission for Housing to become one.

The remaining task is no longer to make Housing conceptually larger.

It is to test whether this smaller architecture can be wired, implemented at low capacity, and handed across the GGF without losing the distinctions the v0.1 series earned.
