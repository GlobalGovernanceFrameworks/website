**On DeepSeek's contributions**

The two strongest are #2 and #3, and #2 is the most consequential thing anyone has raised about this draft.

*Non-leaseholder household members.* Partners, adult children, elders, carers, friends — people who live somewhere without being named on any instrument. The corpus check makes this sharper than DeepSeek could see: **exactly one framework in the entire GGF mentions domestic violence, and it's Kintsugi** (1 mention, alongside 17 housing mentions). So the intra-household eviction case — the abusive partner who holds the tenancy, the adult child excluded after a death, the carer with no standing — has no owner anywhere in the corpus, and this framework is the natural place for it. §3.11's factual-residence floor is close but it's aimed at occupation without title against a *landlord*, not at occupation without formal interest against *another resident*. Those are different adversaries and need different process.

*The housing/care/confinement seam.* DeepSeek is right that a care provider controlling entry, exit, visitors and daily life is exercising something other than landlord powers. The corpus check makes it worse than stated: **zero documents contain "care home," "nursing home," "supported living," "residential care," or "congregate care."** Not one. §4 item 13 lists disability-support and supported living as in scope, and there is no other framework that could take the handoff. So the requirement DeepSeek proposes — that an institution providing both housing and care must declare which powers are housing-powers and which are care-powers — currently has nothing on the other side of the seam to hand the care-powers to.

**One corpus finding for DeepSeek's #1**

Short-term rentals aren't only a Bioregional Polis zoning matter. **Regenerative Journeys v1.1** already runs a *Housing Protection Fund* — "a portion of the levy funds affordable housing to prevent resident displacement and eco-gentrification" — and gives councils an *Emergency Tourism Suspension* power explicitly triggerable by housing shortages. That's a live tourism-to-housing displacement mechanism, and Regenerative Journeys appears in neither §14's trigger list nor §19's interfaces. It should be in both, and it's a better anchor for the short-term-rental question than open question 24 currently has.

**Where I'd disagree with DeepSeek**

Its answer to Q2 proposes putting the HAB-16 duty into the CIS as a Tier 1 obligation: *"any framework action foreseeably affecting primary habitation must create a HAB-16 handoff."* The instinct is right and the mechanism is wrong. CIS §1.5 lists what the Specification does *not* define — AUBI eligibility, Hearts, worker standards — precisely because it's shared rules rather than domain rules. A clause naming housing and a `HAB-` record would be the first domain-specific obligation in the CIS, and once one is there the queue forms behind it.

The generic version does the same work without that cost. CIS §2.7 already enumerates protected continuity interests — human rights, AUBI Layer 1, essential services, worker rights. The addition would be a duty rather than a list: *where a framework action foreseeably terminates or degrades a protected continuity interest, it shall identify the competent domain authority and create a handoff record; absence of a competent authority is an interface gap, not a licence to proceed.* That covers housing, employment, care and health at once, it reuses the §10.4.2 pattern where a missing record is an interface defect rather than authorisation, and Housing then supplies HAB-16 as its domain instance.

**Two smaller points**

DeepSeek's #7 — a positive prohibition on repurposing housing data for immigration, tax or criminal enforcement — shouldn't be written fresh. Housing occupancy records are Class C personal information under CIS §10.1, and §10.4's firewall machinery already supplies the necessity, less-intrusive-source and purpose-provenance tests. §15.2 should cite that rather than build a parallel rule, or the two will drift.

And the "failure of last resort" point is the right one to take seriously. `HAB-18` recording an unresolved duty is honest but it's also where the framework's central promise runs out. Adaptive Tax handled the analogous problem by naming CIS §18.1 as the allocation route when responsibility is disputed. The same move works here.
