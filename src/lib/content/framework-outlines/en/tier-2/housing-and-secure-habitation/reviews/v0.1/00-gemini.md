This draft is a massive conceptual clarification for the Global Governance Frameworks. By isolating the **secure-habitation interest** as an independent legal object, you have successfully decoupled the *right to live somewhere* from the *title of ownership* (Hearthstone) and the *zoning of land* (Bioregional Polis). In systems engineering terms, you have prevented property law from being forced to perform the control function of human survival.

The taxonomy of habitation relationships in §4 and the mapping in §16.1 align perfectly with what international housing frameworks refer to as the "continuum of land rights". Where legacy systems rely on sweeping titling programs, acknowledging a spectrum of tenure forms—from customary rights to formal rental—is statistically more likely to secure long-term stability for vulnerable populations. Furthermore, your definition of a minimum secure-habitation floor effectively operationalizes the UN OHCHR's recognized criteria for adequate housing—such as habitability, accessibility, affordability, and legal security of tenure—translating them from human rights aspirations into exact constitutional mechanics.

### Missing elements and systemic vulnerabilities

Here are the immediate gaps and failure modes exposed by an adversarial reading of v0.1:

* **Algorithmic exclusion (The Entry Gap):** Section 15.3 brilliantly blocks automated *evictions*. However, it does not explicitly address the automated *denial of entry*. In highly financialized housing markets, third-party tenant screening algorithms effectively redline populations based on aggregated data, denying habitation before a relationship even begins. The intake record (`HAB-02`) or data rules (`HAB-04`) must govern what automated inferences or aggregated risk scores can lawfully block access to an initial tenancy.
* **Private provider insolvency and creditor seizure:** Section 11 outlines the failure of public, social, or cooperative providers. But what happens when a *private* corporate landlord goes bankrupt? Creditors and liquidators often demand the asset be sold empty (vacant possession) to maximize return, effectively extinguishing the residents' habitation interests to satisfy a commercial debt. Section 13 touches on mortgage finance, but it focuses on the *resident's* financial distress. There must be an explicit interface defining whether a valid secure-habitation interest survives the liquidation of the owner's title.
* **Constructive eviction via capital stripping:** Section 8.5 prohibits eviction via essential-service withdrawal (e.g., shutting off water). However, a slower threat exists: deliberate deferred maintenance. If an owner strips capital from a building until it is condemned by the Habitation Standards Authority (§5.3), they effectively use the state's safety apparatus to execute an eviction they couldn't lawfully authorize themselves. `HAB-06` needs a mechanism to prevent safety condemnations from cleanly erasing the owner's continuity and relocation obligations to the resident.
* **Managed retreat vs. physical permanence:** In Section 19.6 (Disaster Risk Reduction) and open question 31 (climate relocation), there is a necessary tension. When land is declared permanently ecologically uninhabitable, the "continuity of home" cannot remain physical. The framework needs a mechanism that converts a localized secure-habitation interest into a "right to replacement habitation," ensuring residents are not anchored to dangerous geography while still maintaining their continuity protections.

### Brief responses to select §22 open questions

* **Q1 (Naming):** Keep *Housing & Secure Habitation Framework*. "Housing" makes it discoverable and recognizable to legacy policymakers and the public; "Secure Habitation" defines the precise constitutional object it actually governs.
* **Q2 (Tier):** Tier 2 (Foundational Application Layer). It is an application of constitutional rights, spatial planning, and property mechanics, not the base operating system itself.
* **Q6 (Informal procedural protection):** Factual residence beyond a specific transient period *must* attach minimum procedural protections (e.g., minimum notice, delay, emergency shelter handoff). If informal occupants have zero protections, the framework mathematically incentivizes landlords and providers to aggressively keep all housing relationships "informal" to bypass the system's governance entirely.
