## 0. Controlled successor notice

This document preserves the Hearthstone vision of moving from extractive ownership toward shared, regenerative stewardship while replacing the v1.5 assumption that one framework can move directly from moral purpose, ecological metrics, spiritual recognition, or political commitment to title transfer, stewardship revocation, tariffs, arrest warrants, emergency receivership, or external enforcement.

Hearthstone v2.0 is a **transition architecture**, not a global property sovereign, court, registry sovereign, police service, taxing authority, emergency command, or universal representative of Indigenous peoples.

> **Commons purpose does not create commons jurisdiction.**

> **Voluntary conversion is not rematriation. Rematriation is not confiscation. Rights recognition is not title. Sacredness is not conveyance. Stewardship is not ownership. Performance evidence is not revocation. Urgency is not receivership. A registry is not law.**

The protocol may invite, advise, fund where separately authorized, mediate, provide model instruments, support claims, facilitate voluntary transactions, prepare lawful records, monitor agreed stewardship duties, and route disputes. It may not compel transfer, extinguish claims, appoint guardians, designate sacredness for another people, issue tariffs, freeze funds, impose sanctions, arrest, seize, restrain, or use force.

**v2.0.2 labour-boundary clarification.** Hearthstone owns the worker, contractor, pension, livelihood, and service-continuity consequences of an asset or stewardship transition. It does not create ordinary employment law. Substantive wages, employment contracts, bargaining rights, union recognition, dismissal, severance, occupational safety, working time, pensions, unemployment entitlements, and worker classification remain with competent labour/employment law, collective agreements, contracts, regulators, tribunals, and other valid instruments. `HSP-21` makes those interests visible during transition; it does not constitute them.

**v2.0.3 person-continuity clarification.** Hearthstone may identify claimants and stewards, correct registry collisions, and consume externally constituted status events where required for an asset/stewardship transition. It does not create general civil identity, register death, determine heirs, or turn its registry into a universal person graph. CIS/0.2.4 governs purpose-limited same-person continuity; Aurora v1.3.2 governs anti-linkage and data-purpose constraints. Pairwise continuity is non-transitive by default, and succession questions remain with competent succession/property/family/Indigenous/customary law.

## 2. Vision

To provide legal, ethical, financial, cultural, and practical pathways by which land, enterprises, infrastructure, capital, cultural assets, and other shared resources may move—voluntarily or through separately lawful public, reparative, or adjudicative processes—from extractive control toward regenerative stewardship.

The protocol seeks a commonwealth in which:

- ecological and cultural relationships receive meaningful legal protection;
- Indigenous nations may pursue Land Back, rematriation, restitution, and reparations through their own authority and applicable law;
- workers, residents, tenants, users, and communities participate in governance without losing existing rights;
- owners and enterprises may choose respected, economically viable stewardship transitions;
- claims are heard before interests are extinguished;
- guardians represent without owning;
- stewards exercise bounded mandates rather than absolute title power;
- essential services survive every transition;
- metrics inform learning without becoming automated government;
- every exceptional power expires and every authority returns.

## 3. Scope

Hearthstone applies to proposed transitions involving:

- land and buildings;
- farms, forests, watersheds, sacred and cultural places;
- enterprises, cooperatives, and productive infrastructure;
- housing, community facilities, and urban commons;
- shared capital, endowments, funds, and debt arrangements;
- public, private, customary, collective, trust, leasehold, and mixed forms;
- cultural, knowledge, data, and digital commons only where the competent cultural, privacy, data, and Indigenous authorities permit it.

Hearthstone does not itself govern ecological rights recognition, compulsory acquisition, taxation, criminal liability, trade sanctions, border action, search, seizure, custody, force, or emergency command.

## 4. Foundational principles

1. **Right relationship without jurisdictional fiction.** Stewardship expresses relationship and duty, but relationship alone does not create legal power.
2. **Affected-nation authority.** The specific Indigenous nation or nations affected determine their own representation, consent, law, sacred relations, knowledge, and preferred remedy.
3. **Claims before conveyance.** Title, occupancy, use, tenancy, labour, customary, public, and reparative claims must be mapped before consequential transfer.
4. **Stewardship over absolutism.** Stewardship entities receive enumerated purposes and duties rather than undefined sovereignty.
5. **Voluntary means voluntary.** A voluntary pathway must be genuinely withdrawable until the legally defined commitment point and may not hide penalties for refusal.
6. **Public and reparative law remain separate.** Expropriation, restitution, reparations, rematriation orders, and public acquisition require their own lawful basis and process.
7. **No deprivation through transition.** Essential water, sanitation, housing, healthcare, food, disability support, energy, wages, pensions, and emergency access survive conversion disputes.
8. **Metric humility.** BHI, LMCI, Love Ledger, Proof of Care, dashboards, citizen observations, and AI outputs inform review but do not create title or consequence.
9. **Just transition before displacement.** Workers, residents, tenants, users, creditors, and dependent communities receive continuity and remedy before structural change is completed.
10. **Subsidiarity with legal clarity.** Decisions should be made at the lowest capable and legitimate level, but local proximity does not substitute for law, affected-nation authority, or rights.
11. **Guardianship is fiduciary representation.** A guardian does not own the ecosystem, land, or trust and does not acquire general political or enforcement power.
12. **Exit is constitutional.** Stewardship arrangements must provide lawful amendment, succession, withdrawal, dissolution, and title destination.
13. **Every authority ends.** Receivership, transition offices, interim restrictions, credentials, data access, and delegated powers expire or return.

## 5. Legal and adoption states

Every Hearthstone instrument must identify one of these states:

- `conceptual`;
- `advisory`;
- `offered_for_voluntary_adoption`;
- `contractually_accepted`;
- `enacted_in_local_law`;
- `treaty_active_in_named_jurisdiction`;
- `judicially_ordered`;
- `suspended`;
- `expired_or_terminated`.

A target date, framework publication, BAZ vote, council endorsement, pilot milestone, treaty aspiration, percentage of converted assets, or dashboard status does not change the adoption state by itself.

## 6. Institutional architecture

### 6.1 Commons Transition Support Network

The **Commons Transition Support Network (CTSN)** is a non-sovereign service network. It may maintain:

- model contracts and trust instruments;
- claimant and affected-party checklists;
- transition-finance templates;
- legal-aid and mediation rosters;
- worker, tenant, user, and service-continuity tools;
- governance pattern libraries;
- accessibility and offline-record systems;
- training, research, and implementation learning;
- compatibility registers for CERGTA and related interfaces.

It may not:

- declare property defective;
- compel participation;
- certify the true Indigenous representative for another people;
- designate sacredness;
- transfer title;
- adjudicate competing claims;
- appoint ecological guardians;
- impose tax, tariff, sanction, freeze, receivership, or market exclusion;
- command law enforcement or emergency response.

### 6.2 Local Commons Transition Offices

A participating jurisdiction or affected nation may establish a local office under its own law. Its functions may include legal information, counselling, facilitation, valuation procurement, document preparation, capacity support, public education, and case coordination.

Its legal identity, appointment, budget, conflicts, review, expiry, and relationship to courts, registries, Indigenous authorities, and public agencies must be recorded in `HSP-01`.

### 6.3 Competent external authorities

The following remain outside Hearthstone unless a jurisdiction lawfully assigns them to a named institution:

- land and company registries;
- courts and tribunals;
- tax and public-finance authorities;
- expropriation, restitution, and reparations authorities;
- affected Indigenous governments and institutions;
- permit and resource-allocation authorities;
- inspection and investigation bodies;
- emergency authorities;
- enforcement services.

Referral is not transfer of power. Advice is not acceptance. Coordination is not joint command.

## 7. Transition pathways

Hearthstone recognizes distinct pathways that may be combined only when each retains separate authority, consent, records, clocks, and remedies.

### 7.1 Voluntary donation or dedication

An owner or competent collective may donate or dedicate an asset subject to title verification, informed authorization, creditor and tax treatment, affected-party continuity, and registrable conveyance.

### 7.2 Voluntary sale or public-interest purchase

A community, nation, trust, fund, or public authority may purchase an asset through transparent valuation and finance. Ecological or sacred significance may justify fundraising priority; it does not erase bargaining rights or compensation rules.

### 7.3 Cooperative or steward-ownership conversion

An enterprise may become a worker cooperative, multi-stakeholder cooperative, purpose trust, foundation-owned company, stewardship trust, or another recognized form. Employee votes, fiduciary duties, minority rights, pensions, creditors, tax, and governance must follow applicable law.

### 7.4 Inheritance and bequest

A person may bequeath assets to an eligible stewardship entity subject to succession law, family and dependent protections, creditor rights, tax, capacity, and challenge.

Hearthstone may record that a competent external authority has established death or another succession-relevant status event and may apply only the transition consequences within its lawful mandate. It does not register the death, determine heirs, probate validity, family status, legal capacity, or inheritance priority. Inactivity, registry silence, non-use of an asset, disappearance, or non-response does not establish death.

Where person continuity across claimant, owner, beneficiary, trustee, steward, or successor records is consequential, the relation follows CIS/0.2.4: purpose-limited, source-preserving, separately authorized, correctable, and non-transitive by default.

### 7.5 Debt settlement and debt-for-stewardship arrangement

Debt relief may support stewardship only through informed agreement or lawful public process. Distress may not be exploited to obtain under-valued assets, waive claims, or coerce cultural or political conformity.

### 7.6 Reparations, restitution, and rematriation

These routes respond to wrongful dispossession, extraction, cultural harm, or continuing injustice. They require the affected nation or claimant’s authority, a claims process, evidence, competing-claims handling, remedy design, title and use consequences, continuity, and appeal.

Rematriation means return into a life-affirming relationship and authority chosen by the affected people. It does not require transfer to a generic BAZ, global council, or framework-created trust.

### 7.7 Public-law acquisition or regulation

A participating jurisdiction may use public acquisition, land reform, conservation covenant, zoning, compulsory purchase, or another lawful instrument only under its own constituting law, public purpose, necessity, proportionality, compensation rules, hearing, appeal, and service-continuity protections.

Hearthstone supplies no independent compulsory-acquisition power.

## 8. Claims and affected-party constitution

Before title, trust, covenant, or stewardship is changed, the case must identify:

- affected Indigenous nations and their own institutions;
- present and historical titleholders;
- customary owners and users;
- occupants, residents, tenants, leaseholders, and licence holders;
- workers, contractors, unions, pension beneficiaries, and dependents;
- creditors, lienholders, insurers, and public obligations;
- ecological guardians and rights-holders where lawfully constituted;
- public-service and essential-resource dependencies;
- neighbouring and transboundary interests;
- future, child, disability, animal-welfare, and ecological interests requiring representation.

A claimant list does not decide standing. A stakeholder map does not equal consent. Lack of documentary title does not erase customary or occupancy evidence. Ecological urgency does not erase process.

## 9. Indigenous nation and rematriation boundary

### 9.1 No proxy sovereignty

No Earth Council, global Indigenous seat, percentage of Indigenous membership, external elder, BAZ label, PHC, NGO, or Hearthstone office may:

- provide FPIC for an affected nation;
- recognize the nation’s representatives;
- disclose sacred knowledge;
- determine territorial boundaries;
- waive restitution or title claims;
- choose the receiving legal entity;
- impose a stewardship form.

Such bodies may offer resources, solidarity, expert support, process audit, or a forum when invited.

### 9.2 Internal plurality

Where representation is contested, Hearthstone records the contest and follows the nation’s own law and any applicable independent legal process. It does not select the politically convenient representative.

### 9.3 Non-participation

A nation or community may refuse Hearthstone mechanisms without losing its claims, services, funding eligibility under other law, or right to pursue another route.

## 10. Sacred, cultural, and biocultural relations

Sacred or biocultural protection may involve several independent legal effects:

- confidential internal recognition;
- restrictions on access or disclosure;
- cultural-heritage protection;
- ecological covenant or land-use restriction;
- title or rematriation claim;
- guardianship or representation;
- restoration or reparations.

These effects must not be collapsed into one designation.

A petition threshold such as a number or percentage of signatories may open review but cannot authenticate sacredness, establish affected-nation authority, transfer title, or authorize enforcement.

## 11. Title, tenure, trust, and conveyance

### 11.1 Verified baseline

No transition may proceed from a dashboard or aspirational registry. `HSP-12` must identify the legally operative interests and known disputes.

### 11.2 Stewardship entity does not automatically replace title

A stewardship trust may:

- hold legal title;
- hold a lease, covenant, licence, beneficial interest, management mandate, or purpose right;
- operate an enterprise whose shares are held by another entity;
- coexist with customary or public title;
- serve as an administrative vehicle without owning the underlying ecosystem.

The exact relationship must be stated.

### 11.3 No silent extinguishment

Creation of a trust, commons registry, ecological right, sacred designation, or stewardship plan does not silently extinguish mortgages, tenancies, worker claims, customary uses, licences, easements, pensions, public duties, or competing claims.

### 11.4 Conveyance package

A complete conveyance package includes:

- parties and authority;
- title and boundary;
- instrument and effective date;
- consideration or reparative basis;
- conditions precedent;
- residual rights and liabilities;
- tenant, worker, user, and service continuity;
- tax, debt, pension, and creditor treatment;
- registry instructions;
- challenge, correction, and reversal conditions.

## 12. Stewardship trusts and commons entities

### 12.1 Charter requirements

Every stewardship entity must state:

- legal form and jurisdiction;
- asset or mission;
- purpose and beneficiaries;
- relationship to title;
- enumerated powers;
- prohibited acts;
- ecological and social duties;
- affected-nation authority and protected matters;
- governance and representation;
- finance, distributions, reserves, and audit;
- amendment and review;
- guardian relationship;
- suspension, succession, insolvency, exit, and dissolution;
- destination of title and assets at termination.

### 12.2 Prohibited acts

Unless separately authorized, a stewardship entity may not:

- alienate an inalienable asset;
- mortgage or pledge protected assets outside charter limits;
- evict residents or terminate essential access without applicable law and process;
- disclose protected knowledge;
- use ecological or social metrics as loyalty tests;
- exercise criminal, police, border, intelligence, or general regulatory power;
- claim to represent an affected Indigenous nation;
- convert a guardian into an owner or executive sovereign.

### 12.3 Governance patterns

The Commons Governance Pattern Library may offer worker-community, resident-land, ecosystem-support, cooperative-enterprise, public-commons, and digital-commons patterns. A pattern is a template, not legal adoption or evidence of suitability.

## 13. Participation, representation, and youth

Youth, workers, residents, affected communities, and future-generations advocates should hold meaningful roles. Hearthstone does not create universal fixed seat percentages or automatic vetoes.

A charter may establish reserved seats, consent domains, qualified majorities, delay-and-reconsider powers, published dissent, or independent review where lawful and contextually justified.

Affected-nation authority is not reducible to a seat count.

## 14. Workers, residents, tenants, users, and services

No commons transition is complete until it addresses:

- continued occupancy and anti-displacement protections;
- affordability and accessibility;
- water, sanitation, healthcare, food, energy, mobility, communications, and emergency access;
- wages, benefits, pensions, collective bargaining, retraining, and worker ownership;
- safe refusal to participate in unlawful or unsafe conversion activity;
- contractor and informal-worker interests;
- community facilities and public-service obligations;
- grievance and remedy.

For workers and contractors, **addressing** these interests means identifying their competent legal, contractual, collective-bargaining, pension, regulatory, or other source; preserving them through the transition; and routing unresolved duties to the competent owner. Hearthstone does not determine the general wage floor, employment status, dismissal standard, severance entitlement, union-recognition rule, bargaining procedure, statutory working time, occupational-safety code, or unemployment entitlement.

A transition may improve governance while still harming people through disruption. Those harms count.

## 15. Finance, valuation, dividends, and incentives

### 15.1 Valuation pluralism

Financial value, ecological condition, livelihood dependence, replacement cost, cultural relation, and reparative significance must be recorded separately. Non-market value does not provide a mathematically objective purchase price.

### 15.2 Voluntary incentives

Tax benefits, grants, guarantees, transition bonds, technical assistance, Hearts, Leaves, honours, and preferential programmes may be offered only by the competent authority and with transparent eligibility, anti-gaming rules, review, correction, and appeal.

### 15.3 Public financial action

Taxes, levies, mandatory reparations, tariffs, freezes, clawbacks, escrow, forfeiture, redistribution, and funding conditions require their own `CERGTA-45` or `CERGTA-46` route.

A BHI, LMCI, Love Ledger, Proof of Care, or transition-readiness score cannot automatically move money.

### 15.4 Commons dividends

A stewardship entity may adopt a distribution policy, but Hearthstone does not prescribe a universal 40/30/30 formula, exchange rate, crawling peg, or Tobin tax. Distribution must account for maintenance, ecological duties, workers, residents, public obligations, reserves, debt, affected-nation agreements, and beneficiaries.

## 16. Ecological and social evidence

Hearthstone receives ecological evidence through EIOI v2.7 and planetary-health synthesis through PHG v0.9.

Every use must record:

- method and version;
- purpose and permitted use;
- spatial and temporal fit;
- uncertainty and dissent;
- distributional effects;
- baseline and counterfactual;
- external shocks;
- affected-nation and community interpretation;
- correction status;
- prohibited inferences.

LMCI, spiritual well-being, cultural vitality, or cohesion evidence may support voluntary learning. It cannot diagnose a community, create title defects, establish breach, or justify adverse action.

## 17. Performance, correction, revocation, and succession

### 17.1 Performance review

A stewardship review is not an investigation or adjudication. It asks whether charter duties and plans are being met, what evidence supports the conclusion, what external conditions matter, and what support or correction is appropriate.

### 17.2 No automatic escalation

A threshold breach may open review. It cannot automatically:

- remove stewards;
- appoint receivers;
- transfer title;
- freeze funding;
- revoke permits;
- blacklist actors;
- impose sanctions.

### 17.3 Correction and capacity support

Where lawful and safe, the preferred sequence is:

1. notice and evidence access;
2. steward and community response;
3. independent verification;
4. no-fault and structural analysis;
5. voluntary correction and capacity support;
6. time-bound review;
7. separate referral if consequential action remains necessary.

### 17.4 Suspension, revocation, and replacement

A competent authority may act only through `CERGTA-34`, with reasons, notice, hearing, interim relief, proportionality, service continuity, succession, registry correction, and remedy.

## 18. Urgent ecological protection and temporary receivership

### 18.1 Urgent protection

Hearthstone may identify an imminent threat and route a minimum-necessary referral. It cannot itself authorize entry, seizure, closure, control of operations, or security action.

### 18.2 Receivership

Temporary ecological or stewardship receivership requires a separate `CERGTA-43` record specifying:

- competent authority and legal source;
- individualized facts;
- exact asset and powers;
- less restrictive alternatives;
- essential-service and worker continuity;
- conflicts and independence;
- reporting and financial controls;
- review and interim relief;
- automatic expiry;
- return to ordinary governance;
- compensation and remedy if wrongful.

An emergency declaration does not automatically establish these elements.

### 18.3 No-fault support

Disaster, cross-border pollution, inherited damage, and other external shocks may justify grants, technical assistance, insurance, or temporary support without classifying stewards as deficient.

## 19. Registry and public record

The Commons and Stewardship Registry is a record system, not a title sovereign.

It must:

- cite the legally effective source;
- distinguish proposed, disputed, suspended, and final status;
- preserve correction and supersession;
- allow offline and accessible participation;
- protect personal, commercial, cultural, and sacred information;
- avoid publishing sensitive ecological locations;
- propagate corrections;
- support lawful deletion and protected archive.

Blockchain may be used as one integrity mechanism. Immutability cannot override correction, privacy, Indigenous data law, court order, or legal title.

### 19.1 Person-continuity and registry boundary

A Commons and Stewardship Registry may hold purpose-limited references to persons or roles necessary for title, claim, stewardship, guardianship, tenancy, creditor, worker, beneficiary, or transition administration. Those references are not a civil registry or universal identity layer.

Where two records are asserted to concern the same person:

- the specific legal/administrative purpose and authority must be recorded;
- only minimum necessary attributes may cross the boundary;
- pairwise recognition is non-transitive by default;
- source records remain distinct;
- mistaken merge, mistaken split, duplicate, collision, correction, and unlinking must be supported;
- a continuity assertion does not establish title, standing, heirship, citizenship, migration status, family relation, legal capacity, or unrelated eligibility.

Where an externally established death/status event affects title, trust, claim, occupancy, stewardship, or beneficiary administration, Hearthstone preserves unresolved claims, remedies, belongings, service continuity, accrued obligations, and succession questions until the competent authority resolves them. Receipt of the event does not itself authorize transfer, eviction, exclusion, title change, trustee replacement, or dissolution.

HSP-33 may carry the domain registry correction; CIS/0.2.4 Appendix M supplies the constitutional minimum where the correction or linkage crosses person records or frameworks.

## 20. Enforcement and security boundary

Hearthstone v2.0 retires reliance on the former Global Enforcement Task Force and any theory that framework participation or refusal directly triggers tariffs, indictments, arrest warrants, or physical enforcement.

A competent court or authority may issue an order under separate law. `HSP-38` records the handoff and acceptance.

Where execution involves search, seizure, restraint, custody, force, intelligence, cyber action, border action, asset freeze, or other coercive power, the executor must separately satisfy SCPA. Hearthstone supplies neither mission command nor operational authority.

> **A commons claim may justify a hearing. It does not authorize a raid.**

## 21. Communications, culture, and public learning

The Story of Stewardship, art residencies, mentorship, curricula, simulations, and public toolkits may support cultural transition.

They must not:

- misrepresent contested claims as settled;
- shame non-participants;
- target developmental or spiritual profiles;
- use counter-disinformation work as surveillance or censorship;
- condition rights on ideological agreement;
- publish protected knowledge or personal stories without consent.

Former owners, workers, residents, and communities may experience grief, uncertainty, or disagreement without being classified as obstacles to progress.

## 22. Pilots and sandbox zones

Pilots may test:

- voluntary conversion forms;
- governance charters;
- legal-aid and counselling;
- worker, tenant, and service safeguards;
- offline registries;
- stewardship planning;
- financing and transition support;
- monitoring and learning.

Pilots may not test unactivated powers such as compulsory conversion, title extinguishment, automatic sacred designation, tax, tariff, sanction, freeze, receivership, eviction, seizure, or arrest.

A target such as “25% of assets stewarded” is a learning metric, not a mandate to reach the percentage.

## 23. Complaints, review, and remedy

Every consequential pathway must provide:

- accessible notice and reasons;
- language, disability, cultural, and digital-access support;
- counsel or qualified assistance where stakes require it;
- evidence access subject to protected-knowledge safeguards;
- correction and reply;
- independent review and appeal;
- interim relief before irreversible harm;
- registry correction;
- restitution, compensation, or requalification where appropriate;
- whistleblower and retaliation protection.

The Stewardship Ombudsman may assist and investigate service complaints within a lawfully defined mandate. It cannot adjudicate title or impose sanctions.

## 24. Exit, dissolution, and authority return

A complete exit or dissolution plan addresses:

- title and asset destination;
- outstanding claims and litigation;
- residents, tenants, users, workers, pensions, and services;
- debt, tax, contracts, and public obligations;
- guardian and affected-nation relationships;
- permits and licences;
- funds and reserves;
- registry and public notice;
- data, archives, and protected knowledge;
- successor governance;
- return of office, receiver, credential, and delegated authority.

A BAZ or jurisdiction may leave a GGF arrangement without punitive forfeiture merely for leaving. Existing lawful rights and obligations continue according to applicable law.

## 30. Version transition from v1.5

### Preserved

- regenerative stewardship and commons vision;
- voluntary corporate and cooperative transition;
- Land Back, rematriation, reparations, inheritance, and debt-settlement pathways;
- transition finance, mentorship, education, pattern libraries, offline access, and public learning;
- just-transition support for workers and communities;
- stewardship review, correction, succession, and dissolution;
- local and bioregional implementation with transboundary cooperation.

### Reconstituted

- Stewardship Trusts are lawful forms with explicit title relationships, not automatic replacements for ownership.
- Bio-cultural arrangements follow the affected nation’s authority rather than mandatory PHC/Earth Council co-governance.
- Sacred designation is separated from title, guardianship, and enforcement.
- Rematriation is separated from voluntary sale, public acquisition, reparations, and compulsory legal process.
- The registry records lawfully effective facts and cannot create or perfect title by blockchain entry.
- BHI, LMCI, Love Ledger, Proof of Care, readiness scores, and AI monitoring may trigger review only.
- Stewardship correction, suspension, revocation, receivership, and succession use distinct records and authorities.
- youth and future-generations participation gains procedural weight without a universal automatic veto.
- transition offices support rather than rule.

### Retired

- the International Commons Court as a framework-created universal court;
- framework-created enforcement against non-participating nations;
- trade denial, tariffs, indictments, arrest warrants, or GETF protection arising directly from Hearthstone;
- Earth Council ratification of sacredness for affected peoples;
- automatic stewardship escalation or revocation from BHI/LMCI thresholds;
- emergency receivership triggered solely by crisis command;
- universal dividend percentages, exchange rules, or transaction taxes;
- mandatory blockchain or Love Ledger participation;
- asset-conversion targets as activation thresholds.

## 31. Minimum implementation package

Before any consequential transition, an adopter must have:

1. an exact legal-adoption record;
2. competent registries and adjudicative routes;
3. affected-nation and claims procedures;
4. tenant, worker, user, and essential-service protections;
5. valuation, compensation, debt, tax, and pension capacity;
6. independent legal aid and accessible complaints;
7. CERGTA-compatible records and protected data systems;
8. SCPA-compatible execution interfaces where coercive orders might arise;
9. emergency interfaces for urgent ecological action;
10. tested exit, dissolution, succession, and authority-return procedures.

## 32. Closing doctrine

Hearthstone does not abandon the ambition to transform property. It makes that ambition governable.

> **A commons worthy of trust cannot be built by treating claims, consent, title, stewardship, metrics, receivership, and enforcement as the same act.**

The protocol succeeds when people and ecosystems gain durable protection, affected nations regain authority, workers and communities gain meaningful governance, owners can transition without deception, wrongful dispossession can be repaired, and every consequential action remains contestable, correctable, and bounded by law.

## A.2 Controlling rules

1. `CERGTA/0.1` controls the separation of observation, interpretation, rights review, legal recognition, guardianship, affected Indigenous-nation authority, sacred and biocultural designation, claims, title, tenure, stewardship, allocation, permitting, inspection, urgent protection, restrictions, finance, adjudication, restoration, and closure.
2. `EIPHR/0.1` controls the division among EIOI, the Planetary Health Synthesis Office, and the Planetary Health Council. EIOI supplies evidence; PHSO compiles modular synthesis; PHC interprets and recommends. None may create a downstream legal or financial consequence.
3. MOS is advisory only. Its Dynamic Rights Spectrum, philosophical language, developmental translations, structural-failure hypotheses, and guardianship metaphors never create legal status, classify persons, determine credibility, establish Indigenous consent, or activate consequential action.
4. Hearthstone controls the cluster interface for claims, competing claims, title, tenure, trust constitution, voluntary conversion, rematriation, compensation, debt, tenants, workers, registry correction, receivership, succession, and dissolution. Sectoral ecological frameworks may provide evidence or programme support but cannot decide those questions by implication.
5. Water, Biodiversity, Oceans, and Soil retain only the powers separately constituted in applicable law and recorded through the relevant CERGTA route. Framework membership, a council vote, a dashboard value, a target, a pilot, a future date, or a coalition size does not activate authority.
6. Emergency identity and coordination use CDEE and ECRC. Ecological urgency does not create emergency command, and an emergency declaration does not create ecological title, guardianship, permanent restrictions, receivership, or enforcement authority.
7. Search, seizure, restraint, custody, force, cyber action, border action, intelligence use, asset freezing, or analogous coercive execution requires separate SCPA authority. A commons or ecological claim may justify review; it does not authorize a raid.
8. The specific affected Indigenous nation or nations determine their representatives, applicable law, territorial and relational authority, consent process, internal plurality, protected knowledge, and whether to participate. Global or framework-created Indigenous bodies may support or audit but cannot consent or waive rights for another people.
9. Essential minimums for water, sanitation, health, disability support, food, shelter, rescue, crew welfare, and ecological survival remain protected from commercial, tariff, certification, funding, listing, or transition leverage.
10. Every handoff uses the canonical CERGTA states. Receipt, technical ingestion, silence, consultation, or partial use is not acceptance and never creates authority.
11. Every consequential route keeps its own legal basis, decision-maker, affected persons, evidence, access controls, clock, expiry, challenge, correction, remedy, termination, and data-disposition record.
12. Unknown, missing, stale, or incompatible versions fail closed for consequential action. Non-coercive care, emergency life-safety support, accessibility, explanation, and correction should continue wherever lawful and safe.

## A.3 Record inheritance and packaging

A local record may implement, extend, split, contain, import, or mark a CERGTA record not applicable. It may not erase the canonical identity, merge distinct powers, weaken an affected-person safeguard, or use a compound package to supply a missing legal basis.

The forty-field CERGTA header, access classification, handoff state, independent clocks, correction propagation, remedy, termination, authority return, and data disposition are inherited wherever the substantive base creates or transports a consequential record. Advisory and observation-only records must state their prohibited inferences.

## A.4 Registry and compatibility failure

The file `commons-ecological-authority-cluster-conformance-registry-v0.1.json` is the machine-readable release registry. It is evidence of the exact release set, not an authority record. A hash mismatch, missing component, unsupported local extension, or unresolved version conflict changes the compatibility state to `failed_closed`.

A compatibility failure blocks new consequential cross-framework handoffs. It does not by itself terminate independently valid local law, erase existing rights, interrupt essential services, or authorize emergency or coercive action.

## A.5 Closure

A cluster transaction closes only after all accepted handoffs, powers, services, persons, property, guardianship duties, claims, title or tenure effects, financial actions, emergency dependencies, coercive dependencies, corrections, remedies, clocks, credentials, records, and residual risks are resolved or assigned to a named lawful custodian without preserving expired power.
