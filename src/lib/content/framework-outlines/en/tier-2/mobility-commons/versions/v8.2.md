## 1. Executive Summary

The Mobility Commons reimagines transportation as a regenerative, equitable, and shared global resource. It establishes a fossil-free, universally accessible mobility system that respects ecosystems, prioritizes community sovereignty, and ensures that movement serves human flourishing rather than the other way around.

Transportation accounts for approximately 14% of global greenhouse gas emissions (IPCC, 2022), with freight alone representing nearly half of transport-related carbon. The 20th-century model — car-centric, fossil-fueled, and extractive — has driven climate change, displaced communities, and degraded ecosystems.

This framework offers an alternative. It is built on four pillars:

| Pillar | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **Governance** | Democratic, multi-stakeholder coordination through the Global Mobility Council |
| **Infrastructure** | Regenerative, bioregional networks that restore ecosystems while connecting communities |
| **Economy** | Just transition funding, Hearts/Leaves incentives, and community ownership models |
| **Justice** | Universal access, mobility reparations, and the right to stay |

The framework is designed for phased implementation, beginning with pilot projects in aligned BAZs and scaling through the pull effect of superior outcomes — cleaner air, shorter commutes, stronger communities.

**v8.2 justice clarification:** The people affected by a mobility decision are not necessarily identical to those already represented inside the decision-making jurisdiction. Planning must account for residents, workers, displaced people, cross-border users, and people excluded from access, without treating equal moral standing as an automatic legal right to enter another jurisdiction. The Mobility Commons governs **transport services, infrastructure, and access to mobility**; immigration status, asylum, residence, and political membership remain within the Migration & Human Mobility Framework and applicable law. Projects must consider the effects of both enabling and restricting movement.

## 2. Core Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Mobility as a Commons** | The freedom to move is a public good, governed transparently by multi-stakeholder councils, prioritizing community benefit over private gain |
| **Fossil-Free by Default** | All new infrastructure and vehicles align with net-zero targets by 2030; temporary emergency fuel exemptions require strict sunset clauses |
| **Proximity Priority** | Reduce *unnecessary or forced* journeys by making everyday needs accessible locally, without diminishing the ability to undertake journeys people freely choose |
| **Right to Movement & Access** | Safe, affordable, and accessible mobility for all, regardless of location, income, or ability; distinguish physical transport access from legal authorization to cross borders |
| **Equal Moral Standing Across Borders** | Give due weight to basic interests regardless of birthplace, citizenship, residence, ability, or economic contribution; relevant differences in legal authority do not erase the interests of those excluded from mobility |
| **Right to Stay & Freedom from Forced Movement** | Communities should have viable opportunities to remain in place and protections against displacement caused by infrastructure, climate policy, or avoidable economic pressure |
| **Proportionate, Reviewable Restrictions** | Mobility-service and infrastructure restrictions require documented lawful purposes, evidence of necessity, attention to less restrictive options, and accessible review; the GMC does not determine immigration admission |
| **Universal Design** | Systems designed for universal access, adhering to disability justice standards |
| **Bioregional Integration** | Corridors respect ecosystems, watershed boundaries, wildlife migration, and BAZ-led plans |
| **Community-Led & Owned** | Projects co-designed with communities; clear pathways from private/state to cooperative ownership |
| **Data Sovereignty** | Mobility data is a community-controlled resource, protected against surveillance under the Aurora Accord |
| **Freight as Commons** | The movement of goods is governed by the same regenerative principles as passenger movement, with transparency across supply chains |
| **Indigenous Transportation Sovereignty** | BAZ-level authority over transportation in traditional territories, with Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC 2.0) |

## 3. Governance Architecture

### 3.1 The Global Mobility Council (GMC)

**Role:** Sets global standards for decarbonization, equity, interoperability, and safety. Coordinates transnational corridors. Resolves disputes.

**Composition:**

| Constituency | Representation |
|--------------|----------------|
| **BAZs** | Elected representatives from regional BAZ federations |
| **Indigenous Nations** | Appointed by the Earth Council, with veto over matters affecting traditional territories |
| **Youth** | Permanent seats with decision-making authority; suspensive veto over projects with 50+ year lifespans |
| **Disability Advocates** | Appointed by the Global Disability Alliance |
| **Informal Transport Workers** | Elected by cooperatives and unions (boda bodas, taxi associations, etc.) |
| **Freight & Logistics** | Representatives from port workers, rail unions, and supply chain cooperatives |
| **Migrants & Displaced People** | Delegates selected by accountable migrant, refugee, displaced-person, and cross-border commuter associations, with representation across origins, statuses, and disabilities; protected against retaliation |
| **Technical Experts** | Appointed by the Global Technology Council for term-limited advisory roles |

**Decision-Making:**

- **Standards:** Consensus among BAZ representatives; supermajority (2/3) for major changes
- **Transnational Corridors:** Affected BAZs must approve; Indigenous veto applies. Corridor proposals must also document and respond to input from materially affected people beyond the approving BAZs, including prospective movers and users. Such input does not independently confer territorial siting authority.
- **Reasons for Exclusion:** Where a corridor or public service imposes significant access restrictions, publish a proportionality and distributional assessment with an appeal or review route; lawful immigration controls remain governed elsewhere.
- **Disputes:** Values-Based Conflict Transformation protocol, with the Digital Justice Tribunal as final arbiter within its assigned mobility-governance competence; migration-status and territorial-rights disputes follow their competent bodies.

**Standing Committees:**

| Committee | Mandate |
|-----------|---------|
| **Freight & Logistics Committee** | Oversees freight decarbonization targets, green corridor standards, port transition, and supply chain transparency. Co-chaired by port worker and rail union representatives. Sets freight-specific metrics and reports to full GMC quarterly. |
| **Infrastructure & Design Committee** | Reviews Planetary Health and Community & Cultural Impact Assessments. Coordinates the Global Pathways Initiative. |
| **Ceremonial Governance Committee** | Co-chaired by Indigenous representative. Manages Phase 1 scoping processes and Red Lines registry. |
| **Justice & Access Committee** | Oversees universal access audits, reparations fund, gender/kinship equity compliance, the Mobility Capacity and Fair Burden Protocol, and inclusion of migrants, displaced people, cross-border users, and prospective movers; issues distributional impact findings with independent review. |

### 3.2 Subsidiarity Bridge: How the GMC Relates to Existing Authorities

| Level | Authority | Relationship to GMC |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Global** | GMC sets decarbonization, equity, and interoperability standards | Primary standard-setting body |
| **Transnational** | Regional bodies coordinate cross-border corridors (e.g., EU, ASEAN, African Union) | Implement GMC standards regionally |
| **National** | DOTs, aviation authorities, port authorities regulate safety and licensing | Must comply with GMC standards to access BAZ markets |
| **BAZ** | Control land use, infrastructure siting, local mobility plans | Adopt GMC standards; use procurement power to enforce compliance |
| **Local** | Operate transit systems, manage streets | Implement BAZ plans |

**Enforcement Mechanism:** BAZs control land use and procurement. If a national DOT or corporation wants to operate within a BAZ, they must meet legally adopted GMC standards within the BAZ's competent authority. This creates bottom-up enforcement without requiring the GMC to replace existing authorities.

**Subsidiarity and affected-persons duty:** Subsidiarity identifies the appropriate level of decision-making, **not the limit of moral concern**. For major investments, the BAZ documents the interests of people inside and outside its territory who are substantially affected. An outsider's right to be considered and heard does not automatically override local democratic authority or Indigenous FPIC 2.0; conversely, an incumbent preference is not, by itself, sufficient justification for denying essential accessibility or obstructing mobility opportunities.

### 3.3 Ceremonial Governance: Operational Protocol

Ceremonial governance is not vague aspiration. It is the binding Phase 1 scoping process for any major infrastructure project affecting Indigenous territories or sacred sites.

**Three-Phase Process:**

| Phase | Activity | Output |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Phase 1: Ceremonial Scoping** | Indigenous-led talking circles, elder testimony, community consultations. Facilitated by the BAZ, with resources provided for full participation. Duration: 3-12 months depending on complexity | Recorded "Red Lines" — places that cannot be crossed, routes that must be honored, timing restrictions for ceremonial seasons |
| **Phase 2: Technical Planning** | Engineers and planners design within Red Line constraints. Any proposed deviation requires returning to Phase 1 | Infrastructure plan that respects Red Lines, with documented consultation |
| **Phase 3: Binding Resolution** | If consensus cannot be reached, the Digital Justice Tribunal resolves with a presumption in favor of the community with the deepest documented cultural connection to the land. All decisions are appealable only on procedural grounds | Final binding decision; implementation proceeds or halts accordingly |

**What Ceremonial Governance Does:**
- Establishes constraints that engineers must work within
- Gives communities binding authority over what cannot be done
- Creates a clear, predictable process for developers

**What Ceremonial Governance Does Not Do:**
- Replace engineering with ritual
- Require consensus on technical specifications
- Delay projects indefinitely without resolution

### 3.4 Cross-Framework Mobility and Migration Interface

**Division of responsibilities:** The Mobility Commons plans and governs physical accessibility, transport corridors, safety, affordability, interoperable ticketing, and the distribution of infrastructure impacts. The **Migration & Human Mobility Framework** addresses cross-border admission, immigration status, asylum, residency pathways, labor-migration permissions, and political membership. The Mobility Commons does not create an independent authority to admit, exclude, deport, or enfranchise people.

**Joint coordination rules:**

1. **Shared impact scoping:** For international corridors, displacement responses, or significant relocation-related infrastructure, the GMC and the competent migration bodies jointly identify affected populations, including prospective movers and origin communities; each body retains its own legal mandate.
2. **Safe service design:** Transport access, emergency routes, wayfinding, accessibility, and fare arrangements must not become covert immigration-screening systems. Identity and eligibility checks are limited to lawful, necessary functions and subject to the Aurora Accord.
3. **Rights protection:** Humanitarian movement and evacuation planning incorporate applicable rights, non-discrimination, non-refoulement duties where relevant, Indigenous territorial safeguards, and due process through the competent frameworks.
4. **Accountable disagreement:** A joint issue register records unresolved conflicts between transport access, receiving-community capacity, and migration policy. The GMC may recommend capacity measures and publish evidence, but may not pre-empt admission decisions.
5. **Operational referrals:** Housing, health, education, labor, and social-provision effects are referred to the corresponding GGF bodies rather than treated as consequences the transport council alone can resolve.

## 4. Regenerative Infrastructure

### 4.1 The Global Pathways Initiative

Funded by the Global Commons Fund, with mandates for long-term maintenance, 5-year climate resilience audits, and redundant pathway networks for crisis resilience.

| Infrastructure Type | Description | Priority |
|---------------------|-------------|----------|
| **Global High-Speed Rail** | Connects population centers, replaces short-haul aviation. Wildlife corridors and permaculture-integrated designs | High |
| **Regional Rail & Transit** | Electrified rail networks connecting BAZs and rural areas | High |
| **Non-Motorized Networks** | Pedestrian pathways and cycle networks in all BAZs; separated from motor traffic | High |
| **EV & Hydrogen Infrastructure** | Universal charging/refueling networks with permeable, pollution-filtering materials | Medium |
| **Sustainable Maritime Corridors** | Green corridors for ammonia/hydrogen/battery-powered ships; supports Just Transition for port workers | Medium |
| **Living Infrastructure** | Bridges as wildlife corridors, terraced permaculture walls, aquifer-recharging road surfaces. Projects earn Leaves in the Love Ledger | High where applicable |

### 4.2 Bioregional Design Protocols

All infrastructure projects undergo two assessments:

| Assessment | Purpose | Approval Body |
|------------|---------|---------------|
| **Planetary Health Impact Assessment** | Evaluates carbon footprint, biodiversity impacts, water use, and alignment with Biosphere Health Index | BAZ Environmental Council |
| **Community & Cultural Impact Assessment** | Evaluates impacts on communities, including Indigenous consultation (FPIC 2.0), accessibility, cultural heritage, and effects on residents and prospective users who may be excluded from the corridor | BAZ Community Council + affected communities |
| **Distributional Mobility Assessment (v8.2)** | Identifies who gains or loses access, time, money, housing or service capacity; assesses origin/destination effects, alternatives, displacement and ability to appeal | GMC Justice & Access Committee review + relevant BAZ bodies; migration-related decisions referred to competent migration institutions |

**Design Requirements:**
- Corridors follow watershed boundaries where possible, not arbitrary political borders
- Wildlife crossings integrated every 5 km in ecologically sensitive areas
- Seasonal adaptation protocols for weather extremes
- Ancestral route protection: where traditional travel routes exist, they are preserved or incorporated
- For projects likely to influence relocation or cross-border travel, assess both *unmet mobility demand* and possible pressures on housing, services, livelihoods, and local ecosystems; do not substitute a transport assessment for immigration or housing decisions
- Provide meaningful consultation in accessible languages and formats, including protected avenues for displaced or undocumented people to describe access barriers without unnecessary personal-data collection

### 4.3 Emergency Mobility Activation

For climate disasters and humanitarian crises:

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Disaster Response Mobile Units** | Pre-positioned fleets for rapid deployment |
| **Climate Migration Transportation Corridors** | Pre-planned, accessible routes for safe relocation, including options for voluntary return or remaining safely where feasible; operation must respect lawful protection and admission procedures |
| **Coordination** | Integrated with Crisis Command Protocol and Migration & Human Mobility Framework; housing, social services, and disability-access partners included in operational planning |

### 4.4 Mobility Capacity and Fair Burden Protocol

**Purpose:** Expand meaningful mobility while preventing costs from being concentrated on those least able to bear them. This is a planning and coordination protocol, **not a new migration-admission test, automatic population cap, or residency quota**.

**When required:** Apply to major new corridors, materially expanded passenger capacity, climate-relocation systems, or mobility projects with plausible concentrated costs to vulnerable populations at origin, along the route, or at destination. Scale documentation to project size and risk.

| Gate | Required assessment | Operational response |
|------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **1. Access & unmet need** | Who currently cannot make important desired journeys? Who would benefit from access, including nonresidents and people excluded today? | Publish an accessibility baseline and realistic no-project / alternative-project comparisons |
| **2. Concentrated burden** | Which renters, informal workers, low-income travelers, disabled people, origin communities, or ecosystem-dependent groups may experience disproportionate costs? | Avoid or mitigate displacement, fare burdens, service bottlenecks, and livelihood losses; budget protections before scaling |
| **3. Capacity dependencies** | Could housing, healthcare, education, or public services become constrained? Which institution can act on the constraint? | Coordinate funded capacity responses with competent sector bodies; do not grant an indefinite exclusionary veto to actors who block feasible capacity improvements |
| **4. Rights and legitimate authority** | What are the relevant rights, Indigenous consent requirements, local decisions, and claims of people outside existing electoral boundaries? | Respect FPIC 2.0 and lawful authority; document outside interests; provide review and remedy within the correct jurisdiction |
| **5. Learning and remedy** | What effects are measured, what uncertainty remains, and who may request review? | Set proportional milestones, independent evaluation, and corrective actions rather than automatic exclusion triggered by raw indicators |

**Fair-burden rule:** A project with large aggregate benefits is not automatically justified if its costs produce serious, preventable hardship for a vulnerable group. Compensate, redesign, sequence, or reject the project where proportional mitigation is unavailable. Claims that excluded people will benefit should be evaluated as seriously as claims about current residents' losses.

**No manufactured veto:** A local authority's refusal to undertake feasible housing or service reform may be relevant evidence of institutional capacity, but shall not *by itself* provide indefinite grounds for barring access to mobility infrastructure or public transport. At the same time, corridor approval does not create a cross-border entry entitlement.

**Causal review rather than mechanical brakes:** Changes in housing costs, wages, service pressures, social trust, or local political opposition require independent contextual analysis. Do not use social-trust scores, electoral backlash, nationality, or migration status as automatic transport-service exclusion criteria. The remedy should address identified harms and decision-makers within their lawful authority.

## 5. Freight & Logistics Protocol

The movement of goods is governed by the same regenerative principles as passenger movement. This protocol fills the gap left by frameworks that focus only on moving people.

### 5.1 Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Freight as Commons** | Supply chains are transparent, accountable, and serve community needs, not just corporate efficiency |
| **Shortest Path First** | Where possible, goods move through the shortest, lowest-emissions route; long-haul trucking replaced by rail and ship |
| **Last-Mile Regeneration** | Urban freight shifts to cargo bikes, electric micro-vehicles, and micro-distribution hubs |

### 5.2 Infrastructure Transition

| Mode | Current | Target (2035) |
|------|---------|---------------|
| **Ocean Shipping** | Heavy fuel oil | Sail-freight, wind-assisted propulsion, green ammonia/hydrogen |
| **Rail Freight** | Diesel | 100% electrified |
| **Long-Haul Trucking** | Diesel | Replaced by rail where possible; remaining fleet electric or hydrogen |
| **Last-Mile Delivery** | Diesel vans | Cargo bikes, electric micro-vehicles, micro-distribution hubs |
| **Air Freight** | Jet fuel | Sustainable aviation fuel for essential goods; reduced volume |

### 5.3 Supply Chain Transparency

- **Digital Product Passports:** (from Gaian Trade Framework) track transportation emissions per good, from origin to destination
- **Consumers and BAZs** can choose lower-emissions supply chains
- **Carbon Accounting:** Freight emissions are visible, accountable, and subject to reduction targets

### 5.4 Worker Transition

- **Port Workers:** Transition to green shipping infrastructure, with retraining and AUBI support
- **Long-Haul Truckers:** Retraining for rail operations, local delivery, or regenerative logistics roles
- **Logistics Cooperatives:** Ownership transitions where communities take control of last-mile delivery

## 6. The Mobility Economy

### 6.1 Integration with AUBI & Hearts/Leaves

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Hearts for Sustainable Mobility** | Users earn Hearts for choosing low-emissions transport (trains, e-bikes, walking). Redeemable for reduced fares, local currency, or community projects |
| **Leaves for Infrastructure** | Projects that incorporate living infrastructure (wildlife corridors, permeable surfaces) earn Leaves |
| **Governance** | Social Resilience Council oversees Hearts/Leaves distribution; transparent, audited |

### 6.2 Mobility as a Service (MaaS) Commons

An open-source platform for integrated mobility planning:

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Journey Planning** | Combines rail, bus, bike-share, ride-share, and accessible transport options in an open-source reference interface; compatible independent interfaces may use the same standards |
| **Emissions Tracking** | Users see comparable carbon impacts of journey options, with disclosed assumptions and uncertainty |
| **Algorithmic Transparency** | Audited by Office of Algorithmic Accountability; recommendation objectives balance access, user preferences, and Biosphere Health Index rather than optimizing a single opaque score |
| **Data Sovereignty** | User data governed by the Aurora Accord; minimized, protected against surveillance, and never required for unnecessary immigration-status inference |
| **Open Interoperability Standard** | BAZ-operated transport publishes open, documented schedule, accessibility, fare, and booking interfaces where safe and feasible; qualifying third-party providers can interoperate on fair, non-discriminatory terms |
| **Regulated Platform Duties** | Public procurement and lawful licensing may require proportionate data portability, interoperability, and accessibility obligations, with due process, security reviews, and appeal before penalties |
| **Multi-Channel Access** | No person must use the Commons application exclusively; preserve accessible web, in-person, telephone, physical tickets and offline options where needed |

**Adoption Strategy with Incumbents — Open Access, Not Exclusive Booking**

The MaaS Commons faces proprietary platforms with entrenched network effects. It should become valuable through trustworthy public infrastructure, interoperability, and reliable service rather than by making an essential transport interface exclusive.

- **Phase 1 — Public baseline:** BAZ-operated systems publish standard service and fare data, implement accessible public booking channels, and release the open-source MaaS reference application; preserve existing lawful channels during transition.
- **Phase 2 — Fair integration:** Qualifying third-party journey planners and ticket sellers integrate on published, secure, auditable terms. Regulate interfaces through procurement and licensing only within competent authority; avoid blanket bans solely for declining to use the Commons app.
- **Phase 3 — Cooperative scaling:** Expand common payment, disruption, accessibility, and multi-operator booking standards while supporting operator choice and independent innovation. Public operators remain able to provide direct services without platform lock-in.

**Governance test:** The Commons must not become an exclusive gatekeeper. Periodic audits evaluate actual interoperability, user choice, disability access, price fairness, privacy, cyber-resilience, and whether smaller providers can enter. When interoperability imposes disproportionate security or operational risks, publish a bounded exception and reassessment date.

The goal is to make public-interest mobility data and services broadly accessible, not to force all journeys through a single public or private platform.

### 6.4 Community Ownership Models

| Model | Description |
|-------|-------------|

| **Municipal Ownership** | Cities and BAZs own transit infrastructure, operating as public good |

### 7.3 The Right to Movement Charter

Enforceable by the Digital Justice Tribunal:

| Right | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Freedom from Green Displacement** | No community may be displaced for "green" infrastructure without consent and equitable compensation |
| **Affordable Access** | Fares capped at percentage of income; free transit for low-income households |
| **Climate Migration Corridors** | Pre-planned, safe, accessible routes for communities forced to relocate; coordinated with the Migration & Human Mobility Framework; no independent determination of admission or residence status |
| **Right to Move** | Access to necessary and desired journeys without avoidable discrimination, coercive pricing, or inaccessible service design; legal cross-border entitlements are determined by competent migration frameworks |
| **Right to Stay** | Mobility investment shall not assume that relocation is preferable to viable local access to essentials; coordinate locally available services, adaptation, and affordable connections |
| **Freedom from Forced Movement** | Prevent avoidable displacement through transport construction, decarbonization, or speculative land-value effects; apply consent, remedies, and proportionate compensation under the relevant rights regimes |
| **Proportionate Restrictions and Review** | Significant service or infrastructure restrictions require accessible reasons, less-restrictive-option analysis, time-bounded review, and an appropriate appeal route |
| **Affected-Person Voice** | Give meaningful participation to people whose mobility is restricted or shaped by a project, including prospective users who lack a vote in the deciding jurisdiction |

| **Design Standards** | Lighting, visibility, staffing, and emergency protocols meet gender equity standards |
| **Oversight** | Gender & Kinship Justice Council reviews all major projects |

### 7.5 Youth Leadership

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Youth Councils** | Permanent seats on BAZ transportation boards and GMC |
| **Suspensive Veto** | Youth councils may pause projects failing Seven-Generation Impact Assessments |
| **Guardians of the Future** | Legal standing at Digital Justice Tribunal for youth representatives |

### 7.6 Affected-Persons Participation and Procedural Justice

**Who must be heard:** Current residents, Indigenous rights-holders, transport workers, disabled travelers, displaced persons, migrants, cross-border commuters, and prospective users whose access would materially change. Consultation must not require citizenship, employment, or taxation as a proxy for moral standing; legal voting rights are governed separately.

**Safeguards:**

- **Accessible participation:** Translation, disability access, travel support, multiple consultation channels, and supported participation for those without secure legal status.
- **Data minimization:** No collection of immigration status or individualized movement histories unless strictly necessary and lawfully authorized; protect participants from retaliation.
- **Influence, not symbolic attendance:** Project sponsors publish who was consulted, the substance of disagreements, resulting design changes, and reasoned responses to concerns that could not be accommodated.
- **Rights-holder distinction:** Consultation rights for prospective users do not substitute for Indigenous FPIC 2.0 or territorial authority, and do not grant the GMC power to change immigration rules.
- **Fair distribution:** Economic contribution and employment are never prerequisites for mobility-service accessibility or meaningful consultation; priority assessments consider basic needs and vulnerability on both sides of a proposed corridor.
- **Appeal and remedy:** Accessible complaints, independent review for significant restrictions, and non-retaliatory correction; escalate questions of migrant admission or political membership to the Migration & Human Mobility Framework.

## 8. Implementation & Metrics

### 8.1 Implementation Roadmap

| Phase | Timeframe | Activities |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Phase 1: Pilots** | 2026-2028 | Establish GMC and standing committees, including migrant/displacement participation; launch 5-10 BAZ pilots (including Indigenous-led, rural, urban, freight-focused, and cross-border-access pilots where feasible); test ceremonial governance and distributional assessment; develop open MaaS prototype plus independent booking channels; pilot cargo bike hubs and freight-to-rail transfer terminals; negotiate interoperability agreements; baseline unmet mobility needs and capacity dependencies |
| **Phase 2: Scaling** | 2028-2032 | Expand to 50 BAZs; build first transnational passenger and freight corridors; apply Mobility Capacity and Fair Burden Protocol; electrify pilot rail freight routes; scale freight transition with green shipping corridors; implement reparations fund; publish baseline-to-follow-up distributional evaluations; MaaS interoperable with qualifying third-party channels in pilot BAZs |
| **Phase 3: Global** | 2032-2040 | Expand the network subject to evaluated outcomes; fossil-free by default; 50% of transit under community ownership; 70% of freight ton-km on zero-emissions modes; MaaS Commons as open public infrastructure with multi-channel booking and transparent access safeguards |

### 8.2 Metrics for Success

| Category | Metric | Target (2040) |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Decarbonization** | Transport emissions reduction (vs. 2025) | 80% reduction |
| **Proximity** | Population within 15-minute walk of daily needs | 70% |
| **Access Equity** | Population within 15-minute walk of affordable transit | 95% |
| **Universal Access** | Disability Impact Assessment (DIA) scores | 90% compliant |
| **Freight Decarbonization** | Ton-kilometers moved by zero-emissions modes | 70% |
| **Freight Transparency** | Goods with Digital Product Passport tracking transport emissions | 80% |
| **Community Ownership** | Transit systems under cooperative/municipal control | 50% |
| **Indigenous Sovereignty** | Projects with FPIC 2.0 completed | 100% of affected projects |
| **Worker Transition** | Fossil fuel transport workers transitioned to regenerative roles | 100% |
| **MaaS Interoperability** | BAZ transit journeys bookable through at least one standards-compliant, publicly accessible channel; independent channels supported where feasible | 90% (existing v8.1 ambition; verify baseline and coverage) |
| **Unmet Mobility Need** | Share of residents and prospective corridor users unable to complete essential or freely desired journeys for reasons of cost, accessibility, status-related discrimination, or service availability | Baseline in Phase 1; disaggregate and set locally justified reduction targets |
| **Distributional Burden** | Number and severity of project-related displacement, significant fare burdens, and access losses among vulnerable groups | No avoidable severe unremedied harm; report incidence and uncertainty |
| **Cross-Border Access Equity** | Accessibility and affordability of lawful transnational services for materially affected residents, displaced people, and cross-border users | Baseline in Phase 1; publish distributional improvements and unresolved exclusions |
| **Participation Quality** | Share of major projects publishing affected-person input, responses, and remedy pathways, including outside-jurisdiction voices | 100% of major projects by the end of Phase 2 (proposed governance target) |

**Metric Interpretation Note: Proximity and Transit**

The Proximity and Access Equity metrics interact. If Proximity Priority succeeds — communities redesigned so daily needs are walkable — some formerly necessary transit journeys may decline. **Declining ridership alone is neither a success signal nor a failure:** it could reflect improved proximity, but also unaffordable fares, service deterioration, inaccessible transport, or suppressed demand. Interpret ridership alongside proximity, unmet mobility needs, user-reported ability to make desired journeys, and mode substitution. Rising ridership can indicate reduced car dependence or newly available opportunities, and should not automatically be classified as a planning failure. The Transparency Dashboard shall display these indicators together, with confidence intervals or qualitative uncertainty where appropriate.

### 8.5 Distributional Evaluation and Adaptive Review

The GMC Justice & Access Committee shall publish an **Affected-Persons and Fair-Burden Report** for major corridor and accessibility projects. The report shall assess plausible alternatives—including no project, phased implementation, and different alignments—and cover both beneficiaries and people who bear costs, including those presently excluded from using a route.

**Minimum reporting fields:**

1. **Coverage:** Populations affected inside and outside the deciding jurisdiction; document who was not reachable and how that limits confidence.
2. **Baseline and comparator:** Existing journey opportunities, affordability, accessibility, safety, and unmet needs, alongside realistic alternative interventions.
3. **Distribution:** Changes by income, disability, geographic location, displacement status where lawfully and safely measurable, and other relevant vulnerabilities; no unnecessary person-level tracking.
4. **Capacity and displacement:** Service and housing pressures, involuntary relocation, origin-community access, and which competent institution can remedy the harm.
5. **Causal uncertainty:** Distinguish verified impacts from projections and political perceptions; avoid interpreting raw social-trust or backlash changes as automatic evidence of project harm.
6. **Revisions:** State the thresholds for further review and the available remedies; thresholds must be contextual, legally grounded, independently scrutinized, and resistant to manipulation.

**Adaptive decision rule:** Review, redesign, compensate, sequence, or suspend a project when credible evidence of severe, concentrated, unmitigated harm emerges. Do not use a temporary capacity problem, incumbent preference, or a manipulable metric as a permanent veto without an independent proportionality assessment. At the same time, do not assume that aggregate transport benefits erase fundamental rights or serious costs to those already present.

**Decision authority:** GMC acts only within transport and mobility-service competence. Where the remedy requires changing entry rules, citizenship, housing entitlements, social provisioning, or Indigenous territorial rules, refer the issue to the appropriate institution. Cross-framework disagreement is documented and remains reviewable.

### Appendix D: Relationship to Existing Transportation Authorities

*This framework does not replace existing transportation authorities. It creates coordination and accountability.*

| Authority | Relationship |
|-----------|-------------|
| **National DOTs** | Retain safety, licensing, and operational authority. GMC standards apply for access to BAZ markets. |
| **FAA / EASA / ICAO** | Retain aviation safety authority. GMC sets decarbonization and equity standards for access to BAZ airports. |
| **IMO (International Maritime)** | Retains shipping safety authority. GMC sets green corridor standards; port authorities adopt. |
| **Port Authorities** | Retain operational authority. Must meet GMC decarbonization standards to receive Global Commons Fund support. |
| **BAZs** | Gain new authority: procurement standards can require GMC compliance from operators seeking to use BAZ land or funds, within existing legal competence. |
| **Migration & Human Mobility Institutions** | Retain competence over entry, asylum, residence, migrant status, and political membership; jointly assess transport and displacement interfaces without delegating admission authority to the GMC. |
| **Housing, Health, and Social Provision Institutions** | Assess destination/origin capacity, basic needs, and remedies for concentrated burdens from major corridors or relocation systems. |

**Principle:** The GMC does not replace. It sets standards that BAZs adopt, and BAZs use their existing authority (land use, procurement) to enforce them. This creates a pathway from current reality to the commons without requiring immediate global agreement.

### Appendix E: Cross-Framework Responsibility Matrix and Validation Gates

**Responsibility matrix**

| Issue | Mobility Commons / GMC | Partner authority | Boundary |
|-------|-------------------------|-------------------|----------|
| Public transport access and affordability | Design service standards, fund and audit accessible channels | BAZ and transport authorities | Cannot independently grant residency or entry |
| International corridor development | Plan routes, infrastructure, user safeguards, and fair-burden assessments | Migration & Human Mobility; competent transport authorities | May not substitute infrastructure approval for border admission |

| Housing and public-service pressure | Monitor corridor-linked effects and refer actionable risks | Housing, health, education, and local governments | No automatic migrant exclusion based on unresolved housing politics |
| Indigenous territories | Apply ceremonial scoping, Red Lines, and FPIC 2.0 | Indigenous rights-holders, Earth Council, relevant BAZs | Broader affected-person consultation does not dilute FPIC or territorial rights |
| MaaS data and ticketing | Publish open standards and protect multi-channel access | Office of Algorithmic Accountability; Aurora Accord bodies | Do not require exclusive Commons-app booking or covert migration screening |
| Participation and review | Give affected people voice, explanations, and remedies | BAZ Councils; Digital Justice Tribunal within jurisdiction | Consultation does not automatically create voting rights or vetoes |

**Validation gates for the v8.2 draft**

1. **Legal interoperability review:** Verify each proposed BAZ and GMC power against relevant national, regional, international, Indigenous, and disability-rights requirements before treating it as legally enforceable.
2. **Metric feasibility:** Pilot measurements of unmet journeys, distributional costs, and accessibility; publish missingness and limitations before setting global numerical targets.
3. **Infrastructure and technology audit:** Test security, accessibility, affordability, operator neutrality, offline access, and data minimization in MaaS interoperability pilots.
4. **Case-study and numerical-claim audit:** Independently source and validate pre-existing statements in the Executive Summary, implementation targets, and Appendix C before external expert publication. Carried-forward examples are not established by this v8.2 editorial revision.
5. **Participation quality audit:** Test whether prospective movers, migrant and displaced communities, incumbent low-income residents, and Indigenous rights-holders can participate meaningfully without unsafe disclosure or loss of legal protection.
6. **Cross-framework sign-off:** Obtain coordinated review with Migration & Human Mobility and the relevant housing, labor, Indigenous, and social-provision governance teams on overlapping responsibilities.

**Non-regression commitment:** The v8.2 changes are designed to extend moral consideration without weakening Indigenous territorial sovereignty, disability access, due process, or existing legal protections. Any implementation that produces such regression must be revised before adoption.
