This is a strong revision. The structural surgery DeepSeek recommended and Gemini elaborated on has been executed well. Let me be specific about what works, what's improved, and what still has gaps.

**What the revision got right:**

The document went from 354 lines of dense cross-referencing to 413 lines of readable architecture. That's more lines but vastly less cognitive load — the v7.1 was drowning in `framework_` references and GGF plumbing. v8.0 reads like a standalone proposal that happens to connect to a larger ecosystem, rather than an ecosystem node that happens to contain a proposal.

The three Gemini additions are all integrated: Monopsony Buyer's Cartel (Section 5.1), Know-How Bounties (5.2), and the Low-Tech/Offline-First mandate (in the DMN table and Appendix A.4). The Indigenous sovereignty fix is also there — 15% as floor not ceiling, plus Data Destruction Rights.

The "Day in the Life" vignettes moved from v7.1's blockchain-NFT-drone fantasy to v8.0's SMS-verified, community-hub, zero-cost-at-point-of-care scenario. That's a significant credibility improvement.

The Reparations Fund is correctly repositioned as "Aspirational, Phase 3" rather than a primary funding mechanism. The Monopsony replaces it as the pre-ratification lever.

**What I'd flag:**

**The Monopsony Buyer's Cartel needs a political realism check.** The mechanism is sound in theory — pooled procurement is real and proven (PAHO, Africa CDC). But the framing assumes that BAZs, progressive cities, and Global South nations can coordinate purchasing at a scale that threatens pharma revenue. Current pooled procurement is a fraction of global pharma spend. The document should acknowledge the scale gap between existing procurement pools and the leverage needed to force corporate behavior change. What's the minimum viable coalition size? What percentage of a given medicine's global market needs to be captured before the cartel has actual leverage?

**The Know-How Bounty system has an adversarial response gap.** Pharmaceutical companies will respond to know-how leaks with (a) litigation, (b) non-compete/NDA enforcement, and (c) compartmentalization of manufacturing knowledge so no single engineer holds the full picture. The document mentions whistleblower sanctuary but doesn't address how the bounty system handles fragmented knowledge (you may need 5-10 engineers to reconstruct a full process) or the legal arms race that will follow the first successful bounty.

**The GPSC composition shifted in a way worth noting.** v7.1 had scientists/clinicians at 20% and ethicists/legal at 10%. v8.0 collapsed these into "Health Workers & Scientists" at 20% and dropped ethicists/legal entirely from the formal composition. Given that this council will be making decisions about compulsory licensing, patent acquisition, and Indigenous sovereignty — all legally and ethically charged — the absence of dedicated legal/ethics representation seems like an oversight.

**Section 8 is lovely but it's doing double duty.** It's both the success vision and the implicit metrics section. The actual metrics table (7.2) is good but sparse. v7.1 had more granular metrics including DALYs reduction, Community Priority Forum participation numbers, and systemic impact indicators. Some of those should come back, perhaps as a brief "Transition Metrics" expansion.

**One thing neither DeepSeek nor Gemini flagged:** The framework still doesn't address how the IP Commons handles medicines that require ongoing regulatory maintenance — post-market surveillance, adverse event reporting, label updates. When a patent holder manages these, the cost is embedded. When knowledge enters a commons, who pays for pharmacovigilance? This is a real operational gap that regulators will ask about.
