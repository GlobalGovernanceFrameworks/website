## 1. Introduction: Healing as a Shared Inheritance

### The Problem

The global pharmaceutical system prioritizes profit over people. Patent monopolies keep medicines unaffordable for billions. Research follows market returns, not health needs — neglecting tropical diseases, pediatric formulations, and pandemic preparedness. Indigenous knowledge is extracted without consent or compensation.

The COVID-19 crisis exposed this as "vaccine apartheid." Wealth dictated access. Millions died. Trust in global health systems fractured.

This is not inevitable. It is a choice. A choice embedded in patent law, trade agreements, and corporate business models. And it can be unchosen.

### The Vision

A world where:

- Medicines are priced at the cost of production, not monopoly rents
- Research follows need, not profit
- Indigenous knowledge is sovereign, its use subject to community consent
- Communities manufacture their own medicines
- No one is denied treatment because they cannot pay

This is not a dream. It has precedent. Generic HIV antiretrovirals saved millions, created by breaking patents and enabling competition. The polio vaccine was never patented; its open model eradicated a disease. Open-source platforms like Linux show that commons-based collaboration can outperform proprietary systems.

### The Theory of Change

**If** we build a commons-based infrastructure for medicine discovery, production, and distribution, **then** prices drop and access increases globally, **because** community ownership and open knowledge disrupt profit-driven monopolies.

**Evidence:**

- HIV generic competition dropped prices from $10,000 to under $100 per patient per year
- Open polio vaccine model enabled global eradication
- Open-source software demonstrates that commons can innovate at scale

### The Myth of Prometheus

In Greek myth, Prometheus stole fire from the gods and gave it to humanity. For this, he was punished eternally. The myth encodes a lie: that knowledge and healing belong to the powerful, that sharing them is transgression.

We reject this lie. Healing is humanity's sacred inheritance. This framework reclaims it.

## 3. Three Core Mechanisms

### 3.1 The Pharmaceutical IP Commons

**What It Is**

A stewardship trust that holds patents, data, and manufacturing know-how for essential medicines, making them available for generic production at production cost.

**How It Works**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Patent Acquisition** | Patents are acquired through buyouts, donations, compulsory licensing, or expiration |
| **Open Licensing** | Commons-licensed medicines can be produced by any qualified manufacturer, with royalties capped at 5% (reinvested in the Commons) |
| **Know-How Integration** | Manufacturing processes, trade secrets, and cell lines are documented and shared alongside patents |
| **Pharmacovigilance Stewardship** | The Commons assumes ongoing regulatory obligations for medicines it stewards: post-market surveillance, adverse event reporting, safety signal detection, and label updates. Funded from the 5% royalty pool. Coordinated by the GPSC's Safety & Efficacy Committee, with regional nodes aligned to existing national pharmacovigilance centers |
| **Commons Growth Clause** | Improvements to Commons medicines must be shared back; no proprietary forks |
| **Viral Open Access** | In pandemics, patents for relevant medicines automatically enter the Commons |

**What It Replaces**

The current system where patents create monopolies for 20+ years, allowing prices far above production cost.

**What It Creates**

A global pool of openly available medicine knowledge, enabling competition and driving prices to cost.

---

### 3.2 The Distributed Manufacturing Network

**What It Is**

A global network of community-owned, cooperative, and public manufacturing facilities producing essential medicines under Commons licenses.

**How It Works**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Hubs** | Manufacturing facilities located in BAZs, with 70% in Global South countries by Year 5 |
| **Ownership** | Community-owned cooperatives, public health systems, or worker collectives |
| **Technology Transfer** | Open-source manufacturing protocols, with technical assistance from established hubs |
| **Quality Assurance** | Unified global standards (WHO Prequalification, ICH Good Manufacturing Practice) enforced through independent audits |
| **Capacity** | Surge capacity for pandemics; 5% of capacity can pivot to new medicines within 30 days |
| **Low-Tech First** | Systems work without internet; documentation available as printed manuals; verification via SMS (M-Pesa model) |

**What It Replaces**

Centralized manufacturing controlled by a few corporations, with supply chains vulnerable to disruption.

**What It Creates**

Resilient, distributed production that serves communities directly and can rapidly respond to crises.

---

### 3.3 Indigenous Sovereignty & Biopiracy Prevention

**What It Is**

A binding framework ensuring Indigenous communities control their traditional knowledge and benefit from its use — or withhold it entirely.

**Core Principles**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Binding Veto** | Indigenous councils have absolute veto over any use of their traditional knowledge. No use without consent. |
| **Data Destruction Rights** | If consent is violated or withdrawn, Indigenous councils may demand that all algorithmic training data derived from their knowledge be purged from Commons systems. |
| **Revenue Sharing as Floor** | If communities choose to license knowledge, the minimum is 15% of net revenue. Higher rates may be negotiated. If no agreement, the veto applies. |
| **No Forced Participation** | Communities may choose not to participate. Their knowledge is not required for the Commons to function. |
| **Enforcement** | The Healing Justice Tribunal can issue injunctions, data destruction orders, and damages for violations. |

**What It Replaces**

The current system where Indigenous knowledge is extracted without consent (biopiracy) and used for profit with no benefit to communities.

**What It Creates**

True sovereignty: communities decide whether, how, and on what terms their knowledge is used.

## 4. Governance: The Global Pharmaceutical Stewardship Council

**Role:** Oversees the IP Commons, Manufacturing Network, and Indigenous Sovereignty provisions.

**Composition:**

| Category | Percentage | Selection |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| **Community & Patient Representatives** | 35% | Elected by patient organizations, community health networks, and disability advocacy groups |
| **Indigenous Governing Bodies** | 20% | Appointed by the Earth Council; permanent co-chair with veto over matters affecting traditional knowledge |
| **Health Workers & Scientists** | 20% | Elected by participants in the Discovery Commons and clinical trial networks |
| **Global South Governments** | 15% | Appointed by regional health bodies (Africa CDC, PAHO, ASEAN Health Cluster, etc.) |
| **Legal, Ethics & Pharmacovigilance Experts** | 10% | Nominated by international health law bodies and bioethics institutions; includes pharmaceutical regulatory expertise |

**Standing Committees:**

- **Safety & Efficacy Committee:** Manages pharmacovigilance stewardship for Commons medicines — post-market surveillance, adverse event reporting, safety signal detection, label updates. Coordinates with national regulatory authorities and WHO. Funded from the 5% royalty pool.
- **Indigenous Sovereignty Committee:** Co-chaired by Indigenous co-chair. Reviews all matters involving traditional knowledge. Administers Data Destruction Rights.
- **IP Transition Committee:** Manages patent acquisition, licensing, and the Know-How Bounty verification process.

**Key Functions:**

- Sets the Priority Medicines Research Agenda based on community input and global health needs (DALYs, neglected populations)
- Manages the Pharmaceutical IP Commons, including patent acquisition and licensing
- Oversees the Distributed Manufacturing Network, including hub certification and quality standards
- Monitors Indigenous sovereignty compliance
- Ensures ongoing pharmacovigilance for all Commons medicines through the Safety & Efficacy Committee
- Declares health emergencies triggering compulsory licensing or viral open access
- Reports annually to the Planetary Health Council

**Accountability:**

- Annual public report on Planetary Dashboard
- Decennial review via Meta-Governance Framework
- Recall possible by two-thirds vote of nominating bodies

## 5. Pre-Ratification Strategy: Building Leverage

The Prometheus Protocol does not assume the Treaty for Our Only Home is fully ratified or that the Digital Justice Tribunal has binding authority. This section provides mechanisms for building leverage before those institutions exist.

### 5.1 The Monopsony Buyer's Cartel

**The Insight:** You don't need to tax corporations. You need to control the market.

**The Mechanism:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Coalition Formation** | Pathfinder coalition of BAZs, progressive cities, Global South nations, and aligned health systems |
| **Procurement Pooling** | Coalition members pool their pharmaceutical procurement budgets |
| **Market Power** | The combined coalition becomes the largest single buyer of medicine on Earth |
| **Terms of Purchase** | Coalition commits to purchase exclusively from entities that: (a) open-source patents after 5 years, (b) license manufacturing to the Distributed Manufacturing Network, or (c) are the Network itself |
| **Consequence** | Corporations refusing to transition lose access to a multi-billion dollar combined market |

**Why This Works**

- Legal: Coalition members choose where to spend their money; no taxation required
- Achievable: Builds on existing procurement collaborations (PAHO's Strategic Fund, Africa CDC's pooled procurement)
- Creates leverage: Losing access to a major market is a powerful incentive to negotiate

**Minimum Viable Coalition**

The Monopsony only works at sufficient scale. Most global pharmaceutical revenue comes from high-income markets (US, EU, Japan). A coalition of Global South nations and progressive cities alone is unlikely to control enough market share to force behavior change on blockbuster drugs. The strategy therefore requires a sequenced approach:

- **Phase 0 target:** Achieve leverage on specific medicine categories where Global South procurement is a significant share of the global market (vaccines, antiretrovirals, antimalarials, essential antibiotics). These are the categories where pooled procurement already has precedent and where the coalition's market share is large enough to matter.
- **Phase 1 escalation:** Expand to medicines where coalition market share exceeds 20% of global volume. Below this threshold, corporations can absorb the loss; above it, the economics shift.
- **Long-term:** As the Distributed Manufacturing Network matures, the coalition becomes less dependent on corporate supply entirely, shifting from market leverage to market replacement.

The honest assessment: the Monopsony will not force Pfizer to open-source its entire portfolio in Phase 0. It will create leverage on specific categories where the coalition's purchasing power is concentrated, and expand from there.

### 5.2 Know-How Bounties

**The Problem:** Even with open patents, community hubs cannot manufacture complex biologics, mRNA, or sterile injectables without undocumented "know-how" (temperatures, pressures, cell lines, purification steps).

**The Mechanism:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Bounties** | The Global Commons Fund offers financial rewards to retired or defecting pharmaceutical engineers, chemists, and manufacturing specialists who comprehensively document and open-source manufacturing processes for essential medicines |
| **Tiers** | $50,000–$500,000 based on complexity and impact; plus lifetime AUBI top-up for high-impact contributions |
| **Protection** | Whistleblower sanctuary and Planetary Guardian Visa for those facing retaliation |
| **Verification** | Distributed Manufacturing Network validates submissions before payment |
| **Composite Bounties** | For complex biologics where no single engineer holds full process knowledge, bounties can be split across multiple contributors whose submissions collectively reconstruct a complete manufacturing protocol. Verification includes successful test production. |

**Anticipated Corporate Countermeasures**

Pharmaceutical companies will respond to Know-How Bounties with predictable defensive strategies. The system must be designed for these:

- **NDA/non-compete litigation:** Bounty recipients will face lawsuits. The Whistleblower Sanctuary must include pre-committed legal defense funds and jurisdiction selection (filing bounties from countries with weak NDA enforcement or strong whistleblower protections). The Migration Framework's Planetary Guardian Visa provides relocation for those at personal risk.
- **Knowledge compartmentalization:** Companies will restructure manufacturing teams so no individual holds enough knowledge to reconstruct a full process. The Composite Bounty mechanism addresses this directly, but verification becomes harder. The GPSC should maintain a "process gaps" registry identifying which steps are still missing for priority medicines.
- **Disinformation submissions:** Bad actors may submit incomplete or incorrect processes. All bounties require successful test production at a Distributed Manufacturing Network hub before payment. Failed submissions that appear deliberately misleading are flagged and the submitter is excluded from future bounties.
- **Preemptive retention:** Companies will offer retention bonuses to key engineers. This is acceptable — it raises the cost of hoarding knowledge, which is itself a form of leverage. The bounty system does not need to capture all engineers, only enough to reconstruct priority processes.

### 5.3 Whistleblower Sanctuary

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Protect individuals who expose price-gouging, safety violations, or efforts to block access |
| **Mechanism** | Asylum pathways via Migration Framework; Hearts bonuses for substantiated disclosures |
| **Protection** | Legal defense, relocation support, and Planetary Guardian Visa for those at risk |

### 5.4 Pathfinder Compact

|---------|-------------|
| **Commitment** | Pathfinder members commit to implementing Commons principles within their jurisdictions |
| **Benefits** | Preferential access to Global Commons Fund support; technical assistance; leadership recognition |
| **Coordination** | Pooled procurement, shared manufacturing hubs, coordinated patent challenges |

## 8. What Success Looks Like

*Metrics tell us whether we are meeting targets. But success is also felt. This is what the Prometheus Protocol envisions:*

**A mother in rural Malawi** receives a vaccine for her child. It was produced in a community-owned hub in a neighboring country. Its authenticity is verified by a text message (SMS) on her simple phone. It was delivered by a community health worker who earned Hearts for her work. The cost is zero at point of care.

**A researcher in Nairobi** contributes to the crowdsourced formulary. Her work on a neglected tropical disease tops the leaderboard. She receives Impact Credits recognized across the GGF. Her career is not defined by the profit potential of her work but by its healing impact.

**An Indigenous council in the Amazon** receives a request from a pharmaceutical company to study a traditional plant. The council reviews the proposal, considers the potential benefits and risks. They say no. The request stops. No tribunal, no appeal, no compensation offered to change their mind. Their sovereignty is absolute.

**A retired pharmaceutical engineer in Switzerland** uploads 30 years of manufacturing know-how to the Discovery Commons. She includes the precise temperatures, pressures, and cell lines that made the production possible. She receives a $200,000 bounty, lifetime AUBI top-up, and the knowledge that her work will enable generics for millions.

**A child with Type 1 diabetes** receives insulin produced by a cooperative three blocks from her home. The price is the cost of production. Her family is not impoverished by her survival.

**The question is no longer "can we afford this medicine?"** It is "what medicine do we need to develop next?" Research follows need. Manufacturing follows community. Healing follows the ancient principle that knowledge belongs to all.

### Appendix A: Technical Specifications (Summaries)

*Detailed technical specifications are available in separate appendices. Summaries provided here.*

**A.1: Patent Acquisition Mechanisms**

- **Buyouts:** Global Commons Fund purchases key patents from willing sellers
- **Compulsory Licensing:** Under WTO Doha Declaration flexibilities, for health emergencies
- **Donations:** Regenerative Enterprises contribute patents in exchange for certification
- **Expiration:** Natural patent expiration; Commons stewards expired patents

**A.2: Manufacturing Quality Standards**

- WHO Prequalification (PQ/VP/2019)
- ICH Good Manufacturing Practice guidelines (Q7, Q9, Q10)
- Independent audits with public results
- Low-tech: paper-based audit trails with cryptographic verification

**A.3: Technology Transfer Protocols**

- Open-source blueprints for manufacturing equipment
- Augmented reality training modules
- Personnel exchanges between established and new hubs
- Print-on-demand documentation for offline use

**A.4: Low-Tech / Offline-First Design**

- SMS/USSD verification (M-Pesa model) for Rx coupons
- Paper-based audit trails with printed QR codes
- Downloadable PDF manuals for offline use
- All systems stress-tested in low-connectivity, low-electricity conditions

### Appendix C: Legal Framework Summary

**C.1: TRIPS Compliance**

The Prometheus Protocol operates within existing international law:

- WTO Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health (2001) affirms that public health trumps patent rights
- Compulsory licensing is legal under TRIPS Article 31
- The Protocol expands existing flexibilities without requiring new treaties

**C.2: Model Legislation**

Pathfinder members may adopt model legislation that:

- Establishes compulsory licensing for medicines priced above production cost
- Creates legal pathways for generic competition
- Protects whistleblowers and Know-How contributors
- Recognizes Indigenous data destruction rights

**C.3: Enforcement Pathway**

- Phase 0-1: Coalition procurement power (market incentives)
- Phase 2: BAZ and national legislation within Pathfinder jurisdictions
- Phase 3: Treaty-based enforcement for all members
