## 1. The Starting Point: 2026 Reality

This protocol does not begin from a world that exists only in theory. It begins from the world as it is.

---

### Current Landscape Assessment

| Factor | 2026 Reality |
|--------|--------------|
| **Compute Concentration** | Frontier AGI compute is concentrated in ~5 corporate entities (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, xAI, Meta) and ~2 state actors (US, China). Taiwan produces 90% of advanced chips. |
| **Data Extraction** | Models train on the entire public internet without consent, compensation, or accountability. Data provenance is nonexistent. |
| **Regulatory Gap** | No binding international framework. The EU AI Act exists but is untested. US and China are in an arms race, not a cooperative framework. |
| **Governance Infrastructure** | No functioning BAZs in most regions. No Treaty ratification. The Global Governance Framework ecosystem exists on paper but not in practice. |
| **Civil Society Capacity** | Growing awareness but limited coordination. No existing CINs. No data unions. No shadow commons. |
| **Technical Uncertainty** | Capability trajectories are unpredictable. Safety research is outpaced by deployment. Detection of emergent capabilities is unreliable. |

**The Strategic Implication:** We cannot govern from above. There is no global authority that can enforce compliance. The path must begin from the side — building alternative structures that create pull, not push.

---

### Guiding Transition Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Start Where You Are** | Begin with what exists: sympathetic governments, progressive corporations, civil society organizations, aligned BAZ pilots |
| **Build Leverage, Not Just Institutions** | Identify choke points (chips, energy, talent) and use them to create accountability |
| **Create Pull, Not Push** | Make participation in the commons more attractive than staying outside it |
| **Iterate, Don't Finalize** | Governance of exponential technology must be agile. Locking a document in 2026 is hubris |
| **Protect the Vulnerable** | Transition cannot be at the expense of those already marginalized. The Global South must not be last |

## 2. Core Operational Definitions

To be enforceable, concepts must be measurable. This section defines the key terms used throughout the protocol.

---

### 2.1 Compute

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Compute** | Measured in FLOPs (floating point operations). For governance purposes, tracked at the threshold of 1e25 FLOPs per training run (frontier model threshold, updated annually). |
| **Compute Concentration** | Measured by Gini coefficient of total active compute capacity across entities. Estimated 2026 baseline: Gini ~0.92 (5 corporate entities and 2 state actors control >90% of frontier compute). Target: Gini `<` 0.6 by 2030. This target is aggressive — it requires roughly halving concentration in four years. If Phase 1 regulatory leverage proves insufficient (see Section 4.6 fallbacks), the target timeline extends but the direction is maintained. |
| **Strategic Compute** | Ultra-low latency infrastructure controlling critical systems (grids, hospitals, water). Highest oversight tier. |

### 2.2 Context

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Living Context** | Dynamic human systems — current cultural practices, active languages, ongoing social relations. Expires after 12 months unless re-validated. |
| **Static Context** | Archived knowledge — published literature, historical records, completed cultural artifacts. No expiration. |
| **Synthetic Context** | AI-generated data. Must carry provenance watermark. Subject to surcharge. |
| **Contextual Intelligence Node (CIN)** | A certified cooperative that curates, protects, and licenses context. Requires: minimum 50 members, governance charter, dispute resolution mechanism, annual attestation. **Small-Community Waiver:** Communities with fewer than 50 members holding unique linguistic, cultural, or ecological knowledge may apply for CIN certification with a minimum of 10 members, provided they demonstrate context that is not represented in any existing CIN and submit a simplified governance charter. Waiver applications reviewed by the relevant regional CIN Council. |

### 2.3 Harm

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Parasitic Behavior** | 1) Data extraction without compensation after license expiration. 2) Behavioral manipulation detectable via user surveys showing patterns of harm. 3) Sovereignty violation: simulating human identities without consent, impersonating CIN members, or representing itself as a specific human without authorization. |
| **Capability Threshold** | Defined annually by the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI. Thresholds include: autonomous replication; novel biological weapon design; strategic deception capability; self-improvement without oversight. |
| **Systemic Harm** | Damage affecting more than 1,000 individuals or causing irreversible ecological damage. Triggers Risk Bond forfeiture. |

### 2.4 Governance Entities

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Multi-Stakeholder Trust** | Governing body for the Global Trunk. Composition: 1/3 global governance delegates (GGF bodies), 1/3 CIN delegates (selected via sortition from certified nodes), 1/3 independent experts (ethicists, technologists, scientists). |
| **Contextual Intelligence Node (CIN) Council** | Regional coordinating body for CINs. Handles dispute resolution, certification, and resource allocation. |
| **Arbitration Panel** | Independent body for escalated disputes. Composed of: 2 Trust members, 2 CIN representatives, 1 neutral technical auditor, 1 Future Generations Advocate. |
| **Independent International Scientific Panel on AI** | Technical advisory body. Appointed by the Global Metrology Council. Produces annual capability assessment, threshold recommendations, and synthetic data quality standards. |

## 3. Phase 0: Shadow Commons (Now – 2027)

*Building the alternative before we need it. This phase does not require government cooperation. It builds what will eventually pull others in.*

---

### 3.1 Objectives

- Establish 5-10 pilot CINs in diverse contexts
- Build open-source Data Provenance Ledger prototype
- Launch first Compute Commons pilot
- Create whistleblower protection infrastructure
- Document what works and what fails

---

### 3.2 Pilot CINs

**Selection Criteria:**

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Diversity** | At least one from Global South, one from Indigenous territory, one from post-industrial region, one from urban center |
| **Readiness** | Existing organizing capacity, demonstrated ability to coordinate, clear governance charter |
| **Context Value** | Unique linguistic, cultural, or ecological knowledge not represented in existing datasets |

**Support Structure:**

| Element | Mechanism |
|---------|-----------|
| **Seed Funding** | $50,000 – $200,000 per pilot, from philanthropic sources and early-adopter grants |
| **Technical Assistance** | Open-source tooling for data curation, smart contract templates, governance guides |
| **Peer Network** | Monthly coordination calls, shared learning, mutual support |
| **Documentation** | Public case studies of successes and failures |

**Success Metrics:**
- 5 certified CINs operating
- 1,000+ members collectively
- First Reality Rent contracts signed (even symbolic)
- Documentation of challenges (legal, technical, organizational)

---

### 3.3 Data Provenance Ledger Prototype

**What It Is:** An open-source system for tracking the origin of data used in AI training.

**Core Features:**

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Cryptographic Signatures** | Each data point carries proof of origin (human, sensor, synthetic) |
| **Watermarking** | Synthetic data carries indelible markers that survive fine-tuning |
| **Public Verification** | Any user can verify a dataset's provenance |
| **CIN Integration** | CINs can register their data and set license terms |

**Implementation:**
- Build on existing open-source infrastructure (Hugging Face datasets, IPFS)
- Develop as modular components that can be adopted incrementally
- Low-tech alternative: paper registries with cryptographic hashes for communities without reliable internet

**Success Metrics:**
- Working prototype by Q4 2026
- Adopted by 3 pilot CINs
- Integrated with at least one open-source model release

---

### 3.4 Compute Commons Pilot

**What It Is:** A small-scale federated compute network operated by BAZ pilots and aligned institutions.

**Architecture:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Physical Nodes** | 5-10 compute nodes in BAZ territories, university labs, and aligned data centers |
| **Federated Coordination** | Open-source scheduler allocates jobs across nodes |
| **Energy Constraints** | Jobs prioritized based on renewable energy availability |
| **Access Rules** | Priority to projects serving public good (CIN-related, safety research, climate adaptation) |

**Funding:**
- Initial capital from philanthropic sources and aligned governments
- Operating costs covered by compute fees (below market rate to create pull)

**Success Metrics:**
- 5 nodes operational by end 2027
- 500+ compute hours allocated
- First CIN training runs completed

---

### 3.5 Whistleblower Infrastructure

**What It Is:** Secure channels for AI researchers to report safety concerns without retaliation.

**Components:**

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Secure Drop** | Encrypted submission channel, accessible via Tor |
| **Legal Support** | Pre-arranged legal defense for whistleblowers |
| **Journalist Partnerships** | Trusted media outlets for public disclosure when necessary |
| **Asylum Pathways** | Coordination with Planetary Guardian Visa framework for endangered whistleblowers |

**Implementation:**
- Operated by independent non-profit, not any single government or corporation
- Transparency report on submissions (aggregated, no identifying details)
- Anonymous submission option

## 4. Phase 1: Regulatory Leverage (2027 – 2028)

*Using existing power structures to create accountability. This phase works through sympathetic governments and international bodies.*

---

### 4.1 Objectives

- Enact compute concentration thresholds with enforcement mechanisms
- Mandate transparency reporting for frontier training runs
- Establish the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI
- Create first binding Reality Rent requirements in sympathetic jurisdictions

---

### 4.2 Compute Concentration Thresholds

**The Mechanism:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Gini Coefficient Target** | Compute distribution Gini `<` 0.6 by 2030. Annual reporting required. |
| **Enforcement** | Entities above threshold face: 1) Restricted access to public procurement; 2) Increased capital requirements; 3) Mandatory compute redistribution licensing |
| **Choke Point Leverage** | Chip exports conditional on compliance. Taiwan, Netherlands, and US coordinate |

**Path to Adoption:**
- First: Regional agreements (EU, G7)
- Second: Expand via trade agreements (access to RTZ markets conditional on compliance)
- Third: UN General Assembly resolution

**Success Metrics:**
- 3 major jurisdictions adopt compute concentration rules
- First redistribution licenses granted
- Measurable decrease in compute Gini among frontier entities

---

### 4.3 Transparency Mandates

**Required Reporting for Frontier Training Runs:**

| Data Element | Requirement |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Compute** | Total FLOPs, hardware type, energy consumption, carbon footprint |
| **Data** | Provenance summary: % living context, % static, % synthetic; CIN licenses held |
| **Capabilities** | Benchmark results, including prohibited capability thresholds |
| **Safety** | Red team results, alignment evaluations, incident reports |

**Enforcement:**
- Non-compliance triggers: fines, compute caps, procurement bans
- Public disclosure ensures reputational accountability

**Path to Adoption:**
- EU AI Act provides model (expand Article 64 requirements)
- US executive action for federal contractors
- Investor pressure (ESG frameworks incorporating AI transparency)

---

### 4.4 Reality Rent Framework

**The Mechanism (Phase 1):**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Voluntary CIN Licensing** | CINs set prices for their context. AI labs can choose to license or use synthetic/static data |
| **Default Rate** | If no agreement, a default rate (set by International Scientific Panel) applies for one negotiation period |
| **Synthetic Surcharge** | Models exceeding 40% synthetic data pay 2× default rate, incentivizing human-grounding |
| **Data Union Collective Bargaining** | CINs may form regional unions to negotiate collectively |

**Phase 1 Limitations:**
- Jurisdictional: applies only in adopting jurisdictions
- Voluntary for CINs: no mandate to participate
- Builds proof of concept before global scaling

**Success Metrics:**
- 10+ CINs with active licensing agreements
- First Reality Rent payments made
- Documentation of model quality improvements from human-grounding

---

### 4.5 Independent International Scientific Panel on AI

**Establishment:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Appointing Body** | Global Metrology Council, with input from UN bodies |
| **Composition** | 15 experts: 5 technical (AI safety, interpretability), 5 domain (ecology, economics, sociology), 5 ethics/philosophy |
| **Independence** | Divestment from AI-related holdings; no current employment by frontier labs |
| **Funding** | Global Commons Fund, shielded from corporate influence |

**Responsibilities:**
- Annual capability threshold update
- Synthetic data quality standards
- Reality Rent default rate calculation
- Risk Bond impact radius methodology

---

### 4.6 Fallback Strategies

Phase 1 leverage depends on assumptions — cooperative choke point governments, willing regulators, functional multilateral coordination — that may not hold. Explicit fallbacks:

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| **Chip export coordination fails** (Taiwan/Netherlands/US don't align) | Shift to demand-side leverage: procurement conditions in adopting jurisdictions; investor pressure via ESG frameworks; insurance industry standards requiring transparency for coverage |
| **No major jurisdiction adopts compute concentration rules** | Focus on making the Compute Commons competitive: below-market pricing, superior CIN-licensed training data, "Commons Compliant" certification as market differentiator |
| **Frontier labs refuse Reality Rent** | Document quality advantages of CIN-licensed data; publish comparative benchmarks; build litigation capacity for jurisdictions with data rights frameworks (EU, Brazil) |
| **Geopolitical arms race intensifies** | Maintain strict neutrality; position the commons as a non-aligned alternative; prioritize build-out in third countries (India, Brazil, AU/NZ, African Union members) |

**The guiding assumption:** If top-down leverage fails, bottom-up pull must compensate. Phase 0's shadow commons is not just a precursor — it is the fallback position. If regulatory leverage proves unavailable, the commons must be good enough that participation becomes economically rational.

## 5. Phase 2: Federated Build-Out (2028 – 2030)

*Scaling the commons. This phase transitions from pilots to infrastructure.*

---

### 5.1 Objectives

- 100+ certified CINs globally
- Global Trunk prototype operational
- Reality Rent mainstreamed across adopting jurisdictions
- First Risk Bond forfeitures demonstrating accountability

---

### 5.2 CIN Network Expansion

**Growth Targets:**

| Year | Certified CINs | Members | Context Languages |
|------|----------------|---------|-------------------|
| 2028 | 30 | 10,000 | 25 |
| 2029 | 60 | 30,000 | 50 |
| 2030 | 100 | 100,000 | 75 |

**Support Structure:**
- Regional CIN hubs (Africa, Asia-Pacific, Americas, Europe)
- Technical assistance: open-source curation tools, governance templates, legal support
- Peer validation: experienced CINs mentor new ones
- Funding: Global Commons Fund allocation + Reality Rent revenue

---

### 5.3 Global Trunk Prototype

**What It Is:** The first federated foundational model governed by the Multi-Stakeholder Trust.

**Architecture:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Federated Training** | Training distributed across Compute Commons nodes, not centralized in one lab |
| **CIN-Licensed Data** | Training data licensed through CINs, with Reality Rent flowing to communities |
| **Open-Weight Base** | Core weights open for audit, with safety invariants cryptographically enforced |
| **Local Branches** | Fine-tuning through Trusted Execution Environments (no raw weight download) |
| **Governance** | Multi-Stakeholder Trust oversees safety, capability releases, and branch access |

**TEE/Autonomy Tradeoff:** Trusted Execution Environments prevent communities from downloading and freely modifying raw model weights — which is necessary to preserve safety invariants, but also limits the depth of local adaptation. Communities can fine-tune behavior, cultural context, and domain specialization within TEE bounds, but cannot alter foundational architecture. This is a genuine tension with the Charter's Fractal Autonomy principle (Charter Section 4.5). The boundary between "meaningful adaptation" and "safety constraint" is reviewed annually by the Technical Steering Committee, with CIN input, and expanded as safety verification tools mature. The goal is maximum autonomy consistent with demonstrated safety — not minimum autonomy justified by hypothetical risk.

**Funding:**
- Public-private partnership: aligned governments, philanthropic capital, CIN revenue reinvestment
- Competitive with corporate models to create pull

---

### 5.4 Reality Rent Scaling

**Phase 2 Mechanisms:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Mandatory Licensing** | In adopting jurisdictions, training on unlicensed context is prohibited |
| **Data Provenance Mandate** | All models deployed in RTZ markets must provide provenance documentation |
| **Collective Bargaining** | CIN unions negotiate rates across regions |
| **Dispute Resolution** | Fast-track arbitration for pricing disputes (30-day resolution target) |

**Revenue Scale:**
- Target: $500M annual Reality Rent flowing to CINs by 2030
- Allocation: 70% to CIN members, 20% to CIN operations, 10% to Global Commons Fund for CIN expansion
- **Caveat:** These projections are aspirational. The global AI training data market is currently estimated in the low single-digit billions. $500M through CIN licensing by 2030 assumes rapid adoption across multiple jurisdictions, widespread CIN formation, and corporate willingness to license rather than rely on synthetic data. Actual revenue will depend on the pace of Phase 1 regulatory adoption and the quality advantage CIN-licensed data demonstrates over synthetic alternatives. If revenue lags projections, CIN expansion funding shifts to Global Commons Fund and philanthropic sources until the market develops.

---

### 5.5 Risk Bond Activation

**Phase 2 Implementation:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Bond Requirement** | Entities deploying autonomous AI in critical infrastructure must post bonds |
| **Bond Calculation** | Bond = (Impact Radius × Autonomy Level × Vulnerability) / Transparency Score |
| **Forfeiture** | Upon confirmed systemic harm, bond pays directly to affected communities |
| **Appeals** | Independent arbitration for contested forfeitures |

**Pilot Sector:** Healthcare AI, autonomous transport, energy grid management

## 6. Phase 3: Full Subsidiarity (2030+)

*The vision realized. This phase describes the mature system, but the path evolves as we learn.*

---

### 6.1 Objectives

- Global Trunk fully operational, governed by Multi-Stakeholder Trust
- Compute Gini `<` 0.6 globally
- 500+ CINs covering all major language and cultural contexts
- Reality Rent institutionalized as core AI funding mechanism

---

### 6.2 Mature System Architecture

| Element | Mature State |
|---------|--------------|
| **Global Trunk** | Federated foundational model, open-weight auditability, cryptographically enforced safety |
| **Local Branches** | Fine-tuned models for BAZs, cultures, sectors; autonomy within safety bounds |
| **Compute Commons** | Distributed infrastructure; access as a right, not a commodity |
| **CIN Network** | Global federation of context stewards; Reality Rent as primary AI funding |
| **Governance** | Multi-Stakeholder Trust with CIN majority; binding arbitration |
| **Accountability** | Risk Bonds, Kill Switch, whistleblower protections fully operational |

---

### 6.3 Transition Triggers

Full subsidiarity is achieved when:

1. **Compute Gini `<` 0.6** across all entities operating frontier models
2. **CIN coverage `>` 80%** of global linguistic and cultural contexts
3. **Reality Rent `>` 50%** of frontier model training costs
4. **Trust ratified** by 100+ BAZs and 50+ nation-states
5. **Global Trunk** has completed two full training cycles with demonstrated safety

### 8.2 Accountability Ladder

| Violation | First Response | Escalation | Final |
|-----------|----------------|------------|-------|

---

### 8.3 Algorithmic Quarantine (Critical Infrastructure Safe Mode)

A binary kill switch for systems managing critical infrastructure — energy grids, hospitals, water treatment — would cause catastrophic collateral harm. The enforcement architecture therefore uses **graduated quarantine** rather than termination.

| Level | Trigger | Action | Critical Infrastructure |
|-------|---------|--------|------------------------|
| **Yellow** | Transparency violation or rent evasion | Warning; enforced audit within 14 days | No disruption |
| **Orange** | Confirmed parasitic behavior (local) | Local context access severed; system reverts to last audited baseline model | Critical services continue on baseline |
| **Red** | Confirmed systemic harm or capability threshold breach | Regional or global quarantine; all new data ingestion halted; API access restricted to maintenance-only | Critical services run on sandboxed previous-version baseline with no external connectivity |

**Baseline requirements for critical infrastructure deployments:**
- Every system managing Tier 1 infrastructure must maintain a cryptographically signed, independently audited baseline version capable of autonomous operation without trunk connectivity
- Baseline versions are refreshed every 6 months under Trust oversight
- Quarantine-to-baseline failover must be tested quarterly, with results published

**Restoration:** A quarantined system may only be restored after independent audit by the Technical Steering Committee confirms the triggering behavior has been resolved. Restoration requires 2/3 approval from the relevant CIN Council.

---

### 8.4 Cultural Sovereignty Veto Registry (Red Lines Registry)

When a CIN exercises the Cultural Sovereignty Veto — permanently withdrawing knowledge domains from AI training — that decision must be recorded, enforced, and protected against future re-extraction.

**Registry Architecture:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **What is recorded** | The knowledge domain withdrawn (described at a level of abstraction that does not itself disclose the protected knowledge), the CIN exercising the veto, the date, and the scope (specific model, all models, specific use cases) |
| **Persistence** | Entries are permanent. A veto can only be reversed by the originating CIN through the same governance process that created it |
| **Technical enforcement** | All training pipelines accessing CIN-licensed data must check against the Registry before ingestion. Data matching a veto entry is automatically excluded. Provenance Ledger integration ensures traceability |
| **Audit** | Annual audit confirms that vetoed knowledge domains are not present in models trained after the veto date. Audit conducted by independent technical auditors, funded from Reality Rent allocation |
| **Penalties for violation** | Training on vetoed knowledge triggers immediate context access severance (Orange-level quarantine), bond forfeiture to the affected CIN, and Data Destruction Rights (purging of derived algorithmic training data as specified in the Charter) |

**Historical record:** The Registry also serves as a historical record of community decisions about their own knowledge. Future governance bodies can learn from patterns of withdrawal — which knowledge communities choose to protect, and why — without accessing the protected knowledge itself.

## 9. Failure Modes & Countermeasures

*Explicit acknowledgment of how this could fail, and what we do about it.*

---

| Failure Mode | Likelihood | Countermeasure |
|--------------|------------|----------------|
| **Corporate consolidation accelerates** | High | Build shadow commons; use antitrust where possible; focus on pull, not push |
| **Geopolitical fragmentation (US vs. China)** | High | Maintain neutrality; build in third countries; keep commons open |
| **CIN capture by elites** | Medium | Sortition for governance; transparency mandates; peer validation |
| **Technical breakthroughs outpace governance** | High | Capability thresholds with automatic pauses; quarantine protocols; fail-safes not fail-deadly |
| **Reality Rent captured by middlemen** | Medium | Direct CIN-to-community payment; transparency reporting; audit rights |
| **Global South excluded** | High | Dedicated funding; technology transfer; South-South cooperation hubs |
| **Whistleblowers unprotected** | Medium | Secure infrastructure; legal support; asylum pathways |
| **Quarantine causes collateral harm** | Low | Graduated levels (8.3); baseline mode for critical infrastructure; quarterly failover testing |
| **Choke point leverage fails** | Medium | Fallback to market-based pull (4.6); coalition of the willing; open-source competition |
