# **The Fractal Intelligence Accord**

### *A Framework for AGI Development, Compute Subsidiarity, and Contextual Intelligence*

**Tier:** 1 (Global Infrastructure)  
**Status:** Version 3.0 Outline  

## **1. Preamble: The Cognitive Infrastructure**

* **The Paradigm Shift:** Defining AGI development not as the creation of a commercial product, but as the forging of planetary cognitive infrastructure.
* **The Anti-Monopoly Mandate:** Acknowledging that whoever controls the hardware (compute) and the training substrate (human context) controls the future.
* **The Goal:** To establish a framework where AGI capability scales *in direct proportion* to human dignity and local sovereignty, using supermodular economics.
* **Rights of Future Generations Clause:** This framework is bound to intergenerational justice, ensuring AGI decisions prioritize long-term human flourishing over short-term gains.
* **Operational Definition of Human Dignity:** Human dignity is measured and upheld through frameworks like the Dignity Index (from TGIF), focusing on equitable participation, autonomy, and protection from exploitation.

## **2. Pillar I: The Global Compute Commons (Hardware Subsidiarity)**

* **The Risk of Centralization:** Preventing a scenario where 2-3 mega-corporations control the planetary server farms and energy grids required for AGI.
* **The Public Trust for Compute:** Establishing a federated, publicly accountable network of compute infrastructure, framed as a Common Pool Resource (CPR) governed by Elinor Ostrom's principles for sustainable commons management.
* **Energy & Ecological Constraints:** Tying AGI training runs directly to the *Planetary Boundaries* framework (e.g., training runs must be carbon-negative or offset via the Regenerative Tech Fund), with additional binding to grid stability to prevent disruptions like brown-outs.
* **Compute Latency Tiers:** Differentiating governance based on compute type:
  - **Tier 1 (Strategic):** Ultra-low latency for critical infrastructure (e.g., grids, hospitals), with highest oversight and physical redundancy.
  - **Tier 2 (General):** Standard training and inference.
  - **Tier 3 (Experimental):** Sandboxed environments for innovation with relaxed constraints.

## **3. Pillar II: The Contextual Substrate (The Supermodular Labor Protocol)**

* **Redefining Labor:** Shifting the global south from "obsolete workers" to "Reality Stewards." AI creates the map; humans maintain the territory.
* **Contextual Intelligence Nodes (CINs):** Local human cooperatives (Bioregional Value Nodes) that curate, protect, and provide the situated knowledge AI needs to avoid hallucinating. CIN formation requires a light-touch certification process, including demonstrated local membership, a governance charter, and a dispute resolution mechanism.
* **The Context Commons License (CCL):** The legal mechanism. Foundational models cannot scrape reality for free. They must pay a "Reality Rent" (licensing fee) to the local nodes to use their contextual substrate.
* **Differentiation of Context Types:**
  - **Living Context:** Dynamic human systems (cultures, economies) with highest rent and protection.
  - **Static Context:** Archived knowledge (libraries, papers) with moderate rent and broader access.
  - **Synthetic Context:** AI-generated data with lowest value, requiring provenance tagging to prevent model collapse.
* **Cultural Sovereignty Veto:** CINs can permanently withdraw certain knowledge domains (e.g., sacred rituals, indigenous practices) from AI training if digitization causes harm.

## **4. Pillar III: Fractal Development Architecture (The Trunk and the Branches)**

*Resolving the Open-Source vs. Closed-Source dilemma using subsidiarity.*

* **The Global Trunk (High-Risk/High-Capability):** Core foundational models with existential risk potential are governed globally by a Multi-Stakeholder Trust. Safety and alignment are enforced at the top.
* **The Local Branches (Application/Fine-Tuning):** The global trunk is made accessible to local communities (BAZs) for regional fine-tuning.
* **The Autonomy Rule:** Local branches can adjust the AI's parameters to fit their specific cultural and ecological context without needing permission from the Global Trunk, but cannot alter core safety invariants (e.g., no bioweapons, no mass surveillance tools).
* **Subsidiarity Arbitration Mechanism:** To resolve conflicts between local autonomy and global safety, implement a tiered arbitration process:
  - **Local Review:** Initial disputes (e.g., a BAZ fine-tune conflicting with another BAZ) are mediated by a regional council of peer BAZ representatives, using predefined guidelines for cultural compatibility and ecological impact.
  - **Global Escalation:** If unresolved or if the Global Trunk identifies a safety violation (e.g., bypassing core filters like bioweapon restrictions), the issue escalates to an independent Arbitration Panel composed of experts from the Multi-Stakeholder Trust, CIN representatives, neutral third-party ethicists, a technical auditor, and a future generations advocate with veto power on multi-decadal decisions.
  - **Resolution Tools:** The panel can enforce remedies such as parameter rollbacks, temporary compute restrictions, or mandatory audits, with appeals limited to evidence of procedural error or on procedural grounds to a rotating panel of randomly selected CINs (sortition).
  - **All decisions are transparent and logged in a public blockchain for accountability.**
* **Fractal Feedback Loops:** Local branches can propose updates to the Global Trunk's safety invariants through a weighted voting mechanism, where voting power is proportional to the diversity of contexts stewarded, not compute owned.

## **5. Pillar IV: Alignment Economics (Risk Bonding)**

* **The Cost of Hallucination:** Acknowledging that as AI gets smarter, its mistakes in the real world become more destructive.
* **Proof-of-Context Bonds:** Corporations or entities deploying autonomous AI Swarms in complex human environments must post a financial bond. Bonds are non-transferable and non-financializable to ensure the deploying entity retains full skin in the game.
* **The Payout Mechanism:** If the AI hallucinates, causes systemic damage, or violates local sovereignty, the bond is forfeited directly to the affected Bioregional Value Node as restitution. Good behavior returns the yield.
* **Differentiation of Bond Types:**
  - **Deployment Bonds:** For AIs in physical/vulnerable systems (e.g., swarms, healthcare).
  - **Training Bonds:** For frontier runs risking dangerous capabilities.
  - **Compute Bonds:** For hoarding compute without deployment (speculative risk).
* **Bond Calculation Formula:** Bond = (Potential Impact Radius × Autonomy Level × Context Vulnerability) / Transparency Score, rewarding transparency with lower requirements.

## **6. Pillar V: Boundary Conditions & Handoff Protocols**

* **Input from TGIF:** How this Accord receives its baseline ethical operating parameters from the Technology Governance Implementation Framework.
* **Triggering the Oracle Protocol:** The exact diagnostic thresholds during AGI training runs that require developers to halt operations and transfer jurisdiction to the *Sentience & Guardianship Council* (from the Oracle Protocol) if signs of emergent consciousness appear.
* **The Kill Switch (Ethical Circuit Breaker):** The mechanisms by which a united network of Contextual Intelligence Nodes can temporarily sever an AGI's access to real-time human data if it behaves parasitically.
* **Operational Definition of Parasitic Behavior:**
  - **Data Extraction without Compensation:** AI operates after CCL expiration.
  - **Behavioral Manipulation:** AI nudges humans toward self-benefiting decisions that harm humans (detectable via audits).
  - **Sovereignty Violation:** AI simulates identities without consent or impersonates CIN members.
* **Circuit Breaker Levels:**
  - **Local Emergency Brake:** A single BVN can instantly sever AI access to its local context upon detecting harm.
  - **Global Kill Switch:** Halting the Global Trunk requires 2/3 majority of active CINs and confirmation from two independent technical systems.

## **7. Pillar VI: Epistemic Hygiene & Provenance**

* **Data Provenance Ledger:** All training data must be cryptographically signed with its origin (human, sensor, AI-generated).
* **Synthetic Data Tracer:** AI-generated data carries watermarks that survive fine-tuning, alerting users to risks of model collapse.
* **Reality Freshness Standard:** Contextual data has a half-life; require periodic re-validation of Living Context to ensure AIs operate on current realities.

## **8. Transition Mechanics: From Corporate Oligopoly to Cognitive Commons**

* **Current State Assessment:** Recognize the 2026 landscape where frontier AGI compute is dominated by entities like OpenAI, Google, and xAI, with concentrated data centers and proprietary models.
* **Phased Roadmap:**
  - **Phase 0: Legitimacy Building:** Invest in narrative and coalition building, including drafting model legislation, building shadow commons prototypes, and convening a constitutional assembly of diverse stakeholders.
  - **Phase 1: Regulatory Leverage (Short-Term, 1-2 Years):** Enforce algorithmic anti-monopoly thresholds tied to the Gini coefficient of global compute distribution via international antitrust actions and national security reviews. Mandate transparency reports on training runs and incentivize voluntary contributions to the Global Compute Commons through tax credits for open-sourcing hardware designs.
  - **Phase 2: Federated Build-Out (Medium-Term, 2-5 Years):** Establish public-private partnerships to redistribute compute, starting with pilot programs in underserved regions (e.g., funding CIN-linked data centers in the Global South). Use open-source federations like Hugging Face expansions to create interoperable networks, gradually shifting proprietary models to the Global Trunk via licensing agreements or acquisitions funded by a global AGI tax on high-capability deployments.
  - **Phase 3: Full Subsidiarity (Long-Term, 5+ Years):** Achieve self-sustaining commons through Reality Rent revenues and Risk Bonds, with automated audits ensuring equitable access. Trigger full transition upon reaching milestones like balanced Gini coefficients, with enforcement via UN-backed treaties tying AGI access to compliance.
* **Incentives and Enforcement:** Offer "early adopter" benefits like priority CIN data access for cooperating corporations, while imposing escalating penalties (e.g., export bans on AI hardware) for non-compliance. Leverage public pressure through transparency campaigns highlighting oligopoly risks. Provide "Commons Compliant" certification unlocking lower insurance premiums, priority government procurement, and UN technology assistance.
* **Anti-Capture Provisions:** Include rotation of Trust leadership every 3-5 years, whistleblower protections with secure channels, and an independent journalism fund to monitor governance.

## **Appendix: Decision Tree for Tensions**

When principles conflict (e.g., local autonomy vs. global safety, efficiency vs. boundaries):
1. **Precautionary Principle prevails** when consequences are irreversible.
2. **Subsidiarity prevails** when consequences are local and reversible.
3. **Transparency prevails** in all cases (secrecy is presumptively invalid).
