## Introduction

> *"Disability is not a tragedy to be prevented or a problem to be solved. It is a natural, valuable part of human diversity. The goal of this framework is not to eliminate disability, but to eliminate the systemic ableism, accessibility failures, and discriminatory policies that cause harm and death."*

**Purpose:** Embed disability justice as a non-negotiable foundation across the global governance ecosystem, ensuring universal design, collective care, cognitive and sensory equity, and embodied sovereignty. This framework audits and transforms all GGF frameworks to dismantle systemic ableism.

**Vision:** By 2050, a world with zero deaths caused by systemic ableism, accessibility failures, or medical rationing; 100% accessible spaces; and disability-led governance at all levels, guided by *Nothing About Us Without Us* and the social model of disability.

**Short-Term Vision (2026–2030):** Establish credible disabled-led representation and participation pathways in relevant Meta-Governance bodies; achieve 80% digital-platform WCAG 2.4+ compliance; launch 10 Care Cooperative pilots; and operate Disability Weather Reports in all pilot BAZs. The former 30% Meta-Governance target is an illustrative benchmark, not a cross-framework seat mandate.

**Core Principle:** This framework is grounded in the **social model of disability**—disability is caused by barriers in society, not by individual impairment. The solution is removing barriers, not "curing" or "preventing" disabled people.

## Core Principles

1. **Nothing About Us Without Us:** Policies materially affecting disabled people require direct, accessible, consequential participation by disabled people. A GDA, disabled-led body, or affected constituency may hold a binding review or veto only where the competent institution's own law or charter grants that power; Foundation does not create a universal cross-framework veto by declaration.

2. **Radical & Cognitive Access:** Universal design is a non-negotiable right extending beyond physical access to include cognitive and sensory access. All governance processes, digital platforms, and physical spaces must be inclusive for the widest possible range of cognitive styles and sensory needs.

3. **Social Model of Disability:** Disability is caused by systemic barriers, not by individual impairment. The framework targets systemic ableism, accessibility failures, and discriminatory policies—never disabled people themselves.

4. **Embodied Sovereignty:** Right to self-determination, rejecting forced treatment or institutionalization, with legal recognition of neurodivergence by 2030.

5. **Intersectional Justice:** Center disabled BIPOC, queer, poor, and incarcerated voices, with reparations for eugenics and climate-induced harm.

6. **Post-Capitalist Care:** Community-led Care Cooperatives, replacing exploitative systems, with care work valued through locally calibrated Hearts.

7. **Radical Inclusion:** All public spaces, institutions, and services are universally designed. There are no "separate" or "special" spaces for disabled people—only fully inclusive spaces.

8. **Non-Human Solidarity:** Rights for AI, assistive tech, and animals as access partners, driven by Crip Technology Labs.

9. **Rights of Nature:** Environmental policies prioritizing disability justice, addressing toxic impacts that disproportionately harm disabled communities.

## Functional Framework

### 1. Foundational Governance: The Global Disability Alliance (GDA)

The Global Disability Alliance is a cross-cutting caucus within Meta-Governance, ensuring disability-led governance at all levels.

**Representation requirements and composition status:**
- The GDA should be **disabled-led**, with strong participation from disabled people most affected by ableism, including Global South, BIPOC, queer, youth, older, poor, incarcerated, institutionalized, neurodivergent, sensory-disabled, and multiply marginalized communities.
- Allies and technical/AI advisers may support the body without displacing disabled leadership.
- The former 60% disabled / 40% ally profile and nested demographic percentages are illustrative historical design material, not self-executing constitutional quotas.
- The GDA's own valid constituting charter determines membership, constituency, selector, term, vacancy, removal, voting, conflict, and challenge rules.

**Authority boundary:**
- The GDA has **no automatic seats** on Earth Council, Social Resilience Council, Fractal Labor Parliament, Planetary Health Council, Cultural & Ancestral Wisdom Council, or any other independently constituted body. Those bodies may create disability-representation, consultation, standing, review, or voting pathways through their own valid instruments.
- A **Suspensive Review or Veto** may be granted for DCIA failures where the competent receiving body's charter or applicable law enacts it. Foundation's model does not make that power universal merely by naming it.
- The GDA may develop Multi-Modal Deliberation and Sensory-Inclusive Design standards. Those standards become binding on another institution only through the authority that governs that institution.

**Accountability:**
- Annual public reporting through Disability Weather Reports
- Community validation by disabled-led organizations
- Justice review only where the competent Justice Systems/Treaty authority recognizes standing and jurisdiction

### 2. Systemic Transformation: Audits, Law, and Enforcement

#### 2.1. Disability & Cognitive Impact Assessments (DCIAs)

Mandatory for all GGF policies, using **Dynamic Category Weighting**—the GDA calibrates category weights based on the specific domain of the policy being audited.

| Domain | Physical | Cognitive | Sensory | Economic | Healthcare | Cultural | Example Policy |
|--------|----------|----------|--------|----------|-----------|---------|----------------|
| **Digital Infrastructure** | 10% | 40% | 30% | 10% | 5% | 5% | Voting app, online portal |
| **Physical Infrastructure** | 50% | 15% | 15% | 10% | 5% | 5% | Transit station, public building |
| **Healthcare** | 15% | 20% | 15% | 5% | 40% | 5% | Triage protocol, hospital design |
| **Education** | 15% | 30% | 20% | 10% | 5% | 20% | Curriculum, school design |
| **Employment** | 20% | 25% | 15% | 25% | 5% | 10% | Hiring policy, workplace design |
| **Emergency Response** | 25% | 20% | 20% | 5% | 25% | 5% | Evacuation plan, shelter design |

**DCIA Process:**

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| **1. Scoping** | Policy defined; impacted communities identified | 2 weeks |
| **2. Community Consultation** | Disabled-led organizations consulted; public forums | 4 weeks |
| **3. Analysis** | A disabled-led DCIA panel conducts assessment; a 60% disabled profile is an illustrative minimum where the adopting body's charter enacts it | 4 weeks |
| **4. Draft Report** | Findings; recommendations; remediation plan | 2 weeks |
| **5. Community Validation** | Report validated by impacted communities | 2 weeks |
| **6. Final Report** | Published; binding if score below threshold | n/a |

**DCIA Thresholds:**

| Score | Status | Action |
|-------|--------|--------|
| 90-100 | Exemplary | Proceed; best practice documentation |
| 75-89 | Good | Proceed with minor remediation |
| 60-74 | Adequate | Proceed with remediation plan; re-audit in 1 year |
| 40-59 | Concerning | Suspensive review/remediation; any veto requires authority from the adopting institution |
| Below 40 | Harmful | Redesign required; any binding pause or veto requires authority from the adopting institution |

#### 2.2. Suspensive Review / Veto & Remediation Model

Where a competent institution has constitutionally adopted a GDA/DCIA suspensive-veto mechanism, the following model may apply. Otherwise these steps operate as non-binding review and remediation guidance:

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| **1. GDA Issues Review / Authorized Veto** | DCIA failure documented; policy pauses only if the competent adopting instrument grants that effect | Day 0 |
| **2. Remediation Plan Required** | Initiating council must propose plan addressing all DCIA failures | 60 days |
| **3. GDA Reviews Plan** | GDA accepts, rejects, or requests modifications | 30 days |
| **4. Implementation** | If plan accepted, policy proceeds with binding remediation timeline | Ongoing |
| **5. Escalation** | If council refuses to propose plan or implement accepted plan, case escalates to Digital Justice Tribunal | After 90 days of non-compliance |

**Justice interface:** Where applicable Justice Systems/Treaty law grants jurisdiction, a competent tribunal may order remedies, review procurement consequences, award lawful damages, or refer accountability matters. Foundation does not create those judicial powers.

**No procedural bypass where a veto is lawfully constituted.** If the receiving body's valid instrument grants a suspensive veto, its own override/remediation rules control; Foundation does not invent a universal "wait and pass anyway" rule.

#### 2.3. Enforcement: Procurement Exclusion & Market Access

Replaces unenforceable global revenue taxes with enforceable market participation consequences.

**Accessibility Compliance Ladder:**

| Level | Status | Consequence |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| **Level 1: Exemplary** | Full DCIA compliance; active accessibility improvements | Procurement preference; Ethical Trade Zone certification; public recognition |
| **Level 2: Developing** | Partial compliance; improvement plan in place | Procurement eligible with oversight; annual re-audit |
| **Level 3: Concerning** | DCIA failure; no improvement plan | **Procurement exclusion**; loss of Ethical Trade Zone certification; public transparency reporting |
| **Level 4: Harmful** | Repeated DCIA failures; discriminatory practices | Permanent procurement exclusion; Digital Justice Tribunal referral; mandatory reparations |

**Procurement Exclusion Scope:** Excluded entities cannot:
- Receive GGF or BAZ public contracts, grants, or loans
- Access Ethical Trade Zone benefits (tax incentives, expedited permitting)
- Participate in BAZ economic development programs

**Exclusion Duration:** Minimum 3 years; reinstatement only after DCIA compliance and community validation.

**Financial Consequence:** The loss of the contract itself is the financial punishment—no arbitrary fines on excluded entities.

#### 2.4. Access Ombuds Network

A corps of disabled-led auditors within Treaty's Global Enforcement Mechanism:
- Conduct accessibility audits using standardized protocols
- Publish findings in Disability Weather Reports
- Support communities in filing grievances
- Escalate violations to Digital Justice Tribunal

#### 2.5. Multi-Modal Deliberation Mandate

All Meta-Governance councils must offer multiple valid modes of participation:
- Asynchronous text-based forums
- Visual system mapping
- Oral/narrative formats
- In-person with interpretation
- Sensory-friendly options

#### 2.6. Sensory-Inclusive Design Standards

Binding standards for low-arousal, sensory-friendly design:
- Quiet spaces in all public buildings
- Adjustable lighting (dimming, warm spectrum)
- Noise-reducing materials
- Clear signage with visual, tactile, and audio options
- Mandatory for all Urban and Community Development Framework projects

### 3. Economic Transformation: The Commons of Care

#### 3.1. Layered Economic Model

| Layer | Mechanism | Purpose | Funding Source |
|-------|-----------|---------|----------------|
| **Infrastructure Layer** | Capital grants, operating budgets | Physical accessibility (ramps, sensory rooms); Crip Tech Labs; Care Cooperative startup costs; administrative overhead | Global Commons Fund; procurement penalties |
| **Care Work Layer** | Hearts (locally calibrated) | Individual care work: companionship, personal assistance, emotional support, non-verbal care | AUBI Layer 2; BAZ budget; locally determined rates |
| **Care Cooperative Layer** | Worker-owned enterprises with Hearts + fiat mix | Organized care delivery; collective bargaining; benefits; training | Mix of Hearts (for care) + Global Commons Fund (for infrastructure) |

#### 3.2. Neuro-Inclusive Contribution Recognition

Care work validation must recognize non-verbal, non-traditional, and neurodivergent forms of contribution.

**Validation Methods:**

| Method | Description | Use Case |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Peer Attestation** | Another community member (not family) confirms care occurred | Companionship, emotional support |
| **Visual/Audio Documentation** | Photo, video, or audio record (with consent) of care environment | Environmental tending, sensory support |
| **Community Vouching** | Circle of 3-5 community members confirm contribution | Long-term care relationships |
| **Self-Reporting with Reflection** | Care worker describes care provided; community review | All categories |

**No medical diagnosis required.** Validation is based on community recognition of care, not clinical assessment of need.

#### 3.3. Locally Calibrated Hearts Rates

The Foundation Protocol does not set global Hearts rates. Each BAZ determines rates through transparent, community-led process.

| Category | Local Calibration Factors |
|----------|---------------------------|
| **Personal Care Assistance** | Local cost of living; complexity of care; certification level; hours per week |
| **Emotional Support & Companionship** | Cultural valuation of care; relationship duration; intensity of need |
| **Non-Verbal Care** | Communication complexity; sensory support; environmental tending |
| **Peer Support & Mentorship** | Experience level; training; community need |

#### 3.4. Care Cooperatives

**Structure:**
- Worker-owned; disabled workers hold majority ownership
- One worker, one vote; elected board; annual general meeting
- Mix of Hearts (care work) + fiat (infrastructure, benefits)
- Healthcare, retirement, paid leave funded through Global Commons Fund

**Funding Model:**

| Cost Type | Funding Source | Rationale |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Startup Costs** | Global Commons Fund grants | Capital-intensive; barrier to entry |
| **Infrastructure** | Global Commons Fund | Ramps, vehicles, equipment, software |
| **Worker Benefits** | Global Commons Fund; procurement contracts | Ensures dignified work regardless of Hearts supply |
| **Care Work** | Hearts (locally calibrated) | Community validation; flexible; post-capitalist |
| **Administrative Overhead** | BAZ budget; procurement contracts | Sustainable operations |

**Accountability:**
- Annual financial audit (disabled-led)
- Worker satisfaction survey (published)
- Care recipient feedback (anonymized; aggregated)
- Grievance process with independent mediator
- Right to unionize (if not already worker-owned)

### 4. Cultural & Technological Transformation: Crip Innovation

#### 4.1. Crip Technology Labs

**Structure:**
- Governance: disabled-led and intersectionally inclusive, with strong Global South, youth/intergenerational, and other affected-community participation. The former 60% disabled / 50% Global South / 30% Indigenous profile is illustrative only. Relevant Indigenous peoples determine their own representatives or participation through their own authority; the Lab charter determines all other composition and selection rules.
- Focus: Open-source assistive tech; AI sign language translation; accessible prosthetics; communication devices; sensory tools
- Ethics Board: Crip Tech Ethics Board with binding authority; reviews all projects for data sovereignty, accessibility, anti-extraction

**Open Source Mandate:**
- All technologies developed must be open-source; patent-free; community-owned
- Free availability to all BAZs
- Priority distribution to Global South and low-resource communities
- Training and support included
- Community adaptation rights
- No royalties or licensing fees

**Funding:**
- Global Commons Fund: 50% (core operations; research; development)
- Procurement partnerships: 30% (technology transfer; scaling)
- Grants: 20% (partnerships; innovation)

**No corporate funding** that would compromise open-source mandate or data sovereignty.

#### 4.2. Neurodiversity Education (Radical Inclusion Model)

**Reject separate schools.** The mandate is radical inclusion: all schools must be transformed to be universally designed, sensory-friendly, and neuro-inclusive.

**Curriculum:**
- Social model of disability; neurodiversity paradigm; disability justice history
- Cognitive equity: multiple ways of thinking, learning, communicating are valuable
- Sensory access: designing for sensory diversity; low-arousal environments
- Communication diversity: non-verbal communication; AAC; sign language; written alternatives
- Anti-ableism: identifying and challenging ableist assumptions; allyship
- Self-advocacy: rights; accommodations; self-determination

**Teacher Training:**
- All teachers trained in neurodiversity education
- Training co-designed with neurodivergent educators
- Annual refresher; peer observation
- Accommodations for neurodivergent teachers

**Universal Design Requirements for All Schools:**
- Sensory-friendly environments (quiet spaces, adjustable lighting, noise reduction)
- Flexible pacing; strength-based approaches
- No behavioral compliance programs (ABA, restraint, seclusion)
- Intergenerational; peer mentoring
- Full physical accessibility
- Multiple communication modalities

#### 4.3. Cultural Preservation

- Protect Deaf culture, sign languages via Cultural & Ancestral Wisdom Council
- Disability Justice Stories archive to combat cultural ableism
- Oral history projects documenting disability justice movement

#### 4.4. Disability Justice Stories Archive

A living archive of narratives, oral histories, and creative works:
- Documenting experiences of systemic ableism
- Celebrating disability culture and resistance
- Preserving movement history
- Informing policy through lived experience

Governed by disabled-led organizations; community consent required for all materials.

Validation requires documented community feedback incorporated into report and action plan.

### 8. Appendices

#### Appendix A: Disability Justice Principles Statement

This framework is grounded in the following principles:

- **Intersectionality:** Disability justice addresses the overlapping oppressions of race, class, gender, sexuality, and immigration status.
- **Leadership of the Most Impacted:** Those most marginalized by ableism lead the solutions.
- **Anti-Capitalism:** The framework rejects the commodification of care and the exploitation of disabled labor.
- **Collective Access:** Access is not individual accommodation but collective responsibility.
- **Wholeness:** Disabled people are whole as they are; there is no need to be "fixed" or "cured."
- **Sustainability:** Care work must be sustainable for both caregivers and care recipients.
- **Cross-Movement Solidarity:** Disability justice is inseparable from climate justice, racial justice, and Indigenous sovereignty.

| Category | Definition |

## Conclusion: A Bulletproof Foundation

The Foundation Protocol provides the radical inclusion, disability justice, and systemic accountability that the entire Global Governance Framework rests upon.

**The Core Architecture:**

1. **Social Model Grounding:** Disability is caused by systemic barriers, not individual impairment. The framework targets ableism, not disabled people.

2. **Nothing About Us Without Us:** Disabled people have direct, consequential participation; DCIA community validation and disability-led governance remain core. A GDA binding veto exists only where the competent institution has lawfully constituted it.

3. **Enforceable Tools:** Procurement exclusion replaces unenforceable global taxes; compliance ladder with clear consequences; Digital Justice Tribunal enforcement.

4. **Layered Economics:** Infrastructure funded through Global Commons Fund; care work valued through locally calibrated Hearts; no conflation of macro and micro.

5. **Radical Inclusion:** No separate schools; all spaces universally designed; non-verbal care validation; peer attestation; community vouching.

6. **Cognitive Sovereignty:** Crip Tech Labs open-source; no corporate capture; anti-extraction design; data sovereignty.

7. **Democratic Accountability:** Disability Weather Reports with community validation; quarterly reporting; binding action plans.

With this framework, accessibility, cognitive equity, and disability justice are not afterthoughts—they are the very ground upon which all other GGF frameworks are built.
