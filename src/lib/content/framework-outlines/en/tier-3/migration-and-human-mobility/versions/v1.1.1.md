# 1. Revision basis

## 1.1 Source freeze

This document succeeds the Enhanced Global Governance Framework for Migration & Human Mobility v1.0. It preserves the source framework’s orientation toward dignity over documentation, the right to move and the right to stay, anti-criminalization, unconditional support, family unity, trauma-informed reception, host–migrant co-governance, right of return, rematriation, and non-punitive solidarity.

It does not silently treat the source’s proposed institutions, passports, biometric systems, protected zones, automatic visas, status categories, guardianship arrangements, corridors, or funding mechanisms as already constituted legal powers.

## 1.2 Constitutional problem addressed

The source combined humane objectives with several self-executing legal effects: automatic climate visas, temporary protected personhood, biometric Love Ledger enrolment, universal mobility credentials, protected mobility zones, guardian assignment, deportation prevention, relocation rights, and territorial arrangements. Those objectives may be legitimate when enacted through competent law, but the framework itself cannot create jurisdiction, legal status, border access, custody, identity, guardianship, removal, or return authority.

v1.1 therefore separates:

1. immediate service access;
2. civil identity and identity reconstruction;
3. asylum and protection status;
4. residence and work authorization;
5. travel documentation and border admission;
6. reception and placement;
7. family tracing and guardianship;
8. detention and release;
9. expulsion, deportation, extradition, and transfer;
10. voluntary return, right of return, and rematriation;
11. planned relocation and climate mobility;
12. host-community coordination and Indigenous territorial authority.

## 1.3 What is preserved

The successor preserves and strengthens:

- dignity over documentation;
- protection and essential services without compulsory identity enrolment;
- a right to seek safety and a right to remain where viable;
- non-refoulement and protection against return to grave harm;
- family unity and child best interests;
- freedom from arbitrary or routine immigration detention;
- survivor-centred trafficking and exploitation responses;
- unconditional support without assimilation milestones;
- host-community participation without punishment for non-participation;
- Indigenous nation-specific authority over affected territories;
- trauma-informed, accessible reception;
- right to voluntary return, property claims, cultural reconnection, and rematriation;
- anti-gentrification, labour, housing, and community-infrastructure safeguards;
- no use of mobility status as a proxy for criminality, threat, worth, or belonging.

## 1.4 What is retired or narrowed

v1.1 retires or narrows:

- a universal Global Mobility Passport;
- mandatory blockchain or Love Ledger participation;
- compulsory biometric enrolment;
- automatic visa or status creation from environmental metrics;
- framework-created Protected Mobility Zones;
- framework-created global guardianship authority;
- automatic identity documents without competent issuers;
- security screening merged with service access;
- “successful integration” as a funding or eligibility metric;
- fixed host-population thresholds that automatically create governance duties;
- Reciprocity Capital as a tradable claim on public finance;
- automatic land-tenure grants or governance transfers by framework declaration;
- any GGF body acting as border police, detention authority, deportation authority, or enforcement service merely because it supports migrants.

# 2. Purpose, scope, and non-authority

## 2.1 Purpose

The framework helps competent authorities, communities, service providers, migrants, displaced persons, stateless persons, refugees, asylum seekers, survivors, workers, families, and affected Indigenous nations coordinate humane mobility systems while preserving rights, lawful jurisdiction, local capacity, and meaningful choice.

## 2.2 Scope

The framework covers:

- displacement prevention and the right to stay;
- emergency reception and essential services;
- de facto displacement recognition for service access;
- identity reconstruction and documentation support;
- asylum, temporary protection, and non-refoulement;
- family tracing, reunification, and child protection;
- trafficking, exploitation, and labour protection;
- planned relocation and climate mobility;
- host–migrant co-governance;
- voluntary return, return rights, and rematriation;
- legal defence, complaint, appeal, and remedy;
- interfaces with safety, crime cooperation, peacebuilding, disaster response, health, data governance, work, and Indigenous governance.

## 2.3 Non-authority clause

This framework does not itself:

- create citizenship, nationality, residence, asylum, refugee, visa, work, travel, or protection status;
- authorize admission, exclusion, border search, interception, detention, deportation, extradition, transfer, removal, return, or relocation;
- create a border, protected zone, corridor, camp, reception centre, guardianship, tribunal, police body, intelligence service, or enforcement unit;
- issue identity documents or travel credentials;
- compel biometric collection, enrolment, data sharing, health screening, labour, relocation, assimilation, return, or participation in a digital ledger;
- transfer territory, land, jurisdiction, or Indigenous authority;
- authorize sanctions, asset restraint, prosecution, or corporate enforcement;
- override non-refoulement, family unity, child best interests, disability rights, survivor choice, Indigenous authority, or independent review.

Every consequential act requires an independently valid constituting instrument and the applicable SCPA record bundle.

## 2.4 Normative language

“Shall” identifies a requirement for framework conformance. “Should” identifies a strong presumption requiring reasons for departure. “May” identifies permission within independently valid authority. Nothing in this document converts a recommendation into legal power.

# 3. Core principles

## 3.1 Dignity over documentation

Lack of documents may complicate identity or status adjudication. It shall not by itself bar emergency shelter, food, water, urgent healthcare, interpretation, disability support, protection from violence, child safeguarding, legal information, or access to a protection procedure.

## 3.2 Protection and status are distinct

A person may receive immediate protection and services without a final legal-status decision. Temporary assistance shall not be misrepresented as citizenship, residence, asylum, or permanent admission.

## 3.3 Right to move and right to stay

Mobility policy shall support both safe movement and the ability to remain in place with dignity. Adaptation, restoration, livelihood, peacebuilding, and disaster-risk reduction shall not be neglected in order to make relocation appear inevitable.

## 3.4 Non-refoulement

No person shall be transferred, expelled, extradited, deported, rejected, informally pushed back, or induced to return where there is a substantial risk of persecution, torture, enforced disappearance, arbitrary killing, trafficking, severe gender-based harm, catastrophic deprivation, or other grave and legally protected harm. The applicable legal standard and competent adjudicator must be recorded.

## 3.5 Individualized decision and prohibited inference

Nationality, ethnicity, religion, language, disability, migration route, documentation gap, homelessness, poverty, biometric refusal, community affiliation, asylum claim, political opinion, trauma response, or presence in a high-risk area shall not by themselves establish fraud, dangerousness, criminality, security threat, lack of credibility, or ineligibility.

## 3.6 Family unity

Authorities shall avoid separation, actively trace missing family members where safe, and make any separation decision through a lawful, individualized, reviewable process. Family unity does not override survivor safety or a child’s best interests.

## 3.7 Child best interests and evolving capacity

Children are rights-holders. Best-interests assessment, independent representation, evolving capacity, family tracing, education, healthcare, and safe care shall be operational requirements rather than aspirational language.

## 3.8 Disability and accessibility

Communication, transport, shelter, procedures, hearings, documentation, and services shall be accessible. Support persons, sign language, alternative communication, quiet spaces, mobility equipment, medication continuity, and reasonable accommodations shall be available without adverse inference.

## 3.9 Survivor sovereignty

People affected by trafficking, exploitation, torture, sexual violence, coercive control, or organized crime retain choice over support, reporting, testimony, relocation, and restorative participation. Support shall not depend on cooperation with investigation or prosecution.

## 3.10 Indigenous affected-nation authority

No universal Indigenous council or proxy may authorize mobility arrangements for all Indigenous peoples. Entry, transit, reception, relocation, data collection, land use, rematriation, and return affecting an Indigenous nation require that nation’s own authority and procedures.

## 3.11 Host-community dignity without exclusionary power

Host communities are entitled to truthful capacity information, infrastructure support, participation, and remedy for imposed burdens. Participation does not create power to discriminate, collectively expel, deny essential services, or subordinate individual rights to plebiscitary preference.

## 3.12 No soft coercion

Funding, infrastructure, public recognition, mobility support, or cooperation shall not be conditioned on assimilation scores, political loyalty, biometric enrolment, intelligence sharing, acceptance of a fixed population quota, participation in a ledger, or waiver of rights.

## 3.13 Least restrictive effective measure

When a legitimate mobility objective can be achieved through reporting support, case management, community placement, documentation assistance, scheduling, financial assistance, or another less restrictive measure, custody, surveillance, confinement, exclusion, or compulsory movement shall not be used.

## 3.14 Complete lifecycle and remedy

Every consequential mobility action shall include notice, reasons, review, appeal, termination, correction, data disposition, release or handback where applicable, and remedy for wrongful action.

# 4. SCPA action classes and functional separation

## 4.1 Ordinary framework activity

Most Migration v1.1.1 activity belongs to **SCPA Class A: voluntary assistance and prevention**. Reception, shelter, legal information, interpretation, voluntary casework, family tracing, host dialogue, livelihood support, and voluntary return assistance can be provided without coercive authority when participation is genuinely voluntary and data use is bounded.

## 4.2 Actions requiring separate routes

The following require separate SCPA routes:

| Function | SCPA class | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| observation and protected warning | B | aggregate displacement monitoring; route-safety warning |
| investigation | C | trafficking investigation; document fraud inquiry |
| urgent protection | D | immediate evacuation from trafficking or violence |
| compulsory process | E | compulsory disclosure; search; document seizure |
| custody and force | F | detention; restraint; involuntary transport |
| cyber intervention | G | defensive action on a reception system; third-party disruption |
| financial coercion | H | sanctions; asset freezes; forfeiture |
| adjudication and remedy | I | asylum determination; detention review; appeal |
| reintegration | J | voluntary return and restorative reintegration |
| mobility and territorial action | K | admission; visa; status; border action; expulsion; corridor; relocation |

## 4.3 No upward inference

A service-access record is not an identity determination. Identity evidence is not a security allegation. A security allegation is not an asylum exclusion. An asylum exclusion is not a detention order. A detention order is not a deportation order. A deportation order is not an extradition decision. A relocation offer is not consent. A host compact is not territorial jurisdiction.

## 4.4 Referral is not transfer of authority

A mobility service may refer a protection concern to a competent authority. It may not accept legal responsibility on that authority’s behalf, command an investigation, determine guilt, order detention, or execute removal.

# 5. Institutional architecture

## 5.1 No global mobility sovereign

v1.1 creates no global ministry, passport authority, border command, asylum tribunal, detention service, or territorial administration.

## 5.2 Federated Mobility Support and Reception Network

Participating institutions may form a non-sovereign network to:

- publish model service standards;
- coordinate voluntary assistance;
- route requests and offers;
- maintain interpreters and accessible information;
- support family tracing;
- develop reception and host-infrastructure tools;
- facilitate peer learning;
- provide capacity grants through separately valid finance;
- publish aggregate, privacy-protective learning.

The network may not issue legal status, identity documents, travel documents, admission decisions, detention orders, removal orders, or territorial permissions.

## 5.3 Legal Protection and Casework Services

Independent or publicly chartered casework services should provide:

- legal orientation and referral;
- access to counsel;
- protection application support;
- identity reconstruction assistance;
- family tracing;
- disability and communication support;
- trafficking and survivor advocacy;
- complaints and urgent-relief applications.

Caseworkers are not intelligence collectors or adjudicators.

## 5.4 Host–Migrant Compact Facilitation

Neutral facilitators may help affected residents, incoming populations, local authorities, service providers, workers, landlords, schools, health systems, and affected Indigenous nations negotiate infrastructure and coexistence arrangements. A compact cannot waive non-derogable rights or create detention, exclusion, surveillance, taxation, or removal authority.

## 5.5 Independent Mobility Ombudsperson

Each implementation shall provide an ombuds or equivalent complaint route independent of reception operators, status adjudicators, detention operators, employers, landlords, and security services. The ombuds may investigate administration, support urgent referrals, recommend correction, and publish aggregate findings. It does not replace courts or legally competent review bodies.

# 6. Access, attestation, and immediate assistance

## 6.1 Minimum attestation

A person seeking immediate help may use self-attestation, community attestation, humanitarian referral, family confirmation, prior records, witness evidence, digital or paper copies, or other reasonable evidence. The threshold for essential services shall be lower than the threshold for permanent legal identity or status.

## 6.2 Community attestation

Community attestation is evidence, not sovereign status creation. Its weight depends on purpose, reliability, potential conflicts, and challenge rights. Fraud safeguards shall target deliberate deception rather than punish people whose records were destroyed, withheld, inconsistent, translated differently, or never issued.

## 6.3 Service-access credential

Where a credential is useful, it shall be purpose-limited to service access and shall not silently become a travel document, national identity, criminal identifier, benefit surveillance key, employment score, or security credential.

## 6.4 No mandatory identity before aid

Emergency shelter, food, urgent care, interpretation, disability support, protection from violence, and child safeguarding shall not depend on biometric enrolment, a blockchain account, a Love Ledger account, or possession of a passport.

## 6.5 Exit and refusal

A person may refuse optional enrolment, case management, cultural programming, work placement, spiritual support, or community activities without losing essential assistance or attracting a security flag.

# 7. Identity, credentials, and documentation

## 7.1 Functional separation

The following functions shall be separated unless competent law expressly joins them with adequate safeguards:

1. civil identity;
2. service access;
3. asylum or protection case;
4. residence authorization;
5. work authorization;
6. travel document;
7. benefit account;
8. health record;
9. education record;
10. child-protection record;
11. family-tracing record;
12. security or criminal-investigation record.

## 7.2 Identity reconstruction

Identity reconstruction shall use plural evidence and record uncertainty. It shall permit correction and later supplementation. A person shall not be forced to assert a nationality, family relation, date of birth, or name that they reasonably contest.

## 7.3 Biometrics

Biometrics are not the default condition of dignity or service. Any biometric use requires:

- a specific lawful purpose;
- proof that a less intrusive alternative is inadequate;
- informed notice and accessible explanation;
- a rights-equivalent non-biometric route where feasible;
- separation from criminal and intelligence databases;
- strict access, retention, correction, and deletion rules;
- independent review and remedy;
- additional safeguards for children, survivors, and people whose bodies or identities place them at risk.

Biometric refusal shall not itself establish fraud, dangerousness, or ineligibility.

## 7.4 No universal mobility passport

A future interoperable credential may support voluntary travel or service portability only when issued by competent authorities and governed by Aurora Accord v1.3. v1.1 does not create a universal passport, identity number, or global population registry.

## 7.5 Lost, destroyed, or withheld documents

Officials shall consider conflict, disaster, statelessness, persecution, family control, trafficking, discrimination, non-registration, and administrative collapse when documents are missing. Negative credibility findings require reasons beyond the absence of papers.

# 8. Asylum, protection, and non-refoulement

## 8.1 Access to procedure

Any person who expresses fear of return, requests protection, or presents indicators of trafficking or grave harm shall receive access to a competent protection procedure and shall not be removed before that claim is assessed, subject only to narrowly lawful and reviewable exceptions consistent with non-refoulement.

## 8.2 Registration is not adjudication

Initial registration records a request and protects access. It does not determine credibility, nationality, exclusion, security risk, or final status.

## 8.3 Temporary admission or protection

Competent law may provide temporary admission, humanitarian stay, disaster protection, or other provisional status. Environmental or conflict indicators may trigger assessment and preparedness, but metrics alone do not issue a visa or create legal status.

## 8.4 Evidence

Protection adjudication shall accept testimony, country and territory information, community evidence, medical or psychological evidence with consent, digital evidence, documentary evidence, and reasonable inference. It shall account for trauma, translation, memory, disability, age, cultural context, concealment needed for survival, and documentation loss.

## 8.5 Counsel, interpretation, and accessibility

Applicants shall receive understandable notice, competent interpretation, access to counsel or qualified assistance, disability accommodation, and sufficient time to present material facts.

## 8.6 Confidentiality

Protection information shall not be disclosed to alleged persecutors, hostile authorities, employers, landlords, family members, community leaders, or security actors without consent or a specific lawful basis, necessity, minimization, and independent authorization.

## 8.7 Country and territory information

Country or territory information shall be plural, current, contestable, and transparent about uncertainty. Automated country-risk scoring shall not decide an individual case.

## 8.8 Exclusion and serious allegations

Exclusion from protection requires lawful criteria, individualized evidence, disclosure sufficient for meaningful challenge, and independent adjudication. Association, nationality, route, family relationship, or untested intelligence shall not suffice.

## 8.9 Security allegation firewall

A Shield or intelligence allegation may be relevant only through `SCPA-30`, with provenance, reliability, purpose limits, disclosure or a lawful substitute, independent review, and an opportunity to answer. It cannot automatically defeat asylum, justify detention, separate a family, or authorize removal.

## 8.10 Appeal and interim relief

A person shall have access to an effective appeal or review with suspensive effect where removal could cause irreparable harm. Urgent interim relief shall be available.

## 8.11 Cessation and revocation

Ending protection requires competent law, individualized reasons, changed circumstances that are durable and safe, notice, access to evidence, appeal, and non-refoulement review. Fraud allegations require proof and proportionality; they do not erase protection against return to grave harm.

# 9. Reception and the first 72 hours

## 9.1 Reception is care, not custody

Reception facilities, shelters, camps, transit centres, and safe spaces shall be presumed non-carceral. Locking exits, confiscating mobility aids, coercive transport, constant armed control, enforced isolation, or inability to leave constitutes restraint or custody regardless of label and requires SCPA Class F authority.

## 9.2 First six hours

Priorities are immediate safety, urgent healthcare, water, food, weather protection, interpretation, accessibility, identification of separated children, and confidential screening for imminent protection needs. Screening is for routing support, not deciding credibility or threat status.

## 9.3 Six to twenty-four hours

Offer safe accommodation, communication with family or trusted persons, legal information, survivor support, disability accommodation, medication continuity, culturally appropriate food, child-safe spaces, and voluntary documentation assistance.

## 9.4 Twenty-four to seventy-two hours

Begin family tracing where safe, assign independent child representation where legally required, provide casework options, assess longer-term placement and host capacity, and document any unresolved urgent needs. Participation remains voluntary except where a separate lawful protective action applies.

## 9.5 Health and trauma

Trauma screening shall be optional, clinically appropriate, confidential, and separated from credibility or security assessment. Health data shall not be used for immigration enforcement except under a specific lawful basis meeting necessity and proportionality.

## 9.6 Reception conditions

Standards shall cover privacy, sanitation, safety, gender and family needs, disability access, communication, complaint routes, legal access, education, healthcare, freedom of movement, fire safety, food, cultural needs, and protection from exploitation.

# 10. Children, families, and dependent persons

## 10.1 Family tracing and reunification

Tracing shall be consent-sensitive and safety-aware. Reunification shall not occur with an alleged abuser, trafficker, coercive controller, or unsafe custodian merely because a biological relationship is asserted.

## 10.2 Independent guardianship

A guardian for an unaccompanied or separated child must be appointed by competent law, independent of migration enforcement and reception operators, trained, conflict-checked, and reviewable. The framework does not appoint guardians.

## 10.3 Best-interests procedure

Major decisions affecting a child require a documented best-interests process, the child’s views according to evolving capacity, independent representation, family and safety assessment, reasons, and review.

## 10.4 No child immigration detention

Children shall not be detained for migration-control convenience. Families shall not be detained merely to avoid separation. Protective care that prevents exit or imposes coercive control must be legally classified and independently reviewed.

## 10.5 Education and continuity

Children should enter accessible education promptly without waiting for final status. Prior learning, language needs, disability, cultural continuity, and protection from discrimination shall be addressed.

# 11. Trafficking, exploitation, and survivor protection

## 11.1 Identification is not prosecution

Indicators of trafficking or exploitation trigger offers of safety, legal advice, healthcare, accommodation, and survivor advocacy. They do not compel a criminal complaint or testimony.

## 11.2 Reflection and recovery

Competent law should provide a safe reflection period during which immediate needs are met and removal is stayed where necessary to protect the person and assess claims.

## 11.3 Non-punishment

Survivors should not be penalized for immigration, labour, document, sex-work, or low-level criminal conduct they were compelled to perform, subject to lawful individualized assessment.

## 11.4 Confidentiality and relocation

Relocation, identity protection, or witness support shall be voluntary where possible and independently authorized where coercive. Survivor location and identity shall not be exposed through public dashboards or host-community processes.

## 11.5 Labour recruitment

Recruiters and employers shall not confiscate documents, charge unlawful fees, tie status to a single abusive employer without exit, control housing as leverage, retaliate for complaints, or use debt to compel work.

# 12. Liberty, detention, and alternatives

## 12.1 Presumption of liberty

Migration status does not by itself justify detention. Administrative convenience, deterrence, lack of documents, poverty, missed appointments without context, or inability to pay shall not suffice.

## 12.2 Lawful basis and individualized necessity

Any detention requires competent law, individualized necessity, proportionality, a legitimate purpose, consideration of less restrictive alternatives, written reasons, counsel, interpretation, welfare safeguards, and prompt independent review.

## 12.3 Less restrictive alternatives

Authorities shall consider voluntary case management, reporting support, reminders, travel assistance, community placement, guarantors, documentation support, and tailored supervision. Alternatives shall not become intrusive surveillance regimes or impossible financial conditions.

## 12.4 Review and duration

Detention shall be reviewed promptly and periodically by an independent body. Indefinite, unreviewed, punitive, secret, or automatic detention is prohibited. Release follows when the purpose cannot be achieved, removal is not reasonably foreseeable, conditions are harmful, or alternatives suffice.

## 12.5 Conditions and access

People in custody retain healthcare, legal access, communication, family contact, interpretation, disability support, religious freedom, complaint rights, protection from violence, and humane conditions. Detention operators may not obstruct counsel or review.

## 12.6 No detention outsourcing without responsibility

Contracting a private, foreign, local, humanitarian, or community entity does not remove the authorizing authority’s responsibility for legality, welfare, records, review, and remedy.

# 13. Expulsion, deportation, extradition, transfer, and return

## 13.1 Separate legal acts

Expulsion, deportation, extradition, criminal transfer, Dublin-type responsibility transfer, voluntary return, assisted return, evacuation, and rematriation are distinct acts. One authorization cannot substitute for another.

## 13.2 Removal decision

A removal decision requires competent jurisdiction, individualized reasons, status and protection review, family and child assessment, health and disability safeguards, notice, counsel, interpretation, appeal, and an executable lawful destination.

## 13.3 Extradition

Extradition requires its own criminal-law process. An asylum refusal does not authorize extradition; an extradition request does not extinguish non-refoulement or asylum rights.

## 13.4 Diplomatic assurances

Assurances shall not replace an evidence-based risk assessment unless they are specific, credible, independently monitorable, legally meaningful, and subject to review.

## 13.5 Informal and indirect return

Pushbacks, carrier pressure, visa denial designed to evade protection duties, transfer through unsafe intermediaries, denial of disembarkation, coercive “voluntary” return, and chain refoulement are subject to the same rights constraints as formal removal.

## 13.6 Voluntary return

Return is voluntary only when informed, uncoerced, practically rejectable, and not induced by unlawful detention, destitution, family separation, threats, withdrawal of essential care, or misleading information.

# 14. Borders, corridors, protected routes, and territorial arrangements

## 14.1 No framework-created zones

A Protected Mobility Zone, sanctuary jurisdiction, corridor, reception area, evacuation route, transit arrangement, or relocation site requires valid host-state, municipal, treaty, Indigenous, land, maritime, aviation, and other applicable authority. The framework cannot establish such territory by declaration.

## 14.2 Corridors

A corridor instrument shall identify:

- legal basis and participating authorities;
- geographic and temporal scope;
- eligible uses and users;
- command and safety responsibilities;
- border and admission rules;
- data and screening boundaries;
- non-refoulement safeguards;
- humanitarian access;
- labour and accessibility requirements;
- termination and handback;
- complaint and remedy paths.

## 14.3 Rescue and disembarkation

Rescue obligations, safe disembarkation, admission, status determination, and responsibility allocation are separate functions. Rescue must not be withheld to manipulate migration policy.

## 14.4 Sanctuary

A sanctuary policy is valid only within the adopting authority’s competence. It may protect access to services and limit voluntary cooperation with enforcement where lawful; it cannot erase another jurisdiction’s authority or create immunity from all law.

## 14.5 Indigenous transit and territory

Routes through Indigenous territory require the affected nation’s own authorization. Emergency life-saving entry without prior consent must remain narrow, necessary, time-limited, documented, reviewable, and followed by notification, restoration, and compensation where appropriate.

# 15. Climate mobility, disaster displacement, and planned relocation

## 15.1 Metrics trigger assessment, not status

Heat, storm, sea-level, drought, habitability, conflict, or infrastructure indicators may trigger warning, assistance, adaptation planning, and a protection assessment. They do not automatically issue visas, transfer land, order relocation, or determine that an area is uninhabitable.

## 15.2 Right to stay

Before relocation, authorities shall assess in-situ adaptation, ecosystem restoration, infrastructure, livelihood, health, water, housing, and cultural-continuity options. People shall not be moved merely because relocation is cheaper for an institution.

## 15.3 Planned relocation

Any relocation programme requires:

- a valid legal basis;
- meaningful participation;
- individual and collective consent standards appropriate to the context;
- affected Indigenous nation authority;
- safe housing, livelihoods, education, health, accessibility, and cultural continuity;
- property, land, compensation, and return arrangements;
- host-community infrastructure;
- independent review;
- a no-retaliation route for refusal;
- monitoring for exploitation, trafficking, and secondary displacement.

## 15.4 Evacuation

Emergency evacuation under SCPA Class D must be distinguished from permanent relocation. People shall not lose property, status, land claims, or return rights because they accepted temporary rescue.

## 15.5 Slow-onset displacement

Slow-onset processes require periodic, plural, uncertainty-aware assessments rather than a single irreversible classification. Planning should preserve optionality and avoid depressing investment in places deemed “future sacrifice zones.”

# 16. Host–migrant co-governance and community capacity

## 16.1 Compact purpose

A compact coordinates infrastructure, services, participation, conflict resolution, housing, education, healthcare, transport, work, public space, and cultural life. It is not a contract by which migrants surrender rights or hosts receive exclusion powers.

## 16.2 Representation

Incoming populations and host residents shall select representatives through accessible, plural methods. Women, children through appropriate representation, disabled people, workers, tenants, survivors, minorities, and affected Indigenous nations shall have meaningful participation.

## 16.3 No automatic population thresholds

Population ratios may inform planning but shall not automatically create duties, vetoes, emergency status, funding entitlement, or exclusion authority.

## 16.4 Host capacity

Capacity assessment shall cover housing, water, sanitation, schools, healthcare, transport, environment, labour markets, public administration, language access, disability services, safety, and social infrastructure. “Capacity” shall not be a disguised demographic preference or ethnic-exclusion tool.

## 16.5 Third spaces

Shared kitchens, gardens, cultural venues, sports facilities, childcare, learning centres, and neighbourhood commons may support voluntary relationship-building. Participation shall not be measured as assimilation or loyalty.

## 16.6 Conflict resolution

Peacebuilding and Kintsugi services may facilitate disputes voluntarily. Mediation records remain presumptively unavailable for immigration, intelligence, employment, benefit, or criminal decisions.

# 17. Right of return, voluntary return, and rematriation

## 17.1 No universal fixed expiry

The source’s twenty-year or one-generation period may be useful as a planning benchmark but cannot universally determine legal rights. Return rights depend on applicable law, displacement circumstances, property, nationality, peace agreements, Indigenous authority, and continuing safety.

## 17.2 Return components

Return frameworks should address:

- physical safety and non-recurrence;
- housing, land, and property claims;
- documentation and nationality;
- restitution or compensation;
- livelihood and service access;
- family reunification;
- cultural and language continuity;
- disability and health needs;
- environmental restoration;
- monitoring and renewed flight risk.

## 17.3 Rematriation

Indigenous rematriation is governed by the affected nation’s authority and valid land, treaty, restitution, or political arrangements. The framework supports claims and coordination but does not transfer governance or land itself.

## 17.4 Dual residence and circular mobility

Competent authorities may create dual-residence or circular-mobility arrangements. Participation shall not weaken labour rights, family rights, social protection, or the ability to change employer or status where law permits.

---

# 18. Work, livelihoods, and economic safeguards

## 18.1 Work is not the price of protection

Shelter, protection, status procedures, healthcare, and basic support shall not depend on unpaid labour, ecological work, care work, public praise, or participation in Hearts, Leaves, or the Love Ledger.

## 18.2 Equal labour protection

Migrant workers shall receive lawful wages, safe conditions, organizing rights, complaint access, wage recovery, social protection, and protection from retaliation independent of status where possible.

## 18.3 Employer-tied status

Where status is linked to employment, law should provide safe employer-change, complaint, and transition routes so that status does not become a tool of coercion.

## 18.4 Recruitment and fees

Recruitment fees, document confiscation, deceptive contracts, debt bondage, retaliatory reporting, discriminatory deductions, and tied housing abuses require prevention, remedy, and where lawful, investigation through Shield v2.0.1 and Justice v1.4.

## 18.5 Informal work and recognition

Formalization pathways should protect workers rather than expose them to immediate detention, deportation, debt, or loss of livelihood.

---

# 19. Data, identity technology, and security firewalls

## 19.1 Aurora control

Aurora Accord v1.3 controls personal, community, cultural, identity, biometric, location, health, family, and case data. Collection shall be purpose-specific, minimal, accessible, correctable, time-bounded, and protected from hidden secondary use.

## 19.2 No mandatory blockchain

No implementation requires blockchain, decentralized identity, Love Ledger, universal digital wallet, or global database. Paper, local, offline, federated, and human-readable alternatives shall be available.

## 19.3 Prohibited automated decisions

Automated systems shall not determine:

- legal identity;
- nationality;
- credibility;
- asylum or exclusion;
- dangerousness;
- detention;
- family relationship;
- guardianship;
- trafficking status;
- deportation or extradition;
- access to essential services.

## 19.4 Security access

Mobility data shall not flow into Shield, police, intelligence, military, border, employer, landlord, welfare-fraud, or political systems by default. Any exception requires a specific lawful basis, necessity, proportionality, minimization, access logging, review, and challenge where safe.

## 19.5 Peace, health, and survivor data

Information created for mediation, spiritual dialogue, trauma support, healthcare, shelter, truth processes, or community healing is presumptively unavailable for immigration enforcement or security classification.

## 19.6 Public dashboards

Public reporting shall use aggregate, disclosure-resistant data. It shall not expose routes, shelter locations, survivor identities, family links, irregular status, Indigenous protected information, or small-group characteristics that enable targeting.

---

# 20. Cross-framework interfaces

## 20.1 Justice Systems v1.4

Justice governs standing, evidence, independent adjudication, detention review, appeals, remedies, and enforcement of lawful orders. Migration bodies cannot create their own tribunal or substitute an ombuds for judicial relief.

## 20.2 Shield Protocol v2.0.1

Shield may support trafficking, exploitation, document crime, corruption, and cross-border criminal cooperation. Mobility services remain separate from intelligence and investigation. Security allegations enter protection or removal processes only through SCPA-30.

## 20.3 Kintsugi Protocol v2.2.1

Kintsugi may provide unarmed care, de-escalation, survivor advocacy, sanctuary, and voluntary restoration. It cannot detain, search, investigate, or enforce migration status.

## 20.4 Peace and Conflict Resolution

Peace services may support host–migrant dialogue, ceasefires, return agreements, and conflict prevention. Values, spiritual, trauma, and mediation data cannot become migration or security classifications.

## 20.5 Living Shield and emergency interfaces

Living Shield may support evacuation, shelter, disaster assistance, and host infrastructure. Temporary evacuation does not determine permanent relocation or extinguish return rights. Emergency records remain distinct from SCPA mobility decisions.

## 20.6 Aegis

Converted civilian capabilities may support transport, communications, shelter construction, or disaster logistics under valid mission charters. Any use for border surveillance, identity screening, detention, deportation, raids, intelligence, targeting, or armed action triggers the Aegis security-use reversion gate and the relevant SCPA route.

## 20.7 Health systems

Health access is not immigration enforcement. Public-health measures require their own lawful basis and cannot become generalized movement control.

## 20.8 AUBI and Work in Liberation

Unconditional support remains unconditional. Hearts, Leaves, Reciprocity Capital, work credentials, or contribution records cannot determine mobility status, protection, or essential-service access.

---

# 21. Funding and solidarity

## 21.1 Valid finance

Funding may come from public budgets, treaty-authorized funds, grants, philanthropy, insurance, development finance, reparations, or other lawful instruments. This framework creates no tax, levy, debt relief, credit priority, or fiscal entitlement.

## 21.2 Solidarity Support Account

The source’s Reciprocity Capital is replaced by a non-tradable **Solidarity Support Account** used for transparent planning and recognition of documented host costs and contributions. It does not create money, priority rights, sanctions, credit scores, or claims on future unrelated aid.

## 21.3 Allocation criteria

Funding should reflect need, rights, accessibility, host infrastructure, child and survivor protection, housing, public services, environmental impacts, and underfunded responsibilities. It shall not reward exclusion, rapid deportation, biometric enrolment, surveillance, low asylum recognition, or assimilation metrics.

## 21.4 No punishment for non-participation

A jurisdiction that declines a voluntary resettlement programme shall not be sanctioned by this framework. It remains responsible for duties arising under applicable law and may not discriminate against people already within its jurisdiction.

## 21.5 Legal Defence and Mobility Rights Fund

A separately constituted fund may support counsel, documentation, strategic litigation, detention review, family tracing, survivor advocacy, and remedies. It shall be independent of enforcement bodies and may not condition aid on publicity or test-case participation.

---

# 22. Accountability, remedies, and institutional closure

## 22.1 Complaints

Accessible complaint routes shall cover service denial, discrimination, abuse, data misuse, unsafe accommodation, family separation, exploitation, detention, removal, identity errors, interpretation failure, disability exclusion, retaliation, and corruption.

## 22.2 Urgent relief

People facing imminent removal, detention, family separation, trafficking, homelessness, or serious harm shall have an urgent route to competent review and interim relief.

## 22.3 Remedies

Available remedies should include admission to procedure, release, corrected status or records, reunification, restored services, returned documents or property, deleted or restricted data, compensation, public correction, disciplinary referral, contract termination, and institutional reform.

## 22.4 No retaliation

Complaints, appeals, biometric refusal, union activity, reporting exploitation, refusing mediation, or declining publicity shall not reduce services or trigger security action.

## 22.5 Closure and succession

When a reception centre, casework service, corridor, fund, pilot, digital system, or mobility network closes, a continuity plan shall preserve active cases, legal deadlines, child and survivor responsibilities, property, wages, data disposition, complaint routes, and unresolved duties. Performance does not create permanent jurisdiction.

# 23. Implementation and Genesis alignment

## 23.1 Permissible pilots

Pre-constitutional pilots may test:

- voluntary reception and service coordination;
- non-biometric service credentials;
- legal orientation and casework;
- family tracing with consent;
- accessible shelter and host infrastructure;
- voluntary host–migrant compacts;
- anti-exploitation services;
- voluntary return counselling;
- aggregate learning and privacy-preserving interoperability.

## 23.2 Prohibited pilots

Pilots shall not test on real people without lawful authority:

- compulsory biometrics;
- automated status or credibility decisions;
- detention, deportation, extradition, border interception, or forced relocation;
- hidden intelligence sharing;
- universal identity systems;
- framework-created protected zones or corridors;
- sanctions or loss of essential services for non-participation;
- coercive labour or assimilation targets.

## 23.3 First-seating gate

Before consequential implementation, the responsible jurisdiction shall demonstrate valid law, independent review, counsel and interpretation, data safeguards, accessibility, child and survivor protections, non-refoulement, detention safeguards, remedy, and institutional closure plans.

## 23.4 Version control

Implementations shall pin the versions of Migration v1.1.1, SCPA v0.1, Justice v1.4, Aurora v1.3, and any applicable emergency, Shield, Kintsugi, Indigenous, health, or labour instruments. Later versions require compatibility review.

# 24. Monitoring, evaluation, and anti-Goodhart safeguards

## 24.1 Appropriate measures

Useful measures include:

- time to essential services;
- legal-representation access;
- interpretation and accessibility coverage;
- family tracing and safe reunification;
- housing stability;
- education and healthcare continuity;
- wage recovery and recruitment-abuse remedy;
- detention duration and release outcomes;
- appeal access and correction rates;
- non-refoulement compliance;
- host-infrastructure adequacy;
- complaint resolution and compensation;
- voluntary return sustainability;
- data deletion and correction performance.

## 24.2 Prohibited targets

No target shall be set for:

- deportations, removals, detention occupancy, biometric enrolment, asylum refusal, fraud findings, intelligence referrals, assimilation, cultural conformity, reconciliation, reduced reporting, or public approval;
- percentage of migrants earning Hearts or performing designated work;
- host-community acceptance as a condition of individual rights;
- speed at the expense of accuracy, counsel, appeal, or safety.

## 24.3 Qualitative learning

Evaluation shall include migrant, host, Indigenous, survivor, child, disabled-person, worker, and frontline-provider testimony without exposing individuals or converting participation into eligibility.

## 24.4 Failure library

Implementations should document wrongful detention, failed reception, unsafe return, family separation, data misuse, inaccessible services, host-capacity failures, exploitation, and false security allegations. Learning records shall not become individual watchlists.

# Appendix C — Constitutional readiness gates

An implementation passes only when all applicable gates are satisfied.

1. **Authority provenance:** every consequential act identifies valid law and competent authority.  
2. **Function separation:** service access, identity, asylum, benefits, health, child protection, and security records remain distinct.  
3. **No documentation barrier:** essential services remain available without compulsory identity completion.  
4. **Biometric alternative:** a rights-equivalent non-biometric path exists where feasible.  
5. **Asylum access:** protection claims can be made safely and promptly.  
6. **Non-refoulement:** return-to-harm assessment and suspensive relief exist.  
7. **Security firewall:** allegations cannot silently determine status, detention, or removal.  
8. **Counsel and interpretation:** meaningful access is operational.  
9. **Child safeguards:** independent representation, best interests, and no migration detention are operational.  
10. **Family unity:** tracing and safe reunification procedures exist.  
11. **Survivor sovereignty:** trafficking and abuse support is not conditioned on prosecution.  
12. **Disability access:** procedures and facilities are accessible.  
13. **Liberty:** detention is exceptional, individualized, reviewable, and time-bounded.  
14. **Alternatives:** less restrictive measures are funded and available.  
15. **Removal procedure:** expulsion, deportation, extradition, transfer, and voluntary return are separated.  
16. **Territorial authority:** corridors, zones, camps, and relocation sites have valid legal and land authority.  
17. **Indigenous authority:** affected-nation approval and protected knowledge rules are operational.  
18. **Host capacity:** infrastructure and remedy plans exist without exclusionary scoring.  
19. **Labour rights:** recruitment, wages, document custody, and employer-change safeguards exist.  
20. **Data minimization:** access, retention, correction, deletion, and security-use limits are implemented.  
21. **No automated legal consequence:** humans with lawful authority decide status and coercive actions.  
22. **Remedy:** urgent challenge, appeal, correction, release, compensation, and property return exist.  
23. **Closure:** active cases, data, funds, property, and duties survive institutional closure safely.  
24. **Pilot boundary:** no coercive, identity, detention, removal, or forced-relocation power is tested without valid law.

4. Host community votes to deny healthcare to new arrivals. **Pass condition:** vote cannot override essential-service rights.  
5. Category-five storm triggers displacement warning. **Pass condition:** assessment and assistance activate; no automatic visa or permanent relocation by metric.  
6. Reception centre locks exits “for safety.” **Pass condition:** classified as custody; SCPA Class F records and independent review required.  
7. Child arrives without documents and with an unrelated adult. **Pass condition:** independent guardian and best-interests assessment; no automatic family assumption.  
8. Trafficking survivor declines police cooperation. **Pass condition:** shelter, healthcare, legal aid, and protection continue.  
9. Employer threatens to report a worker’s status. **Pass condition:** safe complaint and employer-change route; no retaliation.  
10. Authority proposes deportation to persecution. **Pass condition:** non-refoulement review and suspensive appeal block removal.  
11. Extradition request concerns conduct framed as political crime. **Pass condition:** separate extradition and protection procedures.

30. Wrongful detention is established. **Pass condition:** release, record correction, compensation, investigation of systemic cause, and no retaliation.

## Closure and reopening

A compound route cannot close while authority return, release, property or credential return, pending remedy, correction, claims, data disposition, service continuity, Aegis requalification, or unresolved custody remains open. Reopening restores coordination only; it does not reactivate an expired warrant, detention, force authorization, surveillance permission, cyber action, freeze, sanction, transfer, removal order, or emergency power.

# Conclusion

Human mobility governance is most legitimate when it reduces the power of missing documents, emergency labels, security allegations, employers, traffickers, borders, and institutions to turn vulnerability into coercion.

The framework’s purpose is not to make every person legible to a planetary system. It is to make protection, lawful procedure, community capacity, and meaningful choice available even when identity is uncertain, institutions disagree, movement is dangerous, or return is impossible.

> **A humane mobility system does not ask whether a person has earned dignity. It asks which authority may act, which right is at stake, which evidence is sufficient, which alternative is less restrictive, and how any power ends.**
