## **The Protocol: Six Phases of Wise Decision-Making**

### **Phase 0: Epistemic Health Assessment (The Ground)**

*Goal: Ensure the information ecosystem can support wise decision-making before the process begins.*

No decision-making process, however well-designed, can produce wise outcomes if the information it operates on is corrupted. Phase 0 assesses whether the epistemic conditions for a legitimate WDIP process exist, and initiates repair if they do not.

**1. Epistemic Baseline Assessment**

Before issuing The Invitation (Phase 1), the Meta-Governance Council commissions an Epistemic Baseline Assessment from the Global Council for Epistemic Integrity (GCEI), evaluating:

| **Dimension** | **Assessment Question** | **Threshold** |
|---------------|------------------------|---------------|
| Information quality | Is the factual basis supported by evidence meeting Synoptic Protocol standards? | Key factual claims verified by at least two independent sources meeting PEI standards |
| Epistemic diversity | Are multiple knowledge systems (scientific, Indigenous, experiential) represented? | No single epistemological tradition accounts for more than 60% of available evidence base |
| Disinformation exposure | Has the topic been subject to coordinated disinformation or systematic reality distortion? | If yes: mandatory Disinformation Impact Assessment |
| Stakeholder epistemic access | Do affected communities have access to high-quality information in their languages and communication modes? | Key materials available in languages covering 80% of affected populations |
| Trust level | Do affected communities trust the information sources and WDIP process sufficiently? | If low trust identified: mandatory Trust Building phase |

**2. Disinformation Impact Assessment**

For decisions where the Epistemic Baseline identifies active or recent disinformation exposure:
- Map specific disinformation narratives, sources, reach, and effects on public understanding
- Assess whether disinformation has created false consensus, manufactured controversy, or polarized stakeholders beyond the WDIP's normal capacity to bridge
- Identify which stakeholder groups have been most affected and what epistemic repair they need
- Design targeted countermeasures in coordination with the Synoptic Protocol's Rapid Response Teams

The assessment is published as part of the WDIP process record.

**3. Epistemic Remediation**

If the Baseline Assessment identifies conditions below threshold, the WDIP process pauses and initiates remediation:
- **Information gaps:** Commission targeted research, translation, or knowledge-gathering via the Global Knowledge Stewardship Council
- **Disinformation contamination:** Deploy Synoptic Protocol Rapid Response resources; conduct community-level epistemic repair through Public Epistemic Institutions and Bridging Dialogues
- **Access barriers:** Fund translation, distribution, and community engagement through Global Commons Fund
- **Trust deficit:** Initiate the Trust Building & Relational Repair Phase before proceeding

Remediation has no fixed timeline — it continues until the GCEI certifies conditions meet threshold. However, the Existential Risk Module's Time Pressure Protocols may override this pause for decisions where delay itself constitutes an existential risk. In such cases, the WDIP proceeds with explicit acknowledgment that epistemic conditions are suboptimal, and the resulting decision carries a mandatory accelerated review cycle (one year instead of the standard seven).

**4. Provenance Requirements**

All information entering the WDIP process at any phase must meet minimum provenance standards:
- Source identified and disclosed (author, institution, methodology)
- Funding and potential conflicts of interest declared
- Verifiable through at least one independent pathway
- Indigenous and traditional knowledge governed by FPIC 2.0 — source communities control how their knowledge is presented and used

Information that does not meet provenance standards may be noted in the process record but may not form the basis for pathway design (Phase 3) or principle-based assessment (Phase 4).

**Integration:** Phase 0 is conducted by the GCEI in coordination with the Meta-Governance Council. It interfaces with the Synoptic Protocol's Decision-Making Under Uncertainty Protocol. Findings are stored in the WDIP Living Archive. For WDIP-Lite (lower-stakes decisions), Phase 0 is abbreviated to a facilitator-conducted epistemic checklist.

### **Phase 1: The Invitation & Framing (The Question)**

*Goal: Define the true question being asked.*
1. **Pre-Decision Legitimacy Audit:** An independent team from the `Meta-Governance Council` assesses public trust in WDIP, initiating a **Trust Building & Relational Repair Phase** if needed, using culturally resonant dialogues and transparency.
2. **Issue Triage:** `Meta-Governance Council` identifies issues requiring WDIP (e.g., AI governance, resource conflicts).
3. **System Mapping:** `Global Institutional AI Assistant` maps affected systems, stakeholders, and feedback loops, using AI-assisted pattern recognition to identify historical parallels.
4. **Seven-Generation Framing:** `Global Council for the Future` and `Youth Councils` reframe the issue for 200-year impacts.
5. **The Invitation:** Public invitation issued, translated into major languages, culturally adapted, and shared via `Global Meaning Infrastructure`, stating the question, process, and principles.

### **Phase 2: Deep Inquiry & Sensemaking (The Listening)**

*Goal: Gather and honor diverse knowledge without premature judgment.*
1. **Scientific & Technical Synthesis:** `Global Knowledge Stewardship Council` compiles scientific data, noting consensus and uncertainty, integrated with the **Indigenous Framework's FPIC 2.0 protocols**.
2. **Indigenous & Spiritual Council:** `Earth Councils` and `Global Council of Civilizations` share traditional knowledge and prophecies via `Indigenous AI Oracles`.
3. **Lived Experience Testimony:** `Citizen Deliberative Councils` and `Digital Deliberation Platforms` collect community experiences, supported by **AUBI** to ensure participation without economic coercion.
4. **Non-Human Consultation:** Representatives for ecosystems, guided by the **Moral Operating System's rights spectrum**, provide testimony per *Rights of Nature* principles.
5. **Process Observers:** Independent monitors ensure authentic engagement, flagging power imbalances via **Anonymous Input Channels**.

### **Phase 3: Synthesis & Possibility (The Weaving)**

*Goal: Generate integrated, life-affirming pathways.*
1. **Integrative Resistance & Resonance (IRR) Framework:**
   - **Resonance Mapping:** `Transdisciplinary Foresight Labs` identify alignment using Bayesian integration and narrative synthesis.
   - **Resistance Analysis:** Analyze contradictions by type:
     - *Ontological* (e.g., "river as resource" vs. "river as ancestor"): Honor both worldviews.
     - *Temporal* (e.g., immediate jobs vs. long-term forest): Bridge timescales.
     - *Ethical* (e.g., genetic modification): Apply Precautionary Wisdom.
   - **Pathway Design:** Generate 3-5 pathways minimizing stakeholder violations of core principles.
2. **Scenario Modeling:** `AI-Mediated Impact Simulation Tools` model long-term consequences, with:
   - **AI Bias Audits:** Regular checks ensure neutrality.
   - **Digital Sovereignty:** Indigenous knowledge protected via community-controlled data protocols.
   - **Transparency & Overrides:** Algorithmic recommendations documented; traditional knowledge prioritized in conflicts.
   - **Cascade Impact Modeling:** Assess second- and third-order effects on non-participating regions.
3. **Option Generation:** Labs produce 3-5 distinct, holistic pathways with implementation, monitoring, and adaptation plans.
4. **Worldview Translation Protocols:** Facilitators use perspective-taking exercises and **Sacred Pause Mechanisms** to navigate worldview differences.

### **Phase 4: Deliberation & Choice (The Turning Point)**

*Goal: Make a collective choice with full awareness.*
1. **Council Deliberation:** `Meta-Governance Council`, `UN Parliamentary Assembly`, and `Youth Councils` deliberate pathways, with **Rotating Facilitation** from a pre-vetted, cross-cultural pool.
2. **Principle-Based Assessment:** Pathways assessed against Core Principles, requiring justification for violations.
3. **Consensus Seeking:** Cultural mediators facilitate dialogue to minimize integrative resistance, using real-time sentiment tracking for transparency.
4. **Sacred Pause Mechanisms:** Triggered by escalation (e.g., dialogue breakdown), include rituals/ceremonies led by `Wisdom Keepers Circle` to restore relationships.
5. **Failure Modes & Circuit Breakers:**
   - **Deadlock Breakers:** If consensus fails, a 2/3 supermajority vote (with `Youth Council` consent, no `Indigenous Council` objection) decides.
   - **Time Pressure Protocols:** Abbreviated processes for urgent decisions, with mandatory post-decision reviews.
   - **Dissent Documentation:** Minority reports record dissenting views for future reference.
6. **The Choice:** Decisions documented transparently, including dissents and uncertainties, verified via **Blockchain-Verified Consensus** (optional for planetary decisions).

### **Phase 5: Regenerative Embodiment & Learning**

*Goal: Implement as a regenerative, learning experiment.*
1. **Regenerative Implementation:** Pathways implemented to improve ecological and social health, tracked via **Regenerative Metrics** (e.g., biodiversity, community cohesion).
2. **Reflexivity Engine:** Real-time monitoring tracks impacts, supported by AI pattern recognition across historical decisions.
3. **Regenerative Failure & Learning Protocol:** If decisions cause harm:
   - **Harm Acknowledgment Rituals:** Co-designed with `Global Council of Civilizations`.
   - **Reparations Mechanisms:** Funded via `Payment for Global Public Goods Fund`.
   - **Process Refinement:** Lessons amend WDIP, stored in the **WDIP Living Archive**.
4. **Learning Loops & WDIP Living Archive:** Annual and seven-year reviews allow adaptation or reversal. A queryable archive stores facilitator notes, video testimonies, and insights, warning of past failures.
5. **Wisdom Succession Planning:** **Mentorship Protocols** pair experienced participants with newcomers; archive ensures cultural continuity.
6. **Narrative Translation:** `Global Meaning Infrastructure` team creates stories, art, and metaphors to build trust.

## **Democratic Interface Protocol**

The WDIP operates within democratic systems, not above them. Its authority is epistemic and moral — the quality of its process and the wisdom of its outcomes — not coercive. Democratic bodies provide the legitimation that WDIP cannot generate on its own: electoral accountability, popular mandate, and the right of peoples to govern themselves.

### The Inform-Don't-Replace Principle

WDIP provides wisdom; democratic bodies provide legitimation. Neither supersedes the other:
- WDIP recommendations are formally transmitted to relevant governance bodies (BAZ councils, national governments, UN Parliamentary Assembly) as advisory inputs, not binding directives
- Democratic bodies are free to accept, modify, or reject WDIP recommendations through their own deliberative processes
- WDIP may not issue directives, impose sanctions, or take enforcement action against democratic bodies that reject its recommendations
- The Treaty for Our Only Home provides the legal bridge: WDIP recommendations engaging Treaty obligations trigger fast-tracked review by the UN Parliamentary Assembly, but the Assembly retains full deliberative authority

If WDIP recommendations are consistently rejected, the appropriate response is to improve the process, not to override democracy.

### The Justification Threshold

When governance bodies reject WDIP recommendations, they must provide substantive public justification — not a veto on rejection, but a requirement for democratic accountability:

| **Level** | **Standard** | **Applies When** |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Acknowledgment | Governance body publicly acknowledges the recommendation and states it has been considered | All WDIP recommendations |
| Reasoned Engagement | Written response engaging with WDIP's specific reasoning, evidence, and analysis — addressing the substance, not merely stating disagreement | Recommendations involving Treaty obligations, cross-boundary impacts, or intergenerational stakes |
| Counter-Evidence | Alternative evidence or analysis supporting the governance body's position, subject to the same epistemic standards (Synoptic Protocol) that governed the WDIP process | Recommendations where the governance body claims the WDIP's factual or analytical basis is flawed |

Justifications are published in the WDIP Living Archive alongside the original recommendation. Failure to meet the applicable threshold does not trigger sanctions — it triggers public disclosure.

### The Citizen Review Mechanism

When a governance body consistently rejects WDIP recommendations (three or more rejections within five years on related issues), citizens within that jurisdiction may petition for an independent review:
- **Petition threshold:** 5% of eligible voters or 10,000 signatures (whichever is lower)
- **Review body:** Independent panel composed via sortition from the affected jurisdiction, supported by WDIP facilitators and Synoptic Protocol epistemic resources
- **Scope:** Examines whether rejections were made in good faith (genuine policy disagreement supported by reasoning) or represent capture, systemic avoidance, or failure to engage with the substance
- **Outcome:** Panel publishes findings. Findings are advisory — they do not override democratic decisions but create a formal public record
- **Safeguard:** The review process itself is subject to Anti-Capture Module protections

The Citizen Review is a transparency mechanism, not an enforcement mechanism.

### The Shadow Government Safeguard

WDIP must never become a parallel authority that bypasses democratic accountability. Explicit prohibitions:
- WDIP may not maintain standing executive capacity (staff are facilitators and analysts, not administrators with policy authority)
- WDIP may not directly allocate resources except for its own operational costs and reparations from the Regenerative Failure protocol
- WDIP may not establish enforcement mechanisms independent of democratic governance (all enforcement flows through the Treaty's Digital Justice Tribunal and participating governance bodies)
- WDIP participants may not simultaneously hold elected office in governance bodies that receive WDIP recommendations (two-year cooling-off period)
- Any proposal to expand WDIP's authority beyond advisory capacity requires unanimous consent of the Meta-Governance Council, the Earth Council, and the Youth Caucus, with a mandatory five-year sunset clause

If WDIP begins to function as a shadow government, this constitutes a framework-level failure documented in the WDIP Living Archive and addressed through the Regenerative Failure & Learning Protocol.

### Sovereignty Interface

- **Opt-out mechanisms** respect national and BAZ-level sovereignty while maintaining global coherence: governance bodies may decline to participate in specific WDIP processes without penalty, but participation in the GGF ecosystem implies acceptance of the Justification Threshold for recommendations engaging Treaty obligations
- **Graduated engagement:** Governance bodies may participate at different levels — from observer status (receiving recommendations without justification obligation) to full participation (co-deliberating and accepting the full Democratic Interface Protocol)
- **Non-participating regions:** For decisions affecting regions outside the GGF ecosystem, the Planetary Scale Module's Cascade Impact Modeling assesses effects, and the Sovereignty Interface Protocols ensure those regions are informed and their concerns documented

## **Funding Transparency & Anti-Capture Protocol**

WDIP operational costs, participant support, and implementation resources are governed by the following principles:

### Multi-Source Funding

No single source may provide more than 25% of WDIP operational funding for any given process. Funding must be drawn from at least three of the following categories:
- Global Commons Fund (primary source for GGF-integrated processes)
- Participating governance bodies (national governments, BAZ councils)
- Philanthropic contributions (foundations, individual donors)
- Community-controlled crowdfunding (via Hearts/Leaves economy or conventional mechanisms)
- In-kind contributions (facilitator time, venue provision, translation services) valued at auditable rates

For WDIP-Lite processes with smaller budgets, the cap is relaxed to 40% but transparency requirements still apply.

### Public Disclosure

All funding sources and amounts are published in the WDIP process record before Phase 1 begins:
- Funder identity, amount, and any conditions attached
- Funder's relationship to the decision topic
- Any prior WDIP processes the funder has supported

Process participants receive this disclosure as part of onboarding materials. The information is accessible through the WDIP Living Archive.

### The No-Strings Principle

Funding is unconditional. Funders may not:
- Specify, suggest, or influence which pathways are generated (Phase 3)
- Participate in Deliberation (Phase 4) by virtue of their funding (only if they qualify as stakeholders on independent grounds)
- Receive privileged access to process deliberations, draft recommendations, or internal communications
- Attach reporting requirements beyond the publicly available process record

### The Poison Pill Clause

If any funder attempts to influence WDIP outcomes — through direct pressure, conditional funding threats, or covert lobbying:
- The attempt is documented by Process Observers or the Integrity & Anti-Capture Module
- The funder's contribution is returned in full
- The funder is publicly named in the WDIP Living Archive
- The funder is prohibited from supporting future WDIP processes for ten years
- If the attempt occurred during an active process, the process pauses for an integrity review

Enforced by the Meta-Governance Council with appeal to the Digital Justice Tribunal.

### Participant Support Independence

AUBI funding for under-resourced participants (per the Marginalized Impact Module) is administered through a separate channel from operational funding:
- Participant stipends, travel, childcare, and accessibility support are disbursed through the AUBI system, not through the WDIP operational budget
- Participants' economic support is not contingent on the WDIP process continuing or producing particular outcomes
- Participants may withdraw at any time without losing support for the remainder of the committed period
