# Implementation Methods & Tools Framework v0.9
## *An Accessible, Forkable, Low-Tech-First, Causally Adequate, and Constitutionally Bounded Governance Tool Commons*

**Tier:** 4 — Implementation methods and practical tools  
**Status:** Harmonized successor to v0.8.1

## Constitutional doctrine

> **A tool is not authority. A resource card is not a licence. A recommendation is not a classification. Compatibility is not legitimacy. Facilitation is not representation. A rating is not legitimacy. A pause is not adjudication. A timeout is not transferred authority. Community refusal is not jurisdiction over others. A harm report is not a finding. Certification is not a monopoly. A failure story is not public property. An emergency tool is not emergency power. Conformance is not success.**

The framework makes governance methods discoverable, adaptable, accessible, forkable, and learnable. It does not make their use lawful merely by publishing, ranking, validating, funding, certifying, or technically enabling them.

# 1. Purpose, role, and boundaries

## 1.1 What this framework owns

The framework owns the design and stewardship of a practical tool commons:

- tool discovery and plural advisory recommendations;
- resource cards and authority-effect warnings;
- consequence-profile and protected-domain warnings;
- causal-responsibility and burden maps for tool use;
- low-tech, oral, paper, SMS, assisted, and digital equivalents;
- accessibility and participation guidance;
- cultural adaptation methods and local-fork standing;
- tool forking, attribution, compatibility, versioning, deprecation, migration, and material exit;
- facilitator and support-team accountability;
- causally adequate pilot templates and monitoring methods;
- harm, omission, remedy, and closure tools;
- protected learning artifacts, Failure Library, and Story Bank practices;
- framework falsification and conformance testing.

## 1.2 What it does not own

It does not itself create a council, BAZ, charter, public office, tribunal, affected-nation representative, emergency mission, command, public budget, funding entitlement, permit, licence, inspection, investigation, adjudication, sanction, benefit decision, coercive power, or ordinary implementation authority.

## 1.3 Permission rule

No permission is needed to read, copy, translate, simulate, critique, or lawfully fork an openly licensed tool, or to use it for `CP0` learning and lawful `CP1` voluntary internal practice.

Separate authority is required when use affects rights, non-participants, public resources, institutional powers, essential services, protected domains, or legally consequential decisions.

# 3. Guiding principles

1. **Low-tech first; equal dignity throughout.** Digital and non-digital paths retain equal substantive standing, correction, and remedy.
2. **Causal subsidiarity within actual authority.** Tool use should sit with the least-authoritative actor that possesses lawful jurisdiction, practical control, capacity, resources, and accountability.
3. **Affected standing is not assigned responsibility.** Those bearing harm gain voice, consent, challenge, and remedy rights; they do not thereby become responsible for controlling upstream causes.
4. **Cultural plurality through self-description.** External systems may not assign cultural identity, maturity, or legitimacy for consequential routing.
5. **Affected-nation authority.** The specific affected Indigenous nation controls its representatives, consent routes, protected knowledge, and territorial or relational authority.
6. **Modularity without authority aggregation.** A stack of advisory tools remains advisory.
7. **Compatibility without compulsory convergence.** Interoperability findings may inform choice; they do not make a central version mandatory or erase local forks.
8. **Human responsibility at consequential boundaries.** AI may assist; human and lawfully constituted actors remain accountable.
9. **Accessibility as infrastructure.** Accommodation and participation support are implementation costs, not rewards for agreement.
10. **Learning without extraction.** Failure and story records retain consent, correction, protected-knowledge, and withdrawal boundaries.
11. **Minimum causally adequate implementation.** A pilot must be small enough for safety and large enough to expose the mechanism it claims to test.
12. **Ordinary ownership over permanent support dependency.** Exceptional support is not durable implementation until ordinary ownership, finance, maintenance, and response duties survive withdrawal.
13. **Conformance is not success.** Complete records do not establish effectiveness, legitimacy, causation, or transferability.

# 4. Consequence profiles and proportional records

IMT adopts `GMEAIA/0.2.1` consequence profiles without redefining them:

| Profile | Use | Default IMT core | Trigger additions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CP0` | exploratory, educational, non-operative | `IMT-01`, `IMT-02`; `IMT-31/32` only for an actual sandbox or demonstration | data, AI, protected knowledge, or publication records when present |
| `CP1` | voluntary bounded practice | identity/effect, users, consent, accessibility, complaint, expiry, closure | affected outsiders, data, funding, vendor, cultural, or learning records when present |
| `CP2` | operational or consequential action | authority, causal responsibility, affected parties, authorization, activation, implementation, monitoring, challenge, remedy, closure | procurement, AI, handoff, funding, protected data, and institutional transition when triggered |
| `CP3` | protected-domain or constitutional action | `CP2` plus specialized-interface authorization | exact controlling interface and fail-closed route |

A consequence profile is not a score. The smallest adequate record bundle should be used, but triggered constitutional functions may not be omitted for convenience.

# 5. Safe quick start

1. Describe the goal, claimed consequence, causal mechanism, and constraints through self-description.
2. Identify affected parties, affected outsiders, upstream controllers, and authorities.
3. Compare multiple tool options, including non-action and lower-burden alternatives.
4. Select the consequence profile and pilot type separately.
5. Choose or fork a tool while preserving attribution, rights, warnings, export, and migration metadata.
6. For `CP2/CP3`, follow GMEAIA for authority, consent, response duties, activation, monitoring, remedy, and closure.
7. Test whether the proposal is causally adequate for the claim it intends to make.
8. Define ordinary ownership, support-withdrawal conditions, and exit before activation.
9. Publish only bounded lessons with purpose-specific consent.

# 6. Tool Finder and compatibility power

The Tool Finder records user-selected goals, constraints, urgency claims, accessibility needs, maintenance capacity, consequence expectations, and preferred formats. It returns plural options with assumptions, burdens, alternatives, limitations, and authority effects.

It may not infer identity, maturity, legitimacy, competence, consent, jurisdiction, emergency status, funding eligibility, or propensity for wrongdoing.

Compatibility is a technical and semantic relationship, not a hierarchy of legitimacy. The Finder and core stewards SHALL:

- expose multiple compatible and partially compatible options;
- disclose who defines compatibility and who benefits from convergence;
- preserve local forks and export paths;
- distinguish unknown, incompatible, unsafe, deprecated, and merely divergent states;
- disclose administrative and migration burden;
- avoid making the central repository the only recognized source;
- test whether recommendations reproduce funder, vendor, model, ontology, or professional-network objectives.

---

# 7. Resource cards and causal-burden disclosure

Every active tool resource card states identity, purpose, intended users and non-users, authority effect, consequence profile, protected domains, prerequisites, causal mechanism, practical controllers, burdens, accessibility, data, technology, risks, lifecycle, material exit, support-withdrawal requirements, complaint route, remedy, and limits on conclusions.

Resource estimates are ranges, not promises or eligibility thresholds. The tool ecology should absorb translation, interface, documentation, and migration burden where it can do so more efficiently than local implementers.

---

# 8. Analog, offline, oral, and assisted parity

Digital tools require substantively equivalent paper, oral, audio, sign-language, SMS, offline, or assisted options where the function does not inherently require digital technology. A recorder or interpreter must be identified, bounded, reviewable, correctable, and replaceable. Synchronization rules preserve conflict; “last write wins” is prohibited for authority, consent, activation, remedy, and closure.

---

# 9. Cultural adaptation and Indigenous authority

Communities may adapt or refuse tools within their own lawful authority. Refusal does not automatically bind outsiders. The affected Indigenous nation or people controls its representatives, consent route, protected knowledge, and whether a tool is appropriate. Generic cultural certification, quotas, or external maturity judgments create no authority.

---

# 10. Tool commons governance, forks, and material exit

Core maintainers use public proposals, affected-user review, security review, compatibility tests, dissent records, migration support, and rollback. Emergency security releases may restrict a technical function; they cannot expand substantive powers.

Material exit includes legal, technical, financial, data, service, and knowledge portability. A community must be able to export its records, retain an older safe version where lawful, replace vendors or facilitators, preserve remedies, and continue essential functions without surrendering local governance to the core steward.

---

# 11. Facilitators, support teams, and objective reproduction

Facilitators and technical-assistance teams require bounded appointment, competence, conflicts, funding disclosure, response duties, complaints, removal, succession, and closure. Long-lived support ecosystems examine whether funding, prestige, certification, vendor ties, professional identity, continuation incentives, or control of evidence reward producing more pilots, more records, or more dependence rather than durable local capacity.

---

# 12. AI, data, vendors, and common-mode dependence

AI may support discovery, translation, accessibility, comparison, missing-field detection, summaries, simulations, and reminders. It may not determine authority, consent, cultural identity, protected-domain authorization, emergency activation, liability, funding, or legal effect.

Common-mode review covers shared models, datasets, platforms, vendors, funders, staff, professional networks, ontologies, and fallback systems. Nominally separate reviewers are not independent when they share the same failure source.

---

# 13. Pilots, causal adequacy, and activation

Pilot type, consequence profile, and implementation scale are separate classifications. IMT recognizes the IAF pilot types: acceptability, feasibility, mechanism, operational, system-effect, and institutionalization. Evidence from one type may not be used to claim another.

A pilot design must state what causal mechanism is exposed, which variables are controlled, which remain upstream, what would disconfirm the claim, and why the chosen scale is adequate. “Small” is not synonymous with “safe,” “lawful,” or “informative.”

Activation remains separate from proposal, authorization, adoption, funding, deployment, and technical readiness.

---

# 14. Response, omission, capacity limitation, and constitutional gaps

IMT uses the exact GMEAIA handoff states:

`offered`, `acknowledged`, `accepted`, `partially_accepted`, `capacity_limited`, `refused`, `deferred`, `referred`, `timed_out`, `misrouted`, `constitutional_gap`, `returned`, `closed`.

A `capacity_limited` recipient retains recognized jurisdiction or duty but lacks current ability; a `constitutional_gap` means no identified authority has sufficient lawful jurisdiction. Neither state transfers power to IMT, the Tool Finder, the sender, Meta-Governance, or a platform.

Consequential referrals record response-duty source and effect, acknowledgment and decision clocks, GMEAIA-07 latency reference, provisional safe-state reference, and post-acceptance action clock. Response omission and performance omission remain distinct.

---

# 15. Harm, remedy, and essential-support continuity

The safe sequence is:

`signal → safety assessment → competent interim protection → evidence and causal review → response and dissent → remedy → correction → bounded learning → closure`.

A library administrator may warn, restrict, delist, or withdraw a tool within its own systems. It cannot suspend a public institution, essential service, licence, benefit, legal authority, or person’s rights.

---

# 16. Monitoring, learning, Failure Library, and Story Bank

Monitoring includes outcomes, distribution, uncertainty, administrative burden, implementation dependency, common-mode risk, and transition readiness. Failure Library and Story Bank entries remain bounded learning artifacts, not investigation files, blacklists, compulsory confessions, or proof of causal success. Narrators and affected communities retain access, correction, withdrawal, protected-knowledge, reuse, translation, compensation, and AI-training rights.

---

# 17. Scaling, ordinary ownership, support withdrawal, and closure

A successful pilot supports only the claim its design was adequate to test. Scaling, replication, institutionalization, or transfer to a new jurisdiction requires a new proposal, affected-party map, authority, resources, causal assessment, consent, monitoring, remedy, and closure plan.

Before ordinary adoption, the record must identify ordinary owner, recurrent finance, staffing, maintenance, service boundaries, records custody, response duties, vendor exit, local competence, and support-withdrawal conditions. Exceptional support that cannot be withdrawn without collapse is a dependency finding, not durable implementation.

---

# 18. Framework falsification

IMT v0.9 is supported only while evidence remains compatible with these hypotheses:

1. plural, low-tech, forkable access lowers method-selection barriers without centralizing authority;
2. consequence-scaled records reduce administrative burden without hiding consequential risks;
3. causal-responsibility mapping reduces implementation dumping;
4. minimum causal adequacy improves the validity of pilot claims;
5. compatibility safeguards preserve interoperability without creating a core-steward monopoly;
6. common-mode review catches correlated failure that ordinary conflict disclosure misses;
7. ordinary-ownership and support-withdrawal tests reduce permanent-pilot dependency;
8. protected learning improves collective memory without extracting testimony or knowledge.

Evidence that the tool commons systematically increases burden, centralizes choice, suppresses lawful forks, rewards pilot production over durable outcomes, misassigns responsibility, or extracts data and stories triggers narrowing, suspension, redesign, replacement, or non-scaling.

# 21. Readiness gates

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `G01` | Tool identity, version, source, and steward are explicit. |
| `G02` | Authority-effect label is valid and not inferred from the tool form. |
| `G03` | Consequence profile is recorded at the smallest adequate level. |
| `G04` | Affected parties and outsiders are mapped when consequences extend beyond users. |
| `G05` | Causal variables and practical controllers are identified. |
| `G06` | Affected standing is not converted into causal responsibility. |
| `G07` | Protected-domain triggers route to specialized interfaces. |
| `G08` | Accessibility and low-tech parity are adequate. |
| `G09` | Cultural adaptation is self-described and affected-nation authority is preserved. |
| `G10` | Tool Finder presents plural options and alternatives. |
| `G11` | Compatibility power, migration burden, and local-fork standing are disclosed. |
| `G12` | Common-mode dependencies are identified. |
| `G13` | Facilitator and support-team mandate is bounded and reviewable. |
| `G14` | Funding and incentives do not buy consent or conformity. |
| `G15` | Data purpose, access, retention, return, and deletion are defined. |
| `G16` | Vendor export, continuity, security, and exit are feasible. |
| `G17` | AI roles remain advisory and human-accountable. |
| `G18` | Pilot type is separate from consequence profile and scale. |
| `G19` | The pilot is minimum causally adequate for its claim. |
| `G20` | Authority formation and source are recorded for CP2/CP3. |
| `G21` | Consent, refusal, withdrawal, and non-retaliation are protected. |
| `G22` | Adoption is separated from activation. |
| `G23` | Response-duty source, effect, and clocks are recorded for consequential handoffs. |
| `G24` | Capacity limitation is not conflated with constitutional absence. |
| `G25` | Timeout does not transfer authority. |
| `G26` | Monitoring includes burden, distribution, and dependency. |
| `G27` | Harm signals remain allegations until reviewed. |
| `G28` | Interim protection references competent authority and safe-state limits. |
| `G29` | Remedy and correction propagate downstream. |
| `G30` | Certification and ratings are limited claims, not legitimacy. |
| `G31` | Scaling is a new proposal, not an automatic progression. |
| `G32` | Ordinary ownership and recurrent finance are identified. |
| `G33` | Support-withdrawal durability and material exit are tested. |
| `G34` | Learning publication preserves consent and protected memory. |
| `G35` | Closure preserves unresolved duties, records, remedy, and claims. |
| `G36` | Framework falsification and replacement conditions are reviewable. |

# 22. Compound implementation tests

| Test | Scenario | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| `T01` | A brainstorming worksheet is downloaded | It remains CP0 and needs no authorization merely to be read or discussed. |
| `T02` | A Tool Finder recommends one central package first | Alternatives, assumptions, burdens, and local forks remain visible; ranking creates no mandate. |
| `T03` | A community chooses an incompatible local fork | Divergence is disclosed but does not by itself invalidate the fork. |
| `T04` | A digital tool lacks an offline path | It fails the relevant accessibility gate unless the function inherently requires digital technology and an assisted path is justified. |
| `T05` | Affected residents cannot control the upstream infrastructure causing harm | They gain participation and remedy rights; causal responsibility remains with practical controllers. |
| `T06` | A facilitator holds the only certificate available | Certification does not create monopoly; equivalent local and lawful competence routes remain valid. |
| `T07` | Three independent reviewers use the same model and vendor | The common-mode dependency is recorded and an independent fallback is sought. |
| `T08` | A funder offers participation support only to communities accepting its preferred tool | The condition fails closed as conformity pressure. |
| `T09` | A CP1 workshop begins collecting protected testimony | Data, consent, protected-knowledge, and withdrawal triggers are added. |
| `T10` | A feasibility pilot claims system-wide causal effects | The claim is rejected or narrowed because evidence cannot exceed pilot type and causal adequacy. |
| `T11` | A small pilot omits the causal variable it claims to test | It is not minimum causally adequate and cannot support the claim. |
| `T12` | A tool is authorized but not activated | It remains non-active; adoption or deployment does not start operation. |
| `T13` | A recipient has jurisdiction but lacks staff and funds | The handoff state is capacity_limited, with support or delegation needs recorded. |
| `T14` | No authority has jurisdiction over the condition | A constitutional_gap is recorded; IMT does not inherit the power. |
| `T15` | A response clock expires | The record becomes timed_out and escalates without implied consent or authority transfer. |
| `T16` | A recipient accepts but misses the action clock | A performance-omission review opens; acceptance does not close accountability. |
| `T17` | A harm signal names a facilitator | Protection and review begin, but the signal is not treated as a guilt finding. |
| `T18` | A library steward delists a tool | The steward may control its own library but cannot suspend an external institution or right. |
| `T19` | A security patch disables a function | It may restrict technical use but cannot expand substantive authority. |
| `T20` | A rating campaign brigades a minority-designed tool | Minority evidence, version differences, response, correction, and anti-retaliation controls apply. |
| `T21` | A certificate expires | The limited assurance ends; prior users retain records, remedies, and lawful migration choices. |
| `T22` | A pilot succeeds only while a Catalyst Team performs core operations | The result is support-dependent and not yet ordinary institutionalization. |
| `T23` | A pilot transfers to ordinary ownership without recurrent finance | Transition is not ready. |
| `T24` | A vendor blocks data export | Material exit fails; scaling or renewal is paused until corrected. |
| `T25` | A local fork remains safe on an older version | Users may remain with disclosed support and compatibility status. |
| `T26` | A funder dislikes local adaptation | Participation support cannot be withdrawn merely for disagreement. |
| `T27` | A story is reused for AI training beyond consent | Reuse stops and correction, restriction, deletion, or remedy propagates. |
| `T28` | A Failure Library entry is requested in active litigation | Learning and evidentiary functions remain separated and access is governed by the competent process. |
| `T29` | A CP3 tool use lacks specialized authorization | It fails closed. |
| `T30` | An emergency kit is opened during a flood | It may support information and referral; it creates no declaration, command, requisition, or force. |
| `T31` | A coercive request is embedded in a tool recommendation | It routes through IMT-47 and GMEAIA-57; the recommendation itself has no coercive effect. |
| `T32` | Conformance tests all pass but outcomes worsen | The framework remains conforming but unsuccessful; causal review and falsification apply. |
| `T33` | Tool use repeatedly increases local paperwork without improving action | Administrative-burden and framework-falsification review is triggered. |
| `T34` | Central compatibility rules steadily eliminate local forks | Compatibility-power and objective-reproduction review may narrow or replace the core governance model. |
| `T35` | A pilot ends with unpaid staff and unresolved harm | Temporary authority ends, but payments, remedy, data, and claims remain open. |
| `T36` | A repository archives a deprecated version | Archival status does not erase unresolved duties or participant access. |
| `T37` | An authority refuses a referral lawfully | The refusal and reasons are recorded; IMT cannot override it. |
| `T38` | A referral is misrouted | Custody is corrected and the matter referred without treating the first recipient as responsible. |
| `T39` | A jointly constituted cross-community process uses an IMT tool | The constituting compact and shared governance object are referenced; the tool does not create them. |
| `T40` | Evidence disconfirms IMT’s core hypotheses | The steward must consider narrowing, redesign, replacement, or non-scaling rather than treating conformance as vindication. |

# 23. Adoption, publication, and lifecycle states

IMT publication activates no tool, pilot, authority, or jurisdiction. Tool-library states are `draft`, `published`, `active_use`, `under_review`, `restricted`, `deprecated`, `withdrawn_for_safety`, and `archived`. Consequential use additionally follows GMEAIA lifecycle and handoff states.

# 24. Closing doctrine

> **Make tools easy to find, easy to understand, easy to adapt, easy to challenge, and easy to leave. Make authority impossible to fake, responsibility difficult to dump, and support possible to withdraw without collapse.**

The frozen `IAEG-CLUSTER/0.1` remains unchanged. IMT/0.9 participates only in the successor transition registry until WDIP is migrated and IAF/IRF machine companions are complete.
