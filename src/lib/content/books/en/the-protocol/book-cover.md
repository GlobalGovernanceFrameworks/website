    <div class="book-cover-description">
      <p>A comprehensive technical manual for the Global Governance Frameworks (GGF)—an open-source ecosystem of interconnected frameworks and protocols for regenerative planetary governance spanning constitutional reform, economics, ecology, and crisis management.</p>
      
      <p>This book provides actionable frameworks for building polycentric governance systems that coordinate at planetary scale without creating world government, integrate ecosystem rights into legal systems, transform extractive economics into regenerative metabolism, and respond to crises through distributed intelligence rather than centralized command.</p>
    </div>
    
    <div class="book-cover-highlights">
      <h3>What You'll Learn</h3>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Fractality</strong>: How the same governance patterns repeat at neighborhood, bioregional, and planetary scales</li>
        <li><strong>Polycentrism</strong>: Building resilient systems through tri-cameral architecture with overlapping jurisdictions</li>
        <li><strong>Dual-Currency Economics</strong>: Separating circulation (Hearts) from trade (Leaves) to prevent extraction</li>
        <li><strong>Ecological Intelligence</strong>: Integrating real-time ecosystem metrics into governance and economics</li>
        <li><strong>Adaptive Governance</strong>: Creating immune-system-like crisis response without centralized bottlenecks</li>
        <li><strong>Sovereign Debt Transformation</strong>: Converting extractive debt into regenerative performance bonds</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    
    <div class="book-cover-audience">
      <h3>Who This Is For</h3>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Policymakers</strong> seeking frameworks for climate adaptation, debt transformation, and crisis management</li>
        <li><strong>Community Organizers</strong> building local resilience through complementary currencies and mutual aid</li>
        <li><strong>Systems Thinkers</strong> interested in polycentric governance and regenerative economics</li>
        <li><strong>Indigenous Communities</strong> protecting sovereignty through cryptographic consent mechanisms</li>
        <li><strong>Researchers</strong> studying commons governance, Rights of Nature, and post-Westphalian coordination</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    
    <div class="book-cover-structure">
      <h3>Book Structure</h3>
      <div class="structure-grid">

        <div class="structure-section">
          <h4>Part II: Metabolism</h4>
          <p>Dual-Currency, Ecological Intelligence, and AUBI create regenerative economic flows</p>
        </div>
        <div class="structure-section">
          <h4>Part III: Authority</h4>
          <p>Subsidiarity and Guardianship distribute power and give nature legal standing</p>
        </div>
        <div class="structure-section">
          <h4>Part IV: Evolution</h4>
          <p>Adaptive Governance and Transition show how systems respond to crisis and transform</p>
        </div>

    </div>
    
    <div class="book-cover-pedagogy">
      <h3>Teaching Approach</h3>
      <p>Each chapter follows the <strong>Bug/Patch/Demo/Integration</strong> pattern:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Bug</strong>: What's broken in current systems (with concrete examples)</li>
        <li><strong>Patch</strong>: The conceptual fix (explained through accessible analogies)</li>
        <li><strong>Demo</strong>: How it manifests in the GGF (specific, implementable protocols)</li>
        <li><strong>Integration</strong>: How this framework connects to all others</li>
      </ul>
      <p>This isn't a manifesto telling you what to think—it's a manual showing you how the parts fit together.</p>
