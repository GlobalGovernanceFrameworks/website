# CIS/0.2.2 — Recognition-Record Purpose Firewall Patch

## Patch scope

This patch adds a cross-framework constitutional rule governing the secondary use of records created for care, ecological, civic, cultural, relational, and other recognition purposes.

It does not create a new data authority, tax exemption, financial secrecy right, evidentiary privilege, or prohibition on governing independently constituted assets and transactions under valid law.

The patch distinguishes:

* the **recognition record** that describes, validates, narrates, or acknowledges an activity; from
* any **asset, payment, entitlement, liability, or transaction** separately created as a legal or financial consequence of that activity.

The purpose is to prevent systems created to recognize contribution or relationship from silently becoming generalized surveillance, credit, tax-capacity, fraud, employment, migration, insurance, or reputation infrastructure.

# Amendment to Decision Observability

In the current **Observation Contract** requirements, add:

* for each material data source, its original constituting purpose and whether the proposed use is primary, compatible secondary, or consequential secondary use;
* where consequential secondary use is proposed, the independently valid authority, necessity, proportionality, and less-intrusive-source analysis required by the Recognition-Record Purpose Firewall;
* any transformation by which a narrative, contribution record, credential, attestation, activity record, or relational record becomes an input to a financial, eligibility, risk, compliance, or enforcement inference.

A data source does not become constitutionally suitable for a decision merely because it is available, technically interoperable, accurate for its original purpose, or convenient to reuse.

## Recognition-record purpose firewall

A **recognition record** is a record whose constituting purpose is primarily to acknowledge, narrate, validate, attest, allocate recognition for, or support participation in:

* care;
* ecological restoration or stewardship;
* gratitude;
* relational activity;
* civic or community participation;
* cultural contribution;
* mutual aid;
* voluntary contribution;
* analogous social, ecological, cultural, or community activity.

A recognition record may be digital, analogue, oral, narrative, quantitative, credential-based, transactional, or locally held.

Its existence does not create permission for unrelated consequential use.

A recognition record SHALL NOT, merely because it exists or is technically accessible, be converted into or used as an input to:

* a wealth estimate;
* a taxable-capacity estimate;
* a creditworthiness score;
* an insurance-risk score;
* an employment, promotion, or labour-access score;
* a migration, residency, or citizenship decision;
* a fraud, abuse, security, or law-enforcement risk score;
* a political-reliability or ideological-conformity assessment;
* a generalized reputation or social-credit score;
* an unrelated benefit, service, licence, procurement, or eligibility determination;
* another consequential classification materially outside the record's constituting purpose.

This prohibition applies equally to direct use and to derived use through models, embeddings, inferred features, data fusion, identity matching, aggregate proxies, or another transformation.

### Consequential secondary use

Consequential secondary use of a recognition record is permissible only where all applicable conditions are satisfied:

1. **Independent authority.** The secondary use has an independently valid legal, constitutional, contractual, Indigenous, or other constituting basis appropriate to the consequence. The existence of the original record does not supply that authority.

2. **Defined purpose.** The receiving institution identifies the specific decision for which the record is proposed and the relationship between that decision and its lawful mandate.

3. **Necessity.** The institution demonstrates why the information is materially necessary rather than merely useful, predictive, inexpensive, or convenient.

4. **Less-intrusive-source test.** Where the required fact can reasonably be established through a source created for that purpose or through a less intrusive observation channel, the recognition record SHALL NOT be repurposed instead.

5. **Proportionality.** The informational intrusion, inference risk, chilling effect, discrimination risk, and possible effect on participation are proportionate to the lawful objective.

6. **Purpose-change provenance.** The record preserves the original purpose, proposed new purpose, receiving authority, transformation performed, date, retention rule, and applicable restrictions. Repurposed data SHALL NOT lose the provenance showing that it originated in a differently constituted system.

7. **Minimum necessary transfer.** A bounded attestation, conclusion, independently constituted financial record, or other narrower interface SHALL be preferred where it can satisfy the legitimate purpose without transferring the underlying recognition record.

8. **Rights and sovereignty.** Applicable privacy, consent, data-sovereignty, Indigenous-authority, protected-non-transfer, worker, disability, child, claimant, and due-process requirements remain in force.

9. **Notice and contestability.** A materially affected person or authority receives the applicable notice, access, explanation, correction, challenge, and remedy rights.

10. **No adverse inference from protected absence.** Refusal to participate in a recognition system, absence of recognition records, protected non-transfer, non-digitization, or lawful deletion SHALL NOT itself become an adverse signal.

11. **No automatic consequence.** A repurposed recognition record or inference derived from it SHALL NOT directly generate an irreversible or punitive consequence without the separately required interpretation, authorization, and adjudication.

Consent alone is not sufficient where structural dependency, essential services, employment, material security, public authority, or another condition makes refusal practically unavailable.

### Recognition record and asset distinction

The Recognition-Record Purpose Firewall protects the **purpose-bound record**. It does not make a separately constituted financial asset, payment, entitlement, liability, ownership interest, or transaction legally invisible merely because that asset or transaction arose from recognized contribution.

For example:

* a record acknowledging care remains a recognition record;
* a Hearts balance lawfully issued as a result of eligible activity is a financial asset governed by the applicable AUBI, Financial Systems, tax, and other valid rules;
* a later redemption, transfer, payment, or legally taxable transaction may be governed through records constituted for those financial purposes;
* the underlying care narrative, Love Ledger history, contribution evidence, or relational record does not thereby become a general-purpose tax, credit, fraud, or financial-surveillance input.

Where an independently constituted asset or transaction record is sufficient for a lawful fiscal or financial purpose, the originating recognition record SHALL NOT be imported merely to enrich the receiving institution's profile of the person.

> **Origin in recognized contribution does not immunize an asset from valid law. Valid law does not erase the purpose boundary of the recognition record that preceded it.**

### Love Ledger application

Love Ledger narratives, Gratitude Tokens, recognition histories, and related relational records are recognition records for purposes of this Specification.

They SHALL NOT serve as a default source for:

* institutional legitimacy;
* cultural scoring;
* participant reputation;
* labour matching;
* public-sentiment measurement;
* Treaty enforcement;
* LMCI-M;
* wealth or taxable-capacity estimation;
* creditworthiness;
* insurance pricing;
* fraud profiling;
* political or security profiling;
* economic sanctions.

Where a separately constituted Hearts asset, payment, or transaction exists, its lawful financial treatment is distinct from the Love Ledger or contribution record associated with its origin.

Nothing in this subsection requires a Love Ledger record to exist, become digital, become transferable, or become visible to a financial authority.

# Conformance amendment

Add the following question to the CIS conformance test for consequential mechanisms:

> **Does this mechanism use information outside the purpose for which it was originally constituted? If so, what independently valid authority, defined purpose, necessity, proportionality, less-intrusive-source analysis, purpose-change provenance, information-minimization rule, challenge route, and remedy permit that secondary use?**

A framework claiming consequential authority from a recognition record merely because the record already exists SHALL be treated as constitutionally non-conforming.

A framework using an independently constituted asset or transaction record under valid authority does not fail this test solely because the asset originated in recognized care, ecological, civic, cultural, or community contribution.

# Interpretive tests

The following examples are non-exhaustive.

### Care recognition → tax-capacity model

A tax authority proposes to ingest Love Ledger care histories because they correlate with Hearts issuance.

**Result:** prohibited by default. The authority should use independently constituted asset, transaction, income, ownership, or other legally authorized fiscal records. Availability of the care history does not make it an admissible tax-capacity sensor.

### Hearts balance → lawful tax treatment

A jurisdiction has enacted rules governing the tax treatment of Hearts as a redeemable financial asset.

**Result:** not prohibited by this firewall. The jurisdiction may govern the asset or relevant transaction within valid authority and applicable safeguards. The rule does not create permission to inspect the underlying relational or care records that generated the Hearts.

### Ecological recognition → creditworthiness

A lender proposes to use Leaves histories or ecological-contribution records as a positive or negative credit score.

**Result:** prohibited unless an independently constituted and rights-compatible secondary-use basis satisfies the firewall. Voluntary ecological participation must not silently become financial reputation infrastructure.

### Civic participation → public-benefit eligibility

A public body proposes to increase access to essential services for people with high participation-recognition records.

**Result:** prohibited where participation is unrelated to the valid eligibility basis. Recognition cannot become a loyalty mechanism.

### Protected absence → fraud score

A person has no Love Ledger or contribution history because they declined participation.

**Result:** absence cannot itself increase a fraud, credit, tax, migration, or compliance risk score.

# Compatibility doctrine

This patch SHALL be interpreted together with:

* protected non-transfer;
* protected unobservability;
* decision-chain separation;
* indicators-are-not-judgments;
* minimum necessary authority;
* Observation Contracts;
* State Estimate Records;
* applicable data-governance and Indigenous-authority rules.

Where a specialized framework imposes a stronger purpose limitation or non-transfer rule, the stronger protection remains.

Where valid law requires a consequential financial, tax, judicial, regulatory, or protective use of independently constituted information, this patch does not nullify that authority. It governs whether purpose-bound recognition records may be silently substituted for, fused into, or used to expand that independently constituted observation channel.

The constitutional rule is therefore:

> **Data availability is not data jurisdiction. Recognition is not reputation. Contribution evidence is not general-purpose behavioural evidence. A resulting asset may be governed as an asset without converting the human relationship that produced it into a universal sensor.**
