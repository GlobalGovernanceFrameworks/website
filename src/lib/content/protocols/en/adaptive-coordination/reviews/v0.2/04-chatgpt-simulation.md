# **Pilot Simulation: EU–North Africa Migration Corridor**

## **Participants (Initial Coalition)**

**EU side:**

* Germany
* Italy
* Spain

**North Africa:**

* Morocco
* Tunisia

**Supporting institutions:**

* European Union
* United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs

---

# **Phase 0: Pre-Crisis Setup (Months 0–6)**

## **What Actually Gets Implemented**

* ACP Secretariat embedded in OCHA (small team)

* Basic **Situational Awareness Layer** goes live:

  * Frontex data
  * UN migration data
  * national reporting

* Countries submit **capacity ranges**:

  * Germany: high intake flexibility
  * Italy/Spain: medium but constrained (frontline pressure)
  * Morocco/Tunisia: limited intake, high transit burden

---

## **Early Friction**

* Italy insists: “We are not a waiting room for Europe”
* Tunisia demands funding before deeper participation
* Germany pushes for stronger coordination

👉 Already: **asymmetry of incentives appears**

---

# **Phase 1: Trigger Event (Month 7)**

## **Shock**

* Conflict escalation in Sahel region
* Rapid increase in migration toward North Africa

**Metrics:**

* Tunisia inflow hits **130% baseline → Stress Mode triggered**
* Coastal departures toward Italy increase sharply

---

# **Phase 2: Stress Mode Activation**

## **System Response**

### **1. Shared Awareness**

ACP dashboard shows:

* Tunisia nearing shelter capacity
* Morocco stable but trending upward
* Italy facing surge projections

---

### **2. Allocation Proposal #1**

Allocation Engine suggests:

* Germany: increase intake
* Spain: moderate intake
* Italy: reduced burden (redistribution support)
* Morocco/Tunisia:

  * receive financial + logistical support
  * expand temporary capacity

---

## **Reactions (Realistic)**

### **Germany**

Accepts most of proposal
→ wants system credibility

---

### **Spain**

Partially accepts
→ domestic politics cautious

---

### **Italy**

Pushes back hard:

> “We’ve heard this before—redistribution never fully happens.”

Accepts only conditional participation

---

### **Tunisia**

Accepts support—but demands **faster funding delivery**

---

### **Morocco**

Cooperates selectively—keeps leverage

---

## **Outcome**

👉 **Partial implementation (~60%)**

System holds—but imperfectly.

---

# **Phase 3: Escalation to Emergency (Month 9)**

## **Shock Intensifies**

* Flows exceed **150% baseline → Emergency Mode**
* Italian reception centers overloaded
* Media pressure spikes across Europe

---

## **System Response**

### **Allocation Proposal #2 (Faster Cycle)**

* Immediate redistribution quotas (non-binding)
* Emergency funding release
* Logistics surge:

  * transport coordination
  * shelter deployment

---

## **Where Reality Bites**

### **Italy**

Threatens unilateral action:

> “We close ports if redistribution fails.”

---

### **Germany**

Pushes system harder:

> “We will take more—but others must follow.”

---

### **Spain**

Stalls slightly—domestic elections looming

---

### **Tunisia**

System strain visible:

* local unrest rising
* demands faster relocation outflows

---

## **Critical Moment**

👉 ACP either:

* stabilizes flow
* or loses credibility

---

# **Phase 4: System Adaptation**

## **Key Adjustment (Yellow-Level Move)**

Instead of insisting on equal burden-sharing:

### **ACP shifts strategy → Differentiated Roles**

* Germany → primary intake hub
* Spain → secondary intake + logistics
* Italy → transit + rapid transfer (not long-term hosting)
* Tunisia/Morocco → compensated stabilization zones

---

## **This Is Crucial**

👉 The system abandons “fairness = equal share”

Moves to:

> **“fairness = functional fit + compensation”**

---

# **Phase 5: Stabilization (Month 12)**

## **What Works**

* Flows redistributed faster than baseline (real-world comparison)
* Italy partially relieved
* Tunisia stabilized with external support
* Germany absorbs more but gains political capital

---

## **What Doesn’t Fully Work**

* Spain underperforms commitments
* Morocco plays both sides (strategic ambiguity)
* Public narratives remain fragmented

---

# **Measured Outcomes**

Compared to non-coordinated baseline:

### **Improvements**

* Faster response time (~30–40%)
* Lower system chaos
* Reduced political breakdown risk

---

### **Persistent Problems**

* Trust gaps remain
* Compliance uneven
* System dependent on a few strong actors

---

# **Where ACP Proves Its Value**

## **1. It Prevents Total Breakdown**

Without ACP:

* Italy likely acts unilaterally
* EU fractures politically

---

## **2. It Enables Real-Time Adaptation**

System changes strategy mid-crisis:

* from equal sharing → functional differentiation

---

## **3. It Builds Proto-Trust**

Even partial success creates:

* precedent
* expectation of coordination

---

# **Where ACP Shows Limits**

## **1. No Hard Enforcement**

Countries still:

* hedge
* delay
* partially comply

---

## **2. Political Cycles Interfere**

Elections, media, narratives:

* distort rational coordination

---

## **3. External Actors Not Included**

Non-participants can:

* disrupt flows
* free-ride

---

# **Key Insight from the Simulation**

The system does NOT create:

> perfect cooperation

It creates:

> **bounded coordination under real constraints**

---

# **Critical Learning (Very Important)**

The biggest breakthrough wasn’t technical.

It was this shift:

> ❌ “Everyone must share equally”
> ✅ “Everyone plays a role that fits system reality”

---

# **What You’d Improve for v0.3**

Based on this pilot:

### **1. Faster Funding Mechanism**

Tunisia delay almost broke system

---

### **2. Stronger Incentives for Mid-Level Players**

Spain-type behavior is a weak link

---

### **3. Narrative Layer Integration**

Public perception lagged behind system reality

→ Meaning Infrastructure needs tighter coupling

---

# **Final Verdict**

This pilot shows:

👉 ACP is **not utopian—but viable**

It:

* reduces chaos
* improves coordination
* survives political reality

But:

> It depends heavily on a few committed actors to anchor the system

---

## **Final Insight**

What you’ve designed is not a solution that eliminates conflict.

It’s something more subtle—and arguably more realistic:

> A system that keeps cooperation **just barely ahead of collapse**
