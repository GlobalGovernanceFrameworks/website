# **1. Governance Clarification: Coordination Secretariat**

## **1.1 Institutional Embedding**

The ACP Secretariat SHALL NOT be created as a standalone entity in early phases.

Instead, it is:

> **Embedded within an existing multilateral coordination body**

**Primary candidate:**

* United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs

**Rationale:**

* Existing crisis coordination mandate
* Established legitimacy
* Operational infrastructure already in place

---

## **1.2 Structure**

* **Core Team (5–15 staff)**

  * Data coordination
  * Facilitation
  * system operations

* **Seconded Experts**

  * From participating states and institutions

---

## **1.3 Funding Model**

Hybrid structure:

* Voluntary state contributions
* Multilateral funding channels (e.g. trust funds)
* Philanthropic/partner support (early phase only)

---

## **1.4 Accountability**

* Annual public reporting
* Independent audit (linked to ACP Audit Layer)
* Oversight via Participant Assembly

---

# **2. Threshold Definitions (Pilot Domain: Migration)**

## **2.1 Stress Mode Trigger**

Activated when ANY of the following occur:

* Migration inflow exceeds **120% of 12-month rolling national baseline**
* Regional displacement increases by **>15% within 30 days**
* Resource strain indicators exceed predefined thresholds (food, shelter capacity)

---

## **2.2 Emergency Mode Trigger**

Activated when ANY of the following occur:

* Migration inflow exceeds **150% of baseline capacity**
* Local system breakdown indicators (e.g. shelter saturation >95%)
* Conflict escalation generating rapid cross-border flows

---

## **2.3 Adaptive Thresholding**

Thresholds are:

* Reviewed quarterly
* Adjusted based on empirical performance
* Context-sensitive (region-specific calibration allowed)

---

# **3. Allocation Engine (Transparency Upgrade)**

## **3.1 Design Principle**

The Allocation Engine MUST be:

> **Transparent, auditable, and contestable**

---

## **3.2 Core Factors**

Allocation proposals are derived from weighted inputs:

* **Capacity (C)** → infrastructure, economic ability
* **Proximity (P)** → geographic/logistical closeness
* **Equity (E)** → fair burden-sharing over time
* **Stability Impact (S)** → risk of destabilization

---

## **3.3 Simplified Functional Form**

Conceptually:

```
Allocation Score = w1*C + w2*P + w3*E + w4*S
```

Weights are:

* Publicly documented
* Adjustable via Participant Assembly
* Scenario-dependent

---

## **3.4 Human Override**

* All outputs are **advisory**
* Participants may:

  * Accept
  * Modify
  * Reject

All deviations are recorded transparently.

---

## **3.5 Adversarial Testing**

Allocation logic SHALL be:

* Open-source
* Subject to stress-testing by independent actors
* Reviewed by adversarial audit functions (e.g. “Office of the Adversary” concept)

---

# **4. Participation Framework**

## **4.1 ACP Participation Agreement (APA)**

Entry requires signing a lightweight compact:

---

### **Core Commitments**

Participants agree to:

1. Share relevant situational data
2. Register capacity ranges
3. Engage in allocation cycles in good faith
4. Accept transparency of actions

---

### **Non-Binding Clause**

Participation does NOT require:

* Surrender of sovereignty
* Binding acceptance of allocation proposals

---

## **4.2 Entry Process**

* Submission of APA
* Technical onboarding to SAL
* Initial capacity declaration

---

## **4.3 Exit Clause**

Participants may withdraw with:

* Notice period (e.g. 30–90 days)
* Completion of active commitments where feasible

---

# **5. Dispute Resolution Layer**

## **5.1 First-Level: Mediation**

* Secretariat-facilitated dialogue
* Rapid resolution focus (hours–days)

---

## **5.2 Second-Level: Peer Mediation Panel**

* Rotating representatives from participant pool
* Non-binding recommendations

---

## **5.3 Third-Level: Escalation Path**

Where applicable, disputes may interface with:

* Existing dispute mechanisms within broader frameworks
* (e.g. GGF-aligned judicial or arbitration structures)

---

## **5.4 Transparency Rule**

All disputes and resolutions are:

* Logged
* Partially public (with sensitivity controls)

---

# **6. Cultural Translation Layer**

## **6.1 Principle**

ACP MUST NOT assume a single cultural or institutional paradigm.

---

## **6.2 Implementation**

Before deployment in any region:

* Apply **Cultural Translation Protocol (CTP)**

This includes:

* Local framing of:

  * sovereignty
  * responsibility
  * cooperation

* Adaptation of:

  * language
  * governance expectations
  * communication style

---

## **6.3 Local Anchoring**

ACP deployment SHOULD:

* Partner with regional institutions
* Incorporate local governance norms
