# Adaptive Coordination Protocol (ACP)

### Tier 3 (Cross-Cutting Protocol)
### Status: Draft v0.3 – Resilience & Incentive Upgrade
### Purpose: Real-World Coordination Under Systemic Stress

---

## 0. Revision Note (v0.3)

This revision incorporates findings from the EU–North Africa pilot simulation and addresses critical operational gaps:

- **Rapid Funding Mechanism** to maintain frontline state cooperation
- **Tiered Incentive Structure** to strengthen mid-level compliance
- **Narrative Layer Integration** with Meaning Infrastructure Protocol
- **Neighbor Engagement Protocol** for external actor management
- **Trust Ledger Interface** formalizing contribution tracking

---

# 1. Rapid Funding Mechanism

## 1.1 Problem Identified

The v0.2 simulation revealed that **delays in funding disbursement to frontline states** (e.g., Tunisia) nearly caused system breakdown. Trust erodes quickly when support is promised but not delivered in operational timeframes.

## 1.2 ACP Emergency Response Fund (ERF)

A **pre-positioned, fast-disbursement fund** is established as a core ACP component.

### 1.2.1 Structure

- **Host:** Embedded within an existing multilateral funding mechanism (e.g., UN Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF) or a dedicated World Bank trust fund)
- **Capitalization:** Voluntary contributions from ACP participants, with a minimum recommended contribution scaled to GDP
- **Governance:** Disbursement authority delegated to the ACP Secretariat, subject to predefined triggers and *post-hoc* audit

### 1.2.2 Activation

- **Automatic Disbursement Window:** Upon **Emergency Mode** activation, a pre-approved funding envelope (e.g., 30% of the fund) is immediately available for frontline states.
- **Expedited Approval:** Additional funds require a 72-hour approval window by a subset of the Participant Assembly (e.g., 5 rotating members).

### 1.2.3 Accountability

- All disbursements are published on the ACP dashboard within 48 hours.
- Annual independent audit, with findings reported to the Participant Assembly.

# 2. Tiered Incentive Structure

## 2.1 Problem Identified

The simulation showed that **mid-level participants** (e.g., Spain) often hedge or partially comply, weakening overall system performance. Uniform incentives do not sufficiently reward full engagement.

## 2.2 Participation Tiers

ACP introduces three tiers of participation with corresponding benefits:

| Tier | Criteria | Benefits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Partner** | Full compliance with APA commitments; participation in ≥80% of allocation cycles; timely data sharing. | Priority access to ERF funds; enhanced voting weight in allocation adjustments; eligibility for ACP leadership roles. |
| **Cooperating Partner** | Partial compliance; participation in 50–79% of cycles; some data sharing gaps. | Standard access to ERF; standard allocation weight; eligibility for technical assistance. |
| **Observing Partner** | Signed APA but minimal compliance; participation <50%. | Access to SAL data; no ERF access; reduced allocation priority in future crises. |

## 2.3 Tier Adjustment

- Tiers are reviewed **quarterly** based on objective compliance metrics.
- Participants may appeal tier designation to the Peer Mediation Panel.
- Movement between tiers is transparently logged on the ACP dashboard.

# 3. Narrative Layer Integration

## 3.1 Problem Identified

The simulation noted that **public narratives remained fragmented**, undermining political support for coordination. Technical coordination without cultural and narrative alignment is brittle.

## 3.2 ACP–Meaning Infrastructure Protocol (MIP) Interface

Upon ACP activation (Stress or Emergency Mode), the following narrative support is triggered:

### 3.2.1 ACP Narrative Brief

- A **1–2 page plain-language document** co-developed with MIP Narrative Stewardship Guilds.
- Framing: *"Shared Responsibility, Differentiated Roles"* — emphasizing that coordination is not surrender of sovereignty but strategic burden management.
- Translated into all relevant regional languages within 72 hours of activation.

### 3.2.2 Community Reflection Cycle

- Within 30 days of de-escalation to Normal Mode, affected communities are invited to participate in **Community Reflection Circles** (per MIP) to process the experience and feed insights back into protocol refinement.

### 3.2.3 Public Dashboard Narrative Layer

- The ACP dashboard includes a **"Story So Far"** section, updated weekly, summarizing the coordination effort in human terms (e.g., "X families relocated, Y tons of aid delivered").

# 4. Neighbor Engagement Protocol

## 4.1 Problem Identified

Non-participating states or actors can **disrupt flows, free-ride, or actively undermine coordination**. ACP must have a strategy for managing the external environment.

## 4.2 Engagement Tiers for Non-Participants

| Tier | Description | ACP Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aligned Neighbor** | Non-signatory but cooperative in practice. | Invitation to observe; access to select SAL data; eligibility for limited ERF support in crises affecting them. |
| **Neutral Neighbor** | Neither cooperative nor obstructive. | Monitoring only; no active engagement. |
| **Disruptive Actor** | Actively undermines coordination (e.g., weaponizing migration flows). | Documentation of disruptive actions on ACP dashboard; potential referral to relevant multilateral bodies (e.g., UN Security Council); exclusion from all ACP benefits. |

## 4.3 Diplomatic Engagement

- The ACP Secretariat maintains a **"Neighbor Liaison"** function to engage non-participants and explore pathways to alignment.
- Regional organizations (e.g., African Union, ASEAN) are leveraged as intermediaries.

# 5. Trust Ledger Interface

## 5.1 Problem Identified

The simulation revealed **persistent trust gaps** and **uneven compliance**. While the Informal Trust Ledger (ITL) is a longer-term GGF aspiration, ACP requires a lightweight, immediately deployable trust-tracking mechanism.

## 5.2 ACP Contribution Registry

A simple, transparent registry tracks:

- **Commitments Made** (capacity ranges, funding pledges)
- **Commitments Fulfilled** (actual intake, funds disbursed)
- **Compliance Ratio** (fulfilled / pledged)

### 5.2.1 Implementation

- Maintained by the ACP Secretariat.
- Publicly accessible via the ACP dashboard.
- Uses a simple **color-coded status** (Green: ≥80% compliance; Yellow: 50–79%; Red: <50%).

### 5.2.2 Future ITL Integration

- The ACP Contribution Registry is designed with a **schema compatible** with anticipated ITL standards.
- When ITL becomes operational, historical ACP compliance data can be migrated, providing a **trust bootstrap** for new participants.

Additional mitigations for v0.3:

| Risk | v0.3 Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Funding Delay** | ERF with automatic disbursement triggers |
| **Mid-Level Free-Riding** | Tiered participation with escalating benefits |
| **Narrative Fragmentation** | Mandated MIP interface and Narrative Brief |
| **External Disruption** | Neighbor Engagement Protocol with documentation and exclusion mechanisms |

# 7. Updated Success Criteria (v0.3)

ACP v0.3 is successful if it demonstrates:

- **Rapid financial response** (ERF disbursement within 72 hours of Emergency Mode)
- **Improved mid-level compliance** (reduction in "Cooperating Partner" hedging behavior)
- **Coherent public narrative** (reduced fragmentation in media and political discourse)
- **External actor containment** (documented reduction in disruptive actions by non-participants)
- **Transparent trust tracking** (Contribution Registry used in allocation and tier decisions)

---

# 8. Strategic Insight (v0.3)

This revision transforms ACP from a **coordination protocol** into a **coordination ecosystem**. It recognizes that technical allocation is necessary but insufficient. The system must also:

- **Reward** good behavior (tiers)
- **Fund** frontline states rapidly (ERF)
- **Tell a coherent story** (MIP interface)
- **Manage the external environment** (Neighbor Engagement)
- **Make trust visible** (Contribution Registry)

These additions do not increase the **formal authority** of ACP—it remains voluntary. But they significantly strengthen its **gravitational pull**, making participation more attractive and defection more costly in reputational and operational terms.

# 10. Conclusion

ACP v0.3 is a **resilience upgrade**. It acknowledges that coordination fails not only because of technical flaws but because of **financial friction, incentive misalignment, narrative incoherence, and external disruption**. By addressing these dimensions directly—while preserving the voluntary, non-coercive core—the protocol becomes significantly more robust to real-world political pressures.

The next step is to test v0.3 in a **second simulated crisis scenario**, or to prepare the protocol for **presentation to potential pilot partners** (e.g., UN OCHA, EU, African Union).
