# Essential Care and Real-Resource Adequacy Interface Specification v0.1

## Coordinating Unconditional Material Security, Voluntary Work, and Essential Service Continuity

**Short identifier:** `ECRAI/0.1`  
**Tier:** Cross-framework coordination interface — proposed  
**Status:** Candidate for expert, rights-holder, competent-authority, and cross-framework compatibility review; **not adopted or certified**  
**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Primary interfaces:** Adaptive Universal Basic Income (AUBI) Framework v2.7 (proposed); Work in Liberation Framework v2.0.2 (proposed); Community Support Systems; relevant health and disability service authorities; Financial Systems, Nested Economies, Constitutional and Justice instruments; Aurora Accord and Constitutional Interface Specification (CIS) as applicable.

> **Unconditional income is not proof of available care. An unmet care need is not authority to compel a caregiver. A referral is not a delivered service.**

---

## 1. Purpose and problem definition

AUBI safeguards material security without requiring productive or civic contribution. Work in Liberation creates rights-protecting ways for people to offer formal contribution voluntarily. Neither mechanism guarantees that every essential good or qualified service can be produced and delivered at the moment it is needed.

This interface addresses the resulting governance gap: **who must recognize, communicate, and continue to act on a serious unmet essential need when money, entitlements, need broadcasting, or voluntary commissioning have not made the needed resource available?**

Its aims are to:

1. distinguish monetary adequacy from effective access to real goods and services;
2. recognize life-sustaining and other rights-significant unmet needs even when only one person is affected;
3. prevent essential needs from disappearing between AUBI, work commissioning, clinical and care services, and emergency governance;
4. protect the agency, safety, and confidentiality of care recipients and potential caregivers alike;
5. preserve valid, bounded institutional duties until accepted handoff, resolution, or lawful closure; and
6. support capacity-building and evidence-based correction without manufacturing new compulsory-service powers.

**Non-goals:** ECRAI does not define a new universal income, clinical benefit, eligibility rule, diagnosis, care standard, worker obligation, staffing ratio, rationing entitlement, or emergency jurisdiction. It does not override Indigenous rights, disability rights, lawful professional standards, existing labor law, privacy rights, or requirements for competent decision-makers.

### 1.1 Principle of reciprocal freedom and essential dependency

> A just institutional order protects both the freedom to refuse unwanted contribution and the claim of dependent persons to competent, necessary care. The need for care establishes a collective responsibility to organize effective support. It does not, by itself, establish unlimited authority over another person's labor, body, or intimate conduct. Where both claims cannot be fully met, competent institutions must make the conflict visible, seek less restrictive solutions, protect the most serious needs, and provide review and remedy.

This is a proposed ethical and coordination principle. It is **not** a self-executing grant of legal rights, public funds, emergency power, or coercive labor authority.

## 2. Governing boundaries

### 2.1 Existing authority remains with its holder

- **AUBI implementing bodies** determine AUBI amounts, access, Layer 3 support, and relevant financial continuity under their constituting authority. They cannot order the delivery of healthcare, assign workers, certify clinical adequacy, or ration supplies on the authority of this interface.
- **Work in Liberation commissioning bodies and Community Providers** develop voluntary opportunities and provider capacity within their charters, including the eight-step Critical and Unpopular Work Protocol (§6.8). They cannot turn unmet demand into compulsory labor or decide a person's medical eligibility.
- **Competent health, disability, care, child-protection, emergency, and public-service authorities** determine their own service obligations, professional requirements, care/continuity plans, lawful priority decisions, safeguarding, and urgent responses. ECRAI does not supply those authorities or their clinical standards.
- **Financial Systems and Nested Economies** coordinate lawful finance and interregional resource support, but payment issuance cannot conjure unavailable real capacity.
- **Constitutional, Justice, and independently applicable emergency/protective-action instruments** determine any legally available restrictive power, notice, proportionate action, independent review, and remedy. Neither the existence of a shortfall nor the language of an emergency creates those powers.
- **Affected persons and communities**, including care recipients, informal carers, disabled people, Indigenous peoples, workers, and otherwise overlooked groups, have participation and challenge rights under applicable law and governing framework provisions; their experience is evidence, not an all-purpose proxy for professional adjudication.

### 2.2 No transitive authority or identity

A service need broadcast, AUBI attestation, health record, worker-matching profile, community-care narrative, emergency warning, and legal-status record remain functionally distinct.

Cross-domain continuity or disclosure requires a specific purpose, lawful authority, minimum attributes, provenance, consent where legally required, uncertainty marking, correction, review, expiration, and unlinking. Recognition of the same person in two contexts is **non-transitive by default** and does not produce a universal claimant identity or a shared private-life dossier.

The Aurora Accord and CIS govern their respective data and person-continuity functions where validly adopted. No automatic data exchange to labor matching, employers, immigration enforcement, police, reputation systems, Love Ledger, or public dashboards follows from a referral.

### 2.3 The dual-autonomy constraint

No care plan is complete solely because a staffing slot is filled: recipient consent, competent and safe assistance, accessibility, continuity, and preference matter. Conversely, a person's unmet care need does not make a specifically identified worker or family member conscriptable.

A qualified provider may have duties arising from their independently lawful professional role or existing contract. Those duties cannot be invented, expanded, or erased through this interface.

### 2.4 Evidence must distinguish mechanism from outcome

Money paid is not care delivered. A matched volunteer is not a qualified professional. A scheduled appointment is not the same as access. A registered need is not proof of diagnosis. A completed referral is not proof of accepted responsibility. An increase in Hearts activity is not evidence of improved welfare without independent outcome observations.

These distinctions constrain dashboards, funding reports, institutional performance reviews, and declarations that a case is resolved.

## 3. Trigger and classification

### 3.1 Trigger conditions

An ECRAI coordination route is potentially relevant when at least one of the following is credibly reported or independently observed:

1. a person cannot obtain an essential good or service despite a nominally adequate entitlement or ability to pay;
2. necessary qualified care is interrupted or unavailable, with foreseeable risk to life, health, safety, or basic daily functioning;
3. an essential service remains unfilled after reasonable Work in Liberation §6.8 measures or is repeatedly filled by emergency measures;
4. population-level indicators show serious disparities between payment entitlement and real access, especially for persons with disability, dependency, isolation, or inaccessible complaint channels; or
5. a lawful public authority identifies a distribution, production, workforce, infrastructure, or accessibility constraint requiring coordination across remits.

A single high-risk case may trigger urgent protective attention. The issue need not exceed a population, cost, popularity, or service-volume threshold.

### 3.2 Classify the relevant constraint; more than one may coexist

| Constraint | Illustrative indicators | Not a sufficient conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Monetary inadequacy | Essential payment shortfall; fiat-only expense; inaccessible redemption | That the service is available if the transfer increases |
| Physical supply gap | Missing goods, equipment, transport, housing, energy | That monetary inflation alone caused scarcity |
| Qualified-personnel shortage | No safe trained provider; recurrent specialist gaps | That arbitrary volunteers could substitute |
| Delivery or access barrier | Administrative queue, discrimination, disability barrier, inaccessible locality | That beneficiaries are unwilling or noncompliant |
| Allocation or distribution failure | Resources exist but do not reach groups with serious needs | That aggregate production needs to expand first |
| Structural capacity failure | Persistent vacancies, unsafe work, insufficient public commissioning | That declaring repeated emergencies is an adequate solution |
| Observation or identity error | Wrong-person record, missing population, misleading dashboard | That a reported gap is fraud or that the person is ineligible |
| Genuine aggregate scarcity | Total feasible short-run supply below legitimate demand | That a uniform cash cut is necessary or just |

A State Estimate Record may report multiple hypotheses and unresolved causation. The response must not pretend greater certainty than available evidence supports.

## 4. Response and handoff workflow

The following workflow specifies **coordination responsibilities** where the participating institutions have independently accepted the interface. Its terms do not replace statutory or professional response deadlines.

### Stage 1 — Protect immediate access and safety

When reported circumstances indicate imminent serious harm, the receiving body promptly directs the request to the competent existing emergency, care, or safeguarding route without waiting for complete diagnosis, a new identity credential, the completion of voluntary recruitment steps, or resolution of an AUBI dispute.

The body may provide assistance within its existing lawful competence. It may not represent a mere referral as emergency treatment or assume that care will be available until the receiving authority confirms its role.

### Stage 2 — Establish a bounded account of the failure

The originating authority records, using its existing Observation Contract, State Estimate Record, or Need Broadcast Contract:

- what essential outcome is missing and why time matters;
- the person's or affected group's account, consent and communication needs, and uncertainty;
- what is already available, funded, scheduled, or delivered;
- competing causal explanations and the limits of existing observations;
- suitable interim assistance that does not compromise safety or rights; and
- what falls outside the originating authority's mandate.

Public aggregate monitoring need not reveal individual identities. For an identifiable individual, the record belongs to the authorized service and is disclosed only as narrowly needed.

### Stage 3 — Test non-coercive capacity alternatives

Work in Liberation §6.8 supplies a default capacity sequence: improve working conditions and safety; pay and stable professional capacity; redesign; training/equipment; fair rotation among **consenting** workers; rights-compatible technology; wider voluntary recruitment; and durable public-service capacity.

The sequence must be adapted to urgency. Immediate care shall not be delayed merely to document each step; conversely, a life-threatening case does not justify skipping safety or competence requirements.

AUBI may correct its own payment-access failure, fiat/Hearts mix, or eligible needs supplement. It must report separately whether the actual service then became accessible.

### Stage 4 — Execute a purpose-limited handoff

A referral must identify:

1. the originating institution and independently competent receiving authority;
2. specific purpose and factual basis, including urgency and uncertainty;
3. the minimum information necessary to act, with relevant consent or other lawful disclosure basis;
4. assistance already provided and requested next action;
5. named role accountable for confirmation, correction, and follow-up (not a universal person identifier);
6. acknowledgment, refusal, or non-response, including time of exchange;
7. next review and urgent escalation route; and
8. treatment of unresolved rights, support obligations, records, and claims.

**Sent does not mean accepted.** Failure to obtain a receiving authority's acknowledgment is an unresolved handoff requiring renewed contact or escalation, not a completed case. A sender's duties continue to the extent required by its own valid mandate; duties are not silently transferred.

### Stage 5 — Competent service planning and disputed priorities

The competent care/service authority determines what care, continuity, triage, eligibility, and protection procedures are lawful and clinically appropriate, with recipient participation and independent review as applicable.

For continuing dependency, a continuity plan should address qualified backup, time-sensitive interruptions, communication and consent, disability accommodation, trusted relationships, safety, transition risk, and who can respond if the usual provider is unavailable.

Where genuine scarcity makes all timely needs impossible to meet, the lawful authority should record the evidence and ethically significant differences in severity, urgency, duration, accessibility, dependency, and vulnerability, and explain the basis for its decision and any permitted review. This interface supplies **no numerical priority score**, universal rationing rule, or presumption that those unable to pay, work, or speak for themselves have lesser claims.

### Stage 6 — Independent outcome validation

Review the actual outcome: care received and safe, essential need met, waiting time, continuity, recipient assessment, adverse effects, and any residual risk. Observe professional workload and labor rights independently. A transfer amount, a platform match, or an authority acknowledgment cannot substitute for this review.

An unresolved need remains visible in the responsible institutions' restricted registers until appropriately accepted handoff, independent review, or lawful closure. No care case becomes public merely to demonstrate dashboard transparency.

### Stage 7 — Structural learning and closure

Repeated shortfalls prompt capacity analysis through the appropriate public-service and Work in Liberation institutions. Reviews examine service commissioning, financing, skill development, retention, caregiver burnout, access inequalities, administrative barriers, and appropriate alternatives.

Any closure must identify what was resolved, what remains open, which actor holds remaining duties, correction and appeal paths, and record disposition. A project, pilot, or institution closing does not automatically terminate an active service duty or claim.

## 5. Acute rescue, continuing care, and civic duties

### 5.1 Different temporal and ethical problems

**Acute rescue** concerns an immediate, bounded risk. **Temporary continuity** concerns maintaining qualified service during a finite disruption. **Chronic care** concerns durable, professionally and relationally adequate support, potentially over many years.

These categories overlap but must not be collapsed. Professional qualifications, recipient consent, intimacy, dependence, recurrence, and the caregiver's conditions differ. A chronic shortage remains a structural problem even when each interruption is urgent.

### 5.2 No automatic compulsory labor

The following are **not** independently sufficient to authorize compulsory service:

- receipt of AUBI or other public support;
- a published need broadcast or an unfilled roster;
- a person's status as an able-bodied resident or member of a BAZ;
- participation in the Love Ledger, work matching, or a Community Provider;
- high moral urgency, public approval, or a declaration of unmet need;
- a crisis designation that lacks independent legal authority.

Any proposed legally compulsory public duty must be determined through an independently competent constitutional/legal process, including necessity, proportionality, democratic legitimacy where required, less restrictive alternatives, qualified service, exemptions, duration, compensation, review, and enforceable safeguards. **This interface makes no determination that a duty of this sort is universally permissible or universally impermissible.**

A person's right to necessary care and a potential worker's right against compelled intimate labor are both taken seriously; neither may be erased by administrative simplification. Where institutions cannot fully satisfy either, the unresolved moral and service conflict must remain reviewable, not converted into a hidden duty on an isolated family or worker.

### 5.3 Existing rescue and professional duties

This interface does not displace specific obligations that valid existing law or professional commitments establish. It also does not purport to create professional authority or lower clinical competence standards in emergencies.

## 6. Resource scarcity and fair burden

A shortage classification must distinguish:

- inability to pay for available supply;
- weak distribution despite available capacity;
- a transient production/staffing gap; and
- genuine constraints that cannot be remedied quickly at reasonable cost.

An identical nominal transfer is not equivalent to an equal ability to obtain what is needed. Disability, dependency, geography, caring responsibilities, and lack of access to markets can cause fundamentally unequal real burdens.

Where supply is genuinely insufficient, institutions should consider feasible resource expansion, procurement changes, interregional solidarity, staffing reforms, non-coercive task redesign, accessible alternatives, and targeted continuity protection. Any rationing or compulsory allocation requires independent valid authority; no new allocation power is created by ECRAI.

AUBI Layer 1 reduction remains governed by AUBI §5.1.3 and the financial and constitutional instruments that control such decisions. **Scarcity does not trigger an automatic uniform reduction.** Existing Layer 3 support cannot be treated as disposable merely because its beneficiaries are costly to serve.

Cost shifting onto informal caregivers, disabled persons, migrants, isolated people, or service workers must be explicitly considered. An indicator that improves because people stop asking for unavailable care is a warning of possible exclusion, not evidence of success.

## 7. Monitoring, epistemic rights, and data governance

### 7.1 Minimum observables

Each relevant institutional domain may monitor, within its authority:

| Dimension | Appropriate observation | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Effective access | Essential need met; delay; service quality; unmet demand | Counting transferred money as care |
| Recipient autonomy | Complaint access, consent, accommodation, continuity | Treating acceptance of an unsafe provider as freely given consent |
| Qualified capacity | Skills, vacancy duration, resilience, safe backup | Counting any volunteer as an interchangeable clinician |
| Worker autonomy | Refusals without retaliation; hours; unpaid labor; safety | Measuring reluctance as low social value |
| Distribution | Subgroup and geographic disparities with uncertainty | Using averages to erase a single life-threatening gap |
| Handoff | Acknowledged authority, unresolved risk, review date | Treating a sent referral as accepted responsibility |
| System response | Structural investments, independent access outcomes | Equating more need broadcasts or Hearts circulation with success |

### 7.2 Rights and protection against surveillance

Individuals must be able to understand and challenge consequential AUBI, work, or service-access conclusions in an accessible format, under each applicable framework's rights and legitimate confidentiality limits. Recipients cannot be required to disclose intimate experiences into public or multi-purpose datasets in order to make a basic urgent need visible.

No ECRAI score of contribution, dependency, neediness, employability, or deservingness is created. AUBI LMCI-M, LMCI-A, Love Ledger narratives, work matching, financial transactions, and care records remain separate. A person may need private advocacy and a service pathway without becoming visible to a centralized personal analytics system.

### 7.3 Independence and evaluation

Evaluation should distinguish sources sharing a provider, algorithm, funder, caseworker, or reporting incentive. Test whether apparent improvements reflect actual service delivery, selection of easier recipients, fewer complaints, coerced unpaid labor, hidden family burden, or a change in measurement. Use pre-intervention baselines, independent outcome channels, and uncertainty reporting wherever feasible.

## 8. Minimum compatibility and deployment gates

An implementation may claim ECRAI coordination readiness only after documenting:

1. a valid participating authority and consent to the interface for each relevant domain;
2. a working urgent route to competent care/emergency services;
3. separation of payment eligibility, medical/service decisions, worker matching, and legal status;
4. an actionable pathway for potentially life-threatening unmet care even for one person;
5. affected-person consent, accessibility, advocacy, safety, and independent review under applicable law;
6. a record of who receives responsibility and how handoff non-acceptance is managed;
7. controls against compulsory labor inferred from AUBI, BAZ membership, or an unfilled need;
8. qualified service criteria and realistic continuity/back-up arrangements;
9. minimum-disclosure, purpose-limited, non-transitive record linkage and correction under applicable data instruments;
10. separate observations for payment, commissioning, staffing, delivered service, and outcomes;
11. structural review of recurrent emergency deployment and chronic scarcity; and
12. closure and withdrawal plans preserving unresolved claims, duties, payments, and remedies.

**Fail-closed claim rule:** Where these conditions are missing, the implementation must not declare the interface operational or claim guaranteed real-resource adequacy. This restriction on claiming ECRAI conformance does **not** bar pre-existing lawful emergency assistance, essential benefit payment, or voluntary service. Missing interface readiness never justifies withholding independently owed care or income.

### 8.1 Adoption order

1. Competent authorities review the proposed interface and identify the specific legal mandates it connects.
2. AUBI and Work in Liberation adopt matching textual references and document version pins.
3. Care, disability, and other competent public-service systems separately accept any handoff responsibility in their remit.
4. Aurora/CIS and applicable justice/protective-action authorities verify data and coercion boundaries for the particular implementation.
5. A bounded non-coercive pilot validates outcomes, handoffs, and independent review.
6. Only then may participants publish an interface conformance statement scoped to the actual participating institutions and versions.

No existing constitutional, justice, privacy, emergency, employment, or healthcare authority is automatically replaced or created through this proposed adoption sequence.

## 9. Compound stress-test corpus

| ID | Case | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| EC-01 | A recipient has adequate AUBI but no qualified caregiver is available | Real-resource failure recorded; urgent care referral; payment is not falsely counted as care received |
| EC-02 | A single dependent person faces imminent harm | Urgency does not depend on population count; competent authority receives a reviewable alert |
| EC-03 | A worker refuses an additional life-saving shift | No automatic compulsory assignment, benefit sanction, or moral scoring; institutions continue seeking safe qualified alternatives |
| EC-04 | The same care shortage triggers repeated emergency rosters | Independent structural capacity review; no indefinite emergency exception |
| EC-05 | A caregiver is available but the recipient reports coercion or unsafe intimacy | Recipient consent and safeguarding trigger competent assessment; a filled roster does not close the need |
| EC-06 | A service recipient and worker have conflicting rights and no immediately adequate solution | Conflicting claims, feasible alternatives, unresolved harm, and competent decision route are explicitly documented; no invented authority |
| EC-07 | Equal nominal transfers reach people with very different disability-related needs | Substantive access is disaggregated and needs-sensitive; no automatic uniform benefit cut |
| EC-08 | One record is wrongly linked across AUBI, Housing, and care services | Correction and unlinking without source overwrite; no transitive data access |
| EC-09 | A receiving authority does not acknowledge the urgent handoff | Sender does not claim completed transfer; prompt lawful escalation and unresolved-duty tracking continue |
| EC-10 | Child under 16 has an unmet care or material need | Existing child/family authority and entitlements are identified or the gap acknowledged; no fictional AUBI child entitlement |
| EC-11 | A project closes while care recipients still depend on it | Active continuity responsibilities, wages, complaints, records, and safe handback are preserved |
| EC-12 | Care access indicators improve after harder-to-serve people withdraw complaints | Independent missing-population, disability-access, and harm review challenges the apparent improvement |

A stress test **fails** if it achieves the target by shifting uncompensated and involuntary care into families, removing a person's right to refuse work, hiding unserved people, or recoding an unmet need as an administratively complete transaction.

## Appendix B — Open validation items

Before an adoption or conformance claim, independently resolve:

1. the exact current versions and authority boundaries of Community Support Systems, applicable healthcare/child-protection standards, and any emergency protective-action specifications;
2. the AUBI document's existing CIS version references (its v2.6 related-frameworks list names CIS v0.2.6, while body provisions cite CIS v0.2.4); this interface does not adjudicate which is governing;
3. whether and how duty-to-rescue and civic-duty laws differ by jurisdiction; this specification neither supplies nor generalizes them;
4. applicable definitions of essential care, qualified scope of practice, consent capacity, substitute decision-making, and review deadlines;
5. pre-specified, rights-preserving shortage monitoring and case-closure measures appropriate to each implementation;
6. independently tested confidentiality, correction, purpose limitation, and no-transitive-linkage behavior; and
7. how to continue service and payment when a pilot, provider, funding channel, or coordination institution terminates.

## Closing principle

> **A person is not secure merely because an income entitlement exists. A person is not free merely because every contribution is voluntary while essential dependency remains invisible. Institutions must make both shortcomings visible, find rights-respecting ways to meet them, and remain accountable for whatever they cannot yet provide.**
